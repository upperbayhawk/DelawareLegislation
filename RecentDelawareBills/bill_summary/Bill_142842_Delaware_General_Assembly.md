## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Recognize **February 15, 2026** as **“International Angelman Day”** in the State of Delaware to honor individuals with Angelman Syndrome and raise public awareness.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Formally designates **February 15, 2026** as **“International Angelman Day”** in Delaware via a **Senate Concurrent Resolution No. 140**.
- Cites Angelman Syndrome as a **rare neurogenetic disorder** occurring in approximately **1 out of every 15,000 births**.
- Notes typical impacts: **developmental delays, seizures, sleep problems, lack of speech**, and an **unusually happy demeanor**, linked to **loss or mutation of the UBE3A gene**.
- States there is **currently no cure**, but researchers have confidence a cure is achievable.
- Highlights economic burden: average household faces **nearly $30,000** in treatment and care expenses and **nearly $43,000** in projected annual caregiver wage losses due to reduced work hours.
- References global scope: an estimated **500,000 people affected worldwide**; **50+ organizations** in **40+ countries** participate.
- Notes research funding: the Angelman Syndrome Foundation has invested about **$15.7 million** in research since **1996**.
- Contains no program creation, appropriations, mandates, or regulatory changes—only recognition.
