## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Senate Bill 205 to clarify that the bill’s Certificate to Operate (COP) is a **transmission certificate** related to the **transmission of electricity**, and to revise thresholds and decision criteria the Public Service Commission (Commission) must use when deciding whether to grant a COP.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Renames/clarifies the relevant permit as a **“Transmission certificate”** (a type of Certificate to Operate tied to electricity transmission).  
- Raises the project size threshold covered by the bill from **30 megawatts (MW) to 100 MW** (replacing “30” with “100” in multiple places).  
- Requires operators to meet **electric distribution company** requirements **in addition to** the Commission’s COP requirements.  
- Directs the Commission, when deciding whether to grant a COP, to consider whether **PJM Interconnection, L.L.C. (or successor)** has completed relevant **energy and capacity supply studies** for the applicable **transmission zone**.  
- Limits COP eligibility to facilities that will **interconnect into the local distribution grid**, either **directly** or **through an electric distribution company**.  
- Clarifies that a referenced portion of SB 205 is **not intended to restrict future energy projects solely to renewable energy**, by adding “**and baseload**” in relevant places.  
- Requires the Commission, when deciding whether to grant a COP, to consider impacts to the **public**, including **local water supply** and **state water supply in general**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More targeted regulation:** Clarifies the certificate is for **electricity transmission**, which may reduce confusion about what activity is being regulated (“Transmission certificate”; “transmission” inserted before “certificate”).  
- **Focus on larger facilities:** Increasing the threshold from **30 MW to 100 MW** narrows coverage to higher-capacity projects, potentially concentrating review on projects with larger grid impacts.  
- **Grid reliability/planning alignment:** Requiring consideration of whether **PJM** has completed relevant **energy and capacity** studies ties Commission decisions to regional planning/study processes.  
- **Water-supply protection:** Explicitly requires consideration of impacts on **local and statewide water supplies** when granting a COP.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced oversight for mid-sized projects:** Raising applicability from **30 MW to 100 MW** could exclude projects between **30–99 MW** from whatever requirements SB 205 imposes on covered projects.  
- **Additional compliance layer:** Operators must meet **electric distribution company** requirements **in addition to** Commission requirements, which may add procedural steps or constraints.  
- **Potentially limiting interconnection options:** The amendment states the Commission shall only grant a COP for facilities that interconnect into the **local distribution grid** (directly or via an electric distribution company), potentially excluding other interconnection configurations.

## Key Policy Changes
- Changes terminology from “Certificate” to **“Transmission certificate”** and inserts “**transmission**” to clarify scope.  
- Raises the covered project threshold from **30 MW to 100 MW** (multiple line edits).  
- Adds requirement that operators must comply with **electric distribution company** requirements **in addition to** Commission requirements to obtain a COP.  
- Replaces/rewrites COP decision criteria to require Commission consideration of PJM’s completion of relevant **energy and capacity** studies for the relevant **transmission zone**.  
- Adds a condition that the Commission shall only grant a COP for facilities that will **interconnect into the local distribution grid**, directly or through an electric distribution company.  
- Adds “**and baseload**” alongside “renewable” (clarifying projects are not limited only to renewable).  
- Requires Commission to consider impacts on **water supplies** (local and statewide) in COP determinations.
