## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To provide full access to civil justice for survivors of childhood sexual abuse in Delaware by eliminating time limits that would otherwise bar civil claims and by allowing such claims to be filed at any time, including retroactively; and to waive the State’s sovereign immunity for these civil actions.  

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Removes the statute of limitations for civil claims “based upon the sexual abuse of a minor by an adult,” allowing suits to be filed in Delaware Superior Court “at any time” after the abuse.  
- Makes the change retroactive by permitting claims previously barred by an expired statute of limitations (and other expired time limits) to be filed “at any time.”  
- Prohibits dismissal of actions filed or pending after the bill’s effective date solely because a civil statute of limitations expired.  
- Expands applicability to claims against any person or entity, including “the government,” and expressly waives the State’s sovereign immunity for these actions.  
- Keeps a provision allowing a defendant to recover attorneys’ fees if the court independently finds a false accusation was made “with no basis in fact and with malicious intent.”  
- Fiscal note: costs are “indeterminable; potentially significant” in FY2026–FY2028 due to unknown number of claims, potential settlements/judgments, and legal/insurance expenses.  
- The fiscal note anticipates potential reliance on the State Self Insurance fund for retroactive claims and possible need for sexual misconduct liability coverage decisions by the Insurance Coverage Determination Committee (18 Del. C. § 6502).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded access to justice:** The bill allows survivors to file civil claims “at any time,” including those previously time-barred, aligning with the stated intent to provide “full access to justice… regardless of when it occurred.”  
- **Accountability across institutions (including State government):** Applies to “any person, institution, public or private entity… and the government,” and waives sovereign immunity, enabling claims against State entities where previously barred.  
- **Prevents procedural dismissal based on time limits:** Bars dismissal of actions filed or pending after the effective date due solely to expiration of a civil statute of limitations.  
- **Deterrence and fact-finding through civil process:** By allowing claims long after events, survivors may pursue civil discovery and adjudication even decades later (bill findings cite average disclosure around 20 years).  

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potentially significant, unquantified State liability and legal costs:** Fiscal note states state agencies may face civil actions with settlements/judgments potentially “ranging from hundreds of thousands to millions of dollars” per case; overall cost is “indeterminable but potentially significant.”  
- **Insurance and self-insurance exposure:** Fiscal note indicates carriers generally do not cover claims made before coverage begins; because the bill is retroactive, settlements/judgments against State entities would be expected to be paid through the Self Insurance fund.  
- **Administrative/legal capacity strain:** Fiscal note notes Department of Justice may need to provide legal services and may require outside counsel due to expertise/staffing constraints, paid through the Self Insurance fund.  
- **Increased litigation volume risk (unknown):** The fiscal note repeatedly states there is no available data on the number of cases that may be brought.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date reference in statute:** § 8145(c) uses bracketed language “[the effective date of this act]”; the enacted bill would need to supply the final effective date language.  
- **Scope of retroactivity vs. final judgments:** The amended text permits filing of claims that were time-barred or had “a final judgment… rendered based on the expiration of any time limitation or other procedural bases,” but it is not specified how this interacts with doctrines of finality/res judicata beyond the text provided.  
- **Insurance program design:** The fiscal note describes that the Insurance Coverage Determination Committee “may need” to determine appropriate/available sexual misconduct liability coverage, but the bill text itself does not specify coverage requirements, limits, or funding.  
- **Gross negligence language for entity liability:** The introduced text shows prior “two year window” language (tied to July 9, 2007) including a “gross negligence” threshold for damages against a legal entity in that window; how that standard applies (or does not apply) to newly revived/any-time claims is not expressly clarified beyond the amendments shown.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Eliminates limitations period:** Amends 10 Del. C. § 8145(a) to allow a civil action for sexual abuse of a minor by an adult to be filed “at any time” following the act(s).  
- **Retroactive revival of time-barred claims:** Permits victims previously barred by expiration of a civil statute of limitations (and other expired time limits, including notice/presentation deadlines) to file claims “at any time.”  
- **Anti-dismissal rule:** Provides that no action filed or pending after the effective date may be dismissed due to expiration of a civil statute of limitations.  
- **Broad coverage of defendants + sovereign immunity waiver:** Applies to actions against “any person” and “any… public or private entity… and the government,” and states: “The State waives sovereign immunity” for these causes of action.  
- **Attorneys’ fees for maliciously false accusations (retained):** Allows recovery of attorneys’ fees only where the court finds the accusation was false, baseless in fact, and made with malicious intent; an acquittal/defense verdict alone is not sufficient.

## Who Benefits
- Survivors/victims of childhood sexual abuse in Delaware whose civil claims were previously time-barred or otherwise barred by expired procedural time limits.  
- Plaintiffs seeking to bring claims against institutions and public entities (including State government) due to the expanded applicability and sovereign immunity waiver.
