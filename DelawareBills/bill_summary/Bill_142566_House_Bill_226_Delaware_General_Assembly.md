## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Provide a **one-time supplemental appropriation of $37,632,955 in General Funds** for the **fiscal year ending June 30, 2026** to the **Office of Management and Budget (OMB), Contingencies and One-Time Items (10-02-11)**, and make related targeted appropriations and statutory changes (notably to **automated/automatic voter registration at DMV** and to **codify/continue the Delaware Grocery Initiative framework**).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Appropriates **$37,632,955 (General Funds)** for **FY2026** (fiscal year ending **June 30, 2026**) for numerous one-time items across state government.
- Funds several large initiatives, including **$5,000,000 for Operations (90-03-01)**, **$3,500,000 Health Care Services Contingency**, **$3,000,000 Teacher-Driven Projects**, **$2,000,000 Student Discipline Program**, and **$2,000,000 ERP Cloud Migration**.
- Provides **$300,000** for **Automated Voter Registration** costs at **DMV** and the **Department of Elections** to allow voters to **register party affiliation at DMV**, and amends **Title 15** to require AVR processes to capture **party affiliation choice**.
- Creates multiple appropriations that are **contingent on passage of other bills** (e.g., **HB 190, HB 42, HB 128, SB 4, SS1/SB 10, SB 11, SS1/SB 17, SB 32, SS1/SB 115, SB 7**).
- Provides **$1,350,000** for **Residential Lead Remediation** with possible use of **up to $350,000** for a **database/tracking system** if **HS1/HB 70 (or similar)** is enacted; requires a spending report by **May 4, 2026**.
- Provides **$1,485,000** for initial implementation of the **Smart Food Program** and requires an implementation/status report **upon implementation** (no date specified).
- Funds public safety wellness supports: **$400,000** to State Police and **$400,000** to the Fire Prevention Commission for **First Responder – Wellness Application Support**; exempts procurement law **29 Del. C. c. 69** “to any extent necessary.”
- Sets multiple education program reporting deadlines, including **April 1, 2026** (Teacher-Driven Projects report) and **May 1, 2026** (Student Discipline Program and School-Based Intervention Services Pilot reports).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **One-time funding to maintain services:** **$3,500,000** Health Care Services Contingency is intended to **supplement FY2026 reductions** to state-operated health programs.
- **Improved election administration and voter experience:** **$300,000** supports DMV and Elections systems so voters can **directly register party affiliation at DMV**, and AVR data transmissions must include **party affiliation choice**.
- **Targeted literacy support:** **$3,000,000** for Teacher-Driven Projects to enable teachers to buy **evidence-based literacy materials**, with priority to schools with **lowest literacy proficiency** and/or **TSI/CSI** designations.
- **Behavioral/discipline supports in schools:** **$2,000,000** for additional seats in existing alternative school programs statewide and **$1,000,000** for on-site interventions for disruptive behavior (K–12).
- **Public health/environmental benefit:** **$1,350,000** to the Lead-Based Paint Abatement and Remediation Fund for **residential lead remediation**, with optional investment in tracking/database capacity (**up to $350,000**) if enabling legislation passes.
- **First responder support:** **$800,000 total** for a wellness application intended to deliver support/resources to law enforcement and firefighters statewide.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **One-time funding may not sustain programs long-term:** The Health Care Services Contingency explicitly states agencies are intended to find **alternative state, federal, or private funding** in later fiscal years if programs continue.
- **Contingent appropriations create uncertainty:** Multiple line items cannot be spent unless separate bills pass (e.g., **HB 190; HB 42; SB 4; SS1/SB 10; SB 11; SS1/SB 17; SB 32; SS1/SB 115; HB 128; SB 7**).
- **Procurement law not applying (limited transparency/competition):** The Act states **29 Del. C. c. 69 shall not apply** for (1) the **nursing home survey** “to any extent necessary,” and (2) the **first responder wellness application support**.
- **Automatic voter registration changes may increase administrative workload:** DMV must transmit AVR information **at least daily**, and Elections must process party affiliation selections and notices; implementation timing depends on IT readiness (see Effective Dates).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Appropriation:** One-time supplemental appropriation of **$37,632,955** in General Funds for FY ending **June 30, 2026**, for specified one-time items.
- **Automated/Automatic Voter Registration (Title 15 amendments):**
  - AVR transactions at DMV cover issuance/replacement, renewal, and change of name/address for specified licenses/ID, including via DMV website.
  - DMV may **not** offer/register to vote when an individual provides documentation demonstrating **noncitizenship**, when DMV has information the person is not a citizen, or for **driving privileges card/permit under 21 Del. C. § 2711(d)**.
  - Voter registration component must include **choice of political party affiliation or choice to not be affiliated**.
  - DMV transmits data **electronically at least daily** in an agreed format.
  - Removes (repeals) various prior notice/primary-election provisions (shown as **[Repealed.]** in amendments), including repeal of the primary-day party enrollment rule in **§ 3161(b)**.
  - Requires Elections to notify individuals registered from **August 17, 2024** to the effective date of the new subsections that they may change/select political affiliation under **15 Del. C. § 2049(a)**.
  - Effective date for subsections (b)–(d) of Section 14 is tied to **publication in the Register of Regulations** of a notice by the State Election Commissioner that IT is fully operational.
- **Delaware Grocery Initiative statutory revisions (Title 16):**
  - Defines “Council” as the Delaware Council on Farm and Food Policy.
  - Provides that the Division (subject to appropriation and in consultation with the Council) **establish** the Delaware Grocery Initiative to expand access to healthy foods in **food deserts** and areas at risk, via **grants/financial assistance** to eligible food resources (including nonprofit/local government food resources or specialty grocers).
  - Removes a prior requirement for the Council to submit a food access strategy report by **June 1, 2025** (marked **[Repealed.]**).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Voter registration signature under penalty of perjury:** AVR voter registration component must include the individual’s **signature under penalty of perjury**.
- **Confidentiality requirement:** The fact that an individual declines to be registered to vote through AVR is **confidential** and may not be used for any purpose other than voter registration/election administration.
- **Program compliance conditions for education funding:**
  - Student Discipline Program: if used for personnel costs, funds may only cover the **state share** per **14 Del. C. ch. 13** schedules; districts must fund **at least 30%** of total cost via tuition mechanism described.
