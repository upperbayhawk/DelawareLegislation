## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Adopt the **Cosmetology Licensure Compact** in Title 24, Chapter 51, Subchapter III to facilitate **interstate practice and regulation of cosmetology**, improve **public access and safety**, reduce unnecessary licensure burdens, and support mobility (including for **active-duty military members and spouses**). (§ 5141; Synopsis)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Enacts Delaware’s participation in the **Cosmetology Licensure Compact**, enabling a new **multistate licensing program** for cosmetologists. (§ 5141; § 5144; Synopsis)
- A **multistate license** is issued by a cosmetologist’s **home state** and must be recognized by other **member states** as authority to practice (subject to each state’s scope-of-practice laws). (§ 5144(d), (g)-(h); § 5143(e))
- Eligibility for a multistate license requires an **active, unencumbered single-state license** in the home state. (§ 5144(a))
- Remote states may take adverse action against a person’s **authorization to practice in that remote state**, while only the home state may take adverse action against the **multistate license itself**. (§ 5147(a), (c))
- Establishes the **Cosmetology Licensure Compact Commission**, with **1 delegate per member state**, at least **one Commission meeting each calendar year**, and requirements for public notice of meetings and rulemaking. (§ 5149(b)(1), (b)(7), (f)(1))
- Creates a shared **data system** to exchange licensure and discipline information, including adverse actions and investigative information; some investigative information is restricted to member states. (§ 5150(c), (e)-(f))
- Commission rules generally have the **force of law** in member states, but are ineffective to the extent they conflict with a member state’s **scope-of-practice laws**. (§ 5151(b))
- Compact-wide effective date occurs when the compact statute is enacted into law in the **seventh member state**; the bill synopsis states the Compact is already “active” because enough states have enacted it. (§ 5153(a); Synopsis)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Increased workforce mobility**: A Delaware resident who qualifies and holds a multistate license may practice in all member states without obtaining separate single-state licenses for each state. (§ 5144(d); Synopsis)
- **Supports military families**: Allows an active military member or spouse to keep a designated home state during active-duty assignment, supporting continuity of licensure. (§ 5148)
- **Improved cross-state discipline and consumer protection**: Requires information sharing on adverse actions and investigations and participation in a shared data system. (§ 5143(a)(7)-(9); § 5150(c))
- **Maintains state scope-of-practice control**: Licensees must follow the scope-of-practice laws in the state where services are provided. (§ 5144(g); § 5146(a))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **New administrative obligations for Delaware** to participate in the data system and share specified information (including adverse actions and investigative information). (§ 5143(a)(6)-(9); § 5150(c))
- **Potential fees/assessments**: The Commission may levy an **annual assessment** on member states and impose fees on licensees to fund Commission operations. (§ 5149(g)(3))
- **Delaware subject to Commission rules with force of law** (subject to the scope-of-practice conflict limitation), and Delaware must comply with Commission rules to remain eligible. (§ 5143(a)(9); § 5151(b))
- **Compliance risk of default/termination**: If Delaware defaults and does not cure, membership can be terminated by a majority vote of delegates, with consequences and ongoing liabilities through termination. (§ 5152(b)(1)-(6))

## Educational Impact

## Health Impact
- Compact purpose includes improving **safety** of cosmetology services and enhancing states’ ability to protect **public health and safety**, prevent fraud and unlicensed activity. (§ 5141; § 5143(a)(2); § 5147; Synopsis)
- Enables information sharing on investigations and discipline to prevent practice under encumbered status across states. (§ 5150(c), (f); § 5147(e))

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds Delaware law adopting the **Cosmetology Licensure Compact** (new Subchapter III, §§ 5140–5155). (§ 1)
- Establishes and authorizes use of a **multistate cosmetology license** recognized across member states. (§ 5144(d); § 5143(e))
- Creates the **Cosmetology Licensure Compact Commission**, including governance, meetings, rulemaking, finance, immunity, and enforcement structure. (§ 5149; § 5151; § 5152)
- Requires use of a shared **data system** with specified categories of reported information (licensure data, adverse actions, investigative information, etc.). (§ 5150(c))
- Sets discipline jurisdiction: remote states can restrict authorization to practice in their state; home state controls the multistate license and deactivation across states when the multistate license is encumbered. (§ 5147(a), (c), (e))
- Provides special home-state designation rule for **active military members and spouses**. (§ 5148)

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware-licensed cosmetologists** who qualify for a multistate license and seek to practice in other member states. (§ 5144(d); Synopsis)
- **Active military members and spouses** who need licensure continuity while on active-duty assignment. (§ 5148; § 5141(4))
- **Consumers** in Delaware and other member states via enhanced cross-state discipline coordination and shared data. (§ 5141(2), (5); § 5150)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Delaware’s licensing authority** (administrative workload) due to participation requirements, reporting, and monitoring the data system. (§ 5143(a)(6)-(9); § 5150(f))
- **Licensees** who must pay Commission and/or state fees for multistate licensure/renewal (amounts not specified) and are subject to multi-jurisdiction enforcement. (§ 5144(f)(2); § 5149(c)(12); § 5147)
- **Licensees disciplined in a home state**: if the home state imposes adverse action on the multistate license, authorization to practice in all member states is deactivated until encumbrances are removed. (§ 5147(e))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Compact effective date (overall):** takes effect on the date the compact statute is enacted into law in the **7th member state**. (§ 5153(a))
- **Delaware bill effective date:** Not specified in source text.
- **Delegate vacancy deadline:** member state must fill a Commission delegate vacancy within **60 days**. (§ 5149(b)(5))
- **Commission meetings notice:** public meeting notice must be posted at least **30 days** in advance. (§ 5149(f)(1))
- **Emergency Commission meeting notice:** at least **24 hours** prior notice. (§ 5149(f)(2))
- **Executive Committee public meeting notice:** **5 business days** advance notice. (§ 5149(d)(4)(b))
- **Rulemaking notice:** notice of proposed rulemaking at least **30 days** before the meeting with the public hearing. (§ 5151(g))
- **Rule effective date:** generally not sooner than **45 days** after notice of adoption/amendment (non-emergency). (§ 5151(k)(3))
- **Emergency rule notice:** **5 days’ notice** with opportunity to comment; normal procedures must be applied retroactively as soon as possible, no later than **90 days** after the emergency rule effective date. (§ 5151(l), (l) last clause)
- **Rule rejection window:** a majority of member state legislatures may reject a rule within **4 years** of the rule’s adoption (by statute or resolution used to adopt the compact). (§ 5151(d))
- **Withdrawal from compact:** effective no earlier than **180 days** after enactment of repealing statute. (§ 5153(b)(1))
- After notice of withdrawal, withdrawing state must recognize compact licenses for at least **180 days**. (§ 5153(b)(3))
- After notice of termination, terminated state must recognize compact licenses for at least **180 days**. (§ 5152(b)(6))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Member state compliance requirements** include complaint investigation capacity, exam and education/training prerequisites, background/disciplinary/criminal history consideration procedures, data system participation, and information sharing on adverse actions and investigations. (§ 5143(a))
- **Discipline and enforcement structure**:
  - Home state exclusively may impose adverse action on the **multistate license**. (§ 5147(a))
  - Remote states may impose adverse action on the **authorization to practice** within that remote state (including cease and desist orders and encumbrances). (§ 5147(c)(1)-(2))
  - Home state adverse action on the multistate license **deactivates authorization to practice in all member states** until encumbrances are removed. (§ 5147(e))
- **Commission oversight and enforcement**:
  - Commission may issue default notices and offer training/technical assistance. (§ 5152(b)(1))
  - Failure to cure may result in **termination** by majority vote of delegates; terminated state remains liable for assessments/obligations through termination. (§ 5152(b)(3), (b)(5))
  - Commission may bring enforcement actions in **U.S. District Court for D.C.** or the federal district where the Commission has its principal offices; prevailing party awarded litigation costs and reasonable attorney’s fees. (§ 5152(d)(2))
  - Defaulting state may appeal to **U.S. District Court for D.C.** or the federal district where the Commission has its principal offices; prevailing party awarded costs and reasonable attorney’s fees. (§ 5152(b)(8))
- **Subpoena enforcement across states:** subpoenas must be enforced by courts in the state where witnesses/evidence are located; issuing authority pays witness fees, travel, mileage, and other required fees. (§ 5147(c)(4))
