## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 11 Del. C. § 787 to reduce barriers for victims of human trafficking to (1) assert a defense to prostitution or loitering charges, and (2) vacate certain convictions or juvenile delinquency adjudications and expunge related police and court records.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes the eligibility standard from crimes committed “as a direct result” of trafficking to crimes committed “as a result” of trafficking for (a) the prostitution/loitering defense and (b) vacatur/expungement relief.  
- Allows a person convicted or adjudicated delinquent of a non-violent felony (as defined in 11 Del. C. § 4201) to seek vacatur if the offense was committed “as a result” of being trafficked.  
- Establishes a process where the court **must summarily vacate** if the movant proves eligibility by **preponderance of the evidence** and the Attorney General either **consents** or **does not respond within 30 days** (or within an approved extension).  
- Makes motions to vacate and related proceedings/records **sealed/closed by default**, unless the court finds **good cause** to open/unseal.  
- Limits hearings: a hearing is **required** if the Attorney General **timely objects**; otherwise the court may proceed without a hearing and may request more information if needed.  
- Creates a presumption based on “official documentation” from a government agency that the crime was committed as a result of trafficking, but clarifies documentation is **not required** for relief.  
- If vacatur is granted, the court must dismiss underlying charges and indicia of arrest and must **discharge any unpaid** fines/fees/restitution/financial obligations; **paid amounts are nonrefundable**.  
- Enables expungement to be sought in the same motion; if vacatur is granted and expungement requested, expungement of police and court records is ordered, with authority for **immediate expungement** (including sua sponte).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader access to relief**: Replacing “as a direct result” with “as a result” expands eligibility for victims whose criminal conduct is related to exploitation but not strictly “direct.” (Bill text and Synopsis ¶(1), last paragraph)
- **Reduced retraumatization**: Uncontested motions can be resolved without a mandatory hearing, and the movant need not attend in person unless ordered; remote testimony is allowed. (§ 787(j)(2)d–f, j; Synopsis ¶(3))
- **Confidentiality protections**: Motions, hearings, and records are sealed/closed unless good cause supports opening, which may reduce risk of revictimization. (§ 787(j)(2)i; Synopsis ¶(2))
- **Clear timelines and standards**: Sets a **30-day** response window for the Attorney General and clarifies the proof standard is **preponderance of the evidence**. (§ 787(j)(2)d, f, i; Synopsis ¶(4))
- **Financial relief going forward**: Requires discharge of **unpaid** fines/fees/restitution/other obligations upon vacatur. (§ 787(j)(2)l.4)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits recovery of amounts already paid**: The bill states paid fines/fees/restitution/financial obligations are **nonrefundable**, even if the conviction/adjudication is vacated. (§ 787(j)(2)n)
- **Reduced public access to proceedings**: Sealing/closing by default limits transparency, with access only upon a “good cause” finding. (§ 787(j)(2)i)
- **Potential impacts on underlying-crime victims’ participation**: The Attorney General must contact “any victim of the underlying crime” and report the victim’s position if known, but the court can still summarily vacate when statutory conditions are met. (§ 787(j)(2)c–d)

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Definition boundary of “as a result”**: The bill changes the causation standard but does not define “as a result,” leaving interpretation to courts. (Change appears in § 787(h) and § 787(j))
- **Scope of “victim of the underlying crime” notice**: The statute requires the Attorney General to contact “any victim of the underlying crime sought to be vacated,” but does not specify procedures if multiple victims exist or if contact fails beyond “last known address or telephone number.” (§ 787(j)(2)c)
- **Interaction with “automatic mandatory expungement”**: The text states the vacated case “is eligible for automatic mandatory expungement” while also providing for expungement by court order; how these pathways operationally interact is not detailed in the bill. (§ 787(j)(3)a–b)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Defense standard updated**: For prostitution or loitering, an affirmative defense applies when the conduct was committed “as a result” (replacing “as a direct result”) of being a trafficking victim. (§ 787(h); Synopsis final paragraph)
- **Vacatur eligibility broadened**: Vacatur available for non-violent offenses (excluding violent felonies under 11 Del. C. § 4201) committed “as a result” of trafficking. (§ 787(j)(1); § 787(j)(2)b)
- **Standard of proof clarified**: Eligibility for vacatur is determined by **preponderance of the evidence**. (§ 787(j)(2)d; § 787(j)(2)l)
- **Summary vacatur mechanism**: Court must issue a vacatur order if eligibility is proven and the Attorney General consents or fails to respond within **30 days** (or extended time). (§ 787(j)(2)d)
- **Hearing rules revised**: Hearing required only upon timely objection; otherwise optional if court needs more information; court can dismiss without hearing if insufficient grounds. (§ 787(j)(2)e–g)
- **Confidentiality default**: Motions/hearings/records sealed/closed unless court finds good cause to open/unseal. (§ 787(j)(2)i)
- **Movant participation**: Movant not required to attend in person unless ordered; remote testimony allowed. (§ 787(j)(2)j)
- **Presumption from government documentation**: Official documentation creates a presumption the crime was committed as a result of trafficking, but documentation is not required. (§ 787(j)(2)k)
- **Mandatory relief elements upon vacatur**: Must dismiss underlying pleading/charges and indicia of arrest; enter final disposition in favor of movant; discharge unpaid financial obligations; may take additional appropriate action. (§ 787(j)(2)l.2–5)
- **Expungement integrated into motion**: Expungement can be requested within the same vacatur motion; if granted, expungement of police and court records is ordered; immediate expungement allowed sua sponte or on request. (§ 787(j)(3))
- **Court of Common Pleas pathway**: If CCP grants vacatur, CCP must send certified order to Superior Court; Superior Court must then order expungement. (§ 787(j)(4))

## Who Benefits
- Victims of human trafficking with eligible non-violent convictions or juvenile delinquency adjudications who seek vacatur and expungement. (§ 787(j))
- Individuals charged with prostitution or loitering who can assert the affirmative defense that the conduct was “as a result” of being trafficked. (§ 787(h))
- Movants seeking privacy protections through sealed filings and proceedings. (§ 787(j)(2)i)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Victims of the underlying crime(s) connected to the conviction being vacated, who may have limited visibility due to confidentiality and may see a case vacated despite their position (though DOJ must attempt contact and report position if known). (§ 787(j)(2)c, i)
- State and local justice agencies (courts, DOJ, law enforcement record custodians) that must process additional sealed matters and expungement orders under specified procedures and timelines. (§ 787(j)(2); § 787(j)(3)–(4))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Attorney General response deadline**: Must respond in writing within **30 days** of being served with the motion, unless an extension is granted. (§ 787(j)(2)d.2; § 787(j)(2)f)
- **Attorney General extension request deadline**: Must request an extension **within 30 days** of service; request must be in writing and show good cause (including difficulty obtaining records or locating a victim). (§ 787(j)(2)h)
- **General effective date of the Act**: Not specified in source text.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Service requirement**: A motion to vacate must be served on the Attorney General. (§ 787(j)(2)b.2)
- **Content requirements for motion**: Must be in writing and describe evidence/provide copies of any official documentation showing entitlement to relief. (§ 787(j)(2)b.1, b.3)
- **Confidentiality compliance**: Courts must seal/close the motion, related proceedings, and associated records (including docket entries), absent a good-cause order to open/unseal. (§ 787(j)(2)i)
- **Expungement order compliance**: When expungement is ordered, the order must state it is ordered under § 787(j)(3) or (j)(4) and specify applicability of referenced expungement provisions (including §§ 4372(e), 4376, 4377, and 10 Del. C. § 1019 as referenced). (§ 787(j)(3)–(4))
- **Financial obligations**: Upon vacatur, unpaid obligations must be discharged; previously paid obligations remain nonrefundable. (§ 787(j)(2)l.4; § 787(j)(2)n)
