## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 6, Chapter 25 (Subchapter XII) of the Delaware Code to regulate when and how sellers that regularly accept credit cards may impose credit card surcharges, and to restrict refusals to accept credit cards for larger transactions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Applies to any “person, retailer, or seller that regularly accepts payment by credit card” in a sales, service, or lease transaction.  
- Defines “surcharge” as any additional amount imposed for a credit card transaction that increases the transaction amount due to using a credit card.  
- For transactions **not in excess of $1,500**, a seller may not impose a surcharge **greater than the percentage processing fee charged by the credit card company**.  
- For transactions **in excess of $1,500**, a seller may not **refuse to accept payment by credit card**.  
- For transactions **in excess of $1,500**, a seller may not **impose any surcharge**.  
- Establishes a **$1,500 threshold** that changes both surcharge rules and acceptance requirements.  
- Bill text provided does **not** specify enforcement mechanisms, penalties, or an effective date.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Limits surcharge amounts for ≤ $1,500 transactions** by capping them at the “percentage processing fee charged by the credit card company,” which may reduce overcharging relative to card processing costs (Section 2601(1)(a)).  
- **Protects consumers/business customers on > $1,500 purchases** by prohibiting both refusal of credit cards and any surcharges (Section 2601(2)(a)-(b)).  
- **Creates clearer statewide rules** on credit card surcharges and acceptance practices tied to a specific threshold (**$1,500**) (Section 2601).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Restricts pricing flexibility for sellers** on transactions **over $1,500** by prohibiting any surcharge even if card acceptance costs are substantial (Section 2601(2)(b)).  
- **May increase costs for sellers** on transactions **over $1,500** because they must accept credit cards and cannot recoup processing costs through surcharges (Section 2601(2)(a)-(b)).  
- **Compliance complexity for sellers** may arise from needing to ensure surcharges for **≤ $1,500** do not exceed the applicable “percentage processing fee” (Section 2601(1)(a)).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds/updates definitions in Subchapter XII:
  - **“Credit card”** defined as any card/document allowing goods/services now with payment later (Section 2600(1)).  
  - **“Surcharge”** defined as an additional amount that increases the transaction amount due to using a credit card (Section 2600(2)).  
- Creates tiered surcharge/acceptance rules based on transaction size:
  - **Transactions ≤ $1,500:** surcharge may not exceed the **percentage processing fee** charged by the credit card company (Section 2601(1)(a)).  
  - **Transactions > $1,500:** seller may neither **refuse** credit cards nor **impose any surcharge** (Section 2601(2)(a)-(b)).

## Who Benefits
- **Consumers and purchasers** using credit cards, especially for **transactions exceeding $1,500**, who are protected from both surcharges and refusals of credit card payment (Section 2601(2)).  
- Purchasers in **transactions of $1,500 or less** who would otherwise be charged a surcharge higher than the card processing percentage fee (Section 2601(1)(a)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Sellers that regularly accept credit cards**, particularly for **transactions over $1,500**, because they must accept credit cards and cannot surcharge to offset processing costs (Section 2601(2)(a)-(b)).  
- Sellers who currently impose credit card surcharges **above** their processing percentage fee on **≤ $1,500** transactions would need to reduce those surcharges (Section 2601(1)(a)).
