## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 24 of the Delaware Code to expand the scope of practice for licensed occupational therapists (OTs) and occupational therapy assistants (OTAs) to include **dry needling**, and to set conditions and limitations on that practice.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds a statutory definition of **“dry needling”** and related terms in **24 Del. C. § 2002**.
- Expands “occupational therapy services”/“practice of occupational therapy” to explicitly include **dry needling** as a potential intervention.
- Creates a new section **24 Del. C. § 2022** governing dry needling by OTs/OTAs.
- Requires a **written physician referral specific to dry needling** before an OT/OTA may perform dry needling; referral **may be transmitted digitally**.
- Prohibits OTs/OTAs from advertising or holding themselves out as **acupuncturists** unless they are licensed acupuncturists.
- Authorizes the relevant Board to set **training and practice standards** for dry needling by regulation.
- Effective date: **6 months after enactment into law**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded treatment options:** Explicitly authorizes OTs/OTAs to use dry needling as part of occupational therapy interventions (added to the list of interventions within “occupational therapy services”).
- **Medical oversight requirement:** Patient access is conditioned on a **written physician referral specific to dry needling** (new § 2022(a)).
- **Clarity and consumer protection:** Prohibits misrepresentation as an **acupuncturist** unless separately licensed as one (§ 2022(b)).
- **Standard-setting authority:** Allows Board rulemaking on **training and practice standards**, which may promote consistent practice expectations (§ 2022(c)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Access barrier:** Dry needling cannot be performed without a **written physician referral specific to dry needling**, which may add an additional step before treatment (§ 2022(a)).
- **Regulatory uncertainty until rules issued:** The bill authorizes (but does not itself specify) Board-established **training and practice standards**, meaning key operational details may depend on later regulations (§ 2022(c)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds/clarifies definitions (24 Del. C. § 2002):**
  - Defines “**Dry needling**” as using a thin filiform needle to penetrate skin and stimulate muscular/connective tissues and myofascial trigger points for management of neuromusculoskeletal pain and movement impairments; based on Western medical concepts; and requiring an OT examination and diagnosis (§ 2002(4)(a)-(c)).
  - Defines “**Trigger points**” (§ 2002(14)).
- **Expands OT scope:** Updates the definition of “occupational therapy services”/“practice of occupational therapy” to include **dry needling** among listed interventions (within § 2002(8)/(9) as redesignated).
- **Creates dry needling practice restrictions (new 24 Del. C. § 2022):**
  - No dry needling without **written physician referral specific to dry needling**; referral may be **digital** (§ 2022(a)).
  - No advertising/holding out as **acupuncturist** unless licensed as an acupuncturist (§ 2022(b)).
  - Board authority to set **training and practice standards** by regulation (§ 2022(c)).
