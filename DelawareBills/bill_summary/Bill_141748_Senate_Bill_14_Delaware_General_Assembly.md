## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Proposes a constitutional amendment to **Article II of the Delaware Constitution** to change how **General Assembly (House and Senate) district boundaries** are established, primarily by creating an **Independent Redistricting Commission** and setting criteria, timelines, publication/public-hearing requirements, and judicial backstops for redistricting following each **federal decennial census**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates an **Independent Redistricting Commission (5 members)** with specified geographic residency slots (**Wilmington; New Castle County outside Wilmington; Kent; Sussex; and one statewide resident**) and a multi-step selection process in **years ending in “1”** (candidate slate by **January 8**; Commission seated by **February 10**).  
- Requires House and Senate **district boundaries** to be determined under **Section 2A**; sets redistricting criteria including **contiguity**, recognizable/natural boundaries, and anti-favoritism (not to “unduly favor any person or political party”), plus a stated **population deviation cap of ≤ 5%** (as described in the Commission criteria notes).  
- Establishes detailed **timelines** in years ending in **“1”**: **preliminary plan by the second Monday in May**, **final plan by June 30** (extendable to **no later than August 1**), and **General Assembly vote by October 1**.  
- Requires extensive **public transparency**: Commission meetings only as public meetings with **7 calendar days’ notice**, virtual participation, and multi-site publication of maps/reports (Commission website plus posting by the **Department of Election** and the **General Assembly** upon receipt).  
- Requires **4 public hearings** after preliminary plan publication (**one in each county and one in Wilmington**) with virtual participation and **7 days’ notice**.  
- Establishes **judicial backstops**: if key Commission deadlines are missed, the **Delaware Supreme Court** must approve/prepare a plan; the Supreme Court also has **original and exclusive jurisdiction** for challenges with a **30-day** petition window after adoption (as specified).  
- Sets commissioner restrictions and ethics rules: disqualifications based on recent political/lobbying roles (**5-year lookback**) and party-switch restrictions (**3 years**), plus **5-year** post-service bans on running for the General Assembly (after plan adoption) and registering as a lobbyist (after service).  
- **Ambiguity/possible conflict in notes:** one portion of the notes also describes an older/alternate process in which the **Governor files a plan within 120 days** after official decennial census reporting (with publication and Superior Court enforcement). The staged notes do not clearly show how that gubernatorial process is reconciled with the Independent Commission framework.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Independence/anti-gerrymandering guardrails (text-based):** prohibits drawing districts to “unduly favor any person or political party,” restricts use of partisan/incumbency information (as described), and requires compliance with federal law including the **Voting Rights Act**.  
- **Transparency:** requires only public meetings, **7-day notice**, virtual participation, and publication of maps/reports across multiple state websites.  
- **Public input structure:** mandates **4 public hearings** and creates **four 12-member Advisory Committees** (Wilmington; New Castle excluding Wilmington; Kent; Sussex) to gather regional input and hold public hearings.  
- **Enforceable timelines:** multiple fixed deadlines and Supreme Court backstops reduce the chance of prolonged delay after a decennial census.

## Key Policy Changes
- Establishes an **Independent Redistricting Commission (5 members)** with:
  - Geographic residency requirements (including a required **Wilmington** seat and seats for **New Castle County outside Wilmington**, **Kent**, **Sussex**, and **statewide**).  
  - Candidate screening/selection timeline in years ending in **“1”** (candidate slate by **January 8**; Commission selected by **February 10**; strike deadlines include **not later than 7 days before** Feb. 10).  
  - Eligibility rules (e.g., no party-switch in the prior **3 years**; no lobbyist/party officer/campaign officer/elected or paid appointed official in prior **5 years**).  
  - Post-service restrictions: **5-year** ban on running for General Assembly (after plan adoption) and **5-year** ban on registering as a federal/state lobbyist (after service).  
  - Compensation of **$150/day** plus reasonable expenses.  
  - Voting rules: **3-member quorum**; most actions require **3 votes**; adopting a final/revised final plan requires **4 votes**, including the unaffiliated/other-party commissioner (as described).  
- Requires public process features:
  - Public meetings only, **7-day notice**, virtual participation, and a Commission website with required postings.  
  - **4 public hearings** after preliminary plan publication (one per county + Wilmington).  
  - Four **12-member Advisory Committees** (regional) with hearing and recommendation roles.  
- Sets redistricting plan deadlines (years ending in **“1”**):
  - Preliminary plan/report by the **second Monday in May** (extendable by majority vote in each House).  
  - Final plan/report by **June 30** (extendable to **no later than August 1** by majority vote in each House).  
  - General Assembly vote deadline: **October 1**.  
  - If not enacted within **5 calendar days** after first consideration, triggers revised-plan timelines (including Commission submission within **25 days** of beginning preparation and General Assembly action within **7 days** after submission).  
- Establishes judicial roles:
  - **Supreme Court** approval/preparation of plans if Commission deadlines are missed; Supreme Court revised plan can **become law** in specified failure scenarios.  
  - Supreme Court has **original and exclusive jurisdiction** for certain petitions; **30-day** filing window after adoption; **any Delaware resident** has standing (as described).  
- Provides recurring reporting/records requirements:
  - By **December 1** of each year ending in **“1”**, Commission must publish/submit a process-improvement report; agencies must publish it on their websites.  
  - Records retained for at least **20 years** after Commission cessation; unspent funds revert to the **General Fund**.
