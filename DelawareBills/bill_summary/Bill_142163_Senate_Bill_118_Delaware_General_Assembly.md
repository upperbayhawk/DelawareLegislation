## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Remove the sunset provision that would otherwise cause Delaware’s expanded ability for pet owners to recover compensatory damages for veterinary bills (for injuries/death to lawfully owned pets caused by another’s negligence, recklessness, or intentional acts) to expire on **October 14, 2025**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Amends **Chapter 472, Volume 83 of the Laws of Delaware** (the act that previously expanded recoverable damages for injured pets).
- The prior law included a sunset that made the changes temporary; this bill eliminates that sunset.
- As described in the synopsis, the underlying policy allows pet owners to pursue **compensatory damages without limitation for veterinary bills** for a pet injured by the **negligent, reckless, or intentional acts** of another person or that person’s pet.
- Practical effect: keeps the “no limitation for veterinary bills” recovery provision in place beyond **10/14/2025**.
- The enacted law (formerly SB 118) was **approved August 20, 2025**.
- No dollar amounts, caps, or thresholds are set in this bill text itself; it operates by removing an expiration date.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Continuity of legal rights:** Prevents the expanded civil remedy for veterinary-bill damages from ending on **October 14, 2025**, maintaining a consistent rule for courts and litigants.
- **Potentially fuller compensation for pet owners:** Preserves the ability (from the prior act) to seek **compensatory damages without limitation for veterinary bills** when injuries are caused by another’s negligence, recklessness, or intentional acts.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potentially increased civil liability exposure:** By preventing expiration of the expanded remedy, individuals whose negligence/recklessness/intentional acts injure another’s pet (or whose pet causes injury) may continue to face **uncapped veterinary-bill damages** (as described in the synopsis).
- **Extended litigation risk/costs:** Keeping the remedy in place may prolong or increase civil claims related to pet injuries (the bill keeps the remedy alive by removing the sunset).
