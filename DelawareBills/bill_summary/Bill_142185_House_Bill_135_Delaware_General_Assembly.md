## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 10 of the Delaware Code to create a new Chapter 82 establishing legal protections for “individuals experiencing homelessness,” including the right to conduct specified life-sustaining activities in public spaces (subject to safety/traffic limits), enhanced protections for personal property and vehicles, an affirmative defense in related prosecutions, and state/private enforcement mechanisms (including waiver of sovereign immunity).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates **Chapter 82: “Protections for Delawareans Experiencing Homelessness”** in **Title 10** and names it the **“Dr. DeBorah Gilbert White Anti-Cruelty to Delawareans Experiencing Homelessness Act.”**
- Allows individuals experiencing homelessness to conduct **“life sustaining activities”** in **public spaces**, so long as activities **do not obstruct pedestrian/vehicular traffic in a manner that creates a hazard**, **unless** the jurisdiction has **sufficient “adequate alternative indoor space”** that has been **offered**, including **transportation for the individual and their belongings**.
- Defines **“adequate alternative indoor space”** with specific conditions, including: **free of charge**, **available indefinitely without daily re-application**, **accepts pets**, **accommodates disabilities**, **allows partners/family/support persons**, and **accommodates personal property**.
- Requires the same degree of protection for personal property stored in public places as property in a private dwelling, including **protections against unreasonable search and seizure**.
- Limits government authority to require moving **motor vehicles or recreational vehicles** parked on public property unless they obstruct traffic or create a hazard; requires a chance to **relocate before ticketing/towing**, and allows retrieval **free of charge or at a reduced rate** based on ability to pay.
- Creates an **affirmative defense** to charges criminalizing protected life-sustaining activities when no adequate alternative indoor space existed; courts must **notify defendants of the defense before a plea is entered**, and prosecution bears the burden once raised.
- Authorizes enforcement via **Attorney General civil actions** and a **private right of action**; mandates **prevailing-plaintiff attorney’s fees**, and **waives sovereign immunity** for suits under the chapter.
- Fiscal note: costs are **“Indeterminable, Potentially Significant”** for **FY 2026, FY 2027, FY 2028**, due to potential litigation/settlements and additional complaint investigations.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces criminalization of basic survival behavior in public** by permitting enumerated “life sustaining activities” in public space under specified conditions (§8202; definitions in §8201).
- **Stronger property protections** for unhoused individuals by requiring protections equivalent to private dwellings, including against **unreasonable search and seizure** (§8202(b)).
- **Vehicle stability protections** for people living in cars/RVs by limiting forced movement/towing unless there is an obstruction/hazard and requiring an opportunity to move before enforcement (§8202(c)-(d)).
- **Improves access to a legal defense** in relevant prosecutions by creating an affirmative defense, requiring court notice before plea, and shifting burden to prosecution once raised (§8203).
- **Enables enforcement and compliance incentives** through AG authority, private lawsuits, injunctive relief, and mandatory fee-shifting to prevailing plaintiffs (§8204–§8206).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potentially significant state/local legal and settlement costs** because the bill **waives sovereign immunity** and allows both AG and private suits; fiscal note states costs are **indeterminable, potentially significant** in **FY 2026–FY 2028**.
- **Administrative workload increases**: the Division of Human and Civil Rights may receive **additional complaints**, with indeterminable resource needs (Fiscal Note assumption #5).
- **Constraints on local enforcement tools**: limits ability to prohibit or criminalize covered life-sustaining activities in public spaces unless “adequate alternative indoor space” (as defined) is available and offered (§8202(a); §8203).
- **Parking/towing operational impacts**: requires allowing relocation before ticket/tow and requires free/reduced-cost retrieval based on ability to pay (§8202(d)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What constitutes **“sufficient”** adequate alternative indoor space “in a given jurisdiction” for purposes of §8202(a) and §8203 (the chapter defines “adequate alternative indoor space,” but not “sufficient” capacity thresholds).
- How “**disproportionately enforced**” time restrictions will be measured or proven (§8202(e)).
- The standards and process for determining **“ability to pay”** for free or reduced-rate vehicle retrieval (§8202(d)).
- What specific remedies beyond injunctive/equitable relief may be considered “**appropriate equitable relief**” (§8205(c)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Title 10, Chapter 82**: “Protections for Delawareans Experiencing Homelessness.”
- Defines key terms including:
  - **“Individual experiencing homelessness”** (broad definition including living in cars, parks, shelters, motels due to lack of alternatives, etc.) (§8201(2)).
  - **“Life sustaining activities”** (includes sleeping, eating, resting, storing personal property as needed to safely shelter oneself, etc.) (§8201(3)).
  - **“Public space”** (broadly includes property owned/leased by government, easements held open to public, and certain federal lands under state/local jurisdiction) (§8201(4)).
  - **“Adequate alternative indoor space”** with specific required features (free, indefinite availability, pets, disability accommodation, allows partners/family/support persons, personal property, etc.) (§8201(1)).
- **Permits life-sustaining activities in public** with hazard/traffic limitation and an exception tied to availability and offer of adequate alternative indoor space (including transportation) (§8202(a)).
- **Property protection parity** for property stored in public places (§8202(b)).
- **Limits forced movement of motor vehicles/recreational vehicles** and creates relocation/retrieval requirements, including free/reduced retrieval based on ability to pay (§8202(c)-(d)).
- Allows **reasonable time restrictions** if applied equally and not disproportionately enforced against homeless individuals (§8202(e)).
- Establishes **affirmative defense** and required **court notice before plea**; rebuttable presumption and prosecution burden once defense raised (§8203).
- Authorizes **AG civil enforcement** (§8204).
- Creates **private right of action**, **injunctive relief**, **mandatory prevailing-plaintiff attorney fees**, and limits fee exposure for non-frivolous suits (§8205).
- **Waives sovereign immunity** for state/local governments and officials for suits under the chapter (§8206).
- Includes **severability** (§8207).

## Who Benefits
- **Individuals experiencing homelessness** who engage in life-sustaining activities in public spaces, store property in public, or live in/operate from vehicles on public property (Chapter 82 generally; §§8202–8203).
- **Organizations advocating for or serving unhoused individuals** that may bring suit if “adversely affected” (§8205(a)).
- **Plaintiffs who prevail** in civil actions under the chapter via mandatory fee/cost awards (§8205(d)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State and local governments, agencies, and officials** subject to civil actions, injunctive relief, attorney-fee awards, and loss of sovereign immunity defenses (§§8204–8206; Fiscal Note).
- **Law enforcement and municipal enforcement operations** that must adjust practices regarding public-space enforcement and vehicle ticketing/towing procedures (§8202; §8203).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon signature by the Governor** (Fiscal Note assumption #1).
- Court obligation: must notify charged individual of the affirmative defense **“before a plea is entered”** (§8203).
- No other specific dates or implementation deadlines specified in source text.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Affirmative defense** in criminal/ordinance cases involving life-sustaining activities protected under §8202 when no adequate alternative indoor space existed; **rebuttable presumption** and **prosecution burden** after the defense is raised (§8203).
- **Attorney General enforcement**: AG may commence civil action in **Superior Court** against state/local government entities or officials that violate the chapter (§8204).
- **Private right of action**: any adversely affected individual or organization may sue in **Superior Court** (§8205(a)).
- Remedies:
  - Court must **set aside** unlawful limitation/practice (§8205(b)).
  - Court may grant **temporary, preliminary, or permanent injunctive relief** (§8205(c)).
  - Court **shall** award **litigation costs and reasonable attorney’s fees** to prevailing plaintiffs; plaintiffs generally not liable for defense fees/costs in **non-frivolous** actions (§8205(d)).
- **Sovereign immunity waived** for suits under this chapter (§8206).
