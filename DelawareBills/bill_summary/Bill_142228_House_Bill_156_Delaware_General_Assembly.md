## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Update Delaware’s Healthcare-Associated Infections Disclosure Act (Title 16, Chapter 10A) to (1) modernize and correct terminology and drafting, and (2) align reporting and related processes with CDC National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) requirements, while maintaining public reporting, privacy protections, and enforcement mechanisms.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates statutory terminology throughout Chapter 10A (e.g., “health-care” to “healthcare”; “psychiatric” to “behavioral health” in facility references; updates mental-health phrasing).
- Reaffirms the reporting pipeline: clinicians report certain infections back to the facility; facilities report to DHSS only infections meeting accepted NHSN definitions and “currently required to be reported by law.”
- Keeps hospital quarterly reporting to DHSS and public disclosure structure, including: **data period ending not earlier than 45 days** before submission; hospital review access **45 days after submission**; and a **7-day** hospital review/correction window before public release.
- Maintains DHSS annual report to the legislature and website publication; statute still references first annual report timing (**no later than June 30, 2009**) and quarterly public updates after the initial report.
- Allows DHSS (with Advisory Committee consultation) to revise categories of reported infections after **June 30, 2010** (as existing law, with technical edits/clarification).
- Updates Advisory Committee composition and terminology; changes the “other members” count from **8** to **5**, and revises member descriptions.
- Retains enforcement tools: licensure sanctions (including possible termination) and civil penalties up to **$500 per day per violation**.
- Law is enacted as **Chapter 168, Volume 85** and was **approved August 25, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer, more consistent statutory language** (technical corrections and updated terms), potentially reducing confusion in compliance and implementation.
- **Alignment with CDC NHSN definitions/requirements** is explicitly reinforced in multiple sections (e.g., hospital quarterly reports use NHSN definitions), supporting standardized, comparable infection data.
- **Structured transparency with built-in hospital review**: hospitals receive quarterly reports **45 days after submittal** and have **7 days** to propose corrections before information becomes public.
- **Privacy protections remain explicit** (no patient/employee/provider identifiers; no release of Social Security numbers or identifying information).
- **Ongoing oversight capacity is maintained** through DHSS compliance authority and condition-of-licensure enforcement.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Compliance and administrative burden remains** for hospitals and DHSS due to quarterly reporting, risk adjustment, public reporting, and review processes (requirements are retained and reiterated).
- **Civil penalty exposure continues**: up to **$500/day/violation** and potential licensure sanctions for facilities found in violation.
- **Advisory Committee size reduction** (from **8 other** members to **5 other** members) may reduce stakeholder representation breadth compared to prior law (change is explicit; effect on representation is not otherwise described).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The Act states it updates for “adherence to CDC NHSN requirements,” but **does not enumerate specific new NHSN requirements** being adopted beyond the existing reliance on NHSN definitions/methodologies.
- The statute continues to include legacy dates (e.g., **June 30, 2009** first annual report; **December 31, 2007** NHSN membership), but the bill text **does not clarify** how these historic deadlines operate going forward other than technical edits.
- “Only those infections that meet the accepted NHSN definitions and are currently required to be reported by law” is retained, but the bill text **does not list** which infections are “currently required” (left to law/regulation).
- DHSS may require non-hospital/correctional facilities to report only after certain federal conditions; the bill text **does not define** what constitutes “final federal regulations requiring such” beyond referencing CMS or CDC.

## Health Impact
- Focuses on surveillance, reporting, and public disclosure of **healthcare-associated infections** and related quality measures (examples in statute include **surgical site infections**, **central line-associated bloodstream infections**, and **healthcare personnel influenza vaccination rates**).
- Requires DHSS annual report to compare Delaware hospital infection rates to **national rates published by CDC NHSN**, and to include a plain-language executive summary discussing “findings, conclusions and trends,” including comparisons to prior years.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Terminology modernization** across Chapter 10A (e.g., “health-care” → “healthcare”; “infection control” phrasing shifted toward “infection prevention”; “psychiatric” → “behavioral health” facility terminology; “mentally ill persons” → “persons with a mental illness”).
- **Advisory Committee composition changes**:
  - Specifies “One Infection prevention professional” for each hospital or healthcare system in Delaware.
  - Changes physician expert count from **4** to **3** (“infection disease physicians with expertise in infection control/prevention”).
  - Changes “8 other” members appointed by the Secretary to **5 other** members, with specified representation categories (direct care nursing staff; academic researchers; consumer organization; insurers; population health organizations).
- **Technical clarifications/cleanup** to align with the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (as stated in the Synopsis), without altering the core structure of reporting/public disclosure, penalties, and privacy protections.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Penalties (Section 1010A):**
  - Possible **termination of licensure** or other licensure-related sanctions.
  - **Civil penalty up to $500 per day per violation** for each day the facility is in violation.
- **Compliance/oversight (Section 1011A):**
  - DHSS is responsible for ensuring compliance.
  - Compliance with Chapter 10A is a **condition of licensure** when DHSS licenses a healthcare facility under Title 16.
- **Confidentiality/FOIA limits (Sections 1005A(g), 1013A, 1009A):**
  - Certain hospital performance-improvement commentary is proprietary and **not subject to FOIA** (Title 29, Ch. 100).
  - Infection data provided under the chapter is **privileged** and generally not admissible/disclosable in proceedings, with exceptions for reporting/public reporting sections (§§ 1003A, 1004A, 1005A).
  - No reports may include identifying information for patients/employees/licensed professionals; Social Security numbers and other identifiers may not be released.
