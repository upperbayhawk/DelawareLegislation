## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To encourage and authorize “child-centered, play-based learning” in public-school early childhood grades (prekindergarten through 3rd grade) and to support professional development and potential Department of Education regulations related to play-based learning. (Title 14, new Chapter 48 “Delaware Play to Learn Program”)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Permits early childhood educators in public schools (pre-K through 3rd grade) to use and encourage play-based learning in classrooms and curriculum.  
- Establishes a “Delaware Play to Learn Program” in Title 14, including definitions for play, guided play, play-based learning, and early childhood education/educators.  
- States legislative intent to encourage Local Education Agencies (LEAs) and the Department of Education (DOE) to include play-based learning “in all curricula domains” from preschool through 3rd grade.  
- Allows (but does not require) early childhood educators to create play-based learning environments, including child-directed experiences, movement, creative expression, exploration, reading for pleasure, art, music, and dramatic play.  
- Allows (but does not require) LEAs to provide ongoing professional development in play-based learning, including existing DOE-developed programs.  
- Permits DOE to promulgate regulations to implement the Act.  
- Fiscal note: local school costs are “indeterminable” for FY 2026–FY 2028; professional development cost components include **$80–$310 per educator** for an online training option, **$750 per educator** for four after-school PLCs (EPER), possible substitutes at **$179/day**, and **$200,000** estimated for statewide facilitated sessions for professional development days.  
- Effective date assumption in fiscal note: effective **upon signature by the Governor**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Explicit authorization for play-based learning:** The bill affirmatively permits educators to “create a learning environment that facilitates play-based learning,” potentially reducing uncertainty about whether such methods are allowed in early grades.  
- **Broader developmental focus:** The program language includes physical, social, emotional, cognitive, and academic learning opportunities across curricular domains, including unstructured time to discover student needs/abilities.  
- **Professional development pathway:** LEAs are permitted to provide ongoing training, including existing DOE programs, which could support consistent implementation where adopted.  
- **Regulatory implementation option:** DOE is permitted to promulgate regulations, which could provide clarity/standards if DOE chooses to act.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Uncertain and potentially significant local costs:** The fiscal note states FY 2026–FY 2028 local costs are “indeterminable,” with multiple cost drivers (training fees, EPER pay, substitutes, facilitated sessions).  
- **No required funding source in the bill text:** Fiscal note assumes “all costs…paid through local funds,” which may strain LEA budgets if widely implemented.  
- **Implementation variability:** Because educator use and LEA training are permissive (“may”), adoption may be uneven across districts and schools.  
- **Potential compliance/administrative burden if regulations are issued:** DOE rulemaking authority could add administrative tasks for LEAs/educators, depending on future regulations (details not specified in bill text).
