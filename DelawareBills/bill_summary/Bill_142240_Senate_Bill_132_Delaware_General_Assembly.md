## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Delaware’s Residential Landlord-Tenant Code (Chapter 56, Title 25) to update and clarify the “Right to Representation” framework for eligible tenants, including shifting certain duties to the Attorney General’s State Court Administrator, clarifying key definitions, and extending the implementation phase-in deadline.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Clarifies definitions for the Right to Representation program, including “Coordinator,” “covered individual,” “covered proceeding,” “designated organization,” “Legal Aid Agencies,” and “legal representation.”  
- Expands/clarifies the types of “covered proceedings” to include certain tenant-initiated enforcement matters (e.g., retaliatory actions, habitability, illegal eviction, quiet enjoyment issues).  
- Assigns the **Right to Representation Coordinator (within Delaware Volunteer Legal Services)** responsibility to manage contracts funded through the Attorney General’s State Court Administrator and to lead outreach/education activities (subject to funding availability).  
- Directs the **Attorney General, within the limits of a General Assembly appropriation**, to designate and contract with one or more nonprofit “designated organizations” to provide services and to provide “fair and just compensation” supporting quality representation.  
- Creates a specific funding pass-through mechanism: **the State Court Administrator must provide Delaware Volunteer Legal Services (DVLS) the designated appropriation** for Legal Aid Agencies’ services, notwithstanding Chapter 69 of Title 29.  
- Establishes annual reporting deadlines: **designated organizations report by August 31**; Coordinator report **by November 30** (approved by the Attorney General’s State Court Administrator) and posted online.  
- Adds reporting metrics about **Justice of the Peace Court Form 50 agents** (Rule 57) and requires **Form 50 be updated** to include an **optional** field requesting number of units owned/managed.  
- Extends the full phase-in deadline for legal representation to **no later than June 30, 2028** (from **November 22, 2026**), contingent on sufficient appropriations.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer eligibility and scope:** More explicit definitions of “covered proceeding” include tenant matters like habitability and illegal eviction, which may broaden access to representation in practice.  
- **Stronger program administration and oversight:** Annual reporting requirements (including expenditures and outcome metrics) increase transparency and accountability.  
- **Education and outreach structure:** Requires (subject to funding) know-your-rights trainings, written information distribution, and tenant association outreach—potentially improving compliance and early dispute resolution.  
- **Quality-of-representation emphasis:** Requires “fair and just compensation” and organizational structures with appropriate supervision/caseloads to promote “high-quality legal representation.”  
- **Implementation realism:** Extending the phase-in deadline to **June 30, 2028** may align rollout with appropriated funding and capacity.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Conditioned on funding:** Multiple obligations are explicitly **subject to appropriations/availability of appropriate funding**, which may limit impact if funding is insufficient.  
- **Added administrative workload:** Annual reporting by August 31 and November 30, plus expanded metrics (including Form 50 agent experience data), increases administrative burden for designated organizations and the Coordinator.  
- **Potentially longer wait for full access:** Extending the phase-in from **November 22, 2026** to **June 30, 2028** delays the date by which full implementation must occur.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Definitions updated/clarified** (§5601), including:  
  - Coordinator located within **Delaware Volunteer Legal Services (DVLS)**.  
  - “Covered proceeding” includes eviction/tenancy/housing subsidy termination proceedings and certain landlord-tenant law violation proceedings (retaliation, habitability, illegal eviction, quiet enjoyment).  
  - “Legal representation” includes notice of appearance filing and may include nonattorney representation if permitted by Delaware Supreme Court rules.  
- **Coordinator duties** updated to include contract management and specified outreach/education activities (subject to funding) (§5603).  
- **Attorney General / State Court Administrator role** clarified: designation and contracting with nonprofit designated organizations and funding flow to DVLS notwithstanding Title 29, Chapter 69 (§5604).  
- **Annual reporting structure and required metrics** expanded, including expenditures, outcomes, and Justice of the Peace Court Form 50 agent experience metrics; report must be posted online (§5605).  
- **Implementation phase-in deadline extended** to **no later than June 30, 2028** (from **November 22, 2026**) if sufficient funding is appropriated (§5607).

## Who Benefits
