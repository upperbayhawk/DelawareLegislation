## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 26 to (1) prohibit regulated public utilities from recovering specified non-utility costs from customers (including lobbying, certain dues, political contributions, charitable giving, and certain advertising/PR), and (2) require the Delaware Public Service Commission (PSC) to ensure customer funds are not used to subsidize nonregulated activities.  

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the PSC to ensure regulated utilities do **not use customer funds to subsidize nonregulated activities**. (§ 316(a))
- Prohibits utilities from recovering from customers **lobbying expenses** related to influencing local/state/federal legislation, ordinances, resolutions, or ballot measures. (§ 316(b)(1))
- Prohibits recovery of **membership/organizational dues or contributions** used for lobbying or similar influence activities, including influence of **regulatory decisions**. (§ 316(b)(2))
- Prohibits recovery of **political contributions/expenses** (candidates, campaign committees, issue committees, independent expenditure committees, or similar). (§ 316(b)(3))
- Prohibits recovery of **charitable giving**, including contributions to **501(c)(3)** and **501(c)(4)** entities. (§ 316(b)(4))
- Prohibits recovery of **advertising and public relations** expenses unless directly related to a purpose/program required or authorized by statute or PSC rule/order; defines “advertising” and lists exclusions (e.g., outage/safety notices, certain consumer education, employment ads). (§ 316(b)(5))
- Includes a **severability clause**. (Section 3)
- **Note:** The enrolled/approved act (approved **July 16, 2025**) does **not** include the earlier bill language that would have capped electric distribution company annual capital expenses at **$125 million** for **2026–2028**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Ratepayer protection:** Explicitly bars charging customers for utilities’ lobbying, political spending, charitable donations, and certain brand/public-opinion advertising/PR. (§ 316(b)(1)–(5))
- **Clearer guardrails for rate cases:** Provides specific categories of costs that cannot be included in base rates, riders, or other charges. (§ 316(b))
- **Transparency/accountability:** Requires PSC oversight to ensure customer funds are not subsidizing nonregulated activities. (§ 316(a))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced utility flexibility to recover certain communications costs:** Advertising/PR cost recovery is limited to communications tied to programs required/authorized by statute or PSC rule/order, or within listed exclusions. (§ 316(b)(5))
- **Charitable giving not recoverable from customers:** Utilities may still donate, but cannot include those expenses in customer rates. (§ 316(b)(4))
- **Potential compliance/administrative burden:** Utilities may need to track and segregate costs (e.g., dues “to the extent” used for lobbying) to demonstrate non-recoverability in rate proceedings. (§ 316(b)(2))

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/updated cost recovery prohibitions**: Utilities may not recover from customers (via base rates, riders, or other charges) the following:  
  1) **Lobbying expenses** aimed at influencing legislation/ordinances/resolutions/ballot measures. (§ 316(b)(1))  
  2) **Dues/contributions** to organizations **to the extent used for lobbying** or similar influence activities, including influence of **regulatory decisions**. (§ 316(b)(2))  
  3) **Political contributions/expenses** (candidates/committees/independent expenditure committees or similar). (§ 316(b)(3))  
  4) **Charitable giving expenses**, including donations to **501(c)(3)** and **501(c)(4)** organizations. (§ 316(b)(4))  
  5) **Advertising/PR expenses** unless directly related to a purpose/program required or authorized by statute or PSC rule/order. (§ 316(b)(5))
- **Defines “advertising”** broadly (written/online/video/audio communications to induce patronage, promote brand, emphasize desirable qualities, or influence public opinion on legislative/administrative/electoral matters). (§ 316(b)(5)b.)
- **Advertising not included** (i.e., not treated as prohibited advertising under the definition):  
  - Required/authorized by law/regulation/order (§ 316(b)(5)c.1)  
  - Directly related to programs on income-based service, special rates, pilot programs, energy conservation/efficiency, beneficial electrification, renewable energy, transportation electrification, or other consumer education (§ 316(b)(5)c.2)  
  - Service interruptions, safety measures, or emergency conditions (§ 316(b)(5)c.3)  
  - Employment opportunities (§ 316(b)(5)c.4)
- **Severability:** If any provision is invalidated, remaining provisions continue in effect. (Section 3)

## Who Benefits
- **Utility customers/ratepayers:** Protected from paying (through rates, riders, or other charges) for utilities’ lobbying, political spending, charitable contributions, and certain advertising/PR. (§ 316(b))
- **PSC oversight role:** Statutorily reinforced responsibility to prevent subsidization of nonregulated activities by customer funds. (§ 316(a))
