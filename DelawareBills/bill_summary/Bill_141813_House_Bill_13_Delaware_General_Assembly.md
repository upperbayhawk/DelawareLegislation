## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware personal income tax law (Title 30, §1102) by restructuring tax brackets and rates, including creating two new higher-income brackets, effective for tax years beginning after **December 31, 2025**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Replaces the existing bracket schedule that applies to taxable years **after December 31, 2013 and before January 1, 2026** with a new schedule for taxable years **beginning after December 31, 2025**.
- Lowers the rate on taxable income **$2,001–$5,000** from **2.2% to 2.0%**.
- Combines multiple mid-level brackets into broader brackets: **$5,001–$20,000** taxed at **4.0%** (replacing 3.9% and 4.8% brackets), and **$20,001–$60,000** taxed at **5.5%** (replacing 5.2% and 5.55% brackets).
- Keeps a **6.6%** rate for taxable income **$60,001–$125,000**, but adds two new top brackets:
  - **6.75%** on **$125,001–$250,000**
  - **6.95%** on income **over $250,000**
- Fiscal note examples show decreases for some incomes (e.g., **$50,000: -$79 (-3.3%)**) and increases at higher incomes (e.g., **$500,000: +$979 (+3.1%)**; **$1,000,000: +$2,729 (+4.2%)**).
- Estimated General Fund revenue loss (net): **FY2026: $6.3M; FY2027: $12.0M; FY2028: $10.6M** (Department of Finance estimate using tax year 2023 data adjusted for growth and number of returns).
- Act is **effective upon signature by the Governor**, but bracket changes apply to tax years **beginning after December 31, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Tax reduction for some lower and middle taxable incomes** due to lower or adjusted rates in lower/mid brackets (e.g., fiscal note shows reductions at **$25,000 (-$66)**, **$50,000 (-$79)**, **$75,000 (-$84)**, **$100,000 (-$84)**, **$150,000 (-$46)**).
- **More progressive rate structure at higher incomes** by adding higher marginal rates above **$125,000** (new **6.75%** and **6.95%** brackets), compared with prior single top rate of **6.6% over $60,000**.
- **Simplification of bracket structure** in certain ranges by combining brackets (e.g., merging **$5,001–$10,000** and **$10,001–$20,000** into **$5,001–$20,000**).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Tax increases for higher taxable incomes**, as shown in fiscal note examples: **$200,000: +$29 (0.2%)**, **$500,000: +$979 (3.1%)**, **$1,000,000: +$2,729 (4.2%)**.
- **Net General Fund revenue loss** is projected despite higher top rates: **$6.3M (FY26), $12.0M (FY27), $10.6M (FY28)**, which could reduce available funds for state programs (revenue estimate provided in fiscal note).

## Key Policy Changes
- Updates 30 Del. C. §1102 to apply:
  - Existing bracket schedule to taxable years **after 12/31/2013 and before 1/1/2026**.
  - New bracket schedule to taxable years **beginning after 12/31/2025**.
- Changes personal income tax brackets and rates to:
  - **2.0%** on taxable income **$2,001–$5,000** (was **2.2%**).
  - **4.0%** on **$5,001–$20,000** (replaces **3.9%** on $5,001–$10,000 and **4.8%** on $10,001–$20,000).
  - **5.5%** on **$20,001–$60,000** (replaces **5.2%** on $20,001–$25,000 and **5.55%** on $25,001–$60,000).
  - **6.6%** on **$60,001–$125,000** (previously 6.6% applied to income over $60,000 without upper bound).
  - **6.75%** on **$125,001–$250,000** (new bracket).
  - **6.95%** on taxable income **over $250,000** (new top bracket).

## Who Benefits
- Taxpayers with taxable incomes where the fiscal note shows reduced liability under HB 13, including example incomes of **$25,000, $50,000, $75,000, $100,000, and $150,000** (with reductions ranging from **$46 to $84** in the provided examples).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Taxpayers with higher taxable incomes subject to the new higher brackets/rates above **$125,000**, particularly those **over $250,000** taxed at **6.95%** (examples show increased liability at **$200,000**, **$500,000**, and **$1,000,000**).
