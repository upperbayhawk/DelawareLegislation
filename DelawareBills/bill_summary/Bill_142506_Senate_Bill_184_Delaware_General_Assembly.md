## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 10, Chapter 31 of the Delaware Code to establish specific rules for **service of process on correctional officers** in **civil actions arising out of their role as correctional officers**, including restrictions on serving them at home and listing home addresses in court filings.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new/updated **§ 3115** governing **how to serve process on correctional officers** in certain civil cases.
- While a correctional officer is **employed by the State**, service **must** be made by leaving the summons and complaint at the officer’s **regular place of business** with a person **over age 18**.
- Once the correctional officer is **no longer employed by the State**, service must follow **applicable statutes and court rules** for service of process.
- Prohibits **service of process at the correctional officer’s home** when service is made under this section.
- Requires that the officer’s **home address must not be listed** on any **praecipe or similar document** filed in Delaware courts when service is made under this section.
- States the new section does **not limit or supersede § 3103(c)** of Title 10 when § 3103(c) would otherwise apply.
- The synopsis states the bill creates a “legal framework” for service both **during** and **after** state employment.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved personal safety/privacy for correctional officers:** Explicitly bars home service and prohibits listing the home address on certain court filings (lines 10–12).
- **Clearer service method during state employment:** Mandates workplace-based service with an adult recipient (over 18), reducing uncertainty about where/how to serve (lines 4–6).
- **Consistency with existing service rules after employment ends:** Directs parties back to “applicable statutes and court rules” once the officer is no longer employed (lines 7–9).
- **Preserves other service requirements where applicable:** Explicit non-interference with § 3103(c) when it applies (lines 13–14).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **May make plaintiffs’ service more constrained during employment:** Plaintiffs must serve at the “regular place of business” and cannot use home service under this section (lines 4–6, 10–11).
- **Potential for disputes about valid service:** Service is allowed by leaving papers with “a person over the age of 18” at the regular place of business, which could create factual disputes about who received the documents and their age (line 6).
- **Reduced public record detail in filings:** Prohibiting home addresses on praecipes/similar documents may limit identifying information available in court records (line 11–12).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of covered cases:** The bill applies to civil actions “arising out of their role as a correctional officer,” but does not further define that phrase (lines 4–5, 7–8).
- **Meaning of “regular place of business”:** Not defined; could vary for officers assigned to multiple locations or shifts (lines 5–6).
- **Who qualifies as the “person over the age of 18” at the workplace:** Not specified (e.g., coworker, supervisor, receptionist, contractor) (line 6).
- **Interaction details with § 3103(c):** The bill says it does not limit/supersede § 3103(c) when applicable, but does not explain which situations trigger § 3103(c) (lines 13–14).
- **What documents count as a “praecipe or similar document”:** Not defined (lines 11–12).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates/updates § 3115 (Service of process on a correctional officer)** with rules based on employment status:
  - **During state employment:** Service “must be made” by leaving a copy of the summons and complaint at the officer’s **regular place of business** with a person **over age 18**. (lines 4–6)
  - **After state employment ends:** Service must be made per **applicable statutes and court rules**. (lines 7–9)
- **Privacy/safety protections when service is made under this section:**
  - **No home service** on the correctional officer. (lines 10–11)
  - Officer’s **home address must not be listed** on any **praecipe or similar document** filed with a Delaware court. (lines 11–12)
- **Savings clause:** Does not limit or supersede **Title 10 § 3103(c)** when it would otherwise apply. (lines 13–14)

## Who Benefits
- **Correctional officers employed by the State**, through:
  - protection from **home service of process**; and
  - reduced disclosure of **home addresses** in court filings (lines 10–12).
- **State agencies/employers and workplaces** may benefit from having a specified service method during employment (lines 4–6).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected

- **Courts/clerks/filers** may need to ensure filings do not include home addresses on “praecipe or similar” documents in applicable cases (line 11–12).
