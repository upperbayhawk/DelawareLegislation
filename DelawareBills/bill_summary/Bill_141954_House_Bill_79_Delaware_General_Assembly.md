## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 14, § 4112F to expand required data collection, reporting, and parent notification regarding **mechanical restraint** and **seclusion** in public schools (in addition to existing physical restraint requirements), clarify **SRO** status and training/reporting expectations, and align Department of Education regulations with updated definitions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires Delaware Department of Education (DOE) regulations to mandate **uniform, school-level data collection** for **each use** of: **physical restraint, mechanical restraint, and seclusion**, including student demographics (age, gender, race, ethnicity, disability category, if any).  
- Adds **mechanical restraint and seclusion** to the DOE regulation requirements for **timely parental notice** and **special procedures/safeguards for students with disabilities**.  
- Requires DOE to issue an **annual report by November 30 of each year** on use of **physical restraint, mechanical restraint, and seclusion**, including rates by school and demographic subcategories, trends, and significant results.  
- Requires DOE to **submit the annual report** to specified recipients, including the **Delaware School Boards Association** and each district/charter governing board (in addition to legislative recipients).  
- Requires DOE to **request** from the Police Officer Standards and Training Commission (**POST**) any data POST collects on **law-enforcement officer** use of physical restraint, mechanical restraint, or seclusion on a student in a public school; POST must provide it.  
- For **mechanical restraint** data from POST, POST must provide totals **disaggregated by whether the officer was required to use mechanical restraint**, and if required, **why** it was required.  
- Clarifies that **SROs are law-enforcement officers** and sets/updates annual training participation and reporting alignment requirements for SROs who assist/intervene with students with disabilities.  
- **Effective date:** **August 1, 2026** (Act approved **July 30, 2025**).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Increased transparency and oversight:** Requires reporting on **mechanical restraint and seclusion** (not only physical restraint), plus annual publication by a fixed deadline (**Nov. 30**).  
- **Improved equity monitoring:** Mandates demographic breakdowns (age, gender, race, ethnicity, disability category, if any) for each recorded incident.  
- **Stronger parent awareness:** Requires DOE regulations to ensure **timely parental notice** when physical restraint, mechanical restraint, or seclusion is used.  
- **More complete picture including law enforcement actions:** Requires DOE to incorporate POST-provided data on law-enforcement officer use of restraint/seclusion in schools into the annual report.  
- **Disability-related safeguards reinforced:** Requires special procedures and safeguards in regulations for students with disabilities (IDEA/§504) for all three intervention types.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative burden on schools and DOE:** Uniform incident-level data collection by school for three categories (physical restraint, mechanical restraint, seclusion), plus demographic data, and standardized reporting formats.  
- **Additional interagency reporting workload:** DOE must request POST data; POST must produce additional disaggregations for mechanical restraint (“required” and “why required”).  
- **Training compliance constraint for SROs:** Districts/charters **may not use** SROs covered by the subsection if they do not comply with annual training requirements, potentially limiting staffing flexibility (with a stated limited fill-in exception).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Timely parental notice” is not defined** in the bill text (timelines and method are left to DOE regulations).  
- **Scope and specifics of DOE regulations** (training content beyond listed minimums; reporting format; safeguards procedures) are not detailed in the bill text.  
- **What POST currently collects** and the exact data elements available are not specified; the bill requires provision of POST-collected data, but does not define POST collection standards.  
- **Waiver standard details:** DOE Secretary may issue waivers for an individual student based on “compelling justification,” but the bill does not define that term beyond requiring certain safeguards.

## Educational Impact
- Establishes expanded statewide monitoring and reporting of seclusion and restraint practices in public schools, including disability-related safeguards and reporting to school governance entities.  
- Requires annual SRO awareness training (for covered SROs) that includes basic awareness of **IEPs, functional behavior assessments, and behavior support plans**, and requires consistency with educator training.

## Health Impact
- Reinforces statutory limits on physical restraint, including prohibitions against interfering with breathing or applying pressure on head/throat/neck, and requires ending restraint upon certain medical/behavioral conditions.  
- Requires safeguards and reporting/notice for seclusion and mechanical restraint, which may affect student safety monitoring (including waiver safeguard of **continuous visual staff monitoring**).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds mechanical restraint and seclusion to required data collection** (incident-level) and demographic reporting requirements in DOE regulations.  
- **Adds mechanical restraint and seclusion to parent notice requirements** (via DOE regulations).  
- **Adds mechanical restraint and seclusion to disability-related safeguards** required in DOE regulations.  
- **Sets annual DOE report deadline:** **November 30 of each year**; requires distribution to specified legislative and education governance recipients.  
- **Requires DOE-POST coordination:** DOE must request POST data on law-enforcement restraint/seclusion in schools; POST must provide it; DOE must incorporate it into the annual report.  
- **Defines “School resource officer (SRO)”** and updates SRO training/reporting alignment; prohibits districts/charters from using covered SROs who are noncompliant with training requirements (with a limited temporary fill-in clarification for non-SRO law enforcement).

## Who Benefits
- **Students (especially students with disabilities)**: enhanced safeguards, reporting, and parent notification requirements covering more intervention types.  
- **Parents/guardians**: expanded notice requirements when mechanical restraint or seclusion is used.  
- **Policy makers and school oversight bodies**: regularized, detailed annual reporting (including demographic breakdowns and school-level rates) and distribution to key entities.  
- **Schools/districts seeking benchmarking**: standardized statewide data can enable comparisons by school and subgroup (as reported by DOE).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **School districts and charter schools**: increased data collection, documentation, and reporting requirements; training compliance requirements relating to SROs.  
- **DOE and POST**: additional administrative responsibilities for data requests, provision, disaggregation, report compilation, and distribution.  
- **SRO staffing (covered SROs)**: districts/charters may not use covered SROs who do not meet annual training requirements (though the Act allows a non-SRO law-enforcement officer to fill in temporarily for an absent SRO).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- DOE **must** promulgate/amend regulations implementing § 4112F and consistent with the Act’s definitions.  
- Public school personnel prohibitions/limits (as reflected in § 4112F) include:  
  - Prohibited from using **chemical restraint**.  
  - Prohibited from using **mechanical restraint and seclusion** unless an individual-student **waiver** is issued by the DOE Secretary (unless proscribed by federal law).  
  - Physical restraint allowed only under enumerated safety and procedural standards (e.g., significant and imminent risk of bodily harm; no interference with breathing; ends when risk subsides or medical risk emerges; consistent with Title 11 § 468; conforms to DOE regulations).  
- **Waiver conditions (minimums specified):** must include **continuous visual staff monitoring** and **parental notice of each use** of mechanical restraint or seclusion.  
- **SRO training compliance:** Districts/charters **may not use** SROs covered by § 4112F(d) who do not comply with the required annual training/participation; reporting/notification must align with school personnel requirements and include a **police report identification number if a police report exists**.
