## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend the Charter of the City of New Castle to allow the City Council to levy **different real property tax rates** depending on **property classification** (e.g., **residential, commercial, industrial**), instead of requiring the tax to be levied in “just and equal portions and rates.”

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes New Castle City Council to levy **real property taxes at varying rates** based on **property classification** (examples listed: **residential, commercial, industrial**).
- Changes the Charter’s current requirement that property taxes be levied in **“just and equal portions and rates”** across real property.
- Leaves intact the Charter requirement that Council meet **on or before the first Monday in July** to determine the amount necessary to cover **net expenses** and create a budget.
- Leaves intact that the tax levy must be **sufficient to cover the aggregate of the budget**.
- Does not specify the **classifications**, **rate caps**, or **method** for setting different rates in the bill text.
- Does not establish any new penalty, enforcement mechanism, or administrative process in the bill text.
- Bill is a **charter amendment** specific to the **City of New Castle**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Allows differentiated tax policy by property type:** The City Council may set **varying rates** by classification (explicitly authorized by the Act’s synopsis), which could better align tax burdens with City policy goals for **residential vs. commercial/industrial** property.
- **Increases local fiscal flexibility:** By removing the uniform-rate constraint (“just and equal portions and rates”), the City can structure revenue across property classes while still meeting the requirement to fund the **aggregate budget**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced uniformity in tax rates across property owners:** The Charter’s prior language requiring taxes in “just and equal portions and rates” is changed, enabling different rates for different classes of property.
- **Unclear guardrails in the bill text:** The bill does not specify classifications, rate-setting standards, or limits, which may reduce predictability for taxpayers (not addressed in the source text beyond authorization to vary by classification).
