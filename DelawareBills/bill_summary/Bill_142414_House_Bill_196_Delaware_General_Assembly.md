## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 21 to require law-enforcement officers to record specific traffic-stop data in the Delaware Criminal Justice Information System (DELJIS) and to provide the Statistical Analysis Center access to that data for public reporting, oversight, and analysis of traffic-stop practices.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires officers, whenever a person is stopped under **Title 21** or **§ 5211 of Title 30**, to record traffic-stop information in **DELJIS**.
- Data must be entered **during or immediately after the conclusion of the stop**.
- Required fields include the **time, date, and location** of the stop.
- Requires entry of the **primary reason for the stop**.
- Requires entry of the **reason for the stop communicated to the driver** (which may differ from the primary reason).
- Grants the **Statistical Analysis Center** access to the collected data.
- Data access is expressly for **public reporting, oversight, and analysis** related to traffic-stop practices.
- Takes effect **60 days after enactment into law**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved documentation of traffic stops:** Mandatory entry of time/date/location and reasons may increase consistency in traffic-stop records (required by new § 703B(a)(1)–(3)).
- **Greater transparency and oversight:** Statistical Analysis Center access for **public reporting and oversight** may support accountability in traffic-stop practices (§ 703B(b)).
- **Clearer differentiation of stop rationales:** Capturing both the **primary reason** and the **reason communicated to the driver** may highlight discrepancies and improve review capability (§ 703B(a)(2)–(3)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased administrative workload for officers:** Officers must enter multiple data fields into DELJIS **during or immediately after** each stop (§ 703B(a)), adding time and documentation requirements.
- **Operational impacts during patrol:** The timing requirement (“during or immediately after the conclusion of the stop”) may affect workflow, especially during high-volume traffic enforcement (§ 703B(a)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Definition of “primary reason”:** The bill requires recording the “primary reason for the stop” but does not define how to determine it when multiple violations are involved.
- **Meaning of “immediately after”:** No specific time window is provided for how soon after a stop entry must be completed.
- **Data elements not addressed:** The bill does not specify whether demographic data, search/seizure details, warnings vs. citations, or outcomes must be recorded.
- **Public reporting specifics:** The bill grants access to the Statistical Analysis Center for “public reporting” but does not specify reporting frequency, format, or required metrics.

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates/updates **§ 703B (Traffic stop data)** in Title 21, Chapter 7 to require DELJIS entry of:
  - **Time, date, and location** of the stop (§ 703B(a)(1)).
  - **Primary reason** for the stop (§ 703B(a)(2)).
  - **Reason communicated to the driver** (§ 703B(a)(3)).
- Applies to stops made under **Title 21** and under **§ 5211 of Title 30** (§ 703B(a)).
- Requires entry **during or immediately after the conclusion of the stop** (§ 703B(a)).
- Provides **Statistical Analysis Center** access for **public reporting, oversight, and analysis** of traffic stop practices (§ 703B(b)).
