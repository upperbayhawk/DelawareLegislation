## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 4 (Alcoholic Liquors) to modify the rules for alcohol sold for off-premise consumption by on-premise licensees, including allowing sale of **1 bottle of certain “private label” spirits** and correcting the wine quantity limit.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Allows any **restaurant, brewpub, tavern, taproom, or other entity with a valid on-premise license** to sell alcoholic liquors for **take-out, curbside, drive through, or delivery** transactions (within §512(a)(1)).  
- Requires that **alcohol delivery** be performed by a **licensed third-party delivery vendor** (or that vendor’s employee/independent contractor) under a **written agreement** with the on-premise licensee.  
- Creates/uses a **third-party delivery license**; applicants must be **registered to do business in Delaware**.  
- Prohibits the Commissioner from issuing a third-party delivery license to an entity/person that **also holds an on-premise license**.  
- Prohibits a licensed third-party delivery vendor from being an entity whose business is **primarily the interstate shipment of goods**.  
- Off-premise alcoholic liquors must be sold in **securely closed containers** (no lids with sipping holes or straw openings).  
- Changes the wine quantity limit from “**2750 ML bottles of wine**” to **“2 750 ML bottles of wine”** (i.e., **two 750 mL bottles**).  
- Adds/clarifies that off-premise sales may include **1 bottle of a private label spirit** purchased from a **Delaware licensed importer** that is **limited availability or limited allocation**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded product option** for off-premise sales: on-premise licensees can include **1 bottle of qualifying private label spirits** (limited availability/limited allocation) sourced from a **Delaware licensed importer**.  
- **Clarifies wine limit** by correcting an apparent typo to a clear cap of **2 bottles (750 mL each) per customer**.  
- **Sets packaging standards** (securely closed containers) that may reduce opportunities for immediate consumption during transport.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **New/continued compliance steps** for delivery: delivery must be through a **licensed third-party vendor** with a **written agreement**, potentially adding administrative burden for licensees.  
- **Restricts who can deliver alcohol**: excludes businesses primarily engaged in **interstate shipment of goods**, which may limit vendor options.  
- **Third-party delivery license eligibility restriction**: a person/entity that also holds an **on-premise license cannot obtain** a third-party delivery license, potentially limiting business models that combine on-premise service and delivery operations.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Permitted spirit sale addition:** Off-premise sales may include **“1 bottle of a private label spirit purchased from a Delaware licensed importer that is either limited availability or limited allocation.”**  
- **Wine quantity correction:** Changes wine limit to **2 bottles of 750 mL** per customer (replacing the erroneous “2750 ML bottles”).  
- **Delivery licensing framework (as stated in §512(a)(1)):**
  - Delivery only by a **licensed third-party delivery vendor** (or their employee/independent contractor).  
  - Vendor must have a **third-party delivery license** and be **registered to do business in Delaware**.  
  - Vendor **cannot also hold an on-premise license**.  
  - Vendor cannot be primarily an **interstate shipper of goods**.  
  - On-premise licensee must have a **written agreement** authorizing delivery on the licensee’s behalf.  
- **Container standard:** Off-premise alcohol must be in **securely closed containers** (excluding lids with sipping holes/straw openings).  
- **Server certification requirement:** Alcohol sold/served on premises must be handled only by a person **certified as a responsible alcoholic beverage server** under **§1205**.

## Who Benefits
- **On-premise license holders** (restaurants, brewpubs, taverns, taprooms, etc.) who can sell an added category of spirits (1 qualifying private label bottle) for off-premise consumption.  
- **Delaware licensed importers** supplying qualifying private label spirits that are limited availability/limited allocation.  
- **Third-party delivery businesses** registered in Delaware that can obtain a third-party delivery license (if not also on-premise licensees).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Businesses primarily engaged in interstate shipment of goods** (explicitly excluded from being a licensed third-party delivery vendor).  
- **Entities seeking dual roles** (both on-premise licensee and third-party delivery licensee), which are prohibited.  
- **On-premise licensees** that prefer in-house delivery without using a licensed third-party vendor (delivery is constrained to licensed third parties per the text).
