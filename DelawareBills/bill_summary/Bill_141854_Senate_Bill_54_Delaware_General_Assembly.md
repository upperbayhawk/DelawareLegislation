## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 29, § 10203 (Joint Legislative Oversight and Sunset Committee) to **remove the rule that members attending virtually are not counted toward quorum**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Repeals **29 Del. C. § 10203(c)(3)**, which previously stated that **virtual participants do not count toward quorum** for transacting committee business.
- The committee’s quorum rule in § 10203(c)(1) remains: **quorum = at least 6 committee members**.
- The voting rule in § 10203(c)(2) remains: a proposition passes by a **majority of the entire Committee** (unless another statute/rule requires more); **vacancies are not counted** toward the majority threshold.
- The repealed provision previously allowed virtual members to **vote** even though they didn’t count toward quorum.
- The repealed provision also contained an **emergency exception** allowing virtual meetings when authorized by **public notice** from the President Pro Tempore and the Speaker.
- Material operational impact: the committee is no longer bound by the specific statutory restriction that **virtual attendance cannot establish quorum**.
- Approved and enacted **May 22, 2025** (Chapter 26, Volume 85).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Easier to meet quorum (6 members)** when members participate remotely, because the statutory bar on counting virtual participants toward quorum is removed. (Text evidence: repeal of § 10203(c)(3); quorum rule remains 6.)
- **Improved continuity of committee operations** without relying on the prior “emergency notice” mechanism to make virtual convening workable. (Text evidence: repeal includes removal of the emergency exception language.)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Fewer statutory guardrails around virtual participation**: removing § 10203(c)(3) eliminates explicit statutory direction about how virtual attendance affects quorum and how emergencies are handled for virtual meetings. (Text evidence: paragraph (3) is repealed in full.)
- **Potential uncertainty** about quorum-counting rules for virtual attendance may shift to internal rules or other authorities not stated in this bill. (Text evidence: bill removes the only explicit quorum/virtual rule in § 10203(c).)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Repeals 29 Del. C. § 10203(c)(3)**, which previously provided that:
  - A member participating virtually **was not counted toward quorum** to transact business;
  - A virtual participant **could still vote** as if physically present; and
  - The rule **did not apply during an emergency** when the President Pro Tempore and Speaker issued **public notice** authorizing virtual meetings.
- Leaves intact:
  - **Quorum = at least 6 members** (§ 10203(c)(1));
  - Passing threshold = **majority of the entire Committee**, with **vacancies not counted** (§ 10203(c)(2)).

## Who Benefits
- **Joint Legislative Oversight and Sunset Committee members** who need to participate remotely.
- The committee’s workflow, to the extent quorum can be reached without requiring physical presence (as a result of removing the former prohibition). (Directly tied to repeal of § 10203(c)(3).)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Stakeholders who prefer committee business be conducted only when a quorum is physically present; the statute no longer requires that physical presence standard for quorum. (Based on repeal of the “not counted toward quorum” rule.)
- Any party relying on the prior **emergency public notice** trigger as the statutory framework for virtual meeting authorization. (Based on repeal of the emergency exception language.)
