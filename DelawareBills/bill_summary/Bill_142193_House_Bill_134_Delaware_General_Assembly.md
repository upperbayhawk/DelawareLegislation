## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s animal cruelty statute (11 Del. C. § 1325) to increase escalating criminal consequences for repeat offenders and lengthen/strengthen prohibitions on owning or possessing animals after convictions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Upgrades repeat offenses: after **2 misdemeanor** cruelty-to-animals violations, any further misdemeanor-level violations become a **class F felony**.  
- Further upgrades repeat felonies: after **1 felony** cruelty-to-animals violation, any further felony-level violations become a **class E felony**.  
- Increases the animal-ownership ban for a **second or subsequent misdemeanor** conviction from **5 years to 10 years**, and removes exceptions for those repeat misdemeanants.  
- Clarifies/strengthens that a **second or subsequent felony** conviction results in a **lifetime** prohibition on owning or possessing animals **without exception**.  
- Sets fines for violating animal-ownership prohibitions: **$1,000** (first violation) and **$2,500** (subsequent) for misdemeanor-based bans; **$5,000** (first) and **$10,000** (subsequent) for felony-based bans, plus forfeiture.  
- Allows trained/certified animal welfare officers (DHSS Office of Animal Welfare or Department of Agriculture) to **impound** animals apparently owned/possessed in violation, consistent with **16 Del. C. § 3035F**.  
- For first-offense misdemeanor violations related to **animals left in motor vehicles** or **tethering of dogs**, requires issuance of a **warning**.  
- Grants **exclusive jurisdiction** over offenses relating to **animals left in motor vehicles** or **tethering of dogs** to the **Superior Court**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger deterrence for repeat offenders** by escalating charges to **class F felony** after **2 misdemeanors** and to **class E felony** after **1 felony** for subsequent felony-level conduct.  
- **Longer protection for animals** through extended ownership prohibitions (e.g., **10-year** ban after a second/subsequent misdemeanor; **lifetime** ban after second/subsequent felony).  
- **Clear monetary penalties** for violating ownership/possession bans: up to **$2,500** (repeat misdemeanor-ban violations) and up to **$10,000** (repeat felony-ban violations), plus forfeiture under **16 Del. C. § 3035F**.  
- **Enforcement tool added/affirmed** by expressly authorizing trained/certified animal welfare officers to **impound** animals apparently held in violation.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased criminal exposure and incarceration risk** for repeat offenders due to felony upgrades (class F and class E) triggered by prior convictions (after **2 misdemeanors**; after **1 felony** for subsequent felonies).  
- **Lifetime animal ownership/possession ban** for a second/subsequent felony conviction removes previously stated exceptions, which may be a significant collateral consequence.  
- **Jurisdictional shift to Superior Court** for motor-vehicle and tethering-related offenses may increase complexity, time, or resource demands compared to other venues (bill text assigns exclusive jurisdiction but does not discuss capacity).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill states: “Any person convicted of a second or subsequent felony violation… prohibited… for the remainder of their life without exception,” but the amended text also shows an additional fragment (“**15 years after said conviction without exception**”) creating **internal textual ambiguity** on the duration for repeat felony offenders.  
- “Exclusive jurisdiction… in the Superior Court” for motor-vehicle and tethering offenses: the bill does not specify how this interacts with existing charging, court processing, or whether lower courts previously handled these matters.  
- The warning requirement for first-offense misdemeanor motor-vehicle/tethering violations does not specify documentation standards, appealability, or how warnings interact with later charge enhancements.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Repeat-offense grading changes**
  - After **2 misdemeanor** violations of § 1325: any further violations that would otherwise be misdemeanors become a **class F felony**.  
  - After **1 felony** violation of § 1325: any further violations that would otherwise be felonies become a **class E felony**.
- **Expanded/adjusted conduct provisions (as shown in bill text)**
  - Adds/contains a deception-based offense: capturing/detaining/transporting/removing/delivering certain animals **under false pretenses** to shelters/vet clinics/other facilities via deception/misrepresentation.  
  - Contains motor-vehicle endangerment provision with authority for law enforcement/animal welfare officer/firefighter to use **reasonable force** to remove an animal when probable cause exists; requires reasonable efforts to contact owner and written notice if taken to shelter; includes immunity clause for owners regarding rescuer injuries; excludes certain agricultural transport.
- **Animal ownership/possession prohibitions**
  - Misdemeanor conviction: **5-year** prohibition, with an exception for licensed in-state resale/product operations where the person receives **at least 25%** of annual gross income from such sales.  
  - Second/subsequent misdemeanor: prohibition increased to **10 years** **without exception**.  
  - Felony conviction: **15-year** prohibition, with the same **25% gross income**/licensing exception for certain agricultural commerce.  
  - Second/subsequent felony: **lifetime** prohibition **without exception** (noting the textual ambiguity described above).
- **Penalties for violating ownership/possession bans**
  - Misdemeanor-ban violation: **$1,000** fine; subsequent violations: **$2,500**; plus forfeiture under **16 Del. C. § 3035F**.  
  - Felony-ban violation: **$5,000** fine; subsequent violations: **$10,000**; plus forfeiture under **16 Del. C. § 3035F**.
- **Impound authority**
  - Trained/certified animal welfare officers (DHSS Office of Animal Welfare or Department of Agriculture) may **impound** animals apparently owned/possessed in violation, consistent with **16 Del. C. § 3035F**.
- **Warnings and jurisdiction**
  - First-offense misdemeanor for animals left in motor vehicles or tethering dogs: **warning must be issued**.  
  - Exclusive jurisdiction over those offenses: **Superior Court**.

## Who Benefits
- Animals subject to cruelty/neglect, through stronger repeat-offender penalties and longer ownership prohibitions.  
- Animal welfare agencies and officers, due to explicit impound authority and structured penalties/forfeiture mechanisms.  
- The public, to the extent stronger deterrence reduces repeated animal cruelty incidents (the bill’s mechanism is escalation and bans).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Individuals convicted of animal cruelty offenses, particularly repeat offenders, due to:
  - Escalation to **class F felony** after **2 misdemeanors** and to **class E felony** for subsequent felonies after **1 felony**.
  - Longer bans on animal ownership/possession (**10 years** for repeat misdemeanors; **lifetime** for repeat felonies).
  - Exposure to fines up to **$10,000** for repeated violations of felony-based ownership prohibitions, plus forfeiture.
