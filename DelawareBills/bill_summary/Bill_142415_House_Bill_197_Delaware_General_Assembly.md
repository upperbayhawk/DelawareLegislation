## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s stalking statute (11 Del. C. § 1312) to (1) expand and clarify what qualifies as a “course of conduct,” (2) increase felony grading for certain stalking offenses, and (3) strengthen protections for constitutionally protected activity by requiring exclusion of evidence when such a defense applies.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Expands “course of conduct”** to expressly cover **three or more separate incidents** that may be committed **directly, indirectly, or through third parties**, including following/monitoring/tracking and disrupting daily activities (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)).  
- Clarifies that **no conviction is required for any predicate act** used to establish a “course of conduct,” and that prosecution for stalking **does not preclude** prosecution for other offenses (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)b).  
- **Raises baseline stalking** (currently in § 1312(b)) from a **Class G felony to a Class E felony**.  
- **Raises aggravated stalking under § 1312(c)** from a **Class F felony to a Class D felony** when any listed aggravating factor applies (e.g., victim under 14 with offender 21+, protective order violation, victim 62+, threat of death/serious injury, or physical injury).  
- Retains existing enhanced sentencing minimums: **6 months Level V minimum** when the course of conduct includes acts prohibited by an existing court order/sentence, and **1 year Level V minimum** for a stalking conviction within **5 years** of a prior stalking conviction (11 Del. C. § 1312(f), (g)).  
- Changes the affirmative defense from **“lawful picketing”** to a broader **“constitutionally protected activity”** and requires **exclusion of all evidence of that activity** if the defense is valid as a matter of law (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).  
- Makes technical drafting corrections to align with the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (Synopsis).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader coverage of stalking patterns:** Explicitly includes conduct done **through third parties** and conduct that **disrupts employment, education, or medical care**, potentially capturing more stalking behavior reflected in modern contexts (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)a.3).  
- **Stronger criminal accountability:** Upgrading felony classes (from **G→E** and **F→D**) increases the seriousness of the offense classification (Synopsis; § 1312(b), (c)).  
- **Clarifies prosecution framework:** Allows reliance on predicate acts **without requiring a conviction** for those acts, while preserving the ability to prosecute other crimes too (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)b).  
- **Protects constitutional activity in court:** If conduct is deemed **constitutionally protected**, the bill requires **exclusion of all evidence of that activity** (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher felony grading may increase incarceration exposure:** Raising offenses to **Class E** and **Class D** felonies may increase criminal justice consequences for defendants (Synopsis; § 1312(b), (c)).  
- **Evidentiary exclusion requirement may limit prosecutions in some cases:** When a defense is found valid as a matter of law, **“all evidence of the activity must be excluded,”** which could remove potentially relevant facts tied to that activity (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).  
- **Broader definition may increase charging/coverage:** Expanding “course of conduct” to include more listed behaviors and indirect conduct could expand the set of cases qualifying for stalking charges (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “constitutionally protected activity”** is not defined in the statute text; the bill replaces “lawful picketing” with that broader phrase without listing categories (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).  
- **Boundary between protected activity and stalking conduct** may depend on court interpretation, particularly because evidence exclusion is mandatory once the defense is valid as a matter of law (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).  
- **Interaction with “reasonable person in the victim’s circumstances”** standard may require case-by-case judicial interpretation (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(2)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Felony reclassification:**
  - § 1312(b) stalking increased from **Class G felony → Class E felony**.  
  - § 1312(c) stalking with specified aggravators increased from **Class F felony → Class D felony**.  
- **Expanded definition of “course of conduct”:**
  - Requires **“three or more separate incidents”** (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)a).  
  - Explicitly includes acts done **directly, indirectly, or through third parties** (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)a).  
  - Enumerates included conduct such as **following/monitoring/tracking/observing/surveying**, threatening/communicating in a way that consciously disregards risk of violating § 1312(a), and **interfering with or disrupting** daily activities including **employment, education, or medical care** (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)a.1–3).  
  - States **no conviction is required** for predicate acts; predicate-act convictions do not bar stalking prosecution; stalking prosecution does not bar other prosecutions (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)b).  
- **Affirmative defense adjustment + evidence exclusion:**
  - Defense broadened from **“lawful picketing”** to **“constitutionally protected activity.”**  
  - If valid as a matter of law, **all evidence of the activity must be excluded** (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).  
- **Technical corrections** to conform to drafting standards (Synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- **Victims of stalking**, including victims whose stalkers use **indirect methods or third parties**, and victims whose lives are disrupted in areas like **employment, education, or medical care** (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)a.3).  
- **People engaged in constitutionally protected activity** who could assert the affirmative defense and obtain **exclusion of evidence** related to that protected activity (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).  
- **Law enforcement/prosecutors** may benefit from clarified statutory language on predicate acts and course-of-conduct scope (11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)b).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Defendants charged with stalking**, due to increased felony grading (**Class G→E; Class F→D**) and the expanded course-of-conduct definition that includes more types of behavior and indirect conduct (Synopsis; 11 Del. C. § 1312(e)(1)).  
- **Prosecutors** in cases where conduct is deemed constitutionally protected, because required **evidence exclusion** could reduce admissible proof related to that activity (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Penalty grading increased**:
  - Stalking under § 1312(b): **Class E felony** (was Class G).  
  - Stalking under § 1312(c): **Class D felony** (was Class F) when aggravators apply (e.g., offender **21+** with victim **under 14**; protective order violation; victim **62+**; threat of death/serious injury; physical injury).  
- **Mandatory minimum incarceration provisions retained**:
  - **6 months incarceration at Level V** minimum (first **6 months not suspendable**) when stalking includes acts previously prohibited by an existing court order or sentence (11 Del. C. § 1312(f)).  
  - **1 year incarceration at Level V** minimum (first **1 year not suspendable**) for a stalking conviction within **5 years** of a prior stalking conviction (11 Del. C. § 1312(g)).  
- **No-defense clarifications retained**:
  - Not a defense that the perpetrator lacked actual notice the conduct was unwanted or did not intend to cause fear/distress (11 Del. C. § 1312(h)).  
- **Affirmative defense + evidence exclusion**:
  - If “constitutionally protected activity” defense is valid as a matter of law, **all evidence of that activity must be excluded** (11 Del. C. § 1312(i)).  
- **Exemption retained** for legitimate activities of law enforcement and certain licensed/security professions as defined in Title 24, Chapter 13 (11 Del. C. § 1312(j)).
