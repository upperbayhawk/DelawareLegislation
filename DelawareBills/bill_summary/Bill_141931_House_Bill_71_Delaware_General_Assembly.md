## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To strengthen sudden cardiac arrest preparedness in Delaware secondary and middle school interscholastic athletics by requiring coach certifications, venue-specific emergency action planning, accessible AEDs at athletic venues/events, AED maintenance, and notification to the Office of Emergency Medical Services (OEMS).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the Delaware Interscholastic Athletic Association (DIAA) Board to adopt regulations for secondary and middle school interscholastic athletics covering CPR/first aid/AED certification for **all coaches**.  
- Requires each school with an athletic department or program to develop a **venue-specific athletic Emergency Action Plan (EAP)** for cardiac arrest or similar life-threatening emergencies; the EAP must be **widely distributed, posted, and rehearsed annually**.  
- Requires an **AED** to be **clearly marked** and **easily accessible in an unlocked location** at each school athletic **venue and event** (and accessible during any school-sponsored athletic event or practice with participating students).  
- Requires AED placement to follow **nationally recognized emergency cardiovascular care guidelines**.  
- Requires AEDs be **tested and maintained** according to **manufacturer operational guidelines**.  
- Requires **notification to OEMS** regarding the AED, the type acquired, and its location.  
- Fiscal note assumes approximately **80** affected secondary/middle schools; estimated local one-time costs in **FY2026: $450,000**, and recurring costs in **FY2028: $30,000** (local funds).  
- Act is **effective upon signature** and was **approved July 30, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Faster emergency response readiness at athletic venues/events by requiring **unlocked, clearly marked, easily accessible AEDs** and **venue-specific** EAPs posted and rehearsed **annually** (reduces delays in cardiac emergencies).  
- More consistent emergency preparedness among staff by requiring **all coaches** to maintain **CPR, first aid, and AED** certification aligned with **national evidence-based emergency cardiovascular care guidelines**.  
- Improved statewide awareness/coordination for AED location and type through required **notification to OEMS**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Local fiscal burden: fiscal note assumes **all costs** for AED acquisition, placement, and maintenance are paid through **local funds**, with estimated **FY2026 one-time local cost $450,000** and **FY2028 recurring $30,000**.  
- Operational burden on schools to create, distribute, post, and **rehearse annually** a venue-specific EAP, and to ensure AEDs remain accessible and maintained per manufacturer guidelines.  
- Potential need for additional devices: survey responses indicated some schools may need **up to 5** additional AEDs; assumptions estimate **1–2 additional AEDs per school** to ensure accessibility at each event/venue.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- Enforcement mechanism: the text requires DIAA regulations and school actions but does not specify penalties or explicit enforcement steps for noncompliance.
