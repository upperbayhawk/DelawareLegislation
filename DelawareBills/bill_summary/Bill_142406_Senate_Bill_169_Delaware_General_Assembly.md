## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Make technical, stylistic, and conforming corrections across multiple titles of the Delaware Code and the Charter of the Town of Newport, including fixing drafting errors, clarifying cross-references, removing obsolete language, and conforming terms to the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual. The Act also requires a **three-fourths vote** for passage (per the bill text) as a precaution.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Makes broad **technical corrections** in Titles **3, 6, 9, 10, 11, 14, 16, 19, 21, 24, 26, 29, 30, and 31**, plus the **Town of Newport Charter**.
- Updates Title 3 (agriculture) pesticide and nursery-stock provisions largely for **formatting, clarity, and consistency**, including clearer “must/shall” directives and reorganized definitions.
- Clarifies nursery licensing and compliance language, including that a regulated person must obtain a **nursery license before initiating business operations** and must **complete the nursery license application online** (with accommodations if no internet access).
- Corrects a Title 14 education deadline already in law: strategic plan to expand a mental health services unit must be developed **by December 31, 2025**.
- Completes substitution of **“perinatal” for “maternal”** in Title 16 Chapter 8D to remove gender-specific language.
- Corrects Title 16 civil penalty caps: for continuing violations, **health and safety** penalty cap increases from **$2,500 to $5,000 per day** beyond the initial day; **nonhealth and safety** cap increases from **$1,250 to $2,500 per day** beyond the initial day.
- Standardizes “211 Helpline” references (removes “2-1-1” hyphenation) and makes other drafting-manual conforming edits.
- Amends Newport Charter provisions, including specifying that the Town must adopt a comprehensive development plan **every 10 years or sooner if required**, and makes other technical governance/finance wording corrections.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves legal clarity and administration** by correcting drafting errors and cross-references across many code sections (per Synopsis: corrections beyond Code Revisors’ authority).
- **Higher enforceability/consistency**: multiple provisions convert ambiguous phrasing to clearer requirements (e.g., “shall”/“must,” corrected definitions, corrected list structures).
- **Public health/safety enforcement impact (explicit)**: Title 16 penalty-cap corrections raise maximum daily penalties for continuing violations to **$5,000/day** (health and safety) and **$2,500/day** (nonhealth and safety) beyond the initial day.
- **Modernizes and de-genders statutory language** in perinatal mental health provisions by completing the “maternal” → “perinatal” substitution.
- **Local planning clarity (Newport)**: codifies comprehensive plan cadence (**every 10 years or sooner if required**) and clarifies procedural language for Town governance documents.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased potential penalty exposure** for regulated parties under Title 16 due to corrected higher caps (**$5,000/day** and **$2,500/day** beyond initial day) for continuing violations.
- **Compliance/administrative burden clarified** for nursery businesses: application must be completed **online** (with accommodations if internet not available) and license must be obtained **before** starting operations; advertising must include license number and license copy must be provided to the publisher/producer (Title 3, §1302 and §1308).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Title 16 §1109(e)**: continuing violation penalty caps corrected to:
  - Health and safety: up to **$5,000 per day** beyond the initial day.
  - Nonhealth and safety: up to **$2,500 per day** beyond the initial day.
  - “Each day of a continuing violation constitutes a separate violation.”
- **Title 3 (Nursery stock) compliance/enforcement** (clarified in multiple sections):
  - Must obtain nursery license **before** initiating business operations; must display license; must complete application online (with accommodations if no internet).
  - Shipping into Delaware must be labeled with consignor, state of origin, and consignee; must have a **nursery inspection certificate** affixed.
  - Transportation representatives receiving/moving uncertified nursery stock who fail to immediately notify the Department are subject to Chapter 13 enforcement provisions (§§1310–1313).
  - Violations may result in **abatement** (including destruction, confiscation, treatment, or return shipment) at the person’s expense and **without compensation** (Title 3 §§1304, 1310).
  - Advertising requires providing license copy to publisher/producer and including the **license number** in advertisements (Title 3 §1308).
