## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To clarify which categories of people must undergo a fingerprint-based Delaware and national (FBI) criminal history background check for Delaware marijuana establishment licensure and work, to comply with **Public Law 92-544 (28 U.S.C. § 534)** access requirements for FBI criminal history record information, and to make conforming/clarifying changes to license denial grounds based on background-check information.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Specifies **four categories** of people who must complete a criminal background check for marijuana establishment licensure/work, including certain workers and **owners with ≥10% ownership interest**.  
- Requires covered persons to submit **fingerprints** and information to the **State Bureau of Identification (SBI)** to obtain both **Delaware** and **FBI** criminal history record checks.  
- Makes **SBI** the intermediary responsible for forwarding required background-check information to the **Division of Alcohol and Tobacco Enforcement** (the “Division”).  
- Authorizes use of background checks to consider an individual’s **background, qualifications, and suitability** for **licensure or employment** under the Delaware Marijuana Control Act.  
- Places **all background-check costs** on the person required to obtain the check.  
- Adds **ongoing monitoring**: covered persons are subject to **subsequent criminal history reporting** to the Division (including via the **Rap Back System**, if available) while they are a **license holder or employee** subject to the chapter.  
- Provides that failure to satisfy certain statutory requirements may be grounds for **refusal, suspension, or cancellation** of a license.  
- Clarifies that (except as otherwise permitted under § 1354(b)) a **marijuana-related controlled substance conviction** may not be a ground for denial, while still allowing denial where the applicant presents a **risk to health or public safety** based on background-check information.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves legal compliance and access to FBI records:** The bill states the clarification is necessary to comply with **Pub. L. 92-544** requirements for accessing **FBI criminal history record information**.  
- **Clarifies who must be checked:** Explicitly lists covered roles (including workers and **≥10% owners**), which can reduce uncertainty for license applicants and regulators.  
- **Adds ongoing safety oversight:** Requires reporting of **subsequent criminal history** (including via **Rap Back**, if available) while a person remains covered, supporting ongoing suitability review.  
- **Limits automatic disqualification for marijuana offenses:** Provides that, except as in § 1354(b), marijuana-related convictions (possession, sale, manufacture, transportation, cultivation) **may not** be grounds for denial.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Adds compliance burden for workers and volunteers:** Background checks are required for anyone who “performs work… whether… contractor, employee, or volunteer… with or without compensation,” and must occur **prior to beginning work**.  
- **Individual bears the cost:** The person required to obtain the background check is responsible for **any costs** associated with obtaining it.  
- **Ongoing monitoring requirement:** Continued reporting of subsequent criminal history (including potential Rap Back participation) may increase administrative and privacy burdens for covered individuals.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Fees/cost amounts:** The bill assigns responsibility for costs but does not state **dollar amounts** for background check fees.  
- **Operational details of “ongoing monitoring”:** The bill references reporting through the Bureau, FBI systems, or **Rap Back** “as long as” the person is covered, but does not detail procedures, notice, dispute processes, or timelines.  
- **Rap Back availability:** The bill conditions some reporting on “**if the Rap Back System is available**,” but does not specify when/under what conditions it is considered available for this program.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Definition added/clarified:** “Work” is defined by reference to **19 Del. C. § 3302**.  
- **Who must undergo background checks (§ 1347(a)):**
  1) Applicant for a marijuana establishment license.  
  2) Any person performing work at/for a marijuana establishment (contractor/employee/volunteer; paid or unpaid), **prior to beginning work**.  
  3) Any person who is or seeks to become a director, officer, board member, or agent of a licensed marijuana establishment or applicant business entity.  
  4) Any person with an **ownership interest of 10% or more** in a licensed marijuana establishment or applicant business entity.  
- **Fingerprints + Delaware and FBI checks required:** Submission to **SBI** for Delaware and national criminal history checks, including SBI record and FBI record (or “no record” statements).  
- **Information flow:** **SBI** forwards required information to the **Division of Alcohol and Tobacco Enforcement**.  
- **Permissible use:** Division/Commissioner may use the background check to consider **background, qualifications, and suitability** for licensure or employment under the chapter.  
- **Cost responsibility:** Covered individual pays all costs of obtaining the background check.  
- **Ongoing monitoring:** Subsequent criminal history information must be reported to the Division, including via **Rap Back** (as defined in **11 Del. C. § 8502**) if available.  
- **Licensing consequences:** Failure of certain persons (including **≥10% owners**, current officers/directors) to satisfy specified requirements may be grounds for **refusal, suspension, or cancellation** of a license.  
- **Conforming/clarifying denial grounds (§ 1354):**
  - Allows refusal where conviction may impact qualifications/functions/duties, with required review of nature of crime and rehabilitation if otherwise suitable and public safety not compromised.  
  - Provides that, except as set forth in § 1354(b), marijuana-related convictions may not be grounds for denial.  
  - Allows refusal if applicant presents a **risk to health or public safety** based on background-check information.

## Who Benefits

- **Some applicants with prior marijuana convictions:** The text states marijuana-related convictions generally may not be grounds for denial (except as otherwise permitted under § 1354(b)).
