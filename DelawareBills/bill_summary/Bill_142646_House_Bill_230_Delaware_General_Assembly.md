## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Appropriates State funds for Grants-in-Aid for the fiscal year ending **June 30, 2026**, and sets conditions on eligibility, permitted uses, reporting, and payment timing; also amends certain FY 2026 appropriations acts and makes targeted statutory changes (including tax checkoff and school tax provisions).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Provides **$98,283,625** in FY 2026 Grants-in-Aid appropriations across four major sections: **$37,093,119** (Government Units & Senior Centers), **$47,093,493** (One-Times & Community Agencies), **$13,258,013** (Fire Companies & Public Service Ambulance Companies), and **$839,000** (Veterans Organizations).
- Sets eligibility standards for Grants-in-Aid recipients (generally: **incorporated nonprofit**, operating **2 years**, active board governance, nondiscrimination, accounting documentation, and timely electronic application).
- Prohibits use of grant funds for specified purposes (including **child daycare**, **capital equipment**, **buildings**, **elected officials’ compensation**, **political/partisan purposes**, **lobbying**, and **benefiting non-Delaware residents**).
- Establishes payment structure: generally **four equal quarterly installments** (**July, October, January, April**), with authority to **withhold or delay** payments for noncompliance; certain recipients may receive **full payment in the first quarter**, including those receiving **$6,000 or less**, certain named agencies, all Section 3 fire companies, and one-time items.
- Funds paramedic services and sets related oversight: **$18,275,620** for Paramedic Program Operations plus a **$3,000,000** Paramedic Program Operations Block Grant (allocated **equally among counties** operating an approved paramedic program); includes **annual State Auditor audit** requirement.
- Includes several targeted conditions and deadlines, including: SALLE/EIDE local law enforcement plan reports due **April 1** and a DSHS annual report due **May 1** each fiscal year; EMS/OEMS suggested-change report due **May 1, 2026**; Community Health Center reporting due **February 1, 2026**; Right to Representation funding contingent on an annual report due **November 30, 2025**.
- Makes additional statutory/administrative changes, including reestablishing the **Senior Trust Fund** tax checkoff mechanism (minimum **$1.00**) and exempting **Central Delaware Housing Collaborative** real estate from school district taxation.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broad service support statewide:** Appropriates funds to senior centers, health/disability providers, youth programs, housing/homelessness programs, community organizations, and public safety entities (multiple line-item appropriations throughout Sections 1–4).
- **Operational support for emergency response:** Funds fire companies, public service ambulance companies, and county paramedic program operations (e.g., Section 1 EMS appropriations; Section 3 fire/EMS appropriations).
- **Accountability controls:** Allows performance/financial audits and in-depth assessments (Controller General/JFC), requires financial documentation (audit/review/compilation or explanation), and authorizes withholding installments for noncompliance or prohibited spending (Sections 7–9).
- **Targeted policy outcomes in health and legal services:** Includes **$5,000,000** Health Care Contingency to support enhanced rates for certain skilled nursing facilities beginning **January 1, 2026**, and **$550,000** Right to Representation conditioned on a **November 30, 2025** report (Sections 21 and 24).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Restricted allowable uses may limit flexibility:** Grant funds cannot be used for capital equipment or building-related costs (relocation/rehab/renovation/purchase), and cannot be used for daycare, lobbying, political purposes, or services benefiting non-residents (Section 6).
- **Compliance burden on nonprofits:** Eligibility and ongoing requirements include board governance standards, nondiscrimination, financial documentation within the past **3 years**, and electronic application submission by posted deadlines (Sections 5 and 9).
- **Funding may be withheld or delayed:** Quarterly installments can be withheld/delayed for missing required expenditure statements, improper spending, or ceasing operations; remaining funds may be reallocated (Section 8).
- **Automatic disqualification for financial noncompliance:** Noncompliance with Section 9 financial-information requirements “shall automatically disqualify” the applicant for the next fiscal year (Section 9(c)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Grant-in-Aid eligibility criteria codified for awards under Sections 1 and 2** (e.g., incorporated nonprofit; operating **2 years**; governance; nondiscrimination; accounting documentation; must not use a fiscal agent; electronic application by posted deadline) (Section 5).
- **Prohibited uses of appropriated funds** explicitly listed (Section 6).
- **Audit/assessment authority expanded/clarified** for Controller General and JFC Co-Chairs, including random in-depth assessments (Section 7).
- **Quarterly installment payment schedule set/confirmed** (July/October/January/April) with explicit withholding/delay and reallocation procedures; special handling if a **senior center closes** (Section 8).
- **Financial documentation requirements** (audit/review/compilation within **3 years** or explanation/supporting documents); noncompliance leads to **automatic disqualification next fiscal year**; separate financial reporting process for fire companies (Section 9).
- **Paramedic program funding rules and oversight:** state share tied to prior-year adopted county budgets; **$3,000,000** block grant split equally; annual State Auditor audit (Section 12).
- **Right to Representation funding condition:** disbursement contingent on an annual report due **November 30, 2025** and JFC Co-Chair approval; directs funds to DVLS coordinator notwithstanding Title 29, Ch. 69 (Section 24).
- **Reestablishes Senior Trust Fund donation mechanism** via Delaware income tax returns with minimum **$1.00** contributions; includes effectivity and allows DHSS to use remaining balance for senior assistance (Section 29).
- **School tax change:** exempts **Central Delaware Housing Collaborative** property from district school taxes (Section 30).
- **University of Delaware restriction:** UD may not pay City of Newark **utility bills** and **building permit fees** by **credit card** (Section 28).

## Who Benefits
- **Grant recipients** listed throughout Sections 1–4, including senior centers, community agencies, fire companies, public service ambulance companies, and veterans organizations.
- **Counties operating approved paramedic programs** (equal shares of the **$3,000,000** block grant) and counties receiving state-share paramedic operations funding (Section 12).
- **Community Health Centers** receiving Community Health Contingency funds (Section 20).
- **Individuals eligible for medical debt relief** under the thresholds (**<400% FPL** or **debt >5% of annual income**) (Section 23).
- **Private, non-pediatric skilled nursing facilities** benefiting from enhanced rates starting **January 1, 2026** (Section 21).
- **Central Delaware Housing Collaborative** (school tax exemption for its real estate) (Section 30).

- **Grant recipients** whose payments may be **withheld/delayed** or **reallocated** for noncompliance, improper expenditures, or ceasing operations (Section 8).
- **University of Delaware** (restricted from using credit cards for specified City of Newark payments) (Section 28).
- **School districts in the New Castle County Tax District pool** (must contribute/receive fixed amounts equal to FY 2025 beginning in FY 2026) (Section 32).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Eligibility requirements** to be considered for Grants-in-Aid under Sections 1 or 2 (nonprofit status, 2-year operation, governance, nondiscrimination, accounting documentation, no fiscal agent, electronic application by posted deadline) (Section 5).
- **Use restrictions:** funds may not be expended for prohibited purposes listed in Section 6.
- **Audits/assessments:** Controller General may contract for or conduct audits; JFC Co-Chairs and Controller General conduct random in-depth assessments (Section 7).
- **Payment withholding/delay:** Treasurer directed to withhold installments upon notification; installments may be delayed/withheld for missing required quarterly expenditure statements, improper spending, prohibited uses, or operational shutdown; remaining funds may be reallocated (Section 8).
- **Senior center closure rule:** remaining quarterly payments retained in Controller General’s Office; disbursements require consultation with University of Delaware and approval of JFC Co-Chairs (Section 8(e)).
- **Financial documentation compliance:** required documentation within past **3 years** (audit/review/compilation) or explanation/supporting documents; **noncompliance automatically disqualifies** the applicant from consideration in the **next fiscal year** (Section 9(c)).
- **Right to Representation disbursement condition:** contingent upon submission of report by **November 30, 2025** and approval by JFC Co-Chairs (Section 24(a)).
