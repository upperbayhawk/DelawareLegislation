## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware unemployment compensation law (Title 19, §3314) to allow certain workers unemployed due to a **labor dispute other than a lockout** to receive unemployment benefits **after a 2-week waiting period**, with specified **waivers** and an **employer penalty** for certain false certifications about reinstatement after hiring “permanent replacement” workers.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes §3314(4) so workers unemployed due to a **labor dispute (other than a lockout)** may receive unemployment benefits **starting with the third week** (i.e., **after a 2-week waiting period**) if otherwise eligible.  
- **Waives the 2-week waiting period** if either: (1) the dispute is caused by the employer’s failure/refusal to comply with a contract/agreement (including a CBA) or relevant hours/wages/conditions-of-work law; or (2) the employer **hires a permanent replacement worker** for the individual’s position.  
- Defines/clarifies lockout criteria: contract expired and negotiations ongoing; union offers to work under existing terms to avert stoppage; employer refuses to allow work/status quo to continue pending negotiations.  
- Creates a presumption that a replacement worker is **permanent unless** the employer **certifies in writing** the worker can return to the prior position when the dispute ends (subject to timing limits described in the bill).  
- If the employer certifies return-to-work but then **does not permit the individual to return**, the individual may **recover benefits lost** during the disqualification period.  
- The Department of Labor may impose a penalty on the employer of **up to $750 per employee per week of benefits lost** in that scenario; penalties are deposited into the **Special Administration Fund** (§3166).  
- Fiscal note: Department of Labor anticipates **one-time technology upgrades** costing **$20,000 (FY 2026)** from **Appropriated Special Funds (ASF)** funded by unemployment insurance taxes; no costs listed for FY2027–FY2028.  
- Effective date assumption in fiscal note: **effective upon signature by the Governor**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded access to unemployment benefits** for workers affected by non-lockout labor disputes after a **2-week waiting period** (or immediately if a waiver condition applies), if they meet eligibility requirements.  
- **Worker protection** where the employer allegedly caused the dispute by failing/refusing to comply with a contract/agreement (including a CBA) or applicable labor laws—because the **waiting period is waived** in that circumstance.  
- **Deterrence and remediation** when an employer certifies in writing that a worker can return after a dispute (in the permanent replacement context) but then does not allow return: worker can **recover lost benefits**, and the Department may impose a **penalty up to $750 per employee per week** of benefits lost.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential increased unemployment benefit payments** by allowing benefits in labor-dispute situations where benefits were previously disallowed (non-lockout disputes), subject to eligibility and waiting-period rules. (The bill changes disqualification criteria in §3314(4).)  
- **Administrative and system changes** required to implement the new eligibility/waiver/penalty framework; fiscal note identifies **one-time technology upgrades** ($20,000 in FY2026).  
- **Employer exposure to penalties** up to **$750 per employee per week** of benefits lost if the employer certifies return-to-work but does not permit return (as described in §3314(4)b.2.B).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Amends 19 Del. C. §3314(4)** to allow benefits in certain labor-dispute situations:
  - General rule: individual disqualified **before the third week** of unemployment due to work stoppage from a labor dispute **other than a lockout** (i.e., a **2-week waiting period**).  
  - Creates exceptions (waivers) so the individual is **not disqualified before the third week** if:
    1) The dispute is caused by employer failure/refusal to comply with an agreement/contract (including a CBA) or state/federal law on hours, wages, or other conditions of work; **or**
    2) The employer **hires a permanent replacement worker** for the position.
- **Replacement worker presumption and certification**:
  - Replacement worker presumed **permanent** unless employer **certifies in writing** that the individual can return to the prior position at the conclusion of the dispute (subject to the bill’s timing condition: “if the labor dispute terminates before the conclusion of the individual’s eligibility for benefits under this chapter”).  
- **Back benefits + employer penalty**:
  - If employer certifies return but then does not permit return, worker may recover **benefits lost** during disqualification period, and the Department may impose a penalty of **up to $750 per employee per week** of benefits lost; penalties deposited into the **Special Administration Fund** (§3166).
- Makes “technical corrections” to conform drafting standards (per synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- Workers unemployed due to a **labor dispute other than a lockout**, who meet unemployment eligibility requirements, because they may receive benefits **after 2 weeks**, with possible **waiver** of the waiting period under specified conditions.  
- Workers in positions where the employer hires **permanent replacement workers**, due to waiver and protections tied to employer certification and potential recovery of lost benefits.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Employers involved in labor disputes, due to:
  - Potentially more employees qualifying for unemployment benefits (changing prior disqualification in non-lockout disputes).  
  - Potential penalties up to **$750 per employee per week** of benefits lost if they certify return-to-work but do not permit return as specified.  
- Unemployment insurance system/ASF resources may face additional benefit payouts (mechanism changed by the bill), though specific amounts are not stated in the source text.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon signature by the Governor** (stated as an assumption in the Fiscal Note).  
- **2-week waiting period / “before the third week”** rule applies to qualifying labor-dispute unemployment cases under amended §3314(4), unless waived by the specified conditions.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Employer written certification requirement (optional but consequential):** Replacement worker presumed permanent unless employer **certifies in writing** that the individual will be able to return to the prior position at conclusion of the labor dispute (with the bill’s stated condition tied to benefit eligibility duration).  
- **Penalty authority:** If, after such certification, the employer does not permit the worker to return to the prior position, the Department may impose a penalty of **up to $750 per employee per week of benefits lost**; penalty proceeds must be deposited into the **Special Administration Fund** under **§3166**.  
- **Benefit recovery:** In that certification/return-denial scenario, the individual is qualified for and may recover **benefits lost during the period** the individual was disqualified.
