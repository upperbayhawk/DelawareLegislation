## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s alcohol service laws to (1) allow people ages **18–20** to bartend under specified **direct supervision** requirements, (2) bar anyone **under 21** from entering or working in taverns/taprooms offering **sexually oriented entertainment**, and (3) increase the frequency of mandatory responsible server training from **every 4 years to every 2 years**. (Title 4 amendments: **§ 904, § 1204, § 1205**)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Lowers the effective bartending age by allowing individuals **18 years or older** to be involved in the **sale, service, and (with limits) preparation** of alcoholic liquor.  
- Establishes a rule that a person **may not** be involved in the **sale, service, or preparation** of alcoholic liquor unless the person is **at least 18**.  
- Allows **18-, 19-, and 20-year-olds** to engage in **sale and service** of alcoholic liquor.  
- Limits **18–20-year-olds’ preparation** of alcoholic liquor to situations where they are **directly supervised** by a person **21+** who is **working behind the bar with them**.  
- Applies these age/supervision rules across settings addressed in § 904, including **restaurants**, **clubs with authorized dining facilities**, **hotels**, **racetracks**, **catering businesses**, **bowling alleys**, and **taverns/taprooms** (with specific tavern/taproom conditions).  
- Prohibits any person **under age 21** from entering into or working in **any capacity** in a **tavern or taproom** that offers **sexually oriented entertainment**, including certain adult-entertainment-licensed or advertised venues.  
- Changes statutorily mandated alcoholic beverage server training validity/renewal from **4 years to 2 years** (training exemption window also changes from **4 years to 2 years**).  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded labor pool for licensed establishments:** Individuals **18–20** may now perform alcohol **sale and service**, and may **prepare** alcoholic liquor under **direct 21+ supervision** (Title 4, § 904(g), (h), (i), (j)).  
- **More frequent mandated training:** Certification/renewal shifts from **every 4 years to every 2 years**, increasing the frequency of required responsible server training (Title 4, § 1204(d); § 1205).  
- **Explicit restriction for sexually oriented venues:** People **under 21** are barred from entering or working in taverns/taprooms offering sexually oriented entertainment, including venues licensed under **Title 24, Ch. 16** or offering sexually explicit performances defined in **Title 11, § 787** (Title 4, § 904(h)(4)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher training/recertification burden:** Server training certification and renewal become valid for **2 years** instead of **4 years**, requiring staff to retrain more frequently (Title 4, § 1204(d); § 1205).  
- **Operational supervision requirement:** If an **18–20-year-old** is involved in **preparation** of alcoholic liquor, a **21+** person must be **directly supervising** and **working behind the bar with** them, which may require staffing adjustments (Title 4, § 904(g)(2), (h)(1), (h)(2), (i)(2), (j)(2)).  
- **Under-21 staffing prohibition for certain venues:** Taverns/taprooms offering sexually oriented entertainment may not employ anyone **under 21 in any capacity**, potentially reducing hiring options for those businesses (Title 4, § 904(h)(4)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Directly supervised” definition:** The bill requires direct supervision by a **21+** person “working behind the bar with” the 18–20-year-old, but does not further define what qualifies as direct supervision beyond that phrasing (Title 4, § 904(g)(2), (h)(1), (h)(2), (i)(2), (j)(2)).  
- **Scope of “preparation of alcoholic liquor”:** The bill does not specify whether “preparation” includes tasks like garnishing, batching, opening wine/beer, or mixing non-alcoholic components (Title 4, § 904).  
- **Implementation details for training renewal:** The bill changes the statutory renewal period to **2 years** but does not specify transitional timing for currently certified individuals (Title 4, § 1204(d); § 1205).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Minimum age floor:** Prohibits involvement in **sale, service, or preparation** of alcoholic liquor by anyone **under 18** (Title 4, § 904(g)(1), (i)(1), (j)(1)).  
- **18–20 sale/service permitted:** Individuals **18, 19, or 20** may be involved in the **sale and service** of alcoholic liquor (Title 4, § 904(g)(2)).  
- **18–20 preparation permitted with conditions:** Individuals **18–20** may be involved in **preparation** only when **directly supervised** by a **21+** person who is **working behind the bar with them** (Title 4, § 904(g)(2), (h)(1), (h)(2), (i)(2), (j)(2)).  
- **Tavern/taproom rules updated:** A person **18+** may work in any capacity in a tavern or taproom; preparation by **18–20** is allowed only with the same **21+ direct supervision behind the bar** requirement (Title 4, § 904(h)(2)).  
- **Sexually oriented entertainment restriction:** A person **under 21** may not **enter into or work in any capacity** in a tavern or taproom offering sexually oriented entertainment, including:  
  - tavern/taproom also licensed as an adult entertainment establishment under **Title 24, Ch. 16**;  
  - tavern/taproom offering sexually explicit performances as defined in **Title 11, § 787**;  
  - tavern/taproom advertised as “strip club,” “adult entertainment club,” “gentlemen’s club,” or offering sexually oriented performances/services (Title 4, § 904(h)(4)).  
- **Training timing shortened:** Responsible alcoholic beverage server training lookback exemption changes from **4 years to 2 years** (Title 4, § 1204(d)).  
- **Certification validity shortened:** Certification validity changes from **4 years to 2 years**, and renewal recertification validity also changes from **4 years to 2 years** (Title 4, § 1205).

## Who Benefits
- **18–20-year-old workers** seeking bartending/service roles, because they may now participate in **sale and service**, and in **preparation** when **directly supervised** by a **21+** bartender behind the bar (Title 4, § 904).  
- **Licensed on-premises establishments** (restaurants, clubs with dining, hotels, racetracks, taverns/taprooms, catering businesses, bowling alleys) that can hire **18–20-year-olds** for expanded alcohol-service functions (Title 4, § 904).  

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Taverns/taprooms offering sexually oriented entertainment** that wish to hire or allow entry to individuals **under 21** (prohibited) (Title 4, § 904(h)(4)).  
- **Employees and employers** subject to responsible server training requirements due to the increased renewal frequency (**2 years** instead of **4 years**) (Title 4, § 1204(d); § 1205).
