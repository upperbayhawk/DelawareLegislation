## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 21 to require the Delaware Department of Transportation (DelDOT) to install and operate a traffic light signal violation monitoring system at an intersection when requested by a member of the General Assembly, within a set timeframe.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires DelDOT to **install and operate** a **traffic light signal violation monitoring system** at an intersection **requested by a member of the General Assembly**.
- Sets a firm deadline: **within 60 days** of the member’s request.
- Applies **“notwithstanding any other provision”** in the relevant section of Title 21.
- The bill text itself does **not** specify feasibility criteria, prioritization standards, or a process for screening intersections.
- Fiscal Note states the Act is assumed to become effective **upon signature by the Governor**.
- Fiscal Note indicates DelDOT’s typical deployment timeline is **approximately 6–9 months per location**, and increased request volume could extend that timeframe.
- Fiscal Note cites prior system costs of **$10,000–$35,000 per camera** plus **~$6,000 per month per location** for services (data collection, processing, citation prep, mailing).
- Fiscal impact is listed as **indeterminable** for **FY2026, FY2027, and FY2028** (Transportation Trust Fund).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Faster deployment**: Creates a clear statutory requirement to deploy a system within **60 days** of a legislator request (bill text).
- **Potential for quicker targeted enforcement** at intersections identified by policymakers (bill text requires install/operate upon request).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)

- **Uncapped demand risk**: Bill provides no numeric limit on requests; Fiscal Note warns increased request volume would extend DelDOT’s typical timeframe.
