## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 15 (Chapter 71) to revise the process for filling Delaware General Assembly vacancies by adding **special primary elections**, adjusting the **timelines and notices**, and modifying procedures in **general election years** to avoid infeasible or closely spaced elections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a two-step vacancy-election process: a **special primary election** (when needed) followed by a **special general election**.  
- Requires the **writ of election** to be issued **within 5 days** of a vacancy (shortened from 10 days) and to include the **special primary date** and **candidate filing/withdrawal deadlines**.  
- Sets the **special primary election** date to **30–40 days** after the writ date (with additional rules in general election years).  
- If a special primary is needed, requires the **special general election** to be **35–40 days after** the special primary.  
- In general election years, if the seat is already on the general election ballot and the special primary date would be **on or after May 15**, the vacancy is filled at the **general election** (no special primary).  
- Requires fast-turnaround candidate steps: candidates must file within **5 days** after the writ; withdrawal is due **no later than 2 days after** the filing deadline; background-check appointment must be scheduled **within 48 hours after** the filing deadline.  
- Requires the State Election Commissioner to notify the writ-issuing officer **within 24 hours** after the withdrawal deadline whether a special primary is necessary; the officer must then issue an **amended** or **supplemental** writ accordingly.  
- Fiscal note: cost estimate of **$196,000** to hold a **primary special election with 10 days of early voting**; overall annual fiscal impact is **indeterminable** because special elections are unpredictable.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Adds voter choice within parties** by allowing a **special primary election** rather than relying solely on party nomination processes (Synopsis; §§ 7101–7102).  
- **More predictable, standardized timelines** (e.g., writ within **5 days**, primary within **30–40 days** of writ, general within **35–40 days** of primary), which may improve administrative planning (§§ 7101–7102).  
- **Avoids closely spaced elections** in general election years by shifting certain vacancy contests to the **primary/general election dates** or postponing to **December 12–20** in limited cases (§ 7102(b); § 7102A; Synopsis).  
- Requires earlier **candidate qualification checks** (Commissioner determination at least **15 days** before the special election) and clarifies that criminal-history information received is **not a public record** (§ 7103(d)(3), (d)(5)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher potential election administration costs** when a special primary is needed; fiscal note estimates **$196,000** for a primary special election with **10 days of early voting** (Fiscal Note, Assumption #4).  
- **Very compressed candidate timelines** (file within **5 days** of writ; schedule background-check appointment within **48 hours** after filing deadline), which may burden prospective candidates (§ 7103(a), (d)).  
- Candidates must **pay for the criminal history background check** (though it may be reimbursed as a campaign expenditure) (§ 7103(d)(4)).  
- In some scenarios, filling the vacancy at the **general election** (when special primary date would be **on/after May 15** and the office is on the general election ballot) may leave the seat vacant until that election (§ 7101(c)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Writ issuance timing and contents**
  - Writ must be issued **within 5 days** of vacancy creation (previously 10 days) and must include:  
    1) **Date of special primary**; 2) **candidate file/withdraw deadlines** (§ 7101(a)).  
  - If there is no presiding officer / house not in session, the **Governor shall issue** the writ (§ 7101(b)).
- **Adds special primary election framework**
  - Special primary date generally **no less than 30 days and no more than 40 days** after writ date (§ 7102(a)).  
  - If special primary is necessary, special general must be **35–40 days after** the special primary (§ 7102(c)(2)).
- **General election year modifications**
  - If office is on the general election ballot and special primary date is **on/after May 15**, vacancy filled at **general election** (§ 7101(c)).  
  - If office is not on the general election ballot, special election dates are modified under § 7102A (including tying special elections to the **primary** and **general** election dates in some cases).
  - If vacancy would otherwise require a special election after **June 30** but before general election, election may be held at general election, with a limited exception producing a **December 12–20** window (§ 7102(b)).
- **Candidate filing, withdrawal, and nominations**
  - Candidate filing due **no later than 5 days** after writ date (§ 7103(a)(1)).  
  - Withdrawal due **no later than 2 days after** the filing deadline (§ 7103(e)).  
  - If no party candidates file, nomination by party committee must be submitted **no later than 48 hours after** the end of the 5-day filing period (§ 7103(a)(3)b).
  - Unaffiliated candidate petition threshold: signatures of at least **1%** of registered voters in the district (as of the first day of the month preceding the month the vacancy was created); due **no later than 25 days** before the special general election (§ 7103(b)).
- **Criminal history background check requirements**
  - Candidate must request background check **no earlier than 90 days before** the filing deadline and **no less than 25 days before** the special election, and must schedule appointment **no later than 48 hours after** the filing deadline; proof required before filing/nomination/petition (§ 7103(d)).  
  - SBI reporting deadline: **within 10 days** of receipt (§ 7103(d)(2)).  
  - Background-check information received by Commissioner is **not a public record** (§ 7103(d)(3)).  
  - Cost is paid by candidate; may be reimbursed as authorized campaign expenditure (§ 7103(d)(4)).  
  - Commissioner qualification determination required at least **15 days** before the special election (§ 7103(d)(5)).
- **Notice requirements**
  - Department must post proclamation the next day after receiving a writ/amended writ/supplemental writ (or next business day if Sunday/holiday) and must post on courthouse door, state website, Department website, and state calendar (§ 7104).  
  - Newspaper notice must be published on the **5th, 3rd, and last day** before the special election (§ 7104(3)).

## Who Benefits
- **Voters** in affected legislative districts, who may have a **special primary** to choose party nominees (Synopsis; §§ 7101–7102).  
- **Unaffiliated candidates**, who have a defined petition route (with a **1%** signature threshold) to access the special general election ballot (§ 7103(b)).  
- **Election administrators**, to the extent modified timing rules aim to prevent elections being held too close together and align some contests with existing election dates (Synopsis; §§ 7101(c), 7102(b), 7102A).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Candidates**, due to compressed filing/withdrawal and background-check scheduling deadlines and requirement to pay for the background check upfront (§ 7103(a), (d), (e)).  
- **State/Department of Elections budgets**, when special primaries are triggered (estimated **$196,000** per primary special election with 10 days early voting) (Fiscal Note).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon signature of the Governor** (Fiscal Note, Assumption #1).
- Key deadlines/thresholds in the process:
  - Writ issued: **within 5 days** of vacancy (§ 7101(a)).  
  - Candidate filing: **within 5 days after** writ date (§ 7103(a)(1)).  
  - Candidate withdrawal: **no later than 2 days after** filing deadline (§ 7103(e)).  
  - Commissioner notice on need for special primary: **within 24 hours after** withdrawal deadline (§ 7102(b)).  
  - Officer issues amended/supplemental writ: **within 48 hours** of receiving Commissioner notice (§ 7102(c)).  
  - Special primary election: generally **30–40 days after** writ date (§ 7102(a)).  
  - Special general election (if special primary needed): **35–40 days after** special primary (§ 7102(c)(2)).  
  - General election-year thresholds: **May 15**, **June 30**, **September 1**, and (in one scenario) **December 12–20** (§ 7101(c); § 7102(b); § 7102A).  
  - Unaffiliated petition due: **25 days before** special general election (§ 7103(b)).  
  - Commissioner qualification determination: at least **15 days before** special election (§ 7103(d)(5)).
