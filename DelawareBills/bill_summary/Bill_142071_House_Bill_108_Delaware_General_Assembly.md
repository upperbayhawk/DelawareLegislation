## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware personal income tax law (Title 30, §1106) to increase the maximum pension/retirement income subtraction (exclusion) from Delaware taxable income.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Increases the pension/retirement income exclusion cap to **$25,000** for certain taxpayers.  
- For **taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025**, taxpayers **age 60 or older** may subtract up to **$25,000** of eligible pension/retirement income.  
- For **taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025**, taxpayers **under age 60** may subtract the **greater of**: (A) up to **$2,000** of qualifying pension income, **or** (B) up to **$25,000** of a **U.S. military pension**.  
- Prior law (for **2022–2024 taxable years**) generally capped the exclusion at **$12,500** (with a military pension special rule for under-60 taxpayers).  
- The bill text shown does not add new eligibility categories; it primarily increases the **dollar cap** (from **$12,500** to **$25,000**) beginning in 2025.  
- The synopsis states the change plainly: increase the pension exclusion from **$12,500** to **$25,000**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Tax relief for retirees and pension recipients**: Allows eligible taxpayers to subtract up to **$25,000** (instead of **$12,500**) of qualifying pension/retirement income for taxable years beginning **on or after January 1, 2025**.  
- **Enhanced benefit for military retirees under 60**: Under age 60, the military pension subtraction cap rises to **$25,000** (up from **$12,500** for 2022–2024 taxable years per the existing statute language shown).

## Key Policy Changes
- Amends **30 Del. C. §1106(b)(3)** to increase the maximum subtraction amounts for certain pension/retirement income categories for **taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2025**:
  - **Under age 60**: greater of  
    - up to **$2,000** in qualifying pension income, **or**  
    - up to **$25,000** in a **U.S. military pension**.  
  - **Age 60 or older**: up to **$25,000** as pensions from employers, the United States, the State, or subdivisions, **or** as **eligible retirement income**.
- Leaves earlier-year structures in place as shown (including **$12,500** cap for **taxable years beginning on or after January 1, 2022 and before January 1, 2025**).
