## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 11 Del. C. § 1911 to expand statewide arrest authority for police officers (including county and municipal officers), including in specified circumstances while off-duty, and to add/clarify notification requirements when acting outside an officer’s jurisdiction.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands § 1911(c)(1) to allow a police officer (not limited to “on-duty,” subject to new conditions) to make a **warrantless arrest anywhere in Delaware** when probable cause exists for certain serious offenses committed/attempted in the officer’s presence.  
- Covers crimes that **create a substantial risk of death or serious physical injury** to another person.  
- Authorizes statewide warrantless arrest authority for certain traffic offenses committed in the officer’s presence, including **DUI (21 Del. C. § 4177)** and adds **reckless driving (§ 4175)**, **aggressive driving (§ 4175A)**, and **overtaking/passing a stopped school bus (§ 4166)**.  
- Requires an officer acting outside the officer’s jurisdiction to take **reasonable measures** to notify the **primary jurisdictional police agency** **as soon as practicable** of the crime location.  
- For the listed traffic offenses, the officer must also report **vehicle registration number, vehicle description, and number of occupants**.  
- Expands § 1911(d) by removing the “on-duty” limitation so a police officer may execute an **arrest warrant statewide** for an offense committed within the officer’s employing agency’s jurisdiction.  
- Requires notice to the primary jurisdictional agency of the **intended time and place** of executing the arrest warrant when outside jurisdiction.  
- Requires a **two-thirds vote of the members elected to each house** for passage (Delaware Constitution Art. IX, § 1) because it indirectly amends municipal charter authority.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Faster intervention for high-risk crimes statewide:** Officers may act immediately (without a warrant) when a crime poses a **substantial risk of death or serious physical injury** (11 Del. C. § 1911(c)(1)).  
- **Broader enforcement for serious driving conduct:** Explicit statewide arrest authority includes **DUI** and adds **reckless driving, aggressive driving, and passing a stopped school bus** when committed in the officer’s presence (21 Del. C. §§ 4177, 4175, 4175A, 4166).  
- **Improved interagency coordination requirement:** When outside jurisdiction, officers must provide notice “**as soon as practicable**,” plus specified vehicle/occupant information for the listed traffic offenses (11 Del. C. § 1911(c)(2)).  
- **More flexibility in executing warrants statewide:** Allows a police officer (not limited to “on-duty”) to arrest statewide on an issued warrant for an offense committed within the officer’s employing agency jurisdiction (11 Del. C. § 1911(d)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced reliance on local jurisdiction boundaries:** The bill authorizes arrests “**at any location within the State**,” which may increase cross-jurisdiction encounters and operational complexity (11 Del. C. § 1911(c)(1), (d)).  
- **Vague notification standard:** Requires “**reasonable measures**” and “**as soon as practicable**” notice, which may be inconsistently applied and could complicate compliance assessment (11 Del. C. § 1911(c)(2), (d)).  
- **Potential for increased administrative workload:** The requirement to relay vehicle registration, description, and occupant count for listed traffic offenses could add reporting steps (11 Del. C. § 1911(c)(2)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **11 Del. C. § 1911(c)(1):** Removes the explicit “**on-duty**” limitation (replacing with “Subject to paragraph (c)(2) and (c)(3)”) and confirms statewide authority to arrest without a warrant upon view when probable cause exists for:
  - crimes creating a **substantial risk of death or serious physical injury**, or
  - violations of **21 Del. C. § 4177 (DUI)**, **§ 4175 (reckless driving)**, **§ 4175A (aggressive driving)**, **§ 4166 (passing a stopped school bus)**.
- **11 Del. C. § 1911(c)(2):** Creates/clarifies an out-of-jurisdiction notification requirement “**as soon as practicable**,” including additional vehicle/occupant details for the specified traffic offenses.
- **11 Del. C. § 1911(d):** Removes “on-duty” from warrant execution authority; a police officer may arrest statewide on an issued warrant for an offense committed within the employing agency’s jurisdiction, with notice of intended time and place when acting outside jurisdiction.
- **Voting threshold:** Requires **two-thirds** approval in each chamber due to Delaware Constitution Art. IX, § 1 (municipal charter implications).

## Who Benefits
- **Residents and road users statewide** potentially benefit from expanded authority to address **DUI, reckless/aggressive driving, and passing stopped school buses** committed in an officer’s presence.  
- **Law enforcement agencies and officers** benefit from clearer statewide arrest authority and warrant execution flexibility outside their employing agency’s jurisdiction.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Individuals suspected of covered offenses** may be subject to arrest by officers outside the officers’ usual jurisdictions, including in circumstances where the officer is not “on-duty” (as described in the synopsis and reflected by removal of “on-duty” language in the statute).
