## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Establish a publicly accessible “animal abuse offender list” maintained by the Office of Animal Welfare (OAW) and require animal shelters to check the list before approving an adoption, prohibiting adoptions to listed individuals.  

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new Subchapter IV in Title 11 establishing an **Animal Abuse Offender List** for **adult** individuals convicted of specified animal abuse offenses.  
- Requires OAW to **publicly post** the list on its website and include: **name(s)/aliases**, **date of birth**, **booking photo**, and **crime of conviction**.  
- Prohibits publishing certain identifiers on the list: **Social Security number**, **driver license number**, and **any other state/federal identification number**.  
- Requires **court clerks** to send OAW the **judgment and date of birth** for each qualifying conviction **within 21 calendar days** of the judgment date.  
- Requires OAW to keep an individual on the list **5 years** (misdemeanor animal abuse offense) or **15 years** (felony animal abuse offense), with removal if no new animal abuse conviction occurs during that period.  
- Adds an **early removal** process: petition allowed **2 years after conviction** (misdemeanor) or **7 years after conviction** (felony); the Attorney General may answer **within 30 days** of service; court has discretion using specified factors.  
- Requires **automatic removal** if the underlying conviction is **expunged**, subject to **11 Del. C. § 4372(e)**.  
- Amends Title 16 to require an **animal shelter** to consult the list before adoption and **bar** adoptions to listed individuals; provides a limited **pet store premises exemption** (no ownership interest in animals).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved screening for adoptions:** Requires shelters to consult the offender list before adoption and **prohibits adoptions** to listed individuals (Title 16, § 3003F(f)(1)).  
- **Public transparency:** The list is **publicly available** on OAW’s website, potentially helping the public and animal agencies identify individuals convicted of covered offenses (Title 11, § 4131(a); synopsis).  
- **Defined publication limits for sensitive data:** Explicitly bars posting SSNs, driver license numbers, and other government ID numbers (Title 11, § 4131(c)(1)-(3)).  
- **Due process/relief mechanism:** Allows **early removal petitions** after specified waiting periods and requires court consideration of enumerated factors (Title 11, § 4133).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Public disclosure of personal information:** The public list includes **date of birth** and a **booking photograph**, which may raise privacy and reputational concerns (Title 11, § 4131(b)(2)-(3)).  
- **Long retention periods:** Standard listing periods are **5 years** (misdemeanor) and **15 years** (felony), which may impose extended consequences even after sentence completion (Title 11, § 4131(e)).  
- **Administrative workload:** Court clerks must transmit documents within **21 days**, OAW must maintain and update a public website list, and courts/AG must handle early removal petitions (Title 11, § 4131(d); § 4133(d)-(e)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Title 11 “Animal Abuse Offender List” framework** (new Subchapter IV).  
- **Defines covered “animal abuse offense”** as convictions under:  
  - **11 Del. C. § 775 (Bestiality)**  
  - **11 Del. C. § 1325 (Cruelty to animals; class A misdemeanor; class F felony)**  
  - **11 Del. C. § 1325A (Unlawful trade in dog or cat byproducts; class B misdemeanor/class A misdemeanor)**  
  - **11 Del. C. § 1326 (Animal fighting and baiting; class E felony)**  
  *(Note: The enrolled/approved version shown removes § 1250 from the list of offenses.)*  
- **Specifies what information is posted** and what is prohibited from posting (Title 11, § 4131(b)-(c)).  
- **Imposes reporting deadline on court clerks:** **21 calendar days** from judgment (Title 11, § 4131(d)).  
- **Sets listing durations:** **5 years** (misdemeanor) and **15 years** (felony), with removal if no new animal abuse conviction during the listing period (Title 11, § 4131(e)-(f)).  
- **Creates early removal process:** petition after **2 years** (misdemeanor) or **7 years** (felony); AG response window **30 days**; court discretion guided by enumerated factors (Title 11, § 4133).  
- **Automatic removal after expungement** of the underlying conviction, subject to **11 Del. C. § 4372(e)** (Title 11, § 4133(f)).  
- **Adoption restriction:** Shelters must check the list and **may not approve adoption** to listed individuals; limited exemption for certain pet store-hosted adoption events where the pet store has **no ownership interest** (Title 16, § 3003F(f)(1)-(2)).

## Who Benefits
- **Animals and animal welfare agencies/shelters:** Shelters gain a mandated tool to prevent adoption to listed offenders (Title 16, § 3003F(f)).  
- **General public:** Gains access to a public OAW website list of adult individuals convicted of specified animal abuse offenses (Title 11, § 4131(a)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected

- **Animal shelters and state entities (OAW, courts, court clerks, AG):** Additional administrative duties and compliance steps (Title 11, §§ 4131(d), 4132, 4133; Title 16, § 3003F(f)).
