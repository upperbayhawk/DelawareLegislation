## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Propose the first leg of a Delaware Constitution amendment to require most elected officials to **resign from their current elected office before becoming eligible to run as a candidate for a different elected office**, with an exception for the Governor and Lieutenant Governor.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Amends **Article II** to require a **member of the House** or **member of the Senate** to **resign** before being **eligible as a candidate** for a **different elected office**.
- Amends **Article III** to require a person holding elected office (except the **Governor** and **Lieutenant Governor**) to **resign** before being **eligible as a candidate** for a **different elected office**.
- Defines “elected office” broadly to include **state, county, or municipal positions** selected by voters in Delaware, including the **General Assembly**; Article II text also expressly lists **Governor** and **Lieutenant-Governor** as included elected offices.
- Creates an explicit **exception**: the **Governor and Lieutenant-Governor are not required to resign** if they run for a different elected office.
- Operates as a **constitutional amendment proposal** and therefore requires a **two-thirds vote** in each chamber under **Article XVI, § 1**.
- The bill is described as the **“first leg”** of a constitutional amendment (implying additional constitutional steps beyond this act, though those steps are not detailed in the bill text).
- No appropriations, fees, penalties, or enforcement mechanisms are stated in the bill text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces dual-role conflicts during campaigns:** Requires resignation before candidacy for a different office (Article II §14A; Article III §11A), potentially limiting officeholders from campaigning while continuing to exercise powers of their current office.
- **Applies broadly across government levels:** Covers “any other state, county, or municipal position…selected by voters” (Article II §14A; Article III §11A), creating a uniform rule across many elected roles.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Forces vacancies and potential loss of continuity:** Because an officeholder must resign **before being eligible to be a candidate** for another office, this can trigger early departures from elected positions (Article II §14A; Article III §11A).
- **Unequal treatment for top executive offices:** The **Governor and Lieutenant-Governor are exempt** from the resignation requirement (Synopsis; Article III §11A), while other officeholders are not.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Timing of “before being eligible as a candidate”:** The bill does not specify whether eligibility is tied to filing for candidacy, party nomination, ballot qualification, or another election-law milestone.
- **Scope differences between Article II and Article III language:**  
  - Article II §14A states “Elected office includes the office of the Governor, the Lieutenant-Governor, the General Assembly…”  
  - Article III §11A states elected office includes “the General Assembly, and any other state, county, or municipal position…” and separately exempts Governor/Lt. Governor.  
  The practical effect of these different formulations is not clarified in the text.
- **Interaction with existing election and vacancy procedures:** The bill does not state how resignations interact with Delaware statutes on filling vacancies or with election calendars.
- **Applicability to running for re-election:** The text applies to running for a **“different elected office”**; it does not address re-election to the same office.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New constitutional resignation-before-candidacy requirement for legislators:**  
  - House members must resign before running for a different elected office (Article II, §14A).  
  - Senators must resign before running for a different elected office (Article II, §14A).
- **New constitutional resignation-before-candidacy requirement for most elected officials statewide:**  
  - Any person holding elected office in Delaware (excluding the Governor and Lieutenant-Governor) must resign before running for a different elected office (Article III, §11A).
- **Defines covered “elected office” to include** state, county, and municipal positions chosen by voters in Delaware (Article II, §14A; Article III, §11A).
- **Explicit exception for Governor and Lieutenant Governor:** They are **not required to resign** if they run for a different elected office (Synopsis; Article III §11A).
- **Supermajority requirement for passage:** Requires **two-thirds** of members elected to each chamber to approve because it amends the Constitution (Synopsis referencing Article XVI, §1).

## Who Benefits
- **Voters**, to the extent the rule increases clarity that officeholders seeking a different office must step down first (Article II §14A; Article III §11A).
- **Potential challengers/candidates**, because incumbents for other offices (except Governor/Lt. Governor) cannot retain their current office while becoming eligible as a candidate for a different one (Article III §11A).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Current elected officials (other than Governor and Lieutenant Governor)** who want to run for another office, because they must resign first to be eligible as a candidate (Article III §11A; Article II §14A).
- **Constituents of resigning officeholders**, due to potential vacancies and turnover caused by required resignations (Article II §14A; Article III §11A).
