## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To require that school board candidates and members be “inhabitants” of the school district (or the designated portion of the district) they represent, defining “inhabitant” to include both legal residence and physical presence, and establishing a process to declare a vacancy when a member no longer meets that standard.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines **“inhabitant”** as a person who **both (1) claims legal residence and (2) physically resides** in the relevant designated area or school district.  
- Updates school board member qualification language statewide to require a member be a **“resident inhabitant”** of the district.  
- Creates an objective physical-presence test: a school board member **not physically present for more than 75% of the days** in **any consecutive year-long period** during the term is **no longer an inhabitant** (and thus no longer qualified).  
- Provides exceptions: **military deployment** time is treated as **physically present** for the calculation; and absence due to an **unanticipated, uncontrollable event** is **not counted as absence**.  
- If a board does not declare a vacancy, a **district resident** with **good cause** may file an action in **Superior Court** to have the seat **declared vacant**.  
- Applies the “inhabitant” requirement across multiple provisions, including **vo-tech districts (gubernatorial appointees)** and specified school districts’ **election/candidacy** rules.  
- Enacted as **Chapter 50, Volume 85** and **approved June 30, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer eligibility standard:** The bill expressly defines “inhabitant” to require both legal residence and physical residence, reducing ambiguity in board eligibility determinations.  
- **Objective trigger for loss of qualification:** The **“more than 75% of the days”** absence rule provides a measurable threshold for when a member ceases to qualify.  
- **Continuity protections for service members:** Military deployment is expressly excluded from counting as absence, preventing loss of office based on deployment.  
- **Backstop enforcement mechanism:** Allows a resident to seek a **Superior Court** order to declare a vacancy when a board does not act.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential for litigation costs and disputes:** The bill authorizes residents to sue in **Superior Court** to declare vacancies, which could increase legal proceedings tied to board membership disputes.  
- **Administrative burden of tracking presence:** The 75% physical-presence standard may require documentation and factual determinations about “days” present/absent over a “consecutive year-long period.”  
- **Undefined standard may create disagreements:** The bill relies on terms like **“good cause”** and **“event that can neither be anticipated nor controlled,”** which may be contested in application.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What constitutes being **“physically present”** for a “day” (e.g., partial-day presence, commuting, temporary lodging)?  
- How should a school board or court **calculate** the **“more than 75% of the days”** threshold (calendar days vs. school days; treatment of travel days)?  
- What qualifies as **“good cause”** for a resident to bring the Superior Court action?  
- What events qualify as an **“event that can neither be anticipated nor controlled”** (scope and required proof)?  
- Whether boards must adopt any specific **procedures** to assess inhabitancy before a resident files suit (not addressed in the text).

## Key Policy Changes
- **New definition added (14 Del. C. § 1041):**  
  - “Inhabitant” = **claims legal residence + physically resides** in the designated area or school district.
- **Member qualification updated (14 Del. C. § 1052(b)):**  
  - Each member must be a **citizen of the State** and a **resident inhabitant** of the school district and **qualified to vote** at a school election in that district **at the time** of election/appointment.
- **Vacancy standard and process revised (14 Del. C. § 1054(a)):**  
  - If a member ceases to be an **inhabitant**, the member **ceases to be a member**.  
  - **75% threshold:** Not physically present **for more than 75% of days** in any **consecutive year-long period** of the term ⇒ **no longer an inhabitant**.  
  - **Exceptions:**  
    - **Military deployment** counts as physically present.  
    - Absence due to an **unanticipated, uncontrollable event** is not counted as absence.  
  - **Judicial remedy:** A resident with **good cause** may file in **Superior Court** if the board has not declared a vacancy; court may order the seat **vacant**.
- **Conforming updates across district-specific provisions:**  
  - Vo-tech board appointees must be **resident inhabitants** (14 Del. C. § 1064).  
  - Candidacy requirement updated for **Brandywine, Christina, Colonial, Red Clay** seats (14 Del. C. § 1066A).  
  - Continuing-qualification language for **Indian River** ties to being a **resident inhabitant** (14 Del. C. § 1068).  
  - Cape Henlopen and Milford area/at-large filing and eligibility language updated to **resident inhabitant** (14 Del. C. §§ 1069, 1070).

## Who Benefits
- **District voters/residents** seeking assurance that board members representing them **physically reside** in the district/area.  
- **School districts/boards** that want a clearer statutory basis to address residency/inhabitancy concerns.  
- **Service members** on school boards, due to the explicit **military deployment** protection in the physical-presence calculation.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **School board members/candidates** who claim legal residence but do not **physically reside** in the district/area consistent with the new definition.  
- **Members frequently away** from the district who may cross the **>75% of days absent** threshold in any consecutive year-long period (unless an exception applies).  
- **School districts/boards** that may need to respond to or participate in **Superior Court** proceedings challenging a member’s status.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Automatic loss of office upon loss of inhabitancy:** If a member ceases to be an inhabitant, the member **ceases to be a member** and the office is treated as **vacant** under the statute.  
- **Presence threshold for compliance:** Member must not be **absent more than 75% of days** in any **consecutive year-long period** of the term (with specified exceptions).  
- **Court enforcement:** A resident with **good cause** may bring an action in **Superior Court**; if the Court orders the seat vacant, the member **shall cease** to be a member and remaining members proceed to fill the vacancy under existing subsection (b) procedures (referenced but not reproduced in the text).
