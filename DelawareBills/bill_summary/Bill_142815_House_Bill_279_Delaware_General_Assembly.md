## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Promote marine equipment owner choice and competition for repair and maintenance by requiring original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) to provide owners and independent repair providers access—on “fair and reasonable terms”—to the same documentation, parts, and tools the manufacturer uses to diagnose, maintain, and repair marine equipment, including updates. (23 Del. C. § 1401)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires OEMs of marine equipment and parts sold or used in Delaware to make available, on fair and reasonable terms, any documentation, parts, and tools needed for diagnosis, maintenance, or repair, including updates. (§ 1403(a))
- Prohibits OEMs from using “parts pairing” or similar mechanisms to prevent installation/function of otherwise-functional parts (including non-OEM parts) or to reduce functionality after independent/owner repair. (§ 1403(b)(1)-(2))
- Bars false, misleading, deceptive, or non-dismissible repair alerts/warnings about parts. (§ 1403(b)(3))
- Prohibits charging additional fees or increased prices for future repairs and limiting who can purchase repair materials or perform repairs. (§ 1403(b)(4)-(5))
- For equipment with electronic locks, requires OEMs to provide access/reset documentation, tools, and parts to owners or independent repair providers with the owner’s express permission; may be provided via a “secure release system.” (§ 1403(c))
- Defines “fair and reasonable terms,” including: no minimum/maximum parts purchase quantities; documentation available without contracts/accounts and at no charge (except reasonable actual cost for printed copies); tools at no charge (except reasonable actual cost for physical form), and tools cannot require authorization or internet access. (§ 1404(5))
- Enforcement is via the Attorney General under Delaware’s consumer protection/unfair trade practice authorities if noncompliance constitutes an unfair/deceptive practice. (§ 1405)
- Applies only to equipment first manufactured and sold on or after the act’s effective date; the act takes effect **6 months after enactment**. (§ 1407; Section 2)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Expands repair options for owners by requiring OEMs to provide repair documentation/parts/tools on “fair and reasonable terms.” (§§ 1401, 1403(a), 1404(5))
- Helps independent repair providers compete by prohibiting OEM practices that block third-party parts, reduce functionality after repair, or restrict who can purchase tools/documentation/parts. (§ 1403(b)(1)-(2), (5))
- Reduces potential “lock-in” behaviors by banning parts pairing practices that prevent installation/function of otherwise-functional parts, including non-OEM replacement parts. (§ 1403(b)(1); § 1404(10))
- Improves access to repair materials by requiring documentation without contracts/account creation and at no charge (except reasonable actual printing/shipping cost for physical copies), and tools at no charge (except reasonable actual cost for physical form). (§ 1404(5)b, (5)b.1)
- Provides a pathway to address electronic locks needed for repair by requiring OEM support to access/reset locks with the owner’s express permission. (§ 1403(c))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- OEMs may need to provide documentation, tools, and parts broadly to owners and independent repair providers, including updates, which could increase OEM administrative burden. (§ 1403(a); § 1404(5))
- The act limits OEM use of parts pairing and related mechanisms, potentially reducing OEM control over repair ecosystems and post-repair performance management. (§ 1403(b))
- The act restricts tool access conditions (e.g., cannot require authorization or internet access for use/operation), which may constrain OEM preferred distribution or cybersecurity controls, subject to the bill’s stated limitations. (§ 1404(5)b.2; § 1406(d), (f))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- § 1405 references a “failure to provide documentation, parts, or tools as described in **§ 1803** of this Title,” but the requirements section in this bill is **§ 1403**; it is unclear if “§ 1803” is a drafting error and what precise cross-reference is intended.
- The standard “fair and reasonable terms” relies on multiple qualitative phrases (e.g., “substantial obligation,” “reasonably necessary,” “reasonable actual costs,” “impair the efficient and cost-effective performance”), which may require interpretation in enforcement. (§ 1404(5))
- The bill allows electronic-lock access materials to be provided through an “appropriate secure release system,” but does not define required features or standards for that system. (§ 1403(c))

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates/updates a statutory “Delaware Marine Equipment Right to Repair Act” framework in Title 23, Chapter 14. (§§ 1401-1407)
- Mandates OEM disclosure/provision (on fair and reasonable terms) of documentation, parts, and tools required for diagnosis/maintenance/repair for marine equipment and parts sold or used in Delaware, including updates; can be provided directly or via an authorized repair provider/distributor. (§ 1403(a))
- Prohibits OEM use of parts pairing or other mechanisms to:
  - prevent installation or functioning of otherwise-functional parts, including non-OEM parts; (§ 1403(b)(1))
  - reduce functionality/performance after independent/owner repair; (§ 1403(b)(2))
  - generate false/misleading/deceptive/non-dismissible part alerts; (§ 1403(b)(3))
  - charge additional fees or increased prices for future repairs; (§ 1403(b)(4))
  - limit who can buy repair materials or perform repairs. (§ 1403(b)(5))
- Requires access to electronic lock reset/access materials for repair when needed, available to owners or independent repair providers with the owner’s express permission, potentially via secure release. (§ 1403(c))
- Defines “fair and reasonable terms,” including:
  - no minimum/maximum part purchase quantity requirements; (§ 1404(5)a.2)
  - no contract beyond a purchase order for parts; (§ 1404(5)a.3)
  - documentation free without contract/account creation (except reasonable actual cost for printed copies); (§ 1404(5)b)
  - tools free (except reasonable actual cost for physical form), and tools cannot require authorization or internet access. (§ 1404(5)b.1-2)
- Limits and protections:
  - does not require disclosure of trade secrets except as necessary to perform diagnosis/maintenance/repair on fair/reasonable terms; (§ 1406(a))
  - preserves authorized repair provider/OEM contractual arrangements, but voids any term that waives/avoids/restricts OEM obligations under the act; (§ 1406(b), (b) (void/unenforceable waiver))
  - limits OEM/authorized repair provider liability for damage/injury caused by independent repair/owner unless attributable to OEM design/manufacturing defects. (§ 1406(c))
  - does not require bypassing anti-theft measures without owner authorization; (§ 1406(d))
  - does not require sale of unavailable parts; (§ 1406(e))
  - does not require sale of service materials illegal under federal/state law. (§ 1406(f))
  - clarifies parts dealers may mark up above wholesale price. (§ 1406(g))

## Who Benefits
- Marine equipment owners and lessees in Delaware seeking more repair choices and access to repair materials. (§§ 1401, 1403; § 1404(8))
- Independent repair providers operating in Delaware who lack OEM authorization arrangements. (§ 1403(a); § 1404(6))
- Potentially purchasers of non-OEM replacement parts, since OEMs cannot use parts pairing to block otherwise-functional parts. (§ 1403(b)(1); § 1404(10))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Original equipment manufacturers required to provide documentation/parts/tools (including updates) on specified terms and prohibited from using certain parts pairing/repair restriction practices. (§ 1403; § 1404(5))
- Authorized repair providers may face increased competition from independent repair providers with improved access to OEM materials. (Purpose § 1401; requirements § 1403)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** The act takes effect **6 months after it is enacted.** (Section 2)
- **Applicability threshold:** Applies to equipment **first manufactured and sold on or after the effective date** of the act. (§ 1407)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (OEMs):** Must make available to owners and independent repair providers documentation, parts, and tools required for diagnosis/maintenance/repair (including updates) on “fair and reasonable terms”; must comply with prohibitions on parts pairing and specified restrictive practices; must support electronic lock access/reset for repair with owner permission. (§ 1403(a)-(c); § 1404(5))
- **Enforcement:** If the Attorney General determines the failure to provide required materials constitutes an unfair method of competition, false advertising, or unfair/deceptive trade practice under **Chapter 25 of Title 6**, then all remedies/penalties/authority under that law are available for enforcement. (§ 1405)
