## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To prohibit “number plate flipping devices” on motor vehicles and set criminal penalties for possessing, using, or trafficking in such devices by amending Title 21, §§ 2115 and 2116 of the Delaware Code.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new unlawful act in 21 Del. C. § 2115: possessing, operating a motor vehicle with, purchasing, installing, manufacturing, selling, offering to sell, or otherwise distributing a **number plate flipping device**.
- Defines a “number plate flipping device” as a **manual, electric, or mechanical** device designed/adapted to be installed on a motor vehicle that either:
  - switches between **two or more number plates**, or
  - hides a number plate by turning it so the **registration number is not visible**.
- Applies enhanced penalty provision in 21 Del. C. § 2116(b) to the new § 2115(9) offense (not the general penalty only).
- Penalties for violating § 2115(9): **first offense**: **$50–$200 fine** and/or **30–90 days imprisonment**; **subsequent offense**: **$100–$300 fine** and/or **90 days–6 months imprisonment**.
- Makes technical drafting changes to conform to the Legislative Drafting Manual and updates internal references/numbering.
- Removes an outdated reference implying farm vehicles may be exempt from registration (synopsis notes the exemption was removed from the Code in **2014**).
- Bill required a **two-thirds vote** in each chamber under Delaware Constitution Article IV, § 28 because it creates a new crime within specified lower-court jurisdictions (as stated in the synopsis).
- The enacted act was **Approved August 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Improves enforceability against deliberate concealment or switching of license plates by explicitly criminalizing possession, use, and commercial distribution of plate-flipping equipment (new § 2115(9)).
- Targets not only drivers but also the supply chain (purchase, install, manufacture, sell, offer to sell, or otherwise distribute), potentially reducing availability (new § 2115(9)).
- Provides clear statutory definition of prohibited devices, including both “switching” and “hiding” functions, and covering manual/electric/mechanical designs (definition in § 2115(9)).
- Establishes specific fines and potential incarceration for the conduct by tying the new offense to § 2116(b) penalty levels.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Creates a criminal offense with potential jail time (minimum **30 days** for first offense under § 2116(b)), increasing criminal justice involvement for covered conduct.
- Broadly criminalizes “possession” and “otherwise distribute,” which may increase enforcement reach beyond actual on-road use (new § 2115(9)), without detailing exceptions in the bill text.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New prohibition (21 Del. C. § 2115(9))**: Makes it unlawful to **possess**, **operate any motor vehicle with**, **purchase**, **install**, **manufacture**, **sell**, **offer to sell**, or **otherwise distribute** a **number plate flipping device**.
- **New definition**: “Number plate flipping device” includes any manual/electric/mechanical device installed on a vehicle that:
  - switches between **two or more** plates to change the displayed plate, or
  - turns/hides the plate so the **registration number is not visible**.
- **Penalty alignment (21 Del. C. § 2116(b)(1))**: Adds § 2115(9) to the list of violations subject to heightened penalties:
  - **First offense**: **$50–$200** and/or **30–90 days** imprisonment.
  - **Subsequent**: **$100–$300** and/or **90 days–6 months** imprisonment.
- **Technical/code-cleanup changes**: Conforms language to drafting manual; removes outdated farm-vehicle registration-exemption reference (synopsis notes exemption removed in **2014**).

## Who Benefits
- Law enforcement and courts, by having an explicit statutory basis to charge conduct involving plate flipping/hiding devices (new § 2115(9); penalties in § 2116(b)).
- The general public, to the extent clearer prohibitions deter plate concealment/switching intended to evade identification (purpose reflected by the conduct prohibited and device definition).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Individuals who possess, use, buy, install, manufacture, sell, offer to sell, or distribute number plate flipping devices, who become subject to fines and potential incarceration (new § 2115(9); § 2116(b)).
- Businesses or sellers of such devices (including those who “offer to sell” or “otherwise distribute”) newly exposed to criminal penalties (new § 2115(9)).
