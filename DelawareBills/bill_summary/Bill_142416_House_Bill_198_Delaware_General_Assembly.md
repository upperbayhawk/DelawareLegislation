## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 10, §1045 to allow a respondent in a Protection From Abuse (PFA) proceeding to **consent to a protective order lasting more than 2 years, up to and including a permanent protective order**, without requiring the Court to make findings regarding aggravating factors under §1045(f).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes the maximum duration rules for certain PFA relief under **10 Del. C. §1045**.
- Keeps the general rule that PFA relief is for a **fixed period not to exceed 1 year**, with certain relief eligible for longer.
- Maintains that relief under **§1045(a)(1)** (restrain domestic violence) and **§1045(a)(2)** (restrain contact) may be entered for up to **2 years**.
- Allows **more than 2 years** for §1045(a)(1) and (a)(2) if either condition is met:
  - The **respondent consents**, for a fixed period “**up to and including a permanent protective order**”; or
  - A longer period is ordered under **§1045(c) or §1045(f)**.
- Explicitly provides that a respondent’s consent can support a permanent order **without the Court needing to find aggravating factors under §1045(f)** (per Synopsis).
- Does not add dollar amounts, fees, or appropriations in the bill text.
- Does not specify an effective date in the provided bill text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Enables permanent or longer-than-2-year protection by agreement**: Victims/petitioners may obtain longer-lasting no-abuse/no-contact relief under **§1045(a)(1)-(2)** when the respondent **consents**, without litigating aggravating factors (Synopsis).
- **May reduce evidentiary burden and hearing complexity** for cases resolved by consent, because the Synopsis states the court need not find aggravating factors under **§1045(f)** when the respondent consents.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Permanent protective orders may be entered based on consent** without a required judicial finding of aggravating factors (Synopsis), which may raise concerns about:
  - Respondents agreeing to long-term restrictions without the same factual findings that would otherwise be required under **§1045(f)**.
- The bill text does not describe additional procedural safeguards specific to consent-based permanent orders beyond the fact of consent.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Duration framework retained with expansion by consent**:
  - General PFA relief remains effective for a fixed period **not to exceed 1 year**.
  - Relief under **§1045(a)(1)** and **§1045(a)(2)** remains eligible for a fixed period **not to exceed 2 years**.
  - **New/clarified authority**: §1045(a)(1)-(2) relief may be entered for **greater than 2 years** if:
    1) The **respondent consents** to entry “for a fixed period of time, up to and including a **permanent protective order**”; or  
    2) A longer period is ordered under **§1045(c) or §1045(f)**.
- **Synopsis-stated change**: Consent permits a permanent order **without requiring a court finding of aggravating factors under §1045(f)**.

## Who Benefits
- **Petitioners/victims of domestic violence** seeking longer-lasting protection under **§1045(a)(1)-(2)** when the respondent is willing to consent.
- **Courts and parties** in cases that can be resolved by consent (potentially fewer contested findings related to §1045(f), per Synopsis).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Respondents** who consent to long-duration or permanent protective orders, as such orders can impose long-term restraints (no-abuse and no-contact) without the aggravating-factor findings otherwise associated with §1045(f) (per Synopsis).
