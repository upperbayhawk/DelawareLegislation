## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29, Chapter 69 of the Delaware Code to modernize the definition of “reverse auction” and expressly allow the State’s procurement office (the Section of Government Support Services in the Office of Management and Budget) to use reverse auctions for **professional services** when advantageous.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates the statutory definition of reverse auction from a process limited to **goods and nonprofessional services** to one that can be used for **goods and professional or nonprofessional services**.  
- Authorizes the Section of Government Support Services (“the Section”), with approval by the Section director or designee, to use reverse auctions for **professional services** procurements when the Section determines it is advantageous.  
- Requires vendors to **preregister before the auction opens** to allow vetting of vendor responsibility and agreement to solicitation terms/conditions.  
- Requires the solicitation to disclose that the procurement includes a **reverse auction**, and include a schedule of proposing activities and procedures.  
- Makes vendor **prices visible to participants during bidding** but **without revealing vendor identity** during the auction.  
- Allows the Section/procuring agency (with written approval) to use a **third-party vendor to conduct the reverse auction** “without the need for complying with the procurement provisions of this chapter.”  
- Requires each price proposal received through the reverse auction (including the **name and address** of the submitting vendor) to be **recorded**.  
- Delays public inspection of proposals/evaluations until **after a signed contract is received**, then requires release subject to Freedom of Information Act exemptions (Chapter 100, Title 29).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded tool for professional services procurement:** The bill explicitly permits reverse auctions for professional services when deemed advantageous by the Section (§ 6989D).  
- **Potentially increases price competition:** Real-time competitive bidding with the ability for vendors to lower prices during the auction is built into the method (§ 6902(27)).  
- **Vendor responsibility vetting upfront:** Mandatory preregistration before the opening date/time supports screening for responsibility and ensures bidders agree to solicitation terms (§ 6989D).  
- **Process clarity:** Requires the solicitation to state it includes a reverse auction and provide the schedule and procedures (§ 6989D).  
- **Post-award transparency:** After contract award, proposals must be available for public inspection (subject to FOIA exemptions) (§ 6989D).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced procurement safeguards when using a third party:** The bill allows contracting with a third-party vendor to conduct the auction “**without the need for complying with the procurement provisions of this chapter**,” which may reduce otherwise applicable procedural requirements (§ 6989D).  
- **Delayed public access to proposals and evaluations:** Proposal materials and evaluation aspects “may not be open for public inspection until after receipt of a signed contract,” limiting transparency during the selection period (§ 6989D).  
- **Potential fit issues for professional services:** The reverse auction is structured around driving price downward in real time (§ 6902(27)), which may be less aligned with procurements where qualitative factors are significant (the text does not describe how “most advantageous offer” is evaluated in practice).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Definition change (§ 6902(27)):**
  - Replaces “Reverse auctioning” definition limited to “specified goods and nonprofessional services” with a revised “Reverse auction” definition covering **goods and professional or nonprofessional services**.
  - Changes auction mechanics to: vendors submit bids/proposals through real-time electronic competitive bidding; **vendor prices are revealed to all participants without revealing vendor identity**; vendors can lower prices during the auction period.
- **New/updated authorization for professional services reverse auctions (§ 6989D):**
  - Allows the Section (or an agency, if allowed by the Section) to use reverse auctions for **professional services** when advantageous.
  - Requires a simplified administrative process and preregistration for vetting responsibility and agreeing to contractual terms/conditions.
  - Requires solicitations to state reverse auction use, provide schedule and procedures.
  - Permits contracting with a third-party vendor to conduct auctions **without complying with procurement provisions of Chapter 69**, if approved in writing by the Section director/designee.
  - Requires proposal recording; restricts public inspection until after a signed contract; then releases proposals subject to FOIA exemptions (Chapter 100, Title 29).

## Who Benefits
- **Office of Management and Budget / Section of Government Support Services:** Gains express authority and a defined process to use reverse auctions for professional services (§ 6989D).  
- **State agencies (if allowed by the Section):** May be permitted to use reverse auctions for professional services procurements (§ 6989D).  
- **Vendors able to compete effectively in real-time electronic bidding:** Can adjust pricing during the auction period (§ 6902(27)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Vendors preferring traditional proposal processes for professional services:** May be disadvantaged by real-time price competition dynamics (§ 6902(27), § 6989D).  
- **Public/oversight stakeholders seeking earlier transparency:** Public inspection is delayed until after a signed contract (§ 6989D).  
- **Vendors concerned about competitive sensitivity:** Prices are revealed to all participants during bidding (though identity is not revealed) (§ 6902(27)).
