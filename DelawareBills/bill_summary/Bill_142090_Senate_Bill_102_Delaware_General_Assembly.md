## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 24 (Chapter 14) to change licensing/renewal rules for apprentice electricians by limiting how long an individual may remain licensed as an apprentice electrician, and to create/clarify a separate “school apprentice electrician” category tied to vocational high school enrollment. The Act is effective **July 1, 2026**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Sets a **5-year limit** on apprentice electrician licensure/renewal, with limited **1-year discretionary extensions** for “good cause shown” to complete a Board-approved apprentice program.  
- Creates/defines **“school apprentice electrician”** and makes that license **non-renewable**, remaining valid while the holder is enrolled in a Board-approved vocational high school program or has turned **18**, **whichever is later**.  
- For apprentices licensed **≥ 5 years before July 1, 2026**, prohibits renewal **beyond the license’s current expiration date** (subject to possible 1-year extension).  
- For apprentices licensed **< 5 years as of July 1, 2026**, prohibits renewal and instead **extends the expiration date** so the license expires **5 years from the current issue date** (subject to possible 1-year extension).  
- For apprentice licenses issued **on or after July 1, 2026**, sets expiration at **5 years**, with possible **1-year extensions** for good cause.  
- Requires extension requests to be submitted at least **60 days before** the license expiration date.  
- Clarifies that apprentices/student apprentices must work under **direct onsite supervision** of specified licensed electricians, and the supervising licensee is responsible for the apprentice’s activities.  
- Includes technical drafting corrections to align with the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (per Synopsis).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Caps time spent at apprentice level** (5-year expiration framework), supporting movement toward higher licensure levels rather than indefinite apprentice status.  
- **May reduce supervision strain** by limiting long-term accumulation of apprentice licensees (Synopsis notes apprentices have “steadily swollen” beyond supervision capacity).  
- **Consumer/public safety rationale stated in Synopsis**: aims to avoid confusion and “threaten[ed]” safety by ensuring appropriate supervision capacity.  
- **Creates a clear pathway for vocational students** via the school apprentice electrician category, with licensure tied to ongoing enrollment/age threshold.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces flexibility for long-term apprentices**: apprentices licensed **5+ years before 7/1/2026** cannot renew past current expiration (unless a discretionary extension is granted).  
- **Discretionary extension standard is vague** (“good cause shown”), which could lead to inconsistent outcomes.  
- **Administrative burden on apprentices** to track timing and submit extension requests **≥ 60 days** before expiration to avoid losing ability to work under an apprentice license.  
- **Potential disruption to workforce availability** if apprentices lose licensure eligibility and do not transition in time (the bill does not create an automatic conversion to a different license type).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What criteria the Board will use to determine **“good cause shown”** for 1-year extensions.  
- Whether multiple consecutive **1-year extensions** are allowed (text authorizes “extensions of 1 year” but does not explicitly state limits on repeat extensions).  
- How the “apprentice program approved by the Board” and “residential apprentice program approved by the Board” requirements interact (the text lists both in §1408(a)(7)(b) and (c)).  
- The bill references “student apprentice electrician” in §1422 while defining “school apprentice electrician” in §1402; how these terms align is not clarified in the amendments provided.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/clarified license category:** “**School apprentice electrician**” defined as licensed by the Board, enrolled at a Board-approved vocational school, and learning/assisting electrical services (§1402(18)); qualification requires enrollment in a Board-approved vocational program at a **vocational high school in Delaware** (§1408(a)(7A)).  
- **Apprentice electrician renewal limits and expiration restructuring** effective **July 1, 2026** (§1411(b)(1)-(3)):
  - **Licensed ≥ 5 years prior to 7/1/2026:** cannot renew past current expiration date, with possible **1-year** extension(s) for good cause.
  - **Licensed < 5 years as of 7/1/2026:** cannot renew; expiration date is extended to expire **5 years from current issue date**, with possible **1-year** extension(s) for good cause.
  - **Issued on/after 7/1/2026:** expires after **5 years**, with possible **1-year** extension(s) for good cause.
  - **Extension request deadline:** must be made **at least 60 days before** expiration.
- **School apprentice license renewal rule:** no renewal required; valid while enrolled or until **18**, whichever is later (§1411(b)(4)).  
- **Supervision requirement reiterated/updated:** apprentices/student apprentices must work under **direct onsite supervision** of listed licensed electrician types; supervising licensee is responsible (§1422).  
- **Board powers updated** to evaluate credentials for apprentice and school apprentice licenses (§1406(a)(4)).  
- **Technical drafting corrections** throughout definitions (e.g., “shall mean means”) (Synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- **Consumers/public**: per Synopsis, intended safety benefit from limiting long-term apprentice licensure and aligning supervision capacity.  
- **Licensed electricians who supervise** (master, limited, residential, journeyperson): may face fewer long-term apprentices seeking supervision over time due to the 5-year cap structure.  
- **Vocational high school students**: clearer licensing status via the school apprentice electrician category with a defined validity period tied to enrollment/age.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Current apprentice electricians**, especially those licensed **5 years or more before July 1, 2026**, who lose the ability to renew beyond their current expiration date (absent a discretionary extension).  
- **Apprentice electricians nearing expiration** who miss the **60-day** deadline to request an extension.  
- **Employers/contractors** relying on long-term apprentice labor if apprentices are unable to renew and do not transition to another license.
