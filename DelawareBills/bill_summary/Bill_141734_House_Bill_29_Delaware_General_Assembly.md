## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Update and codify Delaware law governing annual public-school reporting by the Department of Education (DOE) by reframing “Education Profiles” as broader “education-related data,” requiring prominent website links to the annual reports, and specifying required report content (including proficiency rates, career pathways, and extracurricular sports offerings).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Renames and reframes the statutory concept of “Education Profiles” to “education-related data,” reflecting that DOE publishes a broad range of school data and resources (e.g., enrollment, staffing, performance, student health and wellness).  
- Maintains the requirement that DOE issue annual statewide school reports on **all Delaware public schools** (including charter schools, reorganized districts, and vo-tech districts) **on or before August 1 each year** (existing baseline referenced as **August 15, 2003**).  
- Requires DOE to place a **link to the annual reports** on (1) **the home page of DOE’s website** and (2) **the school choice website** (Title 14, § 401).  
- Requires the reports to support “educationally meaningful comparisons” among schools and districts using “accurate, reliable, and normalized data.”  
- Specifies the report must include, among other items, **student achievement and proficiency rates**, accountability information, safety/discipline, attendance (including truancy), staffing ratios, and finance data (revenues, expenditures, tax rates, wealth).  
- Expands/clarifies required demographic reporting to include **percent low-income**, **percent of students with IEPs**, and **percent ELL**.  
- Requires reporting on **career pathways offered at a high school**.  
- Adds a new required category: **sports and other extracurricular activities**, including for each sport whether the school has a **girls’ team, boys’ team, or co-ed team**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved public access to required annual school reports**: DOE must place links on the DOE homepage and the school choice website, making the reports easier to find.  
- **More complete and standardized public reporting**: The bill enumerates required content areas and emphasizes “accurate, reliable, and normalized data,” supporting comparisons among schools/districts.  
- **More informative school-choice decisions**: Required inclusion of high school **career pathways** and **sports/extracurricular offerings** may help families evaluate program offerings.  
- **Clearer transparency on student populations**: Requiring demographic percentages (low-income, IEP, ELL) makes key subgroup context explicit in reporting.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Additional reporting/maintenance workload**: DOE must ensure website home-page placement and school-choice-site linking, and must include additional data elements (e.g., sports offerings by gender/co-ed designation; specified demographic percentages).  
- **Potential data sensitivity/administrative burden for schools**: Collecting and updating extracurricular/sports participation and team availability by category (girls/boys/co-ed) may require new or more frequent data submissions from schools.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- Whether the added reporting items (e.g., **sports/extracurricular information**) must be included **in the annual reports only** under § 124A(a)(2) or also across other “education-related data” resources is not fully specified.  
- The bill requires comparisons to national/regional/other-state data “**if informative and practicable**,” but does not define thresholds or standards for “informative” or “practicable.”  
- The bill requires “normalized data” but does not define the normalization methodology or who sets it beyond DOE’s role.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Terminology/Scope change**: Revises statutory framing from “Education Profiles” toward broader “**education-related data**,” while retaining an annual report requirement.  
- **Annual reporting deadline reaffirmed**: DOE must issue annual reports **on or before August 1 of each subsequent year** (with original reference date **August 15, 2003**).  
- **Website access mandates**: DOE must place a link to the annual reports on:
  - **DOE website home page**, and
  - **School choice website** under § 401.  
- **Report content requirements clarified/expanded**, including:
  - Student testing/achievement **including proficiency rates**;
  - Accountability;
  - Safety/discipline;
  - Attendance **including truancy**;
  - Administrator-student and teacher-student ratios and other staffing ratios;
  - Demographics **including % low-income, % IEP, % ELL**;
  - Revenues/expenditures/tax rates/wealth;
  - Curricular offerings **including high school career pathways**;
  - Parent/community involvement;
  - Title IX Coordinator role and **contact information**;
  - **Sports and other extracurricular activities**, and for each sport whether there is a **girls’ team, boys’ team, or co-ed team**.  
- **School choice site linking requirement**: The school choice website must include a link to the reports required under § 124A(a)(2).

## Who Benefits
- **Parents, educators, families, and citizens** seeking accessible, comparable school information (explicitly named in § 124A(b)(1)).  
- **Students and families making school choice decisions**, due to required linking on the school choice website and expanded program/extracurricular information.  
- **Policymakers and the public** interested in accountability and “cost-effective use of public funds,” supported by required finance and performance reporting.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
