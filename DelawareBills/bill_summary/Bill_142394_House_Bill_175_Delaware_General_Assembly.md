## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Authorize and approve updated and new Department of Natural Resources and Environmental Control (DNREC) fees and assessments in Title 7 (permit, lease, license, registration, certification, facility, and volume-based annual fees), set transition rules for when new vs. prior fees apply, and require DNREC to publish a complete fee list on its public website.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates and adds numerous DNREC fees across the Division of Waste and Hazardous Substances, Division of Air Quality, Division of Watershed Stewardship, and Division of Water; revenue is intended to support these responsibilities.
- Act effective date is **180 days after enactment into law**; the bill was **approved June 24, 2025** (effective date calendar calculation not stated in source text).
- Contains broad fee increases (examples): **stormwater/sediment plan review and inspection fee cap** increases from **$80** to **not exceed $975 per disturbed acre per project**; **UST registration** increases **$50 → $150 per tank annually**; hazardous waste per-ton assessments increase (e.g., **$21 → $50/ton** for certain land-disposed generator waste).
- Establishes/updates major air program fees, including **variance $10,000/application** and “complexity” fees up to **$24,000/application** (e.g., PSD and emission offset provisions).
- Establishes beach preservation/coastal permit fees including **Coastal Construction or Mechanical Dune Restoration $4,500/application**, plus **Letter of Approval $500/application** and **Violation Review $500/violation**.
- Creates/updates major water program fees including **NPDES permit application $250 every 5 years**, annual discharge-volume fees generally **$0.25 per 1,000 gallons** capped at **$20,000/year/entity**, and a municipal annual fee **$0.10 per 1,000 gallons** capped at **$15,000/year/entity** (phased in—see deadlines).
- Delays certain **municipal annual fees** to **July 1, 2026**, and bills municipalities at **50% of the new rate** from **July 1, 2026 through June 30, 2027** (for specified municipal annual fees).
- Includes specific exemptions, including generators/boilers **< 100 MMBTU/hour** exempt from **MACT/NESHAP/NSPS fees**, and certain non-delinquent sources exempt from natural minor permit fees (as described in the notes).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Provides dedicated fee revenue intended to support DNREC regulatory programs and staffing costs (“bring revenue…more in line with the cost of the regulatory programs…including the cost of employees”).
- Improves transparency by requiring DNREC to maintain a **complete list of authorized fees and assessments** on its **public website**.
- Phases in certain municipal annual fee increases: **municipal rates begin July 1, 2026**, with **50% billing** during **July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027** (for specified municipal annual fees).
- Some fees decrease in limited cases (e.g., **salvage exploration permit application $300 → $250**; certain salvage-related lease application fees **$300 → $250**).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Raises or imposes substantial new application and annual compliance costs across programs, including:
  - Stormwater/sediment plan review and inspection fee cap: **$80 → up to $975 per disturbed acre per project**.
  - Air permitting: fees up to **$10,000/application** (variance) and up to **$24,000/application** (e.g., PSD; emission offset provisions).
  - Water programs: ongoing volume-based annual fees (generally **$0.25 per 1,000 gallons**, capped at **$20,000/year/entity**; municipal **$0.10 per 1,000 gallons**, capped at **$15,000/year/entity**).
  - Coastal/beach permits: **$4,500/application** for coastal construction or mechanical dune restoration.
  - Underground storage tanks: **$150 per tank per year** (up from $50), plus late charges if unpaid by deadline.
- Adds penalty-like cost in beach preservation: **Violation Review $500 per violation**, described as in addition to permit fees.
- Specifies that **political subdivisions are not exempt** from certain annual biosolids fees (where marked in the fee schedule).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Effective date / transition rules**
  - Act generally effective **180 days after enactment**.
  - Applications submitted **after** the effective date pay **new fees**.
  - If a permit/license expires within **90 days after** the effective date, a **complete** renewal/continuation application may be filed in the **90 days prior** to the effective date and pay **prior fees**; incomplete applications that become complete after the effective date pay **new fees**.
- **Municipal phase-in (specified annual municipal fees)**
  - New municipal annual fee rates take effect **July 1, 2026**.
  - Municipal systems billed at **50%** of the annual fee during **July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027** (for specified municipal annual fees in NPDES, large groundwater disposal, irrigation spray systems, and municipal water allocation annual fees as listed in the notes).
- **Air Quality fee schedule (examples)**
  - **Variance:** **$10,000/application**.
  - Complexity fees include items up to **$24,000/application** (e.g., PSD; emission offset provisions).
  - Exemption: generators/boilers **< 100 MMBTU/hour** exempt from **MACT/NESHAP/NSPS fees**.
- **Waste & Hazardous Substances (examples)**
  - Hazardous waste per-ton assessment increases (e.g., **$4/$16/$21 → $10/$35/$50 per ton** for listed categories in the notes).
  - Solid waste facility annual fee **$14,000/year** (up from $7,000 in notes); hazardous waste facility annual fee **$14,000/year** (up from $7,000 in notes).
  - UST annual registration **$150 per tank** (from $50), due by **February 1**; late charge **$50**.
- **Watershed Stewardship (Beach Preservation)**
  - Coastal construction or mechanical dune restoration: **$4,500/application**.
  - Letter of approval: **$500/application**.
  - Violation review: **$500/violation** (in addition to permit fee).
- **Water (examples)**
  - **NPDES:** **$250** application fee **every 5 years**; amendments **$250/application**; annual discharge fees generally **$0.25 per 1,000 gallons** up to **$20,000/year/entity**; municipal annual fee **$0.10 per 1,000 gallons** up to **$15,000/year/entity** (phased).
  - **Biosolids:** per-dry-ton fees with caps up to **$10,000/year/entity**; septage annual fee **$1,000/year** if **≥ 50,000 gallons/year**, **$500/year** if under; political subdivisions not exempt for specified annual fees.
  - **Water allocation:** permits generally **$250/permit**; annual fees **$0.25 per 1,000 gallons** up to **$20,000/entity** for public/industrial/commercial; **no allocation annual fee** for agricultural systems; municipal allocation annual fee **$0.10 per 1,000 gallons** up to **$15,000/entity** (phased).
  - **Subaqueous leases:** 10-year lease applications generally **$250**; new annual lease fees include **$0.02/sq ft** (filled lands aquatic and wetland vegetation) and **$0.10/sq ft** (beach restoration; vegetative stabilization protection).
- **Transparency**
  - DNREC must maintain a **complete public website list** of authorized fees and assessments.
- **Supersession / continuation**
  - Fees listed in Section 7 supersede equivalent fees approved under **68 Del. Laws, c. 86** or other law; fees not listed remain in effect to the extent utilized by DNREC or permitted by law.
- **Legislative vote threshold (process)**
  - Notes cite **Delaware Constitution, Article VIII, § 10** requiring **three-fifths** vote of members elected to each chamber to increase the effective rate of any **tax or license fee**.

## Who Benefits
- DNREC programs funded by fee revenue (explicitly referenced: Waste and Hazardous Substances; Air Quality; Watershed Stewardship; Water).
- Municipal permit holders subject to specified municipal annual fees that are **delayed until July 1, 2026** and **billed at 50%** during **July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027**.
- Generators and boilers **< 100 MMBTU/hour** (exempt from MACT/NESHAP/NSPS fees).
- Applicants who qualify for lower fees in limited instances (e.g., salvage exploration fee reductions noted).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Individuals, businesses, and other regulated entities applying for DNREC permits/licenses or subject to annual fees, including:
  - Land-disturbing project applicants (fee cap up to **$975 per disturbed acre per project**).
  - Hazardous waste generators/facilities subject to higher per-ton assessments.
  - Underground and aboveground storage tank owners/operators (higher registration and permit fees; late charges).
  - NPDES and other water permittees with higher discharge volumes subject to ongoing annual fees up to **$20,000/year/entity** (or municipal caps **$15,000/year/entity** once effective).
  - Political subdivisions paying certain annual biosolids fees (explicitly not exempt).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approved:** **June 24, 2025**.
- **General effective date:** **180 days after enactment into law** (exact date not stated in notes).
- **Municipal annual fee effective date (specified programs):** **July 1, 2026**.
- **Municipal phase-in period:** **July 1, 2026–June 30, 2027** billed at **50%** for specified municipal annual fees.
- **UST annual registration deadline:** due **on or before February 1** each year; late charge if not received by **February 1**.
- **Aboveground storage tank annual registration deadline:** due **by February 1** each year; late charge **10%** if not received by deadline.
- **Renewal timing window (to qualify for prior fees):** if a permit/license expires within **90 days after** the Act’s effective date, a **complete** renewal/continuation may be filed during the **90 days prior** to the effective date.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Beach Preservation Violation Review:** **$500 per violation** (stated as a penalty in addition to permit fees).
- **Late charges:**
  - UST registration late charge: **$50** if not received by **February 1**.
  - Aboveground storage tank registration late charge: **10%** of total fee if not received by the deadline.
  - Certain license renewals: **$50/application** late renewal fee (wastewater operator and water supply licenses as noted).
- **Compliance/administrative requirements:**
  - DNREC must maintain a **complete fee and assessment list** on its **public website**.
  - Fee applicability depends on **application timing and completeness** (incomplete applications completed after the effective date pay new fees).
