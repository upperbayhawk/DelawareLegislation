## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 3 of the Delaware Code to explicitly allow commercial diversion and composting of yard waste, food residuals, and other organic materials on properties zoned for agricultural purposes, while setting a minimum composting area that local governments must allow.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Allows commercial composting of “yard waste, food residuals, and other organic materials” on properties zoned agricultural. (New/updated § 108(a))
- Applies specifically to composting “for commercial purposes.” (§ 108(a))
- County and local governments may cap composting square footage, but must allow at least a minimum area. (§ 108(b))
- Sets a mandatory minimum allowance of **no less than 5,000 square feet per 4 acres of property**. (§ 108(b))
- Authorizes the **Delaware Department of Agriculture** to create regulations to implement this section. (§ 108(c))
- Bill synopsis reiterates the intent to allow composting on agriculturally zoned property.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Increases legal certainty/permission for commercial composting on agricultural land** by stating it “shall be allowed” for specified organic materials. (§ 108(a))
- **Limits local restrictions** by requiring counties/local governments to allow at least **5,000 sq ft per 4 acres**, preventing outright prohibition via zoning size limits. (§ 108(b))
- **Enables statewide rulemaking** through Department of Agriculture regulations, which may standardize implementation. (§ 108(c))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces local control** by imposing a minimum composting area that must be allowed regardless of local preferences (minimum **5,000 sq ft per 4 acres**). (§ 108(b))
- **Regulatory uncertainty** because the Department of Agriculture “may” create regulations, but the bill does not specify what standards will apply if regulations are not issued. (§ 108(c))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What qualifies as “other organic materials” beyond yard waste and food residuals is **not defined**. (§ 108(a))
- The bill does not define “commercial purposes” (e.g., on-farm use vs. off-site intake, sales thresholds). (§ 108(a))
- How the **minimum allowance** is calculated for properties not divisible into exact **4-acre** increments is **not specified** (e.g., 5 acres, 7 acres). (§ 108(b))
- Whether counties/local governments may impose non-square-footage restrictions (setbacks, hours, traffic routing, screening) is **not specified**. (§ 108(b))
- The scope and content of Department of Agriculture regulations (permitting, enforcement, odor/vector controls, runoff controls) is **not specified**. (§ 108(c))
