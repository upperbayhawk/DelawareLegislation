## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To tighten and standardize municipal eminent domain procedures by (1) requiring municipalities to comply with Chapter 95 of Title 29 and new municipal-specific rules in § 120, Title 22; (2) prohibiting municipal eminent domain for recreational purposes; and (3) requiring a public hearing with specific notice and content requirements before a municipality uses eminent domain.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires municipalities exercising eminent domain to comply with **all limitations and requirements in Chapter 95 of Title 29** and **§ 120 of Title 22**.  
- **Prohibits** a municipality from using eminent domain to acquire real property for **“recreational purposes.”**  
- Defines **“public use”** by reference to **§ 9501A of Title 29**, but explicitly states that for municipalities **“public use” does not include recreational purposes**.  
- Requires a **public hearing before** a municipality uses eminent domain, with at least **10 days’ advance notice**.  
- Notice must be given by **newspaper advertisement** and, if the owner can be identified, by **certified mail** to the owner.  
- Notice must include: **description of the land**, **public use**, and **time/place** of the hearing.  
- At the hearing, the municipality must explain the **public use**, **why the land was chosen**, and owners’ rights to **just compensation** and to **negotiate/accept/reject** the compensation offer required by Title 29.  
- Applies to **condemnation proceedings filed after enactment** (no retroactive application specified beyond this filing-date rule).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More transparency and due process:** Requires a public hearing with specified notice (including **certified mail** when owners can be identified) and required explanations of owner rights (Title 22, § 120(c)).  
- **Clearer procedural accountability:** A municipality’s condemnation complaint must state compliance with **Title 29 § 9505(15)** and, if a municipality, compliance with **Title 22 § 120** (Title 10, § 6105(b)(3) and (b)(4)).  
- **Limits municipal takings for recreation:** Municipalities are barred from using eminent domain for **recreational purposes** (Title 22, § 120(b)(2)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced municipal flexibility:** Municipalities cannot use eminent domain for **recreational purposes**, even if a municipality views a recreational project as serving community needs (Title 22, § 120(b)(2); § 120(a)(3)).  
- **Added procedural steps and potential delays:** Mandatory hearing, specified notice methods, and obligation to consider objections may lengthen timelines prior to filing or proceeding (Title 22, § 120(c)).  
- **Increased administrative burden:** Requires publication plus certified mailing (when owners identifiable) and preparation of required hearing explanations (Title 22, § 120(c)(1)–(3)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “recreational purposes” in practice:** The bill incorporates the definition from **§ 5902 of Title 7**, but the provided text does not include that definition, so the practical boundary of prohibited takings is not visible from the source excerpt (Title 22, § 120(a)(4)).  
- **What it means to “consider” objections:** The bill requires municipalities to “consider any objection raised,” but does not specify standards, documentation, or whether a written response is required (Title 22, § 120(c)(4)).  
- **Timing relative to filing:** The bill requires the hearing “before” using eminent domain, but does not specify how long before filing a condemnation action the hearing must occur beyond the **10-day notice** minimum (Title 22, § 120(c)(1)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 22 (new/expanded § 120):**
  - Defines “Land,” “Owner,” “Public use,” and “Recreational purpose” for this municipal eminent domain section.  
  - Requires municipal compliance with **Chapter 95 of Title 29** and **§ 120 of Title 22** whenever eminent domain is used.  
  - **Prohibits** municipal eminent domain for **recreational purposes**.  
  - Establishes mandatory **public hearing** and **notice requirements** (minimum **10 days** notice; newspaper ad; certified mail to identifiable owners; required notice contents; required hearing explanations; public comment; consider objections).
- **Title 29 (§ 9501A(f)):**
  - Adds a cross-reference requiring municipal compliance with **§ 120 of Title 22** whenever a municipality exercises eminent domain.
- **Title 10 (§ 6105—condemnation complaint contents):**
  - Requires the complaint to include a statement of:
    - authority for the taking,
    - the use consistent with **Title 29 § 9501A**,
    - compliance with **Title 29 § 9505(15)**,
    - and for municipalities, compliance with **Title 22 § 120**,
    - plus other specified property/party description items.

## Who Benefits
- **Property owners** facing municipal eminent domain, who receive guaranteed advance notice (including **certified mail** when identifiable), a public hearing, and required explanations of compensation and negotiation rights (Title 22, § 120(c)).  
- **Community members** who wish to comment before municipal eminent domain is used, due to required public comment opportunities (Title 22, § 120(c)(4)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Municipalities** seeking to acquire land via eminent domain, due to added procedural requirements (public hearing, notice) and the prohibition on eminent domain for recreational purposes (Title 22, § 120(b)–(c)).  
- **Projects characterized as recreational** that would otherwise rely on municipal eminent domain authority (Title 22, § 120(b)(2); § 120(a)(3)–(4)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Applicability:** Applies to **condemnation proceedings filed after the Act’s enactment into law** (Section 4).  
- **Deadline:** Public hearing notice must be provided at least **10 days before** the hearing date (Title 22, § 120(c)(1)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (municipal):**
  - Must comply with **Chapter 95 of Title 29** and **§ 120 of Title 22** when using eminent domain (Title 22, § 120(b)(1)).  
  - **May not** use eminent domain for **recreational purposes** (Title 22, § 120(b)(2)).  
  - Must hold a public hearing and provide required notice and hearing content, allow public comment, and consider objections (Title 22, § 120(c)).  
- **Compliance requirement (court filing):**
  - Condemnation complaint must allege specified authority/use and compliance items, including municipal compliance with **§ 120 of Title 22** (Title 10, § 6105(b)(4)).
