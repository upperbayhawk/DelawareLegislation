## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Align Delaware’s dietitian/nutritionist licensure education requirements in Title 24, Chapter 38 with current national credentialing standards by requiring a **master’s degree** for applicants who graduated **on or after January 1, 2024**, and remove an outdated waiver provision.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates **24 Del. C. § 3806 (Qualifications of applicants)** for licensure as an **L.D.N. (Licensed Dietitian/Nutritionist)**.
- Creates an education split by graduation date:
  - **Before January 1, 2024:** minimum **baccalaureate degree**.
  - **On or after January 1, 2024:** minimum **master’s degree**.
- Requires foreign degrees to be **validated as equivalent** to the required U.S. degree level (baccalaureate or master’s, as applicable).
- Retains requirement for a relevant **major course of study** (e.g., human nutrition, dietetics).
- Retains supervised practice requirement of **not less than 900 hours**, with specified supervisor qualifications and U.S./territories completion requirement.
- Retains requirement to **appear** and submit to **examination** as designated by the Board.
- Eliminates an outdated waiver provision that applied only if an application was filed **not later than June 21, 2010**.
- The enacted law was **approved August 25, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Consistency with national standards:** The synopsis states the change aligns Delaware with the **Commission on Dietetic Registration (CDR)**, which required a **minimum master’s degree beginning January 1, 2024**.
- **Clearer education standard for newer graduates:** Explicitly requires a **master’s degree** for those graduating **on or after January 1, 2024**, reducing ambiguity for that cohort.
- **Removes obsolete statutory language:** Deletes/updates a waiver pathway tied to applications due **by June 21, 2010**, which is no longer practically usable.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher barrier to entry for newer graduates:** Applicants graduating **on or after January 1, 2024** must have a **master’s degree** rather than a bachelor’s degree, which may increase time and cost to qualify for licensure.
- **Potentially limits eligible applicant pool:** Because the education requirement increases for post-2024 graduates, fewer applicants may meet qualifications without additional schooling (master’s degree requirement is explicit in § 3806(a)(2)).
