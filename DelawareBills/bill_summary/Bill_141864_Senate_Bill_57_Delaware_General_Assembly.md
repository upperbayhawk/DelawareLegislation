## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s postconviction DNA testing and DNA-evidence new trial procedures (Title 11, Chapter 45, § 4504) to broaden access to DNA testing, remove certain timing/appellate-review limits, and change standards governing DNA testing orders and new-trial relief.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Removes the **3-year limit** that previously barred a DNA-testing motion filed **more than 3 years after the judgment of conviction is final**.  
- Removes the limitation that barred DNA testing or DNA-based new-trial proceedings **while direct appellate review is available**.  
- Allows DNA testing requests **“at any time”** (as amended) by a person convicted of a crime.  
- Expands eligibility/coverage to include cases involving a **guilty plea** (the statute refers to “trial or date of entry of a plea” for technology-availability considerations).  
- Requires the court to **grant** a DNA testing motion when the statutory criteria are satisfied (changes “may” to **“shall”**).  
- Permits DNA testing when evidence was **previously tested**, if additional testing offers a **reasonable likelihood** of more probative results.  
- Lowers the standard for a DNA-evidence-based new trial from **“clear and convincing evidence”** to **“reasonable probability”** that no reasonable trier of fact would have convicted, considering specified evidence.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded access to postconviction DNA testing:** Eliminating the **3-year filing deadline** and the bar tied to **direct appellate review** increases opportunities to seek testing under § 4504(a).  
- **Stronger entitlement when criteria are met:** Changing the testing decision from discretionary (“may”) to mandatory (“shall”) increases consistency and predictability when statutory criteria (chain of custody, scientific validity, material relevance, etc.) are satisfied.  
- **Addresses advances in forensic technology:** Expressly allows **additional testing** of previously tested evidence when there is a **reasonable likelihood** of more probative results.  
- **More attainable new-trial standard:** Replacing **“clear and convincing evidence”** with **“reasonable probability”** in § 4504(b) lowers the burden for relief when DNA evidence supports actual innocence.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential increase in filings and litigation workload:** Removing the **3-year limit** and allowing motions without regard to whether **direct appellate review is available** may increase the number of DNA-testing motions and related proceedings.  
- **Reduced finality of convictions:** Broader timing and eligibility (including plea-related cases) plus a lower standard for new trials may increase reopened cases.  
- **Possible increased state costs:** If more testing is ordered, the State may bear more costs under § 4504(e) (unless the court orders a non-indigent applicant to pay).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Retroactivity:** The bill does not state whether changes apply to motions filed after enactment regardless of conviction date, or otherwise specify transitional rules.  
- **Interaction with existing court rules/procedures:** The text removes the “except at a time when direct appellate review is available” limitation, but does not specify how courts should coordinate DNA-testing motions with ongoing appeals procedurally.  
- **Scope of “at any time”:** The statute is amended to allow filing “at any time,” but does not define whether any other time limits (elsewhere in law) might still apply.

## Key Policy Changes
- **§ 4504(a) — Timing and availability**
  - Changes from allowing DNA-testing motions only when direct appellate review is not available and within **3 years after conviction is final**, to allowing filing **“at any time”** (removing the **3-year** limit and removing the direct-appeal-availability bar).
- **§ 4504(a) — Standard for granting testing**
  - Changes “The motion **may** be granted” to “The motion **shall** be granted” if all criteria are satisfied.
- **§ 4504(a)(2) — Prior testing / plea coverage**
  - Expands criteria to include: evidence not previously tested because technology was not available at time of **trial or date of entry of a plea**, **or** previously tested evidence eligible for **additional testing** likely to yield more probative results.
- **§ 4504(b) — New trial based on DNA evidence**
  - Removes the “except at a time when direct appellate review is available” limitation.
  - Changes burden from **“clear and convincing evidence”** to **“reasonable probability”** that no reasonable trier of fact would have convicted, considering:
    - evidence presented at trial,
    - evidence available at trial but not presented or excluded, and
    - evidence obtained via § 4504(a) testing.
- **§ 4504(c)-(e) — Existing provisions retained**
  - Court must impose reasonable conditions to protect evidence/testing integrity (§ 4504(c)).
  - Motions must be served on the State; State has an absolute right to appeal an order granting a new trial (§ 4504(d)).
  - Cost-sharing framework for DNA testing remains (§ 4504(e)).

## Who Benefits
- **Persons convicted of crimes** seeking postconviction DNA testing, especially those outside the prior **3-year** window.  
- **Individuals who entered guilty pleas** where DNA testing could support an actual innocence claim (as reflected in the amended reference to “date of entry of a plea”).  
- **Applicants seeking additional DNA testing** on previously tested evidence when new methods may be more probative.  

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State criminal justice agencies and courts** due to potentially increased volume of motions, testing orders, and hearings.  
- **State fiscal resources** if more DNA testing is ordered and the State bears costs in more cases under § 4504(e).  
- **Victims and families** may experience extended case timelines due to reopened litigation (the bill text does not address victim-notification or related procedures).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** Not specified in source text.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Service requirement:** Any motion under § 4504 must be **served upon the State** (§ 4504(d)).  
- **Court-ordered conditions:** The court **shall impose reasonable conditions** to protect the integrity of evidence and the testing process (§ 4504(c)).  
- **Appeal right:** The State has an **absolute right to appeal** any order granting a motion for a **new trial** under this section (§ 4504(d)).  
- **Payment compliance:** If the applicant is **not indigent and has means**, the court may order the applicant to pay DNA testing costs “in the interests of justice” (§ 4504(e)).
