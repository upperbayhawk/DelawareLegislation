## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Family and Medical Leave Insurance Program law (Title 19) to delay when employer civil penalties take effect.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes the penalty provisions in **19 Del. C. § 3719** to take effect **beginning January 1, 2029**.
- Applies to civil penalties for employers who **violate or fail to comply** with program requirements.
- Covered violations explicitly include: **failure to file reports**, **failure to make required contributions**, and **failure to properly handle and remit employee wage deductions** to the Department.
- Also delays penalties for employers who **discharge or discriminate** against an employee for engaging in protected activity (complaints, providing information, instituting proceedings, or testifying).
- Maintains the civil penalty range of **$1,000 to $5,000 per violation**.
- Allows civil penalty claims to be filed in **any court of competent jurisdiction**.
- Requires penalty funds (including **overpayment penalties authorized by § 3712**) to be **deposited in the Fund**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Provides employers additional time until **January 1, 2029** before being subject to civil penalties under § 3719 for specified program compliance failures (as stated in the synopsis and amended effective language).
- Keeps an enforcement mechanism in place after that date by retaining the **$1,000–$5,000 per violation** penalty structure.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Delays the availability of civil penalties as an enforcement tool until **January 1, 2029**, which may reduce near-term deterrence for the specified noncompliance and retaliation/discrimination conduct (based on the delayed “Beginning January 1, 2029” language).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Delays effective date** of **19 Del. C. § 3719 (Penalties)** to **January 1, 2029**.
- Leaves unchanged:
  - Civil penalty amounts: **not less than $1,000 nor more than $5,000 for each violation**.
  - Covered conduct:
    - Compliance failures (including **reports**, **contributions**, and **wage deduction remittance**).
    - **Discharge or discrimination** for protected employee activity.
  - Venue: a civil penalty claim may be filed in **any court of competent jurisdiction**.
  - Deposit rule: civil penalties (including **§ 3712 overpayment penalties**) must be deposited in **the Fund**.

## Who Benefits
- Employers subject to the Family and Medical Leave Insurance Program requirements, due to delayed exposure to civil penalties until **January 1, 2029**.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Employees and the Department (program administration) to the extent delayed penalties reduce near-term enforcement leverage for noncompliance and retaliation/discrimination (based on the delayed effective date).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **January 1, 2029**: § 3719 civil penalties begin to apply (“Beginning January 1, 2029…”).

- **Civil penalties (effective beginning January 1, 2029):**
  - For employer noncompliance with program requirements (including **failure to file reports**, **failure to make contributions**, **failure to handle/remit employee wage deductions**): **$1,000 to $5,000 per violation**.
