## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 4 of the Delaware Code (§ 512D) to expressly authorize and regulate **spirits auctions** (in addition to wine auctions) by auction houses licensed by the Delaware Office of the Alcoholic Beverage Control Commissioner, with restrictions on resale through licensed retail channels.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes the Alcoholic Beverage Control Commissioner to grant **three license types**: **wine auction**, **spirits auction**, or **wine and spirits auction** licenses.  
- Prohibits any person from operating a **wine auction or spirits auction** without a license from the Commissioner.  
- Defines a **“spirits auction”** as auctioning **rare or collectable spirits obtained from private collections** at public auction to persons of legal purchasing age.  
- Maintains/establishes auction frequency caps: **no more than 12 wine auctions per calendar year** and **no more than 12 spirits auctions per calendar year**.  
- Requires license holders to **collect and remit applicable excise taxes and gross receipt taxes** for all products auctioned.  
- Requires license holders to have an **office located in Delaware**, but provides that **nonprofit organizations do not need to maintain an in-state office**.  
- Prohibits products purchased at auction from being **stocked as inventory** or **made available for further retail sale** in any facility/store licensed under Title 4.  
- Allows auctions to occur **at any location within Delaware**, subject to Commissioner rules and regulations (which “shall have the force of law”).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expands regulated auction activity to spirits:** Explicitly permits licensed auction houses to auction **rare/collectable spirits** (Synopsis; § 512D(a)(2)).  
- **Tax compliance is built in:** License holders must **collect and remit excise and gross receipt taxes** for all products auctioned (§ 512D(d)).  
- **Clear annual limits:** Establishes a defined cap of **12 spirits auctions per calendar year** (and keeps 12 for wine) (§ 512D(c)).  
- **Restricts resale into licensed retail market:** Prohibits auction-purchased products from being placed into inventory or resold through Title 4 licensed stores/facilities (§ 512D(d)), aligning with the synopsis condition.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits market activity through caps:** The bill limits spirits auctions to **12 per calendar year** (§ 512D(c)), which may constrain auction volume.  
- **In-state office requirement for licensees:** Wine/spirit auction license holders must maintain an **office within Delaware** (§ 512D(b)), which may deter out-of-state operators.  
- **Compliance responsibility concentrated on auctioneer:** The license holder is responsible for **collecting/remitting taxes** (§ 512D(d)), increasing administrative burden for operators.  
- **Limits downstream sale options for buyers:** Buyers cannot place auction purchases into inventory or retail sale through Title 4 licensed facilities/stores (§ 512D(d)).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds explicit statutory authorization for **spirits auctions** and related licensing (§ 512D heading and § 512D(a)(2)).  
- Creates/recognizes three license categories: **wine auction**, **spirits auction**, and **wine and spirits auction** (§ 512D(a)).  
- Sets an annual cap of **12 spirits auctions per calendar year** (and maintains **12 wine auctions per calendar year**) (§ 512D(c)).  
- Requires tax collection/remittance by the license holder for auctioned products (§ 512D(d)).  
- Prohibits auction purchases from being stocked as inventory or sold further through Title 4 licensed facilities/stores (§ 512D(d)).  
- Requires an in-state office for license holders, with an exception that **nonprofits do not need an in-state office** (§ 512D(b)).  
- Allows auctions anywhere in Delaware, subject to Commissioner rules (§ 512D(b)).
