## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To create a comprehensive statewide licensing, oversight, client-rights, and incident-reporting framework for adult behavioral health treatment provider organizations and programs under the Delaware Division of Substance Abuse and Mental Health (DSAMH), including civil/criminal enforcement tools and confidentiality provisions for oversight records.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires most adult behavioral health treatment provider organizations/programs to be licensed by DSAMH to provide “behavioral health treatment services” or operate a program, with specific exemptions (e.g., licensed practitioners in private practice; programs exclusively serving children).  
- Authorizes DSAMH to set licensing/inspection/monitoring fees by regulation; fees must “approximate and reasonably reflect” DSAMH’s costs; collected fees and civil penalties are appropriated to DSAMH for this chapter’s purposes.  
- Creates new criminal offenses for unlicensed provision of services/operation/advertising, including **Class F felony** (recklessly causing physical injury), **Class G felony** (recklessly creating substantial risk of physical injury), and **Class A misdemeanors** for intentional unlicensed activities/advertising.  
- Establishes DSAMH audit authority with access “without notice” and imposes deadlines to provide financial documents: **15 days** for financial statements and **60 days** for an audited financial report after written notice.  
- Establishes detailed client rights (community and residential), provider duties (plain language, preferred language, reasonable accommodations, confidentiality, grievance processes), and clinical record access rules (including practitioner “detrimental” determination and alternate-practitioner release pathway).  
- Requires staff to report incidents/critical incidents to DSAMH; makes intentional failure to report or falsification a **Class A misdemeanor**; creates staff civil penalties up to **$1,000 per violation**; provides anti-retaliation enforcement with fines up to **$25,000** and **treble damages** plus costs/attorney fees.  
- Sets disciplinary actions/appeals/hearing timelines, including: **10 days** advance notice before discipline (except bona fide emergency), **10 days** to request a hearing after receiving notice, **20 days** minimum notice before the hearing, and **30 days** to appeal a final order to Superior Court.  
- Effective/implementation structure: multiple sections effective immediately, while major licensing/client-rights/incident framework is effective immediately but **implemented** the earlier of **(a) 2 years from enactment** or **(b) DSAMH Director notice in the Register of Regulations** that final implementing regulations are adopted.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved oversight and accountability:** DSAMH may audit and monitor provider organizations; can impose civil penalties (up to **$10,000 per violation**) and suspend admissions or revoke/suspend licenses for noncompliance (§§ 5605–5606).  
- **Stronger client protections:** Codifies rights to be free from abuse/exploitation/neglect/serious injury; rights to evidence-based treatment, individualized treatment plans, privacy, and grievance processes (§ 5633).  
- **Incident reporting and law-enforcement coordination:** Mandates staff reporting of incidents; requires immediate notification to law enforcement and Attorney General when DSAMH receives a credible report suggesting a crime (§§ 5642, 5646).  
- **Anti-retaliation protections for reporters:** Civil fine up to **$25,000** plus liability for **treble damages** and attorney fees/costs for retaliation against employees who report (§ 5644(e)).  
- **Clearer process and timelines:** Establishes defined notice, hearing, order issuance (**30 days after hearing**), and judicial review deadlines (**30 days after final order**) (§§ 5623, 5625–5626).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Compliance burden and potential costs on providers:** Licensing, inspections, monitoring, and DSAMH audits (including rapid financial submission deadlines: **15 days/60 days**) may increase administrative workload (§ 5605; § 5608).  
- **Potential exposure to significant penalties:** Civil money penalties up to **$10,000 per violation** for provider organizations and up to **$1,000 per violation** for staff failing to comply with incident reporting; plus criminal penalties for unlicensed practice/operations/advertising (§§ 5603, 5606, 5644).  
- **Restrictions on marketing/solicitation:** Prohibits advertising/soliciting “within **500 feet** of another provider organization’s facility,” which may limit outreach practices (§ 5604(a)(8)).  
- **Nonpublic records limit transparency:** Audit and incident-report records are expressly not public records under Delaware FOIA (Chapter 100, Title 29) (§§ 5605(e), 5642(e), 5645(e), 5646(g)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Exact license term length:** The Division “shall set the license term,” but no duration is specified in the statute (§ 5614(a)).  
- **Fee amounts and schedules:** Fees are authorized by regulation and must reflect DSAMH costs, but specific dollar amounts are not set in the bill text (§ 5608).  
- **Scope/criteria for discretionary exemptions:** The Director may exempt additional provider organizations/programs/services if the Director “reasonably believes” the exemption does not endanger health/safety/welfare, and may exempt “other” categories designated by the Director (§ 5602(c)(6)).  
- **Detailed reporting timelines/content:** The bill mandates reporting and creates an online system, but leaves report format and timing details largely to DSAMH regulations (§ 5643).  
- **“Earlier of” implementation trigger depends on enactment date and regulation adoption:** The bill sets a “2 years from enactment” trigger but does not state the enactment date in the provided text (§ 12).

## Health Impact
- Establishes statutory client rights to evidence-based treatment, individualized written treatment plans (including discharge planning), privacy, communication accommodations, and freedom from abuse/exploitation/neglect/serious injury (§§ 5633–5634).  
- Mandates incident reporting and investigation pathways, including immediate law enforcement/Attorney General notification for credible crime-suspected reports (§§ 5642, 5646).  
- Requires assistance to obtain a sexual assault nurse examiner exam when staff reasonably believe rape/sexual assault occurred, unless client refuses (§ 5642(d)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Licensing requirement (adult behavioral health):** Prohibits providing treatment services, operating programs, or advertising such authority without a DSAMH license, subject to enumerated exemptions (§ 5602).  
- **Director discretion to exempt certain programs:** Includes State-operated programs, embedded integrated care model programs designated by Medicaid, non-clinical supports (including recovery houses), prevention/education services, and other Director-designated programs (§ 5602(c)).  
- **New criminal penalties for unlicensed behavior:** Class F felony / Class G felony / Class A misdemeanor structure tied to recklessness/intent and harm/risk (§ 5603).  
- **Provider prohibited practices:** Includes deceptive/pressuring solicitation and a **500-foot** advertising/solicitation buffer from other provider facilities (§ 5604).  
- **Audit authority and financial-reporting deadlines:** DSAMH access without notice; **15-day** financial statement submission; **60-day** audited financial report submission; audit records not public (§ 5605).  
- **Civil enforcement tools:** DSAMH civil money penalties up to **$10,000 per violation**, monitoring at provider expense, admissions suspension, license suspension/revocation/denial; emergency management transfer authority for imminent risk (§ 5606).  
- **Client rights and provider duties codified:** Plain language/preferred language, reasonable accommodations, confidentiality, written rights/fee/rules/grievance/privacy statements at admission, treatment plan duties, residential client rights (communication, belongings, finances, voting assistance) (§§ 5633–5634).  
- **Clinical record access framework:** General rule against release; must release to client/representative upon request subject to § 5656; may deny if practitioner determines detriment, but must release to an equivalent-scope practitioner chosen by client/representative (§§ 5635–5636).  
- **Incident reporting regime:** Staff duty to report incidents; crime reporting; record confidentiality; anti-retaliation; Attorney General enforcement; immunity for good-faith reporting with exceptions (§§ 5641–5646).  
- **Interagency referrals:** Required notification to Division of Professional Regulation (and then relevant Title 24 board) upon substantiated abuse/exploitation/neglect/serious injury by a licensed individual; referral to Adult Abuse Registry subject to § 8564 of Title 11 (§ 5647).  
- **FOIA exclusions for oversight records:** Audit and incident/investigation records not public records under Chapter 100, Title 29 (§§ 5605(e), 5642(e), 5645(e), 5646(g)).  
- **Repeals:** Repeals **Chapter 22 of Title 16** in its entirety and repeals **§§ 5181–5186 of Title 16** in their entirety (§§ 8–9).

## Who Benefits
- **Clients** receiving behavioral health treatment services, through codified rights, grievance processes, and protections against abuse/neglect/exploitation (§ 5633).  
- **Employees/staff who report incidents**, through anti-retaliation protections and good-faith immunity provisions (§ 5644(e), (h)).  
- **State oversight entities (DSAMH, Attorney General, protection and advocacy system)** through defined authority to audit, investigate, enforce rights, and coordinate referrals (§§ 5605–5606, 5637, 5646–5647).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Unlicensed operators or those operating outside DSAMH requirements**, due to new criminal and civil penalties (§§ 5603, 5606).  
- **Provider organizations** facing new licensing fees (set by regulation), inspections/monitoring requirements, advertising restrictions (including **500 feet** limitation), audit access without notice, and short turnaround deadlines for financial submissions (§§ 5604, 5605, 5608).  
- **Staff members** who fail to comply with reporting duties, due to potential **Class A misdemeanor** exposure and civil penalties up to **$1,000 per violation** (§ 5644).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective immediately:** Sections **6, 7, 10, 11, and 12** (§ 11).  
- **Effective immediately, but implementation delayed/triggered:** Sections **1–5, 8, and 9** are effective immediately and must be implemented the earlier of:  
  - **Two years from the date of the Act’s enactment**, or  
  - **Notice by the DSAMH Director published in the Register of Regulations** that final implementing regulations have been adopted (§ 12).  
- **Provider operational deadlines/thresholds within the act (once applicable):**
  - **90 days** advance notice to DSAMH before service interruption/closure (except bona fide emergency) (§ 5607(a)).  
  - Financial submissions after DSAMH written notice: **15 days** for financial statements; **60 days** for audited financial report (§ 5605(c)–(d)).  
  - Disciplinary process: **10 days** written notice before disciplinary action (except bona fide emergency) (§ 5623(a)); **10 days** to request a hearing after receiving notice (§ 5623(b)); **20 days** minimum notice before hearing (§ 5623(c)); hearing officer order within **30 days** after hearing (§ 5625(b)); judicial review petition within **30 days** after final order (§ 5626(a)).  
- **Advertising restriction distance:** No advertising/soliciting within **500 feet** of another provider organization’s facility (§ 5604(a)(8)).  
- **Utility interruption threshold in incident definition:** Interruption of utilities lasting more than **8 hours** is an “incident” (§ 5641(4)c).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Criminal penalties for unlicensed practice/operations/advertising:**
  - Recklessly causing physical injury while providing treatment services in violation of § 5602: **Class F felony** (§ 5603(a)).  
  - Recklessly creating substantial risk of physical injury while providing treatment services in violation of § 5602: **Class G felony** (§ 5603(b)).  
  - Intentionally providing treatment services without proper licensing: **Class A misdemeanor** (§ 5603(c)).  
  - Intentionally operating/attempting to operate a program without proper licensing: **Class A misdemeanor** (§ 5603(d)).  
  - Intentionally advertising/holding out as authorized to provide services or operate a program without proper licensing: **Class A misdemeanor** (§ 5603(e)–(f)).  
- **Civil/administrative penalties against provider organizations:**
  - Civil money penalty up to **$10,000 per violation** plus costs for noncompliance; unpaid penalties can be added to required license fee; Superior Court collection action after appeals exhausted (court may not review penalty validity/amount/appropriateness in that collection action) (§ 5606(a), (d)–(e)).  
  - Additional sanctions: monitoring at provider expense, suspension of admissions, license/program license suspension/revocation or application refusal (§ 5606(f)).  
  - Emergency order authority: if imminent risk of substantial harm, Secretary may temporarily transfer program management to another qualified entity (§ 5606(g)).  
- **Incident reporting enforcement (staff and employers):**
  - Intentional failure to report a critical incident: **Class A misdemeanor** (§ 5644(a)).  
  - Intentional falsification of a critical incident report (and intentional falsification of a report): **Class A misdemeanor** (§ 5644(b), (d)).  
  - Civil penalty for staff noncompliance with reporting duty: up to **$1,000 per violation** (§ 5644(c)).  
  - Anti-retaliation: civil violation; fine up to **$25,000**; employer/executive/administrator liable for **treble damages**, costs, and attorney fees (§ 5644(e)).  
  - Attorney General enforcement; Superior Court exclusive original jurisdiction for violations of § 5644 (§ 5644(f)–(g)).  
- **Compliance systems/requirements:**
  - Provider organizations must permit DSAMH audit access “without notice” to facilities/records/clients/staff (§ 5605(b)).  
  - Providers must maintain clinical records and follow minimum record-content regulations to be adopted by DSAMH (§ 5635).  
  - Providers must provide written rights/fees/rules/grievance/privacy statement at admission or upon request (§ 5634(c)).  
  - Providers must establish grievance procedures and respond “fair, timely, and impartial[ly]” (§ 5634(f)–(g)).
