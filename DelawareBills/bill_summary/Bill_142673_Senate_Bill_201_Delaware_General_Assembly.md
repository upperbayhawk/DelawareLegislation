## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Clarify that the business practice commonly called “wholesaling” (marketing/advertising an equitable buyer’s interest in an Agreement of Sale for assignment) is a regulated real estate activity that must be performed by a Delaware real estate licensee; add consumer protections and cancellation rights for wholesale transactions; and increase consumer recovery limits and required balances for the Real Estate Guaranty Fund.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines “wholesaling” and limits the definition’s scope to certain **1-to-4 family residential** properties and a **single residential lot** (with specified exclusions).  
- Adds wholesaling (and attempts) to the statutory definition of **“real estate services”**, making it subject to Delaware Real Estate Commission regulation.  
- Adjusts an exemption so an individual **not engaged in wholesaling as a business** is not required to be licensed when acting as an owner/buyer/lessee/equitable owner.  
- Creates a new consumer **right to cancel** a wholesale agreement/contract until **midnight of the 21st calendar day** after execution (if required disclosures are included) or **until conveyance**, whichever occurs first.  
- If required disclosure information is missing, the wholesale agreement/contract may be canceled **at any time prior to conveyance**.  
- Requires specific **prominent disclosures** in commission-approved forms available in **English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole**.  
- Increases Real Estate Guaranty Fund maximum payout per transaction/claim from **$25,000 to $50,000** and raises the required minimum fund balance from **$250,000 to $350,000**.  
- Establishes staggered effective dates: some provisions **upon enactment**, wholesaler licensure requirement **270 days after enactment**, and cancellation/disclosure protections **90 days after enactment**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer regulatory coverage:** Explicitly treats wholesaling/attempts as “real estate services,” bringing the activity under Commission oversight (amended §2902(21)).  
- **Stronger consumer cancellation rights:** Sellers in wholesale transactions get a **non-waivable** right to cancel for **21 calendar days** after execution (if disclosures included), or any time before conveyance if disclosures are missing (new §2940(a)).  
- **Mandatory refunds and no-damages rule:** Requires refund of **all payments within 10 business days** after cancellation notice and bars damages against a consumer for exercising cancellation (new §2940(c)-(d)).  
- **Improved consumer recovery:** Raises the maximum Guaranty Fund compensation from **$25,000 to $50,000** per transaction/claim (amended §2922(a)).  
- **More robust fund solvency:** Increases required ongoing fund level from **$250,000 to $350,000** (amended §2922(i)).  
- **Standardized, multilingual disclosures:** Requires Commission-approved forms and availability in **English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole** (new §2940(e)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **New compliance burden for wholesalers:** Individuals “engaged in the business of wholesaling” must become licensed once the licensure-related changes take effect (amended §2902(21); delayed effective date in Section 7).  
- **Transaction uncertainty:** A non-waivable **21-day** cancellation window (and potentially longer if disclosures are deficient, up to conveyance) can introduce rescission risk for parties relying on wholesale contracts (new §2940(a)).  
- **Increased Guaranty Fund exposure:** Doubling the per-claim cap to **$50,000** may increase payouts/financial risk to the Fund (amended §2922(a)), while also requiring a higher minimum balance (**$350,000**) (amended §2922(i)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Licensing timing vs. enforcement:** The bill provides a **270-day** delayed effective date for Section 2 (which includes wholesaling under “real estate services”), but does not state how enforcement will be handled during the transition beyond effective dates.  
- **Commission form specifics:** The required disclosure must be made using Commission-established forms “in the manner and method” the Commission establishes (new §2940(e)), but the bill text does not specify the exact format/content beyond the listed statements.  
- **Scope boundaries:** “Wholesaling” is limited to certain residential properties and a single residential lot and excludes assignment of certain auction bids, but the bill does not further define terms like “primary use” or “available for development” (new §2902(27)b).  
- **Meaning of “attempts to engage”:** “Real estate services” includes wholesaling or **attempts** (amended §2902(21)), but the bill text does not define what conduct constitutes an “attempt.”

## Key Policy Changes
- **Exemption narrowed/clarified (license requirement):** Updates the Chapter 29 exemption to exclude those “engaged in wholesaling as a business” from relying on the owner/buyer/lessee/equitable owner exemption (amended §2901(e)(1)).  
- **Wholesaling added to “real estate services”:** Defines real estate services to include wholesaling and attempts (amended §2902(21)).  
- **New statutory definition of “wholesaling”:**  
  - Includes entering into a contract with an owner intending to profit by marketing/advertising an assignment of the buyer’s equitable interest; and marketing/advertising for assignment of that equitable interest (new §2902(27)a).  
  - Applies only to **1-to-4 family residential** property (prior to taking legal ownership) or a **single residential lot** for a 1-to-4 family residence; excludes assignment of certain auction bids (new §2902(27)b).  
- **Guaranty Fund strengthened:**  
  - Increases per-transaction/claim cap **$25,000 → $50,000** (amended §2922(a)).  
  - Increases required Fund level **$250,000 → $350,000** and details permitted uses of excess and reporting (amended §2922(i)).  
- **Creates §2940 wholesale transaction protections:**  
  - Non-waivable right to cancel through **midnight of the 21st calendar day** after execution (with required disclosures) or until conveyance, whichever first; otherwise cancel any time before conveyance (new §2940(a)).  
  - Defines acceptable delivery methods and when notice is effective (new §2940(b)).  
  - Requires refund within **10 business days**; consumer keeps promotional items; no damages for cancellation (new §2940(c)-(d)).  
  - Requires prominent, multilingual disclosures using Commission-approved forms (new §2940(e)).

## Who Benefits
- **Homeowners/sellers** in covered wholesale transactions (fee simple or leasehold sellers) who gain cancellation rights, mandated disclosures, and refund protections (new §2940).  
- **Consumers harmed by fraud/misrepresentation/deceit** in covered real estate services who may recover up to **$50,000** (instead of $25,000) from the Real Estate Guaranty Fund, subject to statutory conditions (amended §2922(a)-(b)).  
- **Delaware Real Estate Commission/regulated marketplace**, through clearer jurisdiction over wholesaling as real estate services (amended §2902(21)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Individuals/businesses engaged in wholesaling** who will need to comply with licensure and Commission regulation (amended §2902(21); effective date delayed 270 days).  
- **Wholesale deal participants (including assignees/investors)** who may face increased contract cancellation risk through the **21-day** cancellation period (and longer if required disclosures are absent) (new §2940(a)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Upon enactment into law:** Sections **1, 3, and 4** (Sections 6).  
  - Includes the exemption clarification (§2901(e)(1)), wholesaling definition (§2902(27)), and Guaranty Fund changes (§2922).  
- **270 days after enactment:** Section **2** (Section 7).  
  - Includes adding wholesaling/attempts to the definition of “real estate services” (§2902(21)).  
- **90 days after enactment:** Section **5** (Section 8).  
  - New §2940 right-to-cancel and disclosure/refund requirements for wholesale transactions.  
- **Consumer claim deadline (Guaranty Fund):** Verified claim must be filed within **60 days after the final judgment** (amended §2922(b)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Licensure/regulatory compliance:** Wholesaling is treated as “real estate services” and therefore subject to Commission regulation and licensure requirements once effective (amended §2902(21); Section 7 delayed effective date).  
- **Mandatory contract disclosures:** Wholesale agreements/contracts must “prominently include” specified disclosures via Commission-approved forms in **English, Spanish, and Haitian Creole** (new §2940(e)).  
- **Cancellation process requirements:**  
  - Cancellation notice methods include certified mail (return receipt) or other bona fide delivery methods (including electronic or personal delivery) with proof of sending; notice is effective based on postmark/transmission/service/deposit date (new §2940(b)).  
  - **Refund requirement:** All consumer payments refunded within **10 business days** after receipt of cancellation notice, plus acknowledgment agreement is void (new §2940(c)).  
- **Guaranty Fund procedural requirements:** Verified claims must be filed within **60 days** after final judgment and include specified documentation (amended §2922(b)).
