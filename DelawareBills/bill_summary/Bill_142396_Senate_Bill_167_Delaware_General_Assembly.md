## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 21, § 706 (Disposition of fines and costs) to require that **fines issued under Title 21 by an off-duty law enforcement officer performing approved “extra duty services”** be **applied to offset or refund** amounts **owed or paid** for those extra duty services (e.g., traffic enforcement hired by a homeowners association).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes how certain **Title 21 (motor vehicle) fines** are distributed when issued by an officer on **extra duty services**.  
- Creates a **new exception** to existing fine-distribution rules so these fines are used to **offset or refund** the cost of hiring the off-duty officer.  
- Defines **“extra duty service”** as law enforcement performed by an **off-duty** officer **contracted by a private person or entity**.  
- Requires all extra duty services to be **authorized and approved by the relevant law enforcement agency**.  
- Applies to **any fine** for a violation of **any provision of Title 21** when the fine is issued in this extra-duty context.  
- Intended (per synopsis) to help address **speeding on subdivision streets**, including for **homeowners associations**.  
- Does **not** specify percentages, caps, or a detailed mechanism for how offsets/refunds are calculated or administered.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Cost recovery for private entities**: Allows a private person/entity (explicitly including **homeowners associations** in the synopsis) to **recoup costs** of hiring off-duty officers by applying collected Title 21 fines to **offset/refund** amounts owed/paid for extra duty services.  
- **Supports targeted enforcement**: Enables funding support for extra duty activities such as **traffic control, patrolling specific locations, and providing security** (as described in the synopsis).  
- **Agency oversight required**: Extra duty services must be **authorized and approved** by the relevant law enforcement agency, which may support accountability for off-duty contracted enforcement.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces revenues otherwise directed to public treasuries/courts**: The bill creates an exception to existing rules that send fines to counties/municipalities or the **State Treasurer (General Fund)**, redirecting certain fine proceeds instead to **offset/refund private extra-duty costs**.  
- **Unclear administrative pathway**: The bill requires fines be “applied to offset or refund” but does not detail **how** the offset/refund is processed (e.g., who holds funds, timing, documentation), potentially creating implementation complexity.  
- **Potential uneven application**: Only applies where a private person/entity can contract for extra duty services; areas without such contracts would not have the same cost-offset opportunity. (This follows from the bill’s limitation to privately contracted extra duty.)

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new subsection to **Title 21, § 706** stating that **notwithstanding** existing fine-distribution rules, **any fines** collected for violations of **any provisions of Title 21** where the fine was issued by an officer performing **extra duty services** must be **applied to offset or refund** amounts **owed or paid** for that officer’s extra duty services.  
- Defines **“extra duty service”** as law enforcement performed by an **off-duty law enforcement officer** contracted by a **private person or entity**.  
- Requires that all extra duty services be **authorized and approved** by the relevant law enforcement agency.

## Who Benefits
- **Private persons or entities** that hire off-duty officers for extra duty services (explicitly including **homeowners associations** in the synopsis) through the ability to **offset/refund** hiring costs using collected Title 21 fine revenue.  
- Potentially, residents in areas where extra duty enforcement is hired for traffic/speeding concerns (as described in the synopsis), to the extent it supports additional enforcement presence.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State General Fund** (State Treasurer) and/or **counties/municipalities** that otherwise would have received certain fine revenues under existing §706 distribution rules.  
- **Jurisdictions/courts** may face additional administrative burden to track and apply qualifying fine proceeds to offsets/refunds (process not detailed).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirement**: Extra duty services must be **authorized and approved** by the relevant law enforcement agency (condition for “extra duty services” as defined in the bill text).  
- **Penalty provisions**: Existing §706(c) states that violation/refusal/neglect to comply with provisions regarding disposal of fines and forfeitures constitutes **misconduct in office** and is **ground for removal**; the bill adds a new distribution rule but does not add a new standalone penalty beyond existing §706(c).
