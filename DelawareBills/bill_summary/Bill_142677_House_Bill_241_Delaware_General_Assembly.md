## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To provide short-term property tax payment relief for certain residential taxpayers—especially after reassessment-driven bill increases—by requiring installment payment plans, waiving late-payment charges for compliant participants, standardizing New Castle County school-tax late penalties, and limiting New Castle County collection actions for compliant taxpayers (for a specified tax year).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires each county tax collecting authority to allow eligible residential, primary-residence taxpayers to enter a payment plan for **county taxes, school taxes, or both** when the tax bill increased by **$300 or more** over the immediately preceding year.  
- Payment plans must permit payment of **school taxes** in **at least 3 equal installments**; if payments are timely, the taxpayer may not be charged **interest, penalty, or late fee**.  
- Mandates waiver of **late-payment penalties** (county taxes, school taxes, or both) for residential taxpayers who **enter into and comply** with the payment plan.  
- Changes school-tax late penalties so that (except as otherwise provided) New Castle County school-tax late penalties are **1% per month after September 30 (the date the tax is due)**, aligning with Kent and Sussex.  
- New Castle County Office of Finance need not send the annual delinquency written demand (normally due **on or before December 15**) to a taxpayer who has entered a qualifying payment plan.  
- For **New Castle County** only, if a taxpayer enters and complies with a payment plan, the county may not use specified forced-collection tools (e.g., civil action, wage garnishment, monition, sheriff sale) for certain taxes.  
- Allows school districts to request (and the State may advance) money from **State Division I funds** if payment plans cause a local school-funds cash flow shortfall.  
- Sunset: **Sections 1–3 expire 3 years after enactment**; the Act was **approved August 12, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces immediate payment burden for eligible homeowners**: requires an installment option of **at least 3 equal installments** for school taxes.  
- **Avoids added charges for compliant taxpayers**: timely payment under the plan eliminates **interest, penalties, and late fees** (and requires waiver of late-payment penalties for plan participants).  
- **Standardizes/lowers New Castle County school-tax penalties**: shifts from a structure that included a **5% penalty after September 1** plus additional monthly penalties to a **1% per month** framework after **September 30** (except where waived under the payment plan provisions).  
- **Limits aggressive collection actions in New Castle County for compliant taxpayers** (for specified taxes/year): prohibits actions such as **wage garnishment, monition, seizure, sheriff sale**, and other listed methods while the taxpayer complies.  
- **Mitigates school-district cash flow risk**: permits districts to request a State advance from **Division I** funds if payment plans create a shortfall.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential delay in revenue receipt** for counties and school districts because taxes may be paid over multiple installments rather than earlier lump-sum payments (payment plan requires at least **3 installments**).  
- **Reduced penalty revenue** due to mandatory waiver of late-payment penalties for plan participants and reduced/standardized New Castle County school-tax penalty structure.  
- **Administrative complexity**: counties must implement and manage payment plans, determine eligibility (including verifying **primary residence**, residential zoning, and **$300+** increase), and track compliance to ensure waivers apply.  
- **Temporary policy structure**: key provisions (Sections 1–3) sunset **3 years after enactment**, which may limit long-term predictability for taxpayers and administrators.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 9, §8604 (payment plans / penalty waiver):**
  - Requires counties to allow eligible taxpayers to enter a payment plan for **county taxes, school taxes, or both** when:
    - Property is **zoned residential** and is the taxpayer’s **primary residence**; and
    - The property tax bill increased by **$300 or more** compared to the immediately preceding year.
  - Payment plan must allow school taxes in **≥ 3 equal installments**.
  - If payments are timely, taxpayer is not charged **interest, penalty, or late fee**.
  - Counties must waive late-payment penalties (for county taxes, school taxes, or both) for residential taxpayers who enter and comply with the plan.
  - Counties may add criteria if not “unduly restrictive or burdensome.”
  - School districts may request State **Division I** advances for cash-flow shortfalls.
- **Title 14, §1917 (school-tax penalties):**
  - Establishes (except as otherwise provided) a **1% per month** penalty after **September 30 (the date the tax is due)** for Kent, Sussex, and New Castle school taxes.
  - Provides waiver of certain New Castle County penalties for residential taxpayers who enter and comply with a §8604 payment plan.
- **Title 9, §8610 (New Castle delinquency notice):**
  - New Castle County’s Office of Finance is not required to send the annual written demand (otherwise due **on or before December 15**) if the taxpayer has entered a qualifying payment plan.
- **Title 9, §8701 (New Castle County collection limits for compliant plan participants):**
  - If a taxpayer enters and complies with a payment plan, New Castle County may not collect taxes owed from real/personal property using specified methods (including **civil action, wage garnishment, seizure, monition, writ of venditioni exponas, attachment proceedings, sheriff sale, tax lien action**).
  - Applies to **New Castle County taxes and school taxes** assessed on residential property for the **2025–2026 tax year**.
- **Title 9, §8603 (quarterly payments):**
  - Clarifies counties may accept real estate taxes on a **quarterly or more frequent** basis, but **does not apply to New Castle County** (as stated in the text provided).
- **Sunset:**
  - **Sections 1 through 3** expire **3 years after enactment**, unless extended by later act.

## Who Benefits
- Residential homeowners whose property is their **primary residence**, where the tax bill increased by **$300 or more** year-over-year, who want/need to pay **in installments**.  
- Eligible taxpayers in **New Castle County** who enter and comply with payment plans, due to reduced penalty structure and temporary limits on certain forced-collection actions (for **2025–2026** taxes).  
- School districts, to the extent they can request State **Division I** advances to address cash flow impacts.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- County tax collecting authorities and New Castle County Office of Finance, due to increased administrative responsibilities and possible reduced/shifted cash receipts and penalty revenue.  
- School districts, if installment payments delay receipt of local school funds (partially mitigated by the Division I advance option).
