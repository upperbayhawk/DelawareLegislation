## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 11 of the Delaware Code to create a specific felony offense for **child abuse committed by a person in a position of trust, authority, or supervision**, with felony grade based on **injury severity** and **mental state**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new criminal offense in **11 Del. C. § 1103F**: **“Child abuse by a person in a position of trust, authority, or supervision in the first degree.”**
- Makes the offense a **felony**, graded from **Class A, B, C, E, or F** depending on the conduct and harm.
- Covers conduct ranging from **serious physical injury** (including injury causing **permanent disfigurement/impairment**) to **physical injury**, to conduct **likely to be injurious** to a child’s welfare.
- Elevates to **Class C felony** when physical injury occurs and any of these apply: child is **under 6 years of age**, child has **intellectual/developmental/physical disability**, or injury caused by a **deadly weapon or dangerous instrument**.
- Specifies **no defense** based on not knowing (or reasonably believing otherwise) the child’s **age** (re: under **6**) or the child’s **disability** status (for the disability-based enhancement).
- Allows **separate charges and sentences** for other crimes in Title 11 or elsewhere in the Delaware Code (no merger requirement stated).
- Bill synopsis states the offense grade also depends on whether the defendant acted **intentionally, recklessly, or knowingly**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Creates a tailored offense** for abuse by people in positions of trust/authority/supervision, which may improve charge alignment with the specific relationship context (bill synopsis and creation of §1103F).
- **Graduated felony levels** (Class A/B/C/E/F) align penalties to **severity of injury** and certain aggravating factors (e.g., under **6**, disability, deadly weapon/dangerous instrument).
- **Closes potential knowledge-based defenses** for age (< **6**) and disability aggravators by explicitly stating these are **not defenses**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Broad criminalization language** includes acting “in a manner likely to be injurious to the physical, mental or moral welfare of a child” as a **Class F felony**, which could expand felony exposure beyond physical injury cases (paragraph (e) with penalty in (f)e).
- **Multiple-charge exposure** is expressly permitted (“separate charge, conviction and sentence for any other crime”), potentially increasing cumulative penalties and prosecutorial leverage (subsection (g)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates a new felony offense** in **11 Del. C. § 1103F** for child abuse by a person in a position of trust/authority/supervision.
- Establishes **five felony grades** tied to conduct:
  - **Class A felony**: intentionally or recklessly causes **serious physical injury** that results in **permanent disfigurement**, **permanent impairment of health**, or **permanent loss or impairment of a bodily organ** (subsection (a), penalty (f)a).
  - **Class B felony**: intentionally or recklessly causes **serious physical injury** (subsection (b), penalty (f)b).
  - **Class C felony**: intentionally or recklessly causes **physical injury** and one aggravator applies:
    - child is **less than 6 years of age**; or
    - child has an **intellectual development, or physical disability**; or
    - injury caused by a **deadly weapon or dangerous instrument** (subsection (c)(1)-(3), penalty (f)c).
  - **Class E felony**: intentionally or recklessly causes **physical injury** to a child (subsection (d), penalty (f)d).
  - **Class F felony**: intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly acts in a manner likely to be injurious to a child’s **physical, mental or moral welfare** (subsection (e), penalty (f)e).
- Adds explicit “**not a defense**” provisions:
  - Not knowing (or reasonably believing otherwise) the child’s age regarding the **under 6** aggravator (subsection (c)(4)).
  - Not knowing (or reasonably believing otherwise) the child’s **intellectual, developmental, or physical disability** regarding the disability aggravator (subsection (c)(5)).
- States the new section does **not preclude additional charges, convictions, or sentences** for other crimes (subsection (g)).

## Who Benefits
- **Children under 6 years of age** and children with **intellectual/developmental/physical disabilities**, by making injury to these groups an explicit **Class C felony** aggravator.
- The State’s enforcement and prosecution efforts, by providing a **specific offense framework** and removing certain **knowledge-based defenses** for age/disability aggravators.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Individuals in a **position of trust, authority, or supervision** over children who face **new or enhanced felony liability** under §1103F, including exposure to cumulative charging under subsection (g).
