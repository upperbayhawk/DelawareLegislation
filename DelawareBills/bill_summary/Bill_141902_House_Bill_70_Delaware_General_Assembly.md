## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Titles 16 and 25 of the Delaware Code to require lead-based paint safety certification for older rental housing, create compliance and disclosure requirements, establish financial assistance (grants/loans) for eligible landlords, add anti-discrimination protections related to lead hazards and elevated blood lead levels, and add worker-safety provisions for state lead abatement/remediation contracts.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **all rental units constructed before January 1, 1978** to be certified by DHSS as **“lead free” or “lead safe”** on a phased schedule with deadlines from **January 1, 2028** through **January 1, 2031**.  
- Allows only **DHSS-approved lead inspectors** (trained under DHSS-approved or equivalently stringent out-of-state programs) to issue certificates; DHSS must maintain a **public list** of inspectors.  
- Imposes civil penalties for noncompliance: up to **$100/day per rental unit** for failure to obtain/file required certification; **$50/day** for failing to provide a requested certificate; **$20/day** for failing to provide required educational materials.  
- Creates a **Lead-based Paint Hazard Control Grant and Loan Program** to help eligible landlords pay for certification, remediation/abatement, and required alternative lodging; **grants available until January 1, 2028**, then **loans only**.  
- If inspection finds a unit **uninhabitable due to a lead-based paint hazard**, landlord must provide **alternative lodging** during abatement/remediation with **at least 10 days’ advance notice** of moving in/out.  
- Requires recertification of **lead safe** units before a rental agreement that begins **more than 4 years** after last lead-safe certification; and requires recertification (lead safe or lead free) if a **lead hazard is discovered** or an occupant has an **elevated blood lead level**.  
- Starting **July 1, 2028**, rental agreements for covered units must disclose certification status and include tenant acknowledgment of receipt of required lead education materials.  
- Act is **effective immediately** but **implementation is delayed** until **6 months after** DHSS publishes a notice in the Register of Regulations confirming **final regulations are adopted** and **sufficient funding is appropriated and available**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Earlier identification and control of lead hazards** in older rental housing by requiring certification for units built before **January 1, 1978** (phased deadlines **2028–2031**).  
- **Tenant information and transparency**: required disclosures in rental agreements (effective **July 1, 2028**) and provision of certificates within **7 days of tenant request**.  
- **Protection for vulnerable populations** through grant preference for units housing or regularly visited by **children under 6** and **pregnant individuals**.  
- **Stronger tenant protections** via anti-discrimination provisions tied to complaints, participation in investigations, elevated blood lead levels, pregnancy, or presence of children under 6.  
- **Worker safety enhancement** in state lead abatement/remediation contracts by requiring **free blood testing at least every 3 months** for workers.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Compliance costs and administrative burden for landlords** to obtain inspections, certifications, recertifications, disclosures, and educational-material acknowledgments; penalties can accrue daily (e.g., **up to $100/day per unit** for missing certification deadlines).  
- **Potential operational impacts from uninhabitable determinations**, requiring landlords to pay **alternative lodging** and manage tenant relocation logistics with **10 days’ notice**.  
- **Limited inspection capacity risk** acknowledged in the bill through a **certificate exemption** process (up to **6 months**) when no approved lead inspectors are available.  
- **Rent increase restriction**: landlords who receive grant/loan funds may not raise rent for **3 years** (from the date of the inspection indicating remediation/abatement need) on the assisted property.

## Educational Impact

## Health Impact

- Triggers recertification when an **elevated blood lead level** is found in a resident.  
- Adds worker health protection in state lead remediation contracts: **free blood testing at least every 3 months**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/updated certification mandate (Title 25, Ch. 54):**  
  - Rental units constructed before **January 1, 1978** must be certified **lead free** or **lead safe** by DHSS on this schedule:  
    - Built before **1950**: by **January 1, 2028**  
    - Built before **1960**: by **January 1, 2029**  
    - Built before **1970**: by **January 1, 2030**  
    - Built before **1978**: by **January 1, 2031**
- **Certificate content requirements** (landlord/property manager identity, unit address, inspector name, issue date, inspection date, and lead free vs lead safe status).  
- **Inspector qualification/approval** required; DHSS must keep a **public list** of inspectors.  
- **Enforcement/penalties:** up to **$100/day per unit** for missing certification; **$50/day** for failure to provide certificate upon request; **$20/day** for failure to provide educational materials.  
- **Tenant access requirement:** tenants must permit **reasonable access** for inspection/evaluation.  
- **Alternative lodging requirement** when unit is uninhabitable due to lead hazard, with **10 days’ advance notice** for moves.  
- **Recertification rules:**  
  - Lead safe: recertify before a new rental agreement starting **>4 years** after last lead-safe certification; also if hazard discovered or elevated blood lead level found.  
  - Lead free: recertify only if hazard discovered or elevated blood lead level found.  
- **Disclosure rules (effective July 1, 2028):** rental agreements for covered units must disclose certification status/date/inspector and include acknowledgment of educational materials receipt.  
- **Multi-unit sampling option:** lead inspector may inspect **5 randomly selected units** in a multi-unit building; if all 5 are lead safe, **all units** may be certified **lead safe** (but **lead free** requires individual inspection).  
- **Grant/loan program:** grants until **January 1, 2028**; loans only thereafter; rent increases barred for **3 years** on assisted properties.  
- **Anti-discrimination provisions** related to lead hazard complaints/participation, elevated blood lead level, pregnancy, and children under 6 (with an exception for lawful “housing for older persons”).  
- **Title 25 landlord obligation updated:** landlord must provide a rental unit **free of lead-based paint hazards and certified** as required by Chapter 54.  
- **Title 16 state program contracting:** bid invitations must require worker safety provisions including **free blood testing at least every 3 months**.

## Who Benefits
- **Tenants** in pre-1978 rental housing (improved disclosure, educational materials, certification, and alternative lodging if uninhabitable).  
- **Children under 6** and **pregnant individuals**, who are prioritized for grant assistance (and protected from discrimination).  
- **Landlords** who qualify for and receive **grants/loans** to offset costs of certification, remediation/abatement, and alternative lodging.  
- **Workers** on state lead abatement/remediation projects (blood testing at least every **3 months**).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Landlords/property managers** of pre-1978 units who must pay for inspections/certifications/remediation and face daily civil penalties for noncompliance.  
- Landlords receiving assistance funds are restricted from raising rent for **3 years** on the assisted property.  
- **Tenants** may experience disruption from inspections or temporary relocation when a unit is deemed uninhabitable (though alternative lodging is required in that circumstance).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Implementation trigger (Section 4):** Act is effective immediately, but must be implemented **6 months after** DHSS publishes notice in the **Register of Regulations** that:  
  1) final implementing regulations are adopted, and  
  2) sufficient funding has been appropriated and is available.
- **Certification deadlines (by construction date):**  
  - Before **1950** → **January 1, 2028**  
  - Before **1960** → **January 1, 2029**  
  - Before **1970** → **January 1, 2030**  
  - Before **1978** → **January 1, 2031**
- **Disclosure/acknowledgment requirement effective:** **July 1, 2028** (rental agreements for units constructed before **January 1, 1978**).  
- **Grant/loan program switch:** grants available **until January 1, 2028**; **loans only** after that date.  
- **Feasibility Committee reports due:** **January 1, 2028**, **January 1, 2029**, and **January 1, 2030** (annual through January 1, 2030 per text).  
- **Tenant certificate request deadline:** landlord must provide copy within **7 days** of request.  
- **Alternative housing notice:** landlord must provide **at least 10 days** advance notice before tenant must move into or out of alternative housing.  
- **Certificate exemption duration cap:** may not exceed **6 months**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Certification compliance:** failure to obtain/file certificate by the applicable deadline is a violation; DHSS may assess **civil penalty up to $100 per day per rental unit** until obtained/ filed.  
- **Certificate exemption:** no penalty if DHSS issued an exemption *before* certification is required; exemption only upon showing that timely certification is impossible because **no DHSS-approved lead inspectors are available**; exemption **≤ 6 months** and limited to time needed to obtain inspection.  
- **Tenant access:** tenant must permit **reasonable access** for required inspection/evaluation.  
- **Certificate disclosure & access:** (effective **July 1, 2028**) rental agreements must include certification disclosure; landlord must provide the certificate within **7 days** of tenant request or pay **$50/day** until provided.  
- **Educational materials:** must be provided **before occupancy**; failure triggers **$20/day** penalty until provided; (effective **July 1, 2028**) tenant must acknowledge receipt in the rental agreement.  
- **Alternative lodging:** required when inspection finds unit **uninhabitable** due to lead hazard, during abatement/remediation.  
- **Anti-discrimination:** makes specified discrimination unlawful relating to lead hazard complaints/participation, elevated blood lead levels (actual or perceived), pregnancy, or children under 6 (subject to “housing for older persons” exception).  
- **State contracting worker safety:** bids for state lead abatement/remediation must include worker safety provisions including **free blood testing at least every 3 months**.
