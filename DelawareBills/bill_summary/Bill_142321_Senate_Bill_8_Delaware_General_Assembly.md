## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 11 to **codify statewide standard conditions of probation (and suspension of sentence)** to be used by Delaware courts and the Department of Correction (DOC), and set an effective date **180 days after enactment**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes a **statutory list of “standard conditions”** that apply to individuals on probation, unless the court imposes other specific or inconsistent conditions.  
- Standard condition: probationer **may not commit a new criminal offense or certain motor vehicle offenses** during supervision (excludes motor vehicle offenses subject to voluntary assessment under **21 Del. C. § 709**).  
- Requires probationer to **report any new arrest, conviction, or police contact within 72 hours** to their probation and parole officer.  
- Requires probationer to **report residence or employment changes within 72 hours**.  
- Requires probationer to obtain **authorization to leave Delaware or the approved state of residence**.  
- Allows officer entry/visits: probationer must permit the probation and parole officer to **enter the home and visit the place of employment**.  
- Firearms/deadly weapons: if not prohibited under **11 Del. C. § 1448**, probationer must have **written approval** from the probation and parole officer to own/possess/control firearms or deadly weapons.  
- Fiscal note: DOC anticipates **one-time DACS system changes** costing **$100,000 in FY 2026** to reflect/enforce the new standard conditions.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Uniformity and clarity:** A single codified list of standard conditions may reduce variability across cases by providing a consistent baseline set of probation rules. (Synopsis and § 4332(a)(1)-(2))  
- **Clear reporting timelines:** Explicit **72-hour** reporting deadlines for arrests/convictions/police contact and for residence/employment changes create clearer compliance expectations. (§ 4332(a)(1)b, e)  
- **Explicit travel authorization requirement:** Clarifies that leaving Delaware (or the approved state of residence) requires officer authorization. (§ 4332(a)(1)d)  
- **System alignment:** Fiscal note indicates planned DACS modifications to “reflect and enforce” the conditions, which may improve tracking/notifications. (Fiscal note assumptions)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expanded/explicit supervision intrusions:** The standard conditions include permission for an officer to **enter the home** and **visit the place of employment**, which may be viewed as intrusive for all probationers by default. (§ 4332(a)(1)c)  
- **Administrative burden on probationers:** Multiple requirements with short timelines (**72 hours**) could increase technical violations if not met. (§ 4332(a)(1)b, e)  
- **Up-front implementation cost:** DOC estimates **$100,000 (FY 2026)** for one-time DACS programming changes. (Fiscal note)

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date depends on enactment date:** The bill sets effectiveness at **180 days after enactment**, but the actual calendar date is not provided in the source text. (Section 2)  
- **Scope of “police contact”:** The bill requires reporting “police contact” within **72 hours** but does not define what qualifies as contact. (§ 4332(a)(1)b)  
- **Standards vs. conditions interaction:** The bill states standard conditions apply “in the absence of any other specific or inconsistent conditions,” but does not specify how conflicts are resolved operationally beyond that statement. (§ 4332(a)(2))

## Key Policy Changes
- **Codifies standard probation conditions** in **11 Del. C. § 4332(a)(1)**, including:
  - No new criminal offenses or certain motor vehicle offenses during supervision (excluding offenses subject to voluntary assessment under **21 Del. C. § 709**).  
  - **72-hour** reporting for new arrest/conviction/police contact.  
  - Reporting requirements and cooperation with supervision (report as directed; permit home entry/employment visits).  
  - Travel restriction requiring officer authorization to leave Delaware/approved state of residence.  
  - **72-hour** reporting for residence/employment changes.  
  - Firearm/deadly weapon possession allowed only with **written approval** if not prohibited under **11 Del. C. § 1448**.  
  - Compliance with special conditions imposed by the court, Board of Parole, or probation and parole officer.
- States that standard conditions apply unless the court imposes other specific or inconsistent conditions. (§ 4332(a)(2))  
- Clarifies/maintains court authority to impose/modify conditions and requires DOC report before modification. (§ 4332(a)(4)-(5))  
- Updates language requiring delivery of a copy of any order to DOC and to the probationer/individual on probation. (§ 4332(a)(6))

## Who Benefits
- **Courts and DOC:** From having codified standard conditions to use statewide. (Synopsis; § 4332(a)(1)-(2))  
- **Probationers and counsel (potentially):** From clearer baseline conditions and explicit timelines (e.g., **72 hours**). (§ 4332(a)(1)b, e)
