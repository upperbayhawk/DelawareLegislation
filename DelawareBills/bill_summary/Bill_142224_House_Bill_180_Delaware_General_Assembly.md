## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Propose a constitutional amendment to Article V of the Delaware Constitution to update voting qualifications and voter disqualification rules, including felony-related disenfranchisement, and to remove certain unconstitutional or outdated provisions (age 21 threshold, durational residency requirements, literacy test), and to remove voting-disqualification penalties tied to certain election offenses.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates the Delaware Constitution’s voting-age language to **“18 years or older”** (from age 21 language) to align with the **26th Amendment**.  
- Removes **durational residency requirements** currently stated as **1 year in the State, 3 months in the county, and 30 days in the hundred/election district**; a voter must still be a **resident at the time of registration** (per synopsis).  
- Eliminates the constitutional **literacy test** requirement (reading the Constitution in English and writing one’s name).  
- Changes felony-related voting disqualification so loss of voting rights is limited to the period a person is **“imprisoned”** for a felony, with restoration upon **pardon** or **end of imprisonment**, whichever comes first.  
- Removes the Constitution’s list of felony categories that previously resulted in permanent disenfranchisement (e.g., murder/manslaughter exceptions, public corruption, sexual offenses).  
- Prohibits making re-enfranchisement contingent on **“monetary payments of any kind.”**  
- Defines key terms including **“imprisoned/imprisonment”** (incarceration including work release and home confinement; **excluding community supervision**) and **“community supervision”** (probation/parole/early release).  
- Removes the **10-year voting incapacity** penalty that follows sentencing for certain election-related misdemeanor offenses in Article V, § 7.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Constitutional conformity:** Updates the Constitution to match federal constitutional and statutory requirements cited in the bill synopsis (e.g., **26th Amendment**, **52 U.S.C. § 10501** on literacy tests).  
- **Earlier and clearer voter-rights restoration for felony convictions:** Restores voting rights after **imprisonment ends** (or **pardon**, whichever occurs first), and clarifies that community supervision (probation/parole/early release) does **not** count as “imprisonment.”  
- **Eliminates pay-to-vote barriers:** Explicitly bars conditioning re-enfranchisement on **any monetary payments**.  
- **Reduces barriers to new residents voting:** Removes the Constitution’s **1-year/3-month/30-day** residency-duration thresholds for eligibility.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative and IT changes required:** Fiscal note states DELJIS must modify systems and interfaces with the Department of Elections, with a projected **one-time cost of $30,000** (FY **2027**).  
- **Removes legislative authority to impose voting forfeiture as punishment for crime:** The amendment deletes constitutional language allowing the General Assembly to impose forfeiture of suffrage “as a punishment for crime,” reducing future legislative flexibility in this area.  
- **Eliminates a post-sentence election-offense disenfranchisement penalty:** Removing the **10-year** voting incapacity for specified election offenses reduces a constitutional penalty that previously applied after sentencing (though other penalties in § 7 remain).

## Key Policy Changes
1. **Voting age updated:** Constitutional voting qualification updated to **“18 years or older”** (replacing “age of 21 years” language).  
2. **Durational residency requirements removed:** Deletes constitutionally stated thresholds of **1 year (State)**, **3 months (county)**, and **30 days (hundred/election district)**.  
3. **Literacy test removed:** Deletes requirement to **read the Constitution in English** and **write one’s name** as a condition of voting.  
4. **Felony disenfranchisement narrowed:** Voting disqualification for felony conviction limited to when a person is **imprisoned due to the felony**, with restoration upon **pardon** or **expiration of imprisonment**, whichever occurs first.  
5. **Permanent-disqualification felony list removed:** Deletes constitutional carve-outs that previously prevented restoration for certain felony categories (murder/manslaughter except vehicular homicide; public administration offenses such as bribery/abuse of office; sexual offenses).  
6. **No monetary-condition rule:** Restoration of voting rights “**must not** be made contingent on **monetary payments of any kind**.”  
7. **Key definitions added:** Adds definitions for **community supervision**, **home confinement**, **imprisoned/imprisonment**, **incarceration**, and **work release** (including that “imprisonment” includes **work release and home confinement**, but **excludes community supervision**).  
8. **Election-offense disenfranchisement penalty removed:** Removes the additional penalty in Article V, § 7 that made a person **incapable of voting for 10 years** following sentence for specified election offenses, while leaving other § 7 penalties (misdemeanor classification; fines and/or imprisonment ranges) in place.

## Who Benefits
- **Eligible voters age 18–20**, due to constitutional alignment recognizing voting rights at **18 years or older**.  
- **New residents** who would have been barred by the Constitution’s **1-year/3-month/30-day** durational residency thresholds.  
- **People convicted of felonies** who have completed incarceration (or receive a pardon), because voting rights restoration is tied to **imprisonment** and cannot be conditioned on **monetary payments**.  
- **People previously subject to the § 7 10-year voting incapacity** following sentence for certain election offenses, because that additional disenfranchisement penalty is removed.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Election-offense penalties (Article V, § 7):** Maintains misdemeanor penalties of **fine not less than $100 and not more than $5,000**, and/or **imprisonment not less than 1 month nor more than 3 years**, at the court’s discretion.  
- **Removed penalty:** Deletes the additional penalty that made an offender **incapable of voting for 10 years next following the person’s sentence** (disenfranchisement penalty).  
- **Compliance requirement:** Re-enfranchisement/removal of disqualification for felony conviction **cannot be contingent on monetary payments of any kind**.  
- **Constitutional amendment vote threshold:** Requires **two-thirds** vote in each chamber to pass (Del. Const. art. XVI, § 1).
