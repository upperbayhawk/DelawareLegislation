## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16, Chapter 55, Subchapter IV to update the structure, membership, appointment authorities, and operational rules for the Interagency Committee on Autism (ICA) and the Delaware Network for Excellence in Autism (DNEA), and to make technical drafting updates.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates statutory definitions (e.g., “ASD” aligned to the current **DSM** definition) and clarifies that related developmental disabilities are included when the individual can benefit from ASD-related improvements.  
- Restates and clarifies ICA duties, including: use of evidence-based practices; implementing the **2013 Delaware Strategic Plan (“Blueprint for Collective Action”)**; monitoring service gaps/duplication; and advising on legislation.  
- Requires the ICA to **collaborate with DNEA** to submit an **annual report** to the **Governor, General Assembly, Director and Librarian of the Division of Legislative Services**, including progress and **major expense categories (public, private, and in-kind)**.  
- Changes ICA membership framework: removes the “Director of Autism Resources” (formerly the Statewide Director of the Delaware Autism Program) as a statutory voting member and instead includes a **Department of Education representative**.  
- Removes the **Delaware Family Voices** seat (per synopsis: Family Voices now falls under the Parent Information Center, which already has a seat).  
- Revises appointment authority so certain **non-governmental entity representatives are appointed by the Governor** (with potential nominee lists allowed), reflecting limits on delegating appointment power to non-state entities.  
- Codifies ICA governance rules: **quarterly meetings** rotating among the **3 counties**; **quorum = majority** (vacancies not counted); actions by **majority vote of members present**; no compensation (but reimbursement allowed for actual and necessary expenses).  
- Clarifies DNEA operational requirements (staffing minimums, website duties, education training collaboration, and contracting standards), including at least **2 FTE family trainer/navigators** (contracted through **Autism Delaware**) and at least **4 team leader staff positions** spanning healthcare, education, vocational, and social services.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More flexible DOE representation on ICA:** Replacing a specific DOE-affiliated title (“Autism Resources”) with a **DOE-appointed representative** may improve continuity and selection flexibility (Synopsis; § 5544(a)(1)).  
- **Clearer appointment legality:** Moves appointment power for certain non-governmental seats to the **Governor**, addressing legal constraints referenced in the synopsis (State ex rel. James v. Schorr).  
- **Enhanced transparency and accountability:** Annual report must include progress toward recommendations and **major expense categories**, including **public, private, and in-kind** support (§ 5542(6)).  
- **Operational clarity for ICA governance:** Explicit quorum, voting, meeting rotation across **3 counties**, and reimbursement rules support consistent administration (§ 5544).  
- **Codified minimum DNEA capacity for family navigation and cross-system training:** Requires at least **2 FTE** family trainer/navigators and at least **4** team leader positions to cover major service domains (§ 5543(f)–(h)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced direct role for Autism Resources in ICA membership:** The Act removes the Director of Autism Resources as a voting member (Synopsis), which may reduce direct representation of that role on the ICA as written in statute.  
- **Potential administrative burden:** Expanded/clarified reporting recipients and content (including expense categories and progress reporting) may increase administrative workload for ICA/DNEA (§ 5542(6); § 5543(d)).  
- **Governor appointment requirement may slow filling seats:** Shifting appointment authority to the Governor for multiple non-governmental seats could add process steps compared with entity-appointed models (§ 5544(a)(5), (6), (8), (10), (13), (14)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- The Act references “appropriate state agency” for DNEA service contracts but does **not name which agency** (§ 5543(a)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Definitions updated/clarified** (including DSM-based ASD definition; inclusion of related developmental disabilities when the individual can benefit; terminology shift to “individuals living with ASD”) (§ 5541).  
- **ICA duties restated and clarified**, including evidence-based coordination, 2013 Blueprint implementation, service monitoring to identify gaps/avoid duplication, DNEA oversight, legislative advising, and annual reporting with expense categories (§ 5542).  
- **ICA membership and appointment authority revised**:
  - Statutory list updated and reorganized; DOE seat is a **DOE-appointed representative** (§ 5544(a)(1); Synopsis).  
  - Certain non-governmental members are **appointed by the Governor**, with the option for entities to submit nominee lists the Governor “may consider” (§ 5544(a)(5), (6), (8), (10)).  
  - Adds/retains gubernatorial appointment for county parent/caregiver seats and an individual with ASD (§ 5544(a)(13)–(14)).  
  - Removes Delaware Family Voices membership (Synopsis).  
- **Governance rules codified**: DNEA Director is **chair** but **non-voting**; quorum and voting rules; meeting rotation among **3 counties**; reimbursement allowed for expenses (§ 5544(b)–(j)).  
- **DNEA operational requirements clarified**: staffing minimums (including **at least 1** administrative support staff; **at least 4** team leader positions; **at least 2 FTE** family trainer/navigators contracted through Autism Delaware), website functions, and contracting standards (§ 5543).

## Who Benefits

- **Families/guardians** seeking navigation and access to services via at least **2 FTE** family trainer/navigators (§ 5543(h)).
