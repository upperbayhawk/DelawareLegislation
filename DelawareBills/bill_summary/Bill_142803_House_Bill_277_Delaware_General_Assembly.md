## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Codifies in Delaware law a “Blue Envelope Program” under Title 21, Chapter 62 (Traffic Stop Safety) to improve safety during traffic stops involving persons with disabilities by making an information envelope available upon request and specifying required envelope contents.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new statute in Title 21, Part IV, Chapter 62 establishing the “Blue Envelope Program” (§ 6201).  
- Directs the Delaware State Police and “designated partners” to provide and make available, **upon request**, an information envelope (“blue envelope”) for use by a **person with disabilities**.  
- Requires the envelope be able to hold key traffic-stop documents: **driver’s license, registration, insurance card, and other relevant documents**.  
- Requires the envelope exterior to identify the holder as a **person with a disability**.  
- Requires written guidance on the envelope for **law enforcement** on interacting with a person with a disability.  
- Requires written guidance on the envelope for a **person with a disability** on safely interacting with law enforcement during a traffic stop.  
- The synopsis states the intent is to increase safety for **both officers and persons with a disability** during traffic stops.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardized communication tool:** Mandates written guidance for both law enforcement and drivers with disabilities, potentially improving clarity during traffic stops (§ 6201(b)(3)-(4)).  
- **Document organization:** Requires the envelope to contain key documents (license/registration/insurance), which may streamline document presentation during a stop (§ 6201(b)(1)).  
- **Safety-focused identification:** Exterior notice that the holder is a person with a disability may help officers tailor interactions (§ 6201(b)(2)).  
- **Program continuity:** Codifying an existing Delaware State Police program may support consistent availability over time (Synopsis; § 6201(a)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Privacy concern inherent in labeling:** The envelope must identify the holder as a person with a disability on the outside, which could disclose disability status if seen by others (§ 6201(b)(2)).  
- **Unclear scope of “designated partners”:** The bill assigns responsibilities to DSP and “designated partners” without defining who they are or what duties they must perform (§ 6201(a)).  
- **No standards for eligibility/verification:** The bill does not specify how “person with disabilities” is defined or confirmed for program access (§ 6201(a)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Definition gap:** What qualifies as a “person with disabilities” for purposes of receiving a blue envelope is not defined in the bill text (§ 6201(a)).  
- **Designated partners:** Which entities may be “designated partners,” how they are designated, and what obligations they have are not specified (§ 6201(a)).  
- **Distribution logistics:** Where and how envelopes are requested and distributed (DMV, police stations, online request, etc.) is not specified (§ 6201(a)).  
- **Content standards:** The bill requires “written guidance” but does not specify required language, format, training alignment, languages, or update frequency (§ 6201(b)(3)-(4)).  
- **Use during traffic stops:** Whether or how law enforcement must respond when presented with a blue envelope is not specified beyond the requirement that guidance be printed (§ 6201(b)(3)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds new Chapter 62, “Traffic Stop Safety,”** and creates § 6201 “Blue Envelope Program” in Title 21, Part IV.  
- Requires Delaware State Police and designated partners to **provide and make available upon request** a “blue envelope” for use by a person with disabilities (§ 6201(a)).  
- Specifies minimum envelope requirements (§ 6201(b)):  
  1) Must be capable of containing **driver’s license, registration certificate, insurance ID card, and other relevant documents**.  
  2) Must include exterior text identifying the holder as a **person with a disability**.  
  3) Must include written guidance for **law enforcement** on interactions with a person with a disability.  
  4) Must include written guidance for a **person with a disability** for safely interacting with law enforcement during a traffic stop.

## Who Benefits
- **Persons with disabilities** who want a standardized way to present documents and communicate needs during traffic stops (§ 6201(a), (b)(2), (b)(4)).  
- **Law-enforcement officers** who receive standardized written guidance about interacting with a person with a disability during a traffic stop (§ 6201(b)(3); Synopsis).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Blue envelope users** who may have concerns about disclosure of disability status because the envelope must identify the holder as a person with a disability on the outside (§ 6201(b)(2)).
