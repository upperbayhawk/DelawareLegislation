## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Authorize and fund Delaware’s FY 2026 bond and capital improvements program (fiscal year ending **June 30, 2026**), including issuance of **up to 20-year** General Obligation (GO) bonds, appropriations from the **General Fund**, **Transportation Trust Fund (TTF)**, Delaware Transportation Authority sources, and other funds; reprogram/transfer certain funds; and impose spending, procurement, reporting, and project closeout controls.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes issuance of **up to 20-year GO bonds** to provide **$339,505,000** in proceeds, including **$90,828,738** identified for the **local share of school bonds** (to be used at district discretion as described in the notes).
- Establishes/continues a large FY2026 capital program across agencies, including cited items such as **ERP Cloud Migration ($50,000,000 General Funds)** and **Legislative Hall A&E renovation/expansion/parking garage ($50,000,000 bond authorization)**.
- Appropriates **DelDOT TTF** funding totaling **$255,221,759** (**Road System $167,890,836; Grants/Allocations $38,680,000; Transit $6,278,400; Support $42,372,523**) and separately references a broader **DelDOT FY2026 capital program authorization of $673,606,963** (relationship between these totals is not clarified in the notes).
- Imposes project closeout rules: “**substantial completion**” includes **75%** planned-tenant occupancy (or Appeals Board determination); after substantial completion, using remaining **unencumbered** funds generally requires **Appeals Board approval**, and **unencumbered** authorization balances **revert 1 year after** substantial completion.
- Requires **quarterly** expenditure status reports (OMB-set format) from **all state agencies and public school districts receiving Bond Bill funds** for **all incomplete projects**, submitted to the **OMB Director** and **Controller General**.
- Creates additional targeted program conditions, including **$10,000,000** for farmland/forestland preservation (up to **$3,000,000** Young Farmer set-aside; **$1,000,000** Forestland set-aside) and cover-crop/conservation funding rules including **up to $400,000** to the Nutrient Management Commission and a **10%** administrative cost cap.
- Adds K–12 facility administrative and design requirements, including annual building-use reporting **by Oct. 30**, DOE custodial allocation verification/reissuance **by Feb. 1**, and a condition that DOE **may not approve** plans for new/major-renovated school buildings unless required bottle-filling stations are included (**≥1 per 200** projected occupants; cooled/filtered; touchless encouraged).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Funds statewide capital needs, including major cited projects (e.g., **$50,000,000** ERP Cloud Migration) and significant transportation investments (e.g., **$255,221,759** DelDOT TTF appropriations; **$673,606,963** DelDOT capital program authorization cited).
- Enhances fiscal oversight and transparency through **quarterly** reporting on **all incomplete projects** funded under the Act (agencies and school districts).
- Encourages timely project completion/closeout by triggering **1-year reversion** of **unencumbered** authorization balances after “substantial completion,” with **Appeals Board** controls over post-completion use.
- Supports conservation and agricultural succession with **$10,000,000** farmland/forestland preservation funding (including up to **$3,000,000** for Young Farmers and **$1,000,000** for Forestland).
- Improves student access to drinking water in new/major-renovated schools by conditioning DOE plan approval on bottle-filling station inclusion (**≥1 per 200** projected occupants; cooled/filtered; touchless encouraged).
- Facilitates certain collaborations by allowing **DOT/DNREC** to contract directly with **UD/DSU/DTCC** notwithstanding **29 Del. C. c. 69**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Increases long-term State debt obligations through authorization of **up to 20-year GO bonds** providing **$339,505,000** in proceeds.
- Adds administrative workload for agencies and public school districts due to **quarterly** reporting requirements for **all incomplete projects**.
- Reduces flexibility to retain or repurpose remaining project balances due to **Appeals Board approval** requirements after substantial completion and mandatory **1-year** reversion of **unencumbered** balances.
- Shifts or increases certain local school costs by limiting State cost-share for building size increases to **≤1%** square footage (any **>1%** must be locally funded) and eliminating “market pressure” allocation for Certificates of Necessity approved **Nov. 2023 or thereafter**.
- May increase charges for some conservation district work when **FAR Subpart 31.2** applies due to a permitted **10%** overhead add-on.
- DelDOT **Community Workforce Agreement (CWA) pilot** (up to **two** projects) imposes mandatory contractor participation and reporting requirements.

## Fiscal or Administrative Impact
- Requires State debt issuance/management for **up to 20-year GO bonds** and administration of multi-agency capital appropriations.
- **OMB** must set a quarterly report format; agencies and public school districts receiving act funds must submit **quarterly** expenditure status reports for **all incomplete projects** to the **OMB Director** and **Controller General**.
- Establishes post-completion governance through an **Appeals Board** (approvals/determinations), with the **Controller General** required to notify **JCCI co-chairs** of Appeals Board decisions.
- By **September 1** of each fiscal year, **OMB** and the receiving agency must agree on certain **equipment-only funding** transfer amounts.
- **DOE** must implement facilities reporting (district reports **by Oct. 30**; DOE verification/reissuance **by Feb. 1**) and condition plan approval for new/major-renovated schools on bottle-filling station compliance.
- **DelDOT** must administer a **CWA pilot** (up to **two** projects) and issue an annual report **on/before Jan. 1**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Bonding/appropriations:** Authorizes FY2026 capital projects and multiple funding sources; authorizes **up to 20-year GO bonds** to provide **$339,505,000** in proceeds.
- **Project closeout and reversions:** Defines “substantial completion” to include **75%** planned occupancy (or Appeals Board determination); requires Appeals Board approval for certain post-substantial-completion uses of unencumbered funds; mandates reversion of **unencumbered** authorization balances **1 year after** substantial completion; cites **General Fund** reversion by **June 30, 2028** for unexpended/unencumbered balances.
- **Quarterly reporting:** Requires **quarterly** expenditure status reports (OMB format) for **all incomplete projects** from agencies and public school districts receiving Bond Bill funds to the OMB Director and Controller General.
- **Procurement/contracting exceptions and rules:** Allows **DOT/DNREC** direct contracting with **UD/DSU/DTCC** notwithstanding **29 Del. C. c. 69**; sets construction manager project bonding/subcontractor listing/insurance requirements (including for public schools) and applies **29 Del. C. c. 69**.
- **MCI/Equipment restriction:** Limits using **MCI/Equipment** funds to complete a major capital improvement involving a **new facility**, except for **legal settlement, emergency, or unforeseen conditions** (as determined by unspecified officials).
- **DelDOT CWA pilot:** Up to **two** projects; awarded contractor must enter a CWA and meet contract reporting requirements; annual report due **on/before Jan. 1**.
- **K–12 facilities conditions:** Building-use reporting **by Oct. 30**; DOE custodial allocation verification/reissuance **by Feb. 1**; bottle-filling station requirement (**≥1 per 200** occupants) for DOE plan approval; State share limited to **≤1%** square footage increase; “market pressure” allocation not available for CNs approved **Nov. 2023+**.
- **Conservation districts cost treatment:** FAR rules apply only when federally required; when **FAR Subpart 31.2** applies, districts may add **10%** overhead; cover-crop/conservation funding includes a **10%** administrative cap and **up to $400,000** for Nutrient Management Commission alternative projects.
