## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Strengthen enforcement of manufactured home community landlord obligations by (1) treating certain repeated violations as an “unlawful practice” under Delaware’s Consumer Fraud Act, (2) authorizing the Attorney General to petition for a manufactured home community receivership under specified conditions, and (3) requiring Justice of the Peace Courts to notify DOJ Consumer Protection when tenants’ receivership petitions are filed.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Deems a **“pattern or practice”** of certain landlord violations in manufactured home communities an **unlawful practice under the Consumer Fraud Act** (**6 Del. C. § 2513; Ch. 25, Subch. II**) if **four conditions** are met (tenant not at fault; landlord notice; not timely corrected; risk to health/safety or tenant property).  
- Allows the **Attorney General** (in addition to tenants) to **petition for receivership** of a manufactured home community in **Justice of the Peace Court** after conditions persist **5 days or more after notice to the landlord**.  
- Receivership grounds include lack of **heat, running water, light, electricity, or adequate sewage facilities** where the landlord has a duty to provide, or **other imminently dangerous conditions** to life/health/safety.  
- Clarifies the **Attorney General may intervene** in any action brought under the manufactured home community receivership subchapter.  
- Requires the **Justice of the Peace Court** to send written notice to DOJ Consumer Protection **within 10 days** of receiving a tenants’ receivership petition under **25 Del. C. § 5901 or § 7061**, including a copy of the petition and the case number.  
- **Exception:** the 10-day notice is **not required** if the petition is filed by the **Attorney General**.  
- Fiscal note anticipates DOJ staffing: **2.0 FTE** and use of **Appropriated Special Funds (ASF)** from the **Consumer Protection Fund**; provides specific FY26–FY28 cost estimates.  
- Act was **approved September 3, 2025** and the fiscal note assumes it is **effective upon the Governor’s signature**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger consumer enforcement tool:** Treating qualifying repeated landlord violations as an **unlawful practice under the Consumer Fraud Act** may expand enforcement leverage where there is a **pattern or practice** and a **risk to health/safety or tenant property** (25 Del. C. § 7005(c)).  
- **State enforcement pathway for severe conditions:** Expressly authorizes the **Attorney General** to seek **receivership** when serious utility/habitability failures or **imminently dangerous conditions** persist **5+ days after notice** (25 Del. C. § 7061).  
- **Improved coordination/visibility:** Mandatory **10-day court notice** to DOJ Consumer Protection for receivership petitions may help DOJ track and respond to deteriorating property conditions (10 Del. C. § 9507).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased compliance and litigation exposure for landlords:** A “pattern or practice” of violations meeting the listed criteria becomes actionable as a **Consumer Fraud Act** unlawful practice (25 Del. C. § 7005(c)), potentially increasing investigations and civil actions.  
- **Administrative staffing costs:** DOJ estimates it needs **1.0 FTE Deputy Attorney General (DAG)** and **1.0 FTE Special Investigator IV**, with ongoing costs funded from the Consumer Protection Fund (fiscal note).  
- **Receivership petition risk:** Attorney General authority to file in Justice of the Peace Court may increase the likelihood of receivership proceedings for qualifying conditions (25 Del. C. § 7061).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill uses the phrase **“pattern or practice”** but does **not define** how many incidents, over what time period, or what proof is required.  
- “**Timely manner**” for correcting violations is not defined in the text.  
- The receivership trigger refers to conditions existing **“for 5 days or more after notice to the landlord”** but does not specify required **form/content** of notice in this bill text.  
- The act references relief DOJ “**deems necessary**” in civil actions (25 Del. C. § 7005(b)) without specifying remedies in this bill text.

## Health Impact
- The Consumer Fraud Act linkage applies only if the condition created a **risk to health or safety** (or risk to tenant/occupant property) and other criteria are met (25 Del. C. § 7005(c)(4)).  
- Receivership may be sought for lack of essential services (e.g., **heat, running water, electricity**) or **other imminently dangerous conditions** to life/health/safety (25 Del. C. § 7061(a)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **25 Del. C. § 7005(c):** Makes a qualifying **pattern or practice** of violations of manufactured home community landlord obligations (Subchapters I–V, Ch. 70, Title 25) or required rental-agreement provisions an **unlawful practice** under **6 Del. C. § 2513** and a violation of **6 Del. C. Ch. 25, Subch. II**, if:
  1) violation not directly caused by tenant/family/others with tenant consent;  
  2) landlord had **actual notice** (final enacted version) of the condition;  
  3) landlord failed to correct in a **timely manner**; and  
  4) condition created risk to **health/safety** or **tenant/occupant property**.  
- **25 Del. C. § 7061:** Adds the **Attorney General** as an eligible petitioner for manufactured home community receivership; requires that qualifying conditions exist **5 days or more after notice** to landlord.  
- **25 Del. C. § 7068:** Explicitly authorizes **Attorney General intervention** in any action under the receivership subchapter.  
- **10 Del. C. § 9507:** Requires JP Court to notify DOJ Consumer Protection **within 10 days** of receipt of tenants’ receivership petitions under **25 Del. C. § 5901 or § 7061**, with petition copy and case number; **no notice required** if AG filed the petition.

## Who Benefits
- **Tenants in manufactured home communities** affected by repeated habitability/health-and-safety related violations.  
- **Department of Justice / Consumer Protection Unit** gains clearer statutory hooks for enforcement and visibility into receivership filings.  
- Potentially, **tenant property and occupant safety interests** due to focus on conditions creating risks.

- DOJ/JP Court operations may experience increased workload (staffing and notice requirements), as reflected in the fiscal note costs.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Enforcement authority:** DOJ Consumer Protection Unit has duty to enforce Subchapters I–V of Chapter 70, Title 25 (25 Del. C. § 7005(a)).  
- **Civil actions:** Attorney General may commence a **civil action** when DOJ has reasonable cause to believe a landlord is engaged in a **pattern or practice** of violating or failing to comply with covered rental-agreement provisions (25 Del. C. § 7005(b)).  
- **Consumer Fraud Act linkage:** A qualifying “pattern or practice” is deemed an **unlawful practice** under **6 Del. C. § 2513** (25 Del. C. § 7005(c)). (Specific penalties/remedies under the Consumer Fraud Act are **not detailed** in the provided text.)  
- **Receivership process:** Tenants or the **Attorney General** may file for receivership in **Justice of the Peace Court** after **5+ days** following notice when specified dangerous conditions exist (25 Del. C. § 7061).  
- **Court notice requirement:** JP Court must notify DOJ Consumer Protection **within 10 days** of receiving certain receivership petitions, with specified contents (10 Del. C. § 9507).
