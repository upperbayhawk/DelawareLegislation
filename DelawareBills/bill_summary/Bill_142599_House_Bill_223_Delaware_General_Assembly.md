## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 16 of the Delaware Code to require the Delaware Department of Health and Social Services (the “Department”), working with the Department of Education, to develop or obtain and maintain informational materials about menstrual disorders (including endometriosis), make them available online, provide them to school districts upon request, and require schools to post access information (a URL) in certain school locations.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the Department, in conjunction with the Department of Education, to **develop or obtain** written materials on **menstrual disorders and their symptoms**.  
- Requires the Department to **make electronic copies available** on a Department-maintained website or provide **URLs/QR codes/links** to reputable materials.  
- Requires the materials and information to be **provided to school districts upon request**.  
- Requires schools to **post a poster containing the URL** to the Department website **in any bathroom that may be used by a menstruating student** and **in student health centers**.  
- Permits (but does not require) schools to **directly distribute** materials or provide **flyers with URLs/QR codes/links** to students.  
- Requires the Department to **review and validate** materials and links **at least every 2 years** for effectiveness, accuracy, and link validity.  
- Specifies minimum required content, including **timing/signs/symptoms**, **statistics**, **treatment options**, and **self-advocacy** best practices.  
- Effective **120 days after enactment into law** (no calendar date provided in the text).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved access to health information:** Required posting of a URL in specified school areas increases student awareness of where to find menstrual-disorder information (bathrooms used by menstruating students; student health centers).  
- **Up-to-date information requirement:** The Department must review materials and links **at least every 2 years**, supporting ongoing accuracy and functional access.  
- **Standardized baseline content:** Required topics (e.g., endometriosis signs/symptoms, basic statistics, treatment options, follow-up care recommendations) promote consistent informational coverage statewide.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative burden on schools:** Schools must create/post posters with the URL in covered bathrooms and student health centers (a mandatory compliance task).  
- **Ongoing administrative workload for the Department:** Biennial review requirement (“at least every 2 years”) creates recurring maintenance and validation duties for materials and electronic links.  
- **Potential uneven distribution to districts:** Materials are provided to school districts **only “upon request,”** which may lead to varying uptake across districts (the requirement to provide is triggered by a request).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- **Poster specifications:** The bill requires a “poster containing the URL” but does not specify size, format, language access, or durability requirements.  
- **Scope of “bathroom that may be used by a menstruating student”:** Not defined; may require interpretation for all-gender restrooms, nurse bathrooms, staff/student shared restrooms, etc.  
- **Enforcement mechanism:** The bill mandates posting but does not specify oversight, complaint process, or consequences for noncompliance.  
- **Website details:** The bill requires a website “maintained by the Department” but does not specify accessibility standards, update frequency beyond the 2-year review, or analytics/reporting.
