## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 19, § 205 (Apprenticeship and Training Fund) to change how money in the Apprenticeship and Training Fund is allocated and administered between the Department of Labor (DOL) and the Department of Education (DOE), including specifying percentage allocations and an administrative cost cap.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes statutory allocation rules for Apprenticeship and Training Fund dollars: **20%** (after administrative costs) to DOL’s Apprenticeship and Training Section and **80%** (after administrative costs) to DOE. (§ 205(c)(1)-(2))
- Limits administrative spending: **no more than 15%** of the money annually deposited into the Fund may be used for administration. (§ 205(c)(3))
- Requires the Department of Labor to allocate Fund money **at least annually**. (§ 205(c))
- Specifies allowable uses for DOL’s share: public awareness of registered apprenticeship/occupational training and support for pre-apprenticeship programs. (§ 205(c)(1))
- Specifies allowable uses for DOE’s share: related technical instruction for registered apprenticeships (including new instruction areas for in-demand crafts) and support for pre-apprenticeship programs. (§ 205(c)(2))
- Prohibits use of Fund money to **supplant existing state funding**. (§ 205(d))
- Allows reimbursement to the General Assembly for money appropriated to implement the section, notwithstanding the administrative cap provision. (§ 205(e))
- Fund sources remain: payments under **29 Del. C. § 6960A** and other appropriations/transfers by the General Assembly. (§ 205(b))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clear spending formula and administrative cap**: Sets explicit allocation percentages (**20%/80%**) and an admin limit (**≤15% annually deposited**) which may improve predictability and constrain overhead. (§ 205(c)(1)-(3))
- **Dedicated support for both training promotion and instruction**: Funds can be used for public awareness/promotion (DOL) and related technical instruction (DOE), and both can support pre-apprenticeship programs. (§ 205(c)(1)-(2))
- **Guardrail against replacing existing funding**: Prohibits using Fund money to supplant existing state funding. (§ 205(d))
- **Regular allocation requirement**: DOL must allocate funds **at least annually**, supporting recurring distribution rather than ad hoc spending. (§ 205(c))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential conflict with the bill synopsis**: The synopsis says the Act “removes the Department of Education from the process,” but the text allocates **80%** to DOE and assigns DOE spending responsibilities—this could create confusion about legislative intent and implementation. (Synopsis vs. § 205(c)(2))
- **Reduced flexibility in program funding**: Mandated **20%/80%** splits may limit the ability to redirect funds based on changing needs or performance, even if one side has higher demand. (§ 205(c)(1)-(2))
- **Administrative cap may constrain administration**: The **15%** cap on administration could restrict capacity for oversight, compliance, and reporting if administrative needs are higher. (§ 205(c)(3))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **DOE “removal” vs. statutory role**: The synopsis claims DOE is removed, but § 205(c)(2) assigns DOE an **80%** share and duties. It is unclear whether the synopsis is inaccurate or whether additional conforming changes are intended elsewhere. (Synopsis; § 205(c)(2))
