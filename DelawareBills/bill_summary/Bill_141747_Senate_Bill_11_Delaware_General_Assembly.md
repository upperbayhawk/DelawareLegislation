## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To amend Article I, § 12 of the Delaware Constitution to “modernize the bail provisions” by (1) retaining a general right to bail before conviction, but (2) authorizing denial of bail for capital offenses and for certain non-capital felony offenses that the General Assembly expressly designates by law, under specified evidentiary standards and supermajority voting requirements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Amends Delaware Constitution Article I, § 12 (Right to bail; access to accused) to allow bail to be withheld not only for capital offenses, but also for other felony offenses expressly prescribed by law.  
- Keeps capital-case standard: bail may be denied for capital offenses “when the proof is positive or the presumption great.”  
- For designated non-capital felonies, bail may be denied only if **(i)** “proof is positive or presumption great” **and (ii)** it is proven by **clear and convincing evidence** that **no** release conditions can reasonably assure: appearance in court, public/victim/witness safety, and integrity of the judicial process (no obstruction).  
- Requires a **two-thirds vote of all members elected to each House** to enact a law designating a felony offense as potentially non-bailable under this new constitutional authority.  
- Requires the same **two-thirds vote** to remove a felony’s “potentially not bailable” designation once enacted.  
- Clarifies access to confined persons before conviction: friends and counsel may have access “at proper seasons” **on reasonable conditions** when confined “on accusation for any offense.”  
- The synopsis states the constitutional change alone does not create non-capital preventive detention without implementing statutes; it references prior statutory revisions in **Chapter 21 of Title 11** enacted **September 30, 2024**, intended to take effect **6 months after enactment of this Act**.  
- Approved date in the provided law text: **July 1, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Enables preventive detention in limited non-capital felony cases** (if later designated by law and evidentiary standards met), addressing the synopsis-identified issue that courts currently cannot order preventive detention in non-capital cases.  
- **Adds defined evidentiary safeguards** for non-capital cases: requires “clear and convincing” proof that no conditions can assure appearance, safety, and judicial integrity (no obstruction).  
- **Supermajority check (two-thirds vote)** is required to add (and to remove) any non-capital felony offense to the potentially non-bailable list, raising the threshold for such expansions.  
- **Preserves general constitutional right to bail** (“All persons, before conviction, shall be bailable…”) except in the enumerated circumstances.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expands constitutional authority to deny bail beyond capital offenses** by allowing the General Assembly to create additional categories of potentially non-bailable felony offenses.  
- **Reduces reversibility of policy choices** by requiring a two-thirds vote not only to add, but also to **remove** a felony’s potentially non-bailable designation.  
- **Creates constitutional framework for detention based on predictions of risk** (appearance, danger, obstruction), which may increase the number of defendants held pretrial if implementing laws and procedures are used accordingly (the bill text authorizes this structure, though numbers are not specified).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Constitutional change to bail eligibility (Article I, § 12(a))**:
  - Maintains general right to bail before conviction, **except**:
    1) **Capital offenses** when “the proof is positive or the presumption great.”  
    2) **Other felony offenses expressly prescribed by the General Assembly** when:
       - (a) “proof is positive or the presumption great,” and  
       - (b) “proof is clear and convincing” that no condition(s) of release will:
         1. reasonably assure court appearance,  
         2. reasonably assure protection of the community/victims/witnesses/any person, and  
         3. reasonably maintain integrity of the judicial process (no obstruction/attempted obstruction), and  
       - (c) the designating law is enacted with **two-thirds concurrence** of all elected members of each House.  
- **Two-thirds vote requirement to remove designation (new § 12(b))**:
  - Removing a felony’s potentially non-bailable designation also requires **two-thirds concurrence** of all elected members of each House.  
- **Access to accused (Article I, § 12(c))**:
  - Applies access right when persons are confined before conviction on accusation for **any offense**; access by friends/counsel permitted “at proper seasons” on “reasonable conditions.”

## Who Benefits
- **Judicial officers and prosecutors**, to the extent the amendment enables (through subsequent/related statutes) detention without bail in certain non-capital felony cases under specified standards.  
- **Victims, witnesses, and the community**, where a court finds clear and convincing evidence that no release conditions can reasonably assure protection or prevent obstruction, in cases designated by law.  
- **Defendants generally**, to the extent the amendment preserves an express general right to bail except in defined circumstances and requires heightened proof for non-capital detention without bail.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Defendants charged with felony offenses designated by law as potentially non-bailable**, who may face pretrial detention without bail if the constitutional standards are met.  
- **Individuals seeking legislative rollback** of a non-bailable designation, because removal requires a **two-thirds vote** in each House.
