## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 9, Chapter 83 (§ 8304A) to define “county authority” and to expressly authorize counties’ finance/assessment authorities to issue and enforce subpoenas for testimony and documents in real property assessment disputes when income or cost comparison valuation approaches are used.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines “county authority” as the **Chief Financial Officer of the Office of Finance**, the **Director of the Department of Finance of Kent County**, or the **Finance Director of Sussex County**.  
- Grants boards of assessment and county finance offices the power to **subpoena witnesses and documents**, administer oaths/affirmations, and take evidence related to the **correctness of assessed value of real property**.  
- Limits the subpoena authority to situations where the county or an appealing property owner **relies on the income approach or cost comparison approach** to determine or contest assessed value.  
- Provides that subpoenas issued under § 8304A are **effective throughout Delaware** and **may be enforced in other states or countries** through available legal means.  
- Specifies permissible **methods of service** for subpoenas (personal service, abode service, or certified mail for individuals; service on registered agent or authorized person for entities).  
- Allows the **Superior Court**, upon application by the county authority, to issue an order compelling compliance; **failure to obey may be punished as contempt of court**.  
- Treats a Delaware corporation or other Delaware business entity’s refusal to obey a Superior Court subpoena-enforcement order as an **abuse/misuse of corporate powers under 8 Del. C. § 284**, supporting possible charter-revocation proceedings by the Attorney General.  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves access to valuation evidence**: Counties (and boards of assessment) can compel production of records and testimony “bearing upon the correctness” of assessed value when income or cost comparison methods are used (§ 8304A(b)).  
- **Strengthens enforceability**: Provides a clear enforcement path through **Superior Court orders** and **contempt powers** (§ 8304A(e)-(f)).  
- **Clarifies who may act**: Defining “county authority” identifies the specific county finance officials authorized to serve subpoenas and seek court enforcement (§ 8304A(a), (d), (e)-(f)).  
- **Potentially increases compliance by business entities**: Noncompliance by Delaware entities can be treated as abuse/misuse of corporate powers under **Title 8 § 284** (§ 8304A(f); Synopsis), raising stakes for refusal to comply.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expanded compelled disclosure**: Property owners and third parties may be required to produce “books, papers, records, memoranda, or other evidence” and provide testimony in assessment disputes involving income/cost approaches (§ 8304A(b)).  
- **Higher compliance burden for subpoena recipients**: Responding to subpoenas and court orders can create administrative and legal costs (implicit in subpoena/court process; § 8304A(e)-(f)).  
- **Significant consequences for Delaware entities**: A Delaware corporation or other Delaware business entity that refuses to obey a court order may face consequences tied to **abuse/misuse of corporate powers** under **8 Del. C. § 284** (§ 8304A(f)), potentially escalating beyond the underlying tax assessment dispute.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “income or cost comparison approaches”**: The bill does not define these approaches or specify which documents are presumptively relevant beyond “bearing upon the correctness” of assessed value (§ 8304A(b)).  
- **Process details**: The bill specifies service methods and court enforcement but does not detail timelines for response, objections, or motions to quash (not specified in § 8304A text).  
- **Out-of-state/country enforcement mechanics**: The bill states subpoenas “may be enforced in other states or countries through available legal means” without specifying procedures (§ 8304A(c)).  
- **Interaction with confidentiality protections**: The bill does not address whether or how confidential business/financial information is protected once produced (not specified in source text).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Defines “county authority”** for § 8304A purposes as specific county finance officials (§ 8304A(a)).  
- **Expressly authorizes subpoena power** (witnesses, testimony, oaths/affirmations, evidence collection, and document production) tied to real property assessed value disputes when **income** or **cost comparison** valuation approaches are relied upon (§ 8304A(b)).  
- **Statewide effectiveness and cross-border enforcement** of subpoenas (§ 8304A(c)).  
- **Codifies service methods** for individuals and entities, including **certified mail** options (§ 8304A(d)).  
- **Creates Superior Court enforcement mechanism** and use of **contempt** for failure to obey court orders enforcing subpoenas (§ 8304A(e)-(f)).  
- **Links Delaware entity noncompliance** with a subpoena-enforcement order to **abuse/misuse of corporate powers** under **8 Del. C. § 284** (§ 8304A(f); Synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- **County finance/assessment authorities and boards of assessment** (ability to compel evidence and testimony and seek court enforcement) (§ 8304A(b), (e)-(f)).  
- **Counties defending assessments** that rely on income or cost comparison approaches (explicitly authorized to compel evidence to support those approaches) (Synopsis; § 8304A(b)).  
- **Appealing property owners** when they rely on income or cost comparison approaches and need evidence supporting their valuation position (§ 8304A(b) includes when “an appealing property owner relies upon” these approaches).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Subpoena recipients** (individuals and entities) who must produce testimony and documents or face court enforcement and contempt risk (§ 8304A(b), (e)-(f)).  
- **Delaware corporations/other Delaware business entities** that refuse to obey a Superior Court enforcement order, because the refusal is treated as abuse/misuse of corporate powers under **8 Del. C. § 284** (§ 8304A(f)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirement**: Properly served subpoena recipients must respond by appearing and/or producing requested evidence (§ 8304A(b), (e)).  
- **Court enforcement**: On application by the county authority, **Superior Court may issue an order** requiring appearance and/or production (§ 8304A(e)-(f)).  
- **Penalty**: **Failure to obey** the Superior Court order “may be punished by the court as a **contempt**” (§ 8304A(e)); refusal to obey makes the person “guilty of **contempt of court**” (§ 8304A(f)).  
- **Additional corporate consequence**: If a person that is a **Delaware corporation or other Delaware business entity** refuses to obey such an order, it constitutes **abuse and misuse of corporate powers under 8 Del. C. § 284** (§ 8304A(f)), which the Synopsis states is sufficient to permit the Attorney General to commence proceedings to revoke a corporate charter pursuant to Title 8.
