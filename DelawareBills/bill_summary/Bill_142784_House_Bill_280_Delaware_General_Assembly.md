## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Allow Delaware residents to voluntarily add a “nonapparent disability” designation (described in the synopsis as a butterfly symbol and “hidden disability” wording) on driver’s licenses and ID cards, and require police training on the notation and best practices for interacting with people with disabilities, including developmental and intellectual disabilities.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV) to provide a method for applicants/renewals to designate a **nonapparent disability** on a Delaware driver’s license or identification card.  
- DMV must place a **notation** on the license/ID indicating the person has a disability that **may require special attention**, using a manner prescribed by DMV.  
- The designation is **voluntary** and can be **removed only upon written notice** to DMV (the applicant must be informed of this at the time they authorize the designation).  
- The designation **cannot be placed on a person under 18** without **written parent/guardian permission**.  
- “License” for this purpose **does not include a temporary license**.  
- Police officer training must include instruction about (1) interactions with minors with disabilities, (2) the **driver’s license notation**, and (3) best practices for **traffic stops** involving people with disabilities (including developmental and intellectual disabilities).  
- Upon enactment, DMV must **consult advocacy groups** on notation design and conduct **public outreach** about availability.  
- **Effective immediately**; must be **implemented within 6 months** of the effective date.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved communication during traffic stops and other encounters:** The bill creates a standardized, DMV-issued notation indicating a disability “may require special attention,” potentially helping individuals convey needs without verbal explanation.  
- **Training requirement ties notation to police practice:** Mandatory training must cover the notation and best practices for safe interactions during traffic stops involving a person with a disability (including developmental and intellectual disabilities).  
- **Parental safeguard for minors:** Written parent/guardian permission is required for individuals **under 18** before the designation can appear.  
- **Stakeholder-informed design and public awareness:** DMV must consult developmental and intellectual disability advocacy groups on the design and conduct public outreach.
