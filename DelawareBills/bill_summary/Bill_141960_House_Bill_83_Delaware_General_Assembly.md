## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14, §1048 of the Delaware Code to require school boards to permit remote (electronic) attendance by school board members in specified circumstances, and to clarify quorum/voting and minimum technology requirements for such participation.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires a school board to allow a member to attend a regular or special meeting by electronic means if the member is unable to attend due to specified reasons: illness, caregiving for ill family member, public health emergency, pregnancy/immediate postpartum responsibilities, or military deployment.  
- Provides that a member attending remotely under these circumstances counts as physically present for **quorum and voting** purposes.  
- Sets a functional standard for the remote participation method: it must allow all participating members and witnesses to **simultaneously hear** each member’s or witness’s comments.  
- Applies to **regular and special** school board meetings.  
- **Effective date:** **30 days after enactment into law**.  
- Requires the **Department of Education**, upon enactment, to notify each school board president and each school district superintendent of the Act’s requirements and effective date.  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves continuity of governance:** Members who are ill, caregiving, pregnant/postpartum, affected by a public health emergency, or deployed can still participate and vote, supporting board operations and decision-making (Section 1, §1048(f)(1)-(2)).  
- **Reduces quorum disruptions:** Remote attendees count toward quorum, which may reduce canceled or postponed meetings (Section 1, §1048(f)(2)).  
- **Supports accessibility for members with temporary constraints:** Explicitly includes pregnancy and immediate post-partum care responsibilities, as well as military deployment (Section 1, §1048(f)(1)d-e).  

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Technology/meeting management burden:** Boards must use electronic means that allow all participating members and witnesses to simultaneously hear comments, which may require reliable equipment and procedures (Section 1, §1048(f)(3)).  
- **Potential inconsistency in implementation:** The bill sets a hearing standard but does not specify platforms, security, or procedures, which could lead to differing practices across districts (Section 1, §1048(f)(3)).  

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Proof/verification:** The bill does not specify whether or how a member must document they are “unable to attend” for a listed reason (Section 1, §1048(f)(1)).  
- **Scope of “electronic means”:** Not defined (e.g., phone vs. video), beyond the requirement that participants can simultaneously hear comments (Section 1, §1048(f)(3)).  
- **Public access/FOIA alignment:** The bill does not address whether or how the public observes/participates when a member attends remotely, beyond witness/audio audibility (Section 1, §1048(f)(3)).  
- **Limits on frequency/number of remote attendees:** No caps or additional constraints are stated (Section 1, §1048(f)).

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates a mandatory right for a school board member to attend meetings remotely when unable to attend due to specified health- or deployment-related reasons (Section 1, §1048(f)(1)).  
- Treats qualifying remote attendance as physical presence for **quorum and voting** (Section 1, §1048(f)(2)).  
- Requires remote participation technology/methods to allow simultaneous hearing of member and witness comments (Section 1, §1048(f)(3)).  
- Requires DOE to provide notice to board presidents and superintendents upon enactment (Section 3).
