## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s State Judiciary pension statutes to (1) preserve the federal qualified tax status of the Delaware Public Employees Retirement System (as described in the bill synopsis) by aligning certain pre-tax contribution rates, (2) set a 5% mandatory pre-tax contribution rate for certain judicial officers newly covered by the revised plan, and (3) delay the effective date of Chapter 446, Volume 84 (SS 1 to SB 174) to a later date.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Increases the employee contribution rate (on compensation above $6,000 up to the Social Security wage base) from **4% to 5%** for members **first covered by the revised plan on or after January 1, 2025**, with a specific exception for certain individuals covered via **§ 5605(e)(2)b.**  
- Keeps the existing revised-plan contribution structure for members covered **before January 1, 2025**: **3%** (over **$6,000** up to the Social Security wage base) and **5%** (over the Social Security wage base).  
- Allows **Justices of the Peace, Commissioners, and Magistrates in Chancery** who **first qualify** for the State Judicial Pension Plan on **January 1, 2025** to **decline** and remain in the **State Employees’ Pension Plan** (constitutional election referenced).  
- Requires those declining participation to do so **in writing to the Board by January 31, 2025**.  
- If such an official joins the Judicial Pension Plan (or does not timely decline), they contribute at the **same pre-tax rate** they previously paid under the State Employees’ Pension Plan (**3% or 5%**, depending on start date), for the first **24 years** after joining.  
- If a person first covered through § 5605(e)(2)b. is later appointed to a different state judiciary position, future contributions become **5%** (over **$6,000** up to the Social Security wage base) and **5%** (over the wage base) for the remaining portion of the **24-year** period.  
- Provides certain officials electing Judicial Pension Plan coverage an accrued annual leave payout in **incremental payments over 5 years**, with the **first payout completed by January 1, 2026**; remaining unpaid amounts must be paid upon termination.  
- Delays the effective date of Chapter 446, Volume 84, Section 11 from **January 1, 2025** to **February 9, 2025**; this Act is approved **February 7, 2025** and takes effect **February 9, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces federal tax qualification risk (as stated in synopsis):** Addresses the identified issue that differing pre-tax contribution rates could “jeopardize the qualified federal tax status” of the retirement system by aligning certain individuals’ rates to their prior State Employees’ Pension Plan rates (**3% or 5%**).  
- **Clearer, uniform rate for new coverage:** Sets a **mandatory 5%** (instead of 4%) rate for members **first covered on/after January 1, 2025** (except specified cases), simplifying contribution expectations.  
- **Provides an opt-out path for certain officials:** Allows specified officials newly qualifying on **January 1, 2025** to remain in the State Employees’ Pension Plan if they act by **January 31, 2025**.  
- **Defined accrued leave payout schedule:** Establishes a **5-year** incremental payout approach and requires payment of any unpaid balance upon termination.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher employee contributions for some new members:** Changes the rate on compensation above **$6,000** up to the Social Security wage base from **4% to 5%** for members first covered on/after **January 1, 2025** (with the stated exception), reducing take-home pay for those affected.  
- **Administrative deadline burden:** Requires a written decline election **by January 31, 2025**, which may create compliance pressure and potential consequences for missing the deadline.  
- **Irrevocability:** The election to decline participation is **irrevocable**, and acceptance/authorization elections are also **irrevocable**, limiting future flexibility.

## Key Policy Changes
- **29 Del. C. § 5601(a)(1)c:** For members first covered by the revised plan on/after **January 1, 2025**, changes the contribution rate on compensation above **$6,000** up to the Social Security wage base from **4% to 5%**; maintains **5%** on compensation above the Social Security wage base.  
- **29 Del. C. § 5605(e)(1)-(3):** For **Justices of the Peace, Commissioners, and Magistrates in Chancery** first qualifying on **January 1, 2025**, permits declining Judicial Pension Plan participation and remaining in the State Employees’ Pension Plan; decline must be **in writing to the Board by January 31, 2025**; election is **irrevocable**.  
- **29 Del. C. § 5605(e)(2)b:** If such an official participates (or does not decline), they contribute for the first **24 years** after joining at the **same rate** as under their prior State Employees’ Pension Plan participation (referenced as **3% or 5%** in the synopsis).  
- **29 Del. C. § 5605(e)(2)c:** If later appointed to a different state judiciary position, contributions become **5%** (over **$6,000** up to the Social Security wage base) and **5%** (over the wage base) for the remainder of the **24-year** period.  
- **29 Del. C. § 5605(e)(4):** Provides a **5-year** incremental payout for accrued annual leave for specified officials electing Judicial Plan coverage, with the **first payout completed by January 1, 2026**; unpaid balance due upon termination.  
- **Chapter 446, Volume 84, Section 11 (amended):** Changes that Act’s effective date from **January 1, 2025** to **February 9, 2025**.  
- **SB 42 effective date:** This Act takes effect **February 9, 2025** (approved **February 7, 2025**).

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware pension systems/administrators:** Benefit from statutory changes intended (per synopsis) to avoid jeopardizing federal qualified tax status by preventing mismatched pre-tax contribution rates for affected electing officials.  
- **Justices of the Peace, Commissioners, and Magistrates in Chancery qualifying on January 1, 2025:** Gain an explicit option to remain in the State Employees’ Pension Plan if they submit a timely written decline.  
- **Those electing Judicial Plan coverage with accrued annual leave:** Gain a defined payout structure over **5 years**, with termination protection for any unpaid balance.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Newly covered revised-plan members on/after January 1, 2025 (not within the § 5605(e)(2)b exception):** Face an increased contribution rate from **4% to 5%** on compensation above **$6,000** up to the Social Security wage base.  
- **Officials who miss the January 31, 2025 deadline:** Could lose the ability to decline participation and remain in the State Employees’ Pension Plan (deadline-driven outcome), since the election right is time-limited and irrevocable once exercised.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Written election requirement:** To decline Judicial Pension Plan participation, covered individuals must provide a **written** notice to the **Board** by **January 31, 2025**.  
- **Irrevocability:**  
  - A declaration of acceptance/authorization of deductions is **irrevocable** (§ 5605(d)).  
  - The decision to decline participation under § 5605(e)(1) is **irrevocable** (§ 5605(e)(3)).  
- **Payroll contribution compliance:** Members must contribute at the specified rates for the first **24 years** (or $500/year for 25 years under the closed plan), with contributions applying to compensation above **$6,000** and tied to the Social Security wage base thresholds.  
- **Accrued leave payout compliance:** Agencies must complete the first incremental payout by **January 1, 2026** and pay any outstanding balance upon termination.
