## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 26 of the Delaware Code to apply a “prudence” standard (instead of a “business judgment rule” approach) in Public Service Commission (PSC) utility rate proceedings, clarifying that utilities must prove costs were prudently incurred and allowing the PSC to disallow imprudent costs (in whole or in part) when setting “just and reasonable, and prudent” rates.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes utility ratemaking standards to require recovery only of costs or expenses “prudently incurred,” and explicitly places the burden on the public utility to prove prudence in rate cases.  
- Defines prudence review as an “objective reasonableness” test based on what the utility “knew or reasonably should have known” when the cost was incurred.  
- Authorizes the PSC to find a cost “only partially prudent” and adjust rate base, operating expenses, or fuel costs accordingly.  
- Updates multiple provisions (e.g., §§ 307, 309, 311, and § 201(e)) to add “imprudent” as a basis for PSC action and to embed “and prudent” into the “just and reasonable” standard.  
- Retains a water-utility facility cost recovery framework tied to a **3-year** customer anticipation period and a **75%** customer-addition threshold before revenue imputation may occur.  
- Keeps requirement that PSC-ordered rate changes generally do not become operative until at least **30 days** after service of the order (unless the utility consents in writing).  
- Effective **January 1, 2026** following enactment; the act was **approved July 16, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger cost-control tool for regulators:** The PSC may deny recovery of imprudent costs (including partially), because § 307(a) requires recovery of “only those costs or expenses prudently incurred” and allows partial prudence adjustments.  
- **Clearer evidentiary standard:** Prudence is tied to “objective reasonableness” based on information available at the time the cost was incurred (§ 307(a)), potentially improving consistency of decisions.  
- **Potential downward pressure on rates:** The synopsis states that more costs in rate base can increase customer rates and that a prudence standard can lead to “less frequent and less impactful rate increases.”  
- **Explicit authority for PSC action on imprudence:** Adds “imprudent” to statutes governing PSC-initiated rate changes and determinations (§§ 309, 311), broadening explicit grounds for intervention.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased regulatory burden on utilities:** Utilities must prove rates and underlying costs are “just and reasonable, and prudent,” and must justify questioned accounting entries (§ 307(a)–(b)).  
- **Greater risk of cost disallowances:** The PSC may find costs partially prudent and reduce recovery accordingly (§ 307(a)), increasing uncertainty about cost recovery.  
- **Potential for more contested proceedings:** Because prudence and “objective reasonableness” determinations may be disputed, proceedings could become more complex (the text requires speed but does not set deadlines beyond prioritization).  
- **Water-utility provision may still allow advance cost recovery for anticipated growth:** Water utilities may recover prudent construction costs for customers “reasonably anticipated…within 3 years,” with imputation only if < **75%** of anticipated customers materialize (§ 302).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope across utility types:** The act updates general PSC ratemaking standards, but the interaction with other utility-specific statutes and practices is not detailed.  
- **Application to already-incurred vs. future costs:** The bill specifies prudence is judged based on what was known “at the time” the cost was incurred (§ 307(a)), but does not specify transition rules for pending cases on the effective date.  
- **How “partially prudent” will be quantified:** The bill authorizes partial prudence findings (§ 307(a)) but does not specify a methodology.  
- **Water-utility review carve-out:** § 302 conditions apply only if the water utility is not under certain Title 16 reviews; the bill does not elaborate on how those reviews are initiated, timed, or overlap with rate cases.

## Key Policy Changes
- **§ 307(a): Burden of proof + prudence requirement**
  - Utility must prove a rate is “just and reasonable, and prudent,” meaning it allows recovery of “only those costs or expenses prudently incurred.”
  - Prudence test: “objective reasonableness…based on what the public utility knew or reasonably should have known” when costs were incurred.
  - PSC may find costs “only partially prudent” and adjust rate base, operating expenses, or fuel costs.
- **§ 307(b): Accounting entries**
  - Utility bears burden to justify questioned accounting entries; PSC may suspend a charge/credit pending “satisfactory and sufficient proof.”
- **§ 309(a) and § 311: Adds ‘imprudent’**
  - PSC may act when existing rates are “unjust, unreasonable, imprudent…” (§ 309(a)); and may reject existing/proposed rates that are “unjust, unreasonable, imprudent…” (§ 311).
- **§ 201(e)(1): Alternative regulation authority**
  - PSC may alter regulation to promote adequate service at “just and reasonable, and prudent rates” and may depart from rate base/rate of return regulation when in the public interest and promoting such rates.
- **§ 302: Water utility facility cost recovery framework retained but keyed to prudence**
  - Allows inclusion in rate base and full recovery (without revenue imputation) of “just and reasonable, and prudent costs” for facilities serving existing customers or those anticipated within **3 years**, with a **75%** threshold before imputation may occur.

## Who Benefits
- **Utility customers (electric, gas, water, etc.):** The prudence standard allows PSC to disallow imprudent costs, which the synopsis links to potentially less frequent/less impactful rate increases.  
- **PSC/regulators:** Clear statutory authority to apply prudence review, including partial disallowance, and to label rates “imprudent” as a basis for action.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Public utilities:** Greater risk that some costs (including portions of costs) will be found imprudent and not fully recoverable through rates; higher evidentiary burden in rate proceedings (§ 307).  
- **Water utilities with growth projections that do not materialize:** Potential revenue imputation if they fail to add at least **75%** of anticipated customers within **3 years** (§ 302).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** **January 1, 2026** following enactment into law (Section 3).  
- **Approval date (as enacted):** **July 16, 2025**.  
- **Water utility customer projection window:** Customers anticipated within **3 years** from the date used by the PSC to recognize rate base in the rate proceeding (§ 302).  
- **Water utility performance check:** End of the **3-year** period triggers the **75%** threshold determination and potential revenue imputation (§ 302).  
- **PSC rate-change orders:** Generally operative no earlier than **30 days** after service of the order unless the utility consents in writing (§ 309(b)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance in rate cases:** Utilities must affirmatively prove rates are “just and reasonable, and prudent” and that costs were prudently incurred (§ 307(a)).  
- **Accounting support requirement:** Utilities must justify questioned accounting entries; PSC may suspend charges/credits until “satisfactory and sufficient proof” is provided (§ 307(b)).  
- **Revenue imputation (water utilities):** If < **75%** of anticipated customers are added by the end of **3 years**, PSC may require the utility to impute revenues, limited to the unadded portion of the originally anticipated customers (§ 302).
