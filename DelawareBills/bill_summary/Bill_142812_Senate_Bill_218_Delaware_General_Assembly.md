## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Adopts the **Interstate Massage Compact** in Delaware to **facilitate interstate practice and regulation of massage therapy** through a **multistate licensing program**, aiming to **improve public access** and **enhance public safety**. (24 Del. C. §§ 5331, 5342; Section 2)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Delaware would join the **Interstate Massage Compact**, enabling eligible massage therapists to obtain a **multistate license** authorizing practice in **all member states**. (§§ 5331, 5334, 5333(d))
- To participate, a state must, among other things, **license and regulate massage therapy**, accept a **national licensing exam**, require **education**, require **FBI fingerprint/biometric-based background checks**, have **continuing competence** requirements, and participate in a shared **data system**. (§ 5333(a))
- Multistate license eligibility includes: **active home-state single-state license**, **≥ 625 clock hours** education (or equivalent by rule), **national exam** (or equivalent by rule), and a **background check**, plus criminal/disciplinary history limits (e.g., certain felonies within **5 years**, certain misdemeanors within **2 years**, and specified offenses at **any time**). (§ 5334(a))
- A new **Interstate Massage Compact Commission** is created with **1 delegate per member state**, must meet **at least annually**, and can **adopt rules with the force of law**, assess **fees**, and maintain a **coordinated database**. (§§ 5338, 5339, 5340)
- **Discipline is shared across states**: home states control adverse actions against the multistate license; remote states can restrict the authorization to practice in their state and issue subpoenas, cease-and-desist orders, and recover investigation costs where state law allows. (§ 5336)
- If a home state takes adverse action on the multistate license or home-state license, the licensee’s authorization to practice in **all other member states is deactivated** until encumbrances are removed. (§ 5336(f))
- Compact becomes operational when enacted by the **7th state**; Delaware’s act takes effect only after a **Secretary of State notice** is published in the **Register of Regulations** that **≥ 7 states** have adopted the Compact. (§ 5342(a); Section 2)
- Withdrawal/termination has a built-in transition: a withdrawing/terminated state must continue recognizing compact licenses for **at least 180 days after notice**. (§ 5342(b); § 5341(f))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Increased license mobility and interstate practice**: a multistate license authorizes practice in all member states. (§ 5331(b)(1), (9); § 5333(d))
- **Potentially improved public protection tools** through shared investigative and disciplinary data and coordinated reporting. (§ 5331(b)(2), (6); § 5339; § 5336)
- **Human trafficking and fraud prevention emphasis** via eligibility restrictions and background check requirements including FBI fingerprint/biometric submissions. (§ 5331(b)(3); § 5333(a)(5); § 5334(a)(4), (7))
- **Support for military families** by allowing active-duty members/spouses to designate and retain a home state during active-duty assignment. (§ 5337)
- **Clear cross-state enforcement mechanisms**, including remote state authority to issue subpoenas that must be enforced in another member state’s courts. (§ 5336(e)(3))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Additional compliance and administrative workload** for Delaware’s licensing authority to meet member-state duties (data submissions; monitoring; notifications of adverse actions and investigative information). (§ 5333(a)(7)-(9); § 5339(c), (f))
- **Costs/fees are authorized**: the Commission may levy **annual assessments on member states** and **fees on licensees** to fund operations (amounts set by Commission budget/rule). (§ 5338(g)(3); § 5338(c)(12))
- **Potential practice limitations for some applicants** due to strict eligibility screens (e.g., certain felonies within **5 years**; certain misdemeanors within **2 years**; specified offenses at **any time**; adverse action history within **2 years**; prior revocation/surrender). (§ 5334(a)(5)-(9))
- **Rulemaking shifts to the Commission**: Commission rules have the **force of law** in member states (subject to scope-of-practice conflict limits), and member state rulemaking requirements do not apply to Commission rules. (§ 5340(b), (n))

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new **Subchapter III, “Interstate Massage Compact”** to Title 24, Chapter 53, establishing Delaware’s participation in the Compact structure. (§ 5330 et seq.)
- Creates a pathway for Delaware licensees (residents of a member state) to obtain a **multistate license** with authorizations to practice in remote member states. (§ 5334; § 5333(d))
- Establishes eligibility thresholds for multistate licensure including:
  - **625 clock hours** education minimum. (§ 5334(a)(2))
  - National exam requirement. (§ 5334(a)(3))
  - Background check requirement. (§ 5334(a)(4))
  - Criminal and disciplinary history exclusions: felony within **5 years**; practice-related misdemeanor within **2 years**; certain offenses **at any time**; adverse action history within **2 years**; no prior revocation/surrender in lieu of discipline. (§ 5334(a)(5)-(9))
- Creates the **Interstate Massage Compact Commission** with rulemaking authority, budgeting, fee assessment, data system administration, and enforcement/oversight mechanisms. (§§ 5338-5341)
- Requires cross-state data sharing and reporting, including investigative and disciplinary information, via a shared **data system** with unique identifiers. (§ 5339)

## Who Benefits
- **Licensed massage therapists** in Delaware (once Delaware and at least 6 other states are members) who seek to practice across state lines via a **multistate license**. (§ 5331(b)(1), (9); § 5334)
- **Active military members and spouses** who can retain a home state designation during active-duty assignments. (§ 5337)
- **Consumers/public** through stated aims of increased access and enhanced safety via coordinated oversight, background checks, and information-sharing. (§ 5331; § 5339)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Applicants/licensees with disqualifying criminal or disciplinary histories (e.g., felony within **5 years**; practice-related misdemeanor within **2 years**; specified offenses at any time; adverse action history within **2 years**; prior revocation/surrender). (§ 5334(a)(5)-(9))
- Delaware’s licensing authority and related administrative systems due to required **data reporting/monitoring** and other member-state obligations. (§ 5333(a); § 5339)
- Licensees and/or Delaware may face **fees/assessments** needed to fund the Commission (amounts unspecified). (§ 5338(g)(3))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Delaware act effective date:** Takes effect **on the date of publication in the Register of Regulations** of a notice by the **Secretary of State** that the Compact has been adopted by **at least 7 states**. (Section 2)
- **Compact effective date generally:** Compact “comes into effect” on enactment into law in the **7th member state**. (§ 5342(a)(1))
- **Delegate vacancy deadline:** Member state licensing authority must fill Commission delegate vacancy within **60 days**. (§ 5338(b)(5))
- **Commission meetings:** Commission must meet **at least once each calendar year**. (§ 5338(b)(7))
- **Public meeting notice:** Regular public Commission meeting notice at least **30 days** prior; emergency public meeting notice at least **24 hours** prior. (§ 5338(f)(1)-(2))
- **Executive Committee public meeting notice:** **5 business days** advance notice. (§ 5338(d)(4)(b))
- **Rule effective date floor:** Except emergencies, rule effective date cannot be sooner than **30 days** after notice of adoption/amendment. (§ 5340(k)(3))
- **Emergency rule retroactive process deadline:** Usual rulemaking procedures must be applied as soon as possible, but **not later than 90 days** after emergency rule effective date. (§ 5340(l))
- **Rule rejection window:** Member state legislatures can reject a rule within **4 years** of rule adoption (by majority of legislatures), after which it has no further force/effect. (§ 5340(d))
- **Withdrawal effective date:** Withdrawal takes effect **180 days** after enactment of a repealing statute. (§ 5342(b)(2))
- **Post-withdrawal recognition:** Withdrawing state must continue recognizing Compact licenses for **minimum 180 days after notice of withdrawal**. (§ 5342(b)(4))
- **Post-termination recognition:** Terminated state must continue recognizing Compact licenses for **minimum 180 days after notice of termination**. (§ 5341(f))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Member-state compliance requirements** include licensing regulation, complaint/investigation mechanisms, national exam acceptance, education and continuing competence, FBI fingerprint/biometric background checks for multistate applicants, participation in the data system, and reporting adverse actions/investigative information. (§ 5333(a))
- **Disciplinary authority structure:**
  - Home state has exclusive authority to impose adverse action against the **multistate license**. (§ 5336(a))
  - Remote states may take adverse action against authorization to practice, issue **cease and desist orders**, impose encumbrances, issue and enforce **subpoenas across states**, and (if allowed by state law) recover investigation/disposition costs from the licensee. (§ 5336(e))
  - Home state adverse action deactivates authorizations in **all** member states until encumbrances removed. (§ 5336(f))
  - Remote state adverse action applies across **all remote states**; ineligibility for a new multistate license until removal period passes and encumbrances satisfied. (§ 5336(g))
- **Compact enforcement against states:** The Commission may issue notices of default, offer technical assistance, and ultimately seek suspension/termination by majority vote; Commission may sue a defaulting member state for injunctive relief/damages; prevailing party awarded costs and reasonable attorney’s fees. (§ 5341(b)-(j))
- **Limits on private enforcement:** An individual or entity other than a member state may not enforce the Compact against the Commission. (§ 5341(j)(4))
