## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 24, Chapter 17 of the Delaware Code to require **written parental consent** before an abortion may be performed on an **unemancipated minor under age 16**, with exceptions for **medical emergencies** and for minors who obtain a **Family Court waiver**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines “minor” as a **female person under the age of 16** for purposes of this subchapter.  
- Prohibits a physician or other medically authorized person from performing an abortion on an **unemancipated minor** unless **written consent** is obtained **at least 24 hours before** the abortion.  
- Expands/identifies who may provide consent/receive notice: **one or both parents (custodial or noncustodial), a grandparent, the legal guardian, or a licensed mental health professional** meeting specified independence criteria.  
- Requires a **consent form** containing the minor’s name, consenting adult’s name/signature, physician’s name/signature, and a statement of understanding and voluntary consent.  
- Allows a minor to petition **Family Court in any county** for a waiver when consent is **not possible or inappropriate** due to **abuse, neglect, or coercion**, or if the minor is **mature and well-informed**.  
- Sets a court decision timeline: if Family Court does not rule within **5 calendar days** of filing, the waiver is **deemed granted** and an order must issue on the **6th day**.  
- Provides an expedited appeal: notice of intent to appeal must be given within **2 days** after actual notice of denial; Supreme Court must offer **court-appointed counsel at no cost** upon request.  
- Effective **30 days after enactment**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clear consent and timing requirement:** Requires **written consent** obtained **≥ 24 hours** before the procedure, creating a defined compliance standard for providers.  
- **Judicial bypass with deadlines:** Provides a waiver process with a firm timeline (**5 calendar days**, then **deemed granted**), potentially reducing delays when waiver is appropriate.  
- **No-cost access to the courts:** Prohibits courts from assessing **any fee or cost** to the minor for waiver proceedings; provides **free court-appointed counsel** on Supreme Court appeal upon request.  
- **Confidentiality requirement:** Directs courts to provide by rule for **confidentiality** of proceedings under the subchapter.  
- **Addresses safety exceptions:** Waiver criteria explicitly include situations where consent is not in the minor’s best interest, including **abuse, neglect, or coercion**, and the law retains a **medical emergency** concept.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Access barrier for minors under 16:** The bill requires written consent **≥ 24 hours** prior to abortion unless an exception applies, which may add steps and time for minors seeking care (requirement is explicit in §1783).  
- **Additional administrative steps for providers:** Providers must ensure the **consent form** includes specified elements and confirm the **24-hour** timing requirement is met.  
- **Court involvement may be necessary:** Minors unable to obtain consent must pursue a **Family Court petition** and potentially an **expedited appeal**, adding procedural complexity (even though fees are barred).  
- **Broader set of potential consent-givers:** Allowing consent/notice via individuals beyond parents/guardian (e.g., **grandparent** or **licensed mental health professional**) may create implementation and oversight complexity (as laid out in §1783(1)).

## Health Impact
- Establishes a **consent/waiver framework** governing abortion access for **unemancipated minors under 16**, including a **medical emergency** definition and a waiver option when consent is not in the minor’s best interest (including **abuse, neglect, or coercion**) or when the minor is **mature and well-informed**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Age threshold:** Sets “minor” as **female under age 16** (§1782(6)).  
- **Consent requirement:** Prohibits abortion for an unemancipated minor without obtaining required **written consent** **at least 24 hours before** the abortion (§1783(1)).  
- **Authorized consent/notice recipients:** Permits consent/notice through **parents (custodial or noncustodial), grandparent, legal guardian, or qualifying licensed mental health professional** (§1783(1)).  
- **No referral fees:** Bars abortion providers from charging a **referral fee** to authorized consent recipients and bars those recipients from charging a referral fee to providers (§1783(1)a).  
- **Mandatory consent form contents:** Specifies required elements of the **consent form** (§1783(2)(a)-(d)).  
- **Waiver process:** Allows Family Court waiver petitions when consent is not possible or inappropriate due to **abuse/neglect**, and sets waiver standards (**clear and convincing evidence** of maturity/well-informed or best-interest findings including abuse/neglect/coercion) (§1784).  
- **Court deadlines and deemed grant:** If no ruling within **5 calendar days**, waiver is **deemed granted**; order must be issued on **day 6** (§1784).  
- **Expedited appeal:** Appeal notice due within **2 days** after actual notice of denial; **court-appointed counsel** available upon request at **no cost** (§1784).  
- **No fees/costs for minors:** Courts may not assess **any fee or cost** for these proceedings (§1784).  
- **Confidentiality:** Courts must provide by rule for **confidentiality** (§1784).

## Who Benefits

- **Minors under 16** who cannot safely obtain parental consent may benefit from a **waiver pathway** based on maturity or best interest (including **abuse, neglect, coercion**) with **no court fees** and time-bound decisions.  
- **Providers** may benefit from more explicit statutory procedures (form elements, timing, and waiver documentation).
