## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Create a statutory process in Delaware for former defendants in summary possession (eviction) actions to seek a court order vacating the judgment and expunging the court record of the eviction case under specified eligibility criteria.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Allows a defendant in a summary possession action to apply to the same court for an order **vacating the judgment and expunging the record** of the eviction case.  
- The court **must grant** the motion if it finds **any one** of seven listed conditions (including a **5+ year** waiting period for certain judgments and payment of any monetary award).  
- If eligible, the court **must issue an order** setting aside the original judgment and expunging the action’s record.  
- After expungement, the action is **“deemed not to have been filed,”** and the defendant may answer questions accordingly.  
- Within **45 days** after the expungement order, the court must provide a **certified copy** to the defendant and remove public access (including from **CourtConnect**) and store records so they are **permanently inaccessible** to the public or the parties.  
- The court **may not charge a filing fee** for the expungement motion.  
- The court may adopt **rules as necessary** to implement the section.  
- Effective **6 months after enactment into law**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Removes public access to eviction filings** in qualifying cases, which the bill states can otherwise hinder securing housing for years even after a judgment is satisfied or even when the tenant prevailed (Synopsis; § 5720(d)).  
- Provides **mandatory relief (“must grant”)** when any listed eligibility condition is met, creating a predictable pathway for qualifying applicants (§ 5720(a)).  
- Includes multiple eligibility routes beyond time-based relief, such as **dismissal, withdrawal, defense win, party agreement, or “interest of justice”** (§ 5720(a)(3)–(7)).  
- **No filing fee** for the motion, lowering cost barriers for applicants (§ 5720(e)).  
- Requires action by the court within a firm **45-day** implementation window after the order (§ 5720(d)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Expungement requires the court to make records **permanently inaccessible to the public or the parties** (§ 5720(d)(4)), reducing access to historical court records once expunged.  
- The fiscal note identifies **additional staffing and system programming needs**, indicating increased workload for the Justice of the Peace Court (Fiscal Note, Assumptions #3).  
- Eligibility for certain judgments requires that the defendant has **satisfied any monetary award** included in the judgment or stipulation (§ 5720(a)(1)–(2)), which may limit access for individuals unable to pay.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What specific factors or standards the court will use to determine whether expungement is **“clearly in the interest of justice”** and how to weigh it against the **public’s interest** (§ 5720(a)(7)).  
- What “**permanently inaccessible**” requires in practice for record retention, archival access, and inter-agency data systems (§ 5720(d)(4)).  
- The fiscal note states programming changes are being evaluated and **future-year costs are unknown** (Fiscal Note, Assumptions #3(b)).  
- Not specified how the statute interacts with **third-party tenant screening reports** or private databases not controlled by the Delaware court system (bill text focuses on court/state publicly accessible systems).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new provision to Title 25, Chapter 57 creating **§ 5720 “Expungement.”**  
- Authorizes defendants in summary possession actions to seek expungement and requires the court to grant if any of these conditions are met (§ 5720(a)):  
  1) Judgment on the merits or default judgment **and** **5+ years** have passed since entry **and** defendant satisfied any monetary award.  
  2) Judgment entered on **stipulation** and defendant complied with the stipulation and satisfied any monetary award.  
  3) Plaintiff **withdrew** the complaint.  
  4) Court **dismissed** the complaint.  
  5) Final judgment **for the defendant**.  
  6) Parties **agree** to expungement.  
  7) Expungement is **clearly in the interest of justice** and not outweighed by public interest.  
- Upon eligibility, court must **set aside the original judgment** and expunge the action (§ 5720(b)).  
- After expungement order, action is treated as **never filed**, and the defendant may answer questions accordingly (§ 5720(c)).  
- Court must, within **45 days**, provide certified copy to defendant, notify the defendant, remove the case from publicly accessible state systems (including **CourtConnect**), and store related records so they are **permanently inaccessible** (§ 5720(d)).  
- Prohibits charging a **filing fee** for the motion (§ 5720(e)).

## Who Benefits
- Former defendants in summary possession (eviction) actions who meet any eligibility criterion in § 5720(a), including those whose cases were dismissed, withdrawn, resolved in their favor, or where **5+ years** have passed and monetary awards are satisfied.  
- Defendants seeking to avoid public visibility of qualifying eviction filings in Delaware court system public access tools (e.g., **CourtConnect**) (§ 5720(d)(3)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Members of the public who would otherwise access eviction case information through publicly accessible Delaware state databases/systems, since qualifying cases must be removed from those systems (§ 5720(d)(3)).  
- Justice of the Peace Court operations due to increased administrative workload and implementation needs (Fiscal Note, Assumptions #3).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** The Act takes effect **6 months after enactment into law** (Section 2; also assumed in Fiscal Note).  
- **Court action deadline:** **Within 45 days** of entry of an expungement order, the court must complete the required steps listed in § 5720(d).  
- **Eligibility time threshold:** For judgments on the merits or default judgments, **5 or more years** must have passed since judgment entry (and monetary award must be satisfied) (§ 5720(a)(1)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- No penalties specified.  
- Mandatory court duties:  
  - Court **must grant** a motion if it finds any condition in § 5720(a).  
  - Court **must issue** an order setting aside the judgment and expunging the record if eligible (§ 5720(b)).  
  - Court **must** complete required expungement implementation steps within **45 days** (§ 5720(d)).  
- **No filing fee** may be charged for filing the motion (§ 5720(e)).  
- Court may adopt **rules** to implement (§ 5720(f)).
