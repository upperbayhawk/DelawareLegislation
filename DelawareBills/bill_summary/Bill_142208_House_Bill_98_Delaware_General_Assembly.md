## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Titles 4 and 16 of the Delaware Code to create a regulatory framework for **THC-infused beverages** (non-alcoholic), including definitions, licensing/endorsement requirements, retail sales restrictions (primarily through package stores), product standards (THC limits and labeling), testing/inspection authority, transportation/warehouse controls, taxation, and enforcement/penalties; and to make related conforming changes affecting certain marijuana/hemp products.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates an **“infused beverage endorsement”** (generally a special permit to manufacture/prepare/package and deliver infused beverages to package stores) that is **valid for 2 years**, with set application/renewal fees (including reduced fees for certain **social equity** and **microbusiness** facilities).  
- Authorizes **licensed package stores** to sell infused beverages **for off-premises consumption**, with requirements for **clear THC signage**, **manufacturer-sealed containers**, and **separate storage/designated store section** for THC beverages.  
- Sets potency limits for lawful infused beverages: **≤ 10 mg total THC per container** and **≤ 60 mg total THC per multi-container package**; containers are defined as **single-serving** beverages of **no less than 12 ounces** with **≤ 10 mg total THC**.  
- Establishes an infused beverage excise-style tax of **$1 per container**, with **monthly reporting and payment** by licensed importers/manufacturers; revenues are credited to the **Marijuana Regulation Fund**.  
- Imposes distribution controls, including **Delaware in-state warehouse delivery/storage**, “at-rest” hold pending **inspection/sampling/testing**, and transportation documentation (including a required **way bill**).  
- Expands enforcement tools: violations of Chapter 13, Title 4 are deemed **“unlawful practices”** under Title 6; the Commissioner may impose administrative penalties including the greater of **$250** or up to **10% of estimated average gross monthly infused beverage sales** (based on the prior **12 months**), plus suspension/revocation/conditions.  
- Applies/links public health oversight via administrative inspections and food safety standards (Delaware Food Code **16 DE Admin. Code 4458**), including authority for sampling/testing upon health complaints and random sample testing requirements by regulation.  
- Makes related changes affecting marijuana/hemp retail practices, including limits that **consumable hemp products** may be sold only by **licensed retail marijuana stores**, and a restriction on marijuana retailers **discounting or giving marijuana products for free** in conjunction with another item.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Establishes clear, enforceable product limits and retail rules for THC beverages (e.g., **10 mg/container**, **60 mg/multi-container package**, sealed containers, age **21+**, separate section and signage).  
- Creates dedicated funding for regulation by directing endorsement fees and the **$1-per-container** tax into the **Marijuana Regulation Fund**.  
- Strengthens health/safety protections through food-code alignment and testing/inspection authority (including contaminant screening requirements for marijuana testing and authority for DPH inspections).  
- Reduces youth exposure risks through restrictions on child-appealing marketing and prohibitions on mass-market campaigns likely to reach minors, and restrictions on public-right-of-way-visible displays.  
- Enhances enforceability and consumer protection remedies by treating Chapter 13 violations as **Title 6** unlawful practices.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Adds direct business costs: endorsement fees (**$1,500/$600** application; **$1,000/$400** renewal) and an infused beverage tax of **$1 per container**, plus potential testing/warehouse compliance costs (including importer-paid random sampling testing costs).  
- Increases operational burdens for retailers and the supply chain (separate storage/section and signage; sealed off-premises removal; warehouse “at-rest” holds; way bills; delivery documentation; limits on “peddling” and requiring definite orders).  
- Restricts sales and marketing models by prohibiting indirect sales (mail/telephone/electronic) and **third-party delivery** of infused beverages, and prohibiting “gifting”/free transfer of infused beverages as part of a commercial transaction.  
- Creates potentially significant penalty exposure tied to sales volume (up to **10%** of estimated average gross monthly infused beverage sales, based on prior **12 months**).  
- Could increase state workload (e.g., Division of Public Health inspections/testing; Division of Revenue reporting/tax administration).

## Health Impact
- Imposes potency and package limits for infused beverages (**≤ 10 mg total THC per container; ≤ 60 mg total THC per multi-container package**) and requires labeling/signage (including a prominent THC/21+ symbol to be set by regulation).  
- Links infused beverages and certain hemp products to food safety oversight: compliance with the **Delaware Food Code (16 DE Admin. Code 4458)** and Title 16, with DPH inspection authority; health-complaint sampling/testing may be arranged via the Public Health Laboratory.  
- Requires/anticipates contaminant screening in marijuana testing (residual solvents, toxins, harmful chemicals, molds/mildew, filth, harmful microbials such as **E. coli**/**salmonella**, pesticides).  
- Maintains under-21 restrictions and clarifies the law does not permit impaired driving involving **marijuana, marijuana products, or infused beverages**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/updated definitions** in Titles 4/16, including “infused beverage endorsement,” “container” (single-serving **≥ 12 oz** with **≤ 10 mg total THC**), “THC/total THC,” “intoxicating cannabinoid,” and limits/restrictions on “hemp oil” composition (including bans and **0.1%** caps on specified cannabinoids as described in notes).  
- **Infused beverage endorsement** created/updated:
  - Valid **2 years**.
  - Fees: **$1,500** application / **$1,000** renewal; reduced fees **$600** application / **$400** renewal for certain social equity licensee-owned or microbusiness manufacturing facilities.
  - Automatic cancellation tied to underlying license cancellation/revocation/suspension (per notes).
- **Lawful sale conditions** for infused beverages (as summarized in notes): sold by **licensed package stores** for off-premises consumption; no alcohol; THC and package caps; sealed containers; separate storage and designated section with clear THC signage; no indirect sales and no third-party delivery; restrictions on child-appealing marketing; prohibition on gifting/free transfer as part of commercial transaction.  
- **Taxation:** new infused beverage tax of **$1 per container** with **monthly** reporting/payment; tax administration generally follows **Title 30, Chapter 5** processes (assessment/collection/appeals/refunds) unless inconsistent.  
- **Distribution/transport controls:** in-state warehouse delivery/storage and “at-rest” hold pending inspection/sampling/testing and approval; way bill requirement for carriers with specified contents; no peddling and definite-order transport rules (per notes).  
- **Enforcement upgrades:** Chapter 13 violations treated as **Title 6 unlawful practices**; Commissioner may cancel licenses granted in violation; administrative penalty for infused beverage-related violations is the greater of **$250** or up to **10%** of estimated average gross monthly infused beverage sales (prior **12 months**), plus suspension/revocation/conditions.  
- **Other related changes:** consumable hemp products limited to **licensed retail marijuana stores**; marijuana retailers prohibited from discounting or giving marijuana products for free when sold with another item.

## Who Benefits
- Consumers **21+**, through standardized potency limits, signage/labeling, and food-safety/testing oversight for infused beverages.  
- Licensed **package stores**, which gain explicit authority to sell infused beverages for off-premises consumption (subject to restrictions).  
- Social equity licensee-owned and microbusiness manufacturing facilities eligible for reduced endorsement fees (**$600** application; **$400** renewal).  
- State regulatory and enforcement agencies funded through the **Marijuana Regulation Fund**, supported by the **$1/container** tax and fee revenues.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Manufacturers/importers/suppliers and package stores subject to new fees, taxes, and operational constraints (warehouse holds, testing, transport documentation, separate section/storage, marketing restrictions, and sales channel limits).  
- Businesses relying on indirect sales, third-party delivery, or promotional “free” product practices (restricted/prohibited as described).  
- Entities facing increased penalty exposure, including sales-linked penalties (up to **10%** of estimated average gross monthly infused beverage sales).  
- DHSS Division of Public Health and Division of Revenue may experience increased workload (testing/inspections; tax administration).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Licensing/endorsement compliance:** manufacturing infused beverages intended for sale in Delaware requires an **infused beverage endorsement** (per notes).  
- **Retail compliance:** package store sales for off-premises consumption with **clear THC signage**, **separate designated section/storage**, and **sealed containers**; products must be delivered to purchaser and removed with seals unbroken (as described in notes).  
- **Age restrictions:** no sales to persons under **21**; civil penalty for selling marijuana/marijuana products/infused beverages to under 21 is **$250–$500** for a first violation; for subsequent violations within **5 years**, up to **$10,000** (Commissioner discretion) (per notes).  
- **Infused beverage-specific administrative penalty:** greater of **$250** or up to **10% of estimated average gross monthly infused beverage sales** for the prior **12 months**, plus possible suspension/revocation/conditions.  
- **Tax compliance:** **$1 per container** tax with **monthly** reporting/payment; Title 30, Chapter 5 processes apply for assessment/collection/appeals/interest/penalties/refunds unless inconsistent. Tax report/return information may be shared with enforcement offices; filing is deemed consent to disclosure (per notes).  
- **Inspections/testing:** Division of Public Health administrative inspections for Title 16/Food Code compliance; violations found are deemed violations of Title 4; sampling/testing authority includes health-complaint testing and random sample testing requirements by regulation. Importer pays random sampling testing costs (per notes).  
- **Transportation:** carriers must possess and produce a **way bill** on request with required contents (date; consignor/consignee names/addresses; delivery statement; package description/contents; carrier signature).  
- **Consumer protection:** any Chapter 13 violation is an **unlawful practice** under **Title 6 § 2513** (and related consumer protection provisions).
