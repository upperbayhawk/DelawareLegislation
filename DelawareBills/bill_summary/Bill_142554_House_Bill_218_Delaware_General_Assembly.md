## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To strengthen Delaware court and law-enforcement responses to interpersonal/domestic violence involving service members by (1) requiring courts to consider existing **military protective orders** when assessing immediate danger, and (2) requiring police to **notify the entering agency** when there is probable cause a **registered military protective order** has been violated.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires Delaware courts to **consider whether a military protective order (MPO) has been issued** against a respondent when deciding if there is an **immediate and present danger** in certain protective-order proceedings.  
- Applies this “consider MPO” requirement in both **domestic violence** proceedings (Title 10, Subchapter II) and **sexual violence** proceedings (Title 10, Chapter 72).  
- Defines “military protective order” as an order issued under **10 U.S.C. § 1567** by a commanding officer in the U.S. armed forces, the **Delaware National Guard**, or **another state’s National Guard**, against a person under that officer’s command.  
- Requires a law-enforcement officer who has probable cause to believe a **service member** violated an MPO **registered in the FBI’s National Crime Information Center (NCIC)** to **notify the law-enforcement agency that entered the MPO** into NCIC.  
- Does **not** create new criminal penalties in the text provided; it adds a notification duty and a court-consideration factor.  
- Effective date: **January 1, 2026**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved risk assessment by courts:** The bill directs courts to consider the existence of an MPO when evaluating “immediate and present danger,” potentially enabling more informed decisions on protective relief (Sections 2 and 5).  
- **Better inter-agency coordination:** Mandatory notification to the agency that entered an MPO into **NCIC** can improve information flow after a suspected violation (Section 3).  
- **Clarifies MPO recognition across components:** Explicitly includes MPOs issued by the U.S. armed forces, **Delaware National Guard**, and **other states’ National Guards** (Sections 1 and 4).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added procedural burden on law enforcement:** Officers must make an additional notification when probable cause exists and the MPO is registered in **NCIC** (Section 3).  
- **Limited scope of notification duty:** The notice requirement applies only when the MPO is **registered in NCIC** and the individual is a **service member** (Section 3), potentially leaving gaps for unregistered MPOs.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 10 § 1043 (Domestic violence ex parte/emergency hearings):** Court must consider whether an MPO was issued against the respondent when determining “immediate and present danger,” including where the petitioner/petitioner’s minor child/infirm adult was a reported victim (Section 2).  
- **Title 10 § 1046 (Enforcement):** If an officer has probable cause a service member violated an **NCIC-registered MPO**, the officer must notify the law-enforcement agency that entered the MPO into NCIC (Section 3).  
- **Title 10 § 7204 (Sexual violence emergency proceedings):** Court must consider whether an MPO was issued against the respondent when determining immediate and present danger of physical injury (Section 5).  
- **Definitions updated/confirmed (Title 10 §§ 1041 and 7202):** MPO definition tied to **10 U.S.C. § 1567**, issued by commanding officers in U.S. armed forces/Delaware National Guard/other states’ National Guard (Sections 1 and 4).

## Who Benefits
- **Petitioners** seeking protective orders in domestic violence and sexual violence proceedings, where an existing MPO may support a finding of immediate danger (Sections 2 and 5).  
- **Law enforcement agencies** that entered MPOs into NCIC, which will receive notification of possible violations (Section 3).  
- **Courts**, by having an explicitly required factor to consider in danger determinations (Sections 2 and 5).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Respondents/service members** subject to MPOs may face increased scrutiny in court danger assessments and increased reporting/notification following suspected violations (Sections 2, 3, and 5).  
- **Law enforcement officers/agencies** may face added compliance steps due to the notification requirement (Section 3).
