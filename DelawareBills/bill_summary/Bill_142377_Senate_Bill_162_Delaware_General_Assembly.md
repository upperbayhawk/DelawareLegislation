## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To amend Delaware’s sex offender registration law (Title 11, § 4120) to require registrants to disclose whether children reside in their current or anticipated residences, and to allow law enforcement to notify a non-custodial parent when their child is identified as residing (or expected to reside) with a registered sex offender.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires sex offenders, on the registration form, to **identify any children residing** in their **current residence(s)** or **anticipated residence(s)**. (11 Del. C. § 4120(d)(2))
- Requires sex offenders who **change name, residence, or place of employment/study** to **re-register in person within 3 business days** and to **disclose whether any children reside** in any residence identified as part of registration. (§ 4120(f)(1))
- Authorizes law enforcement to **notify a non-custodial parent** that their child(ren) were identified by a sex offender as living in the offender’s **current or anticipated** residence. (§ 4120(m))
- Keeps existing limits on public release of certain data: victim identity, arrests not resulting in conviction, Social Security number, and travel/immigration document numbers **may not be released**. (§ 4120(m))
- Maintains the existing registration information set (e.g., identifiers, residences, employment/study, vehicles, passport copies, phone numbers, conditions of supervision) and the requirement that **false statements are punishable by law**. (§ 4120(d)(2))
- Continues the requirement that a **DNA sample** will be taken and the DNA profile submitted to **CODIS**. (§ 4120(d)(2))
- Information collected must be kept in **digitized form** in an electronic database maintained by the designated Delaware Police facility responsible for registration. (§ 4120(d)(2))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved child safety notifications:** Explicitly allows law enforcement to notify a **non-custodial parent** if the registrant reports the parent’s child lives (or will live) with the registrant. (§ 4120(m))
- **Clearer data collection on child co-residence:** Adds an explicit requirement that registrants identify children residing in current/anticipated residences, potentially improving the completeness of registry information. (§ 4120(d)(2), § 4120(f)(1))
- **May support targeted protective actions without broad public disclosure:** The bill adds notification authority while retaining prohibitions on releasing sensitive information (e.g., victim identity, SSN). (§ 4120(m))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expanded reporting obligations for registrants:** Requires disclosure about children residing in any current or anticipated residence(s) and upon re-registration after certain changes. (§ 4120(d)(2), § 4120(f)(1))
- **Administrative workload increase for law enforcement:** Collecting, maintaining, and potentially acting on child-residency disclosures (including notifying non-custodial parents) may increase administrative tasks. (§ 4120(d)(2), § 4120(m))
- **Potential privacy concerns for households/children:** The registry will include whether children reside with the registrant; the bill also enables parent notification based on those disclosures. (§ 4120(d)(2), § 4120(m))

## Key Policy Changes
- **Registration form content expanded:** The registration form must require the sex offender to **identify any children** who reside in the offender’s **current residence(s)** or **anticipated residence(s)**. (§ 4120(d)(2))
- **Re-registration update requirement reinforced:** When re-registering after changing name, residence, or employment/study, the offender must **disclose whether any children reside** in any identified residence(s). Re-registration remains **in person within 3 business days** of the change. (§ 4120(f)(1))
- **New notification authority:** Law enforcement may notify a **non-custodial parent** that their child(ren) were identified by the offender as living in the offender’s **current or anticipated** residence. (§ 4120(m))

## Who Benefits
- **Non-custodial parents** whose child(ren) are identified as living (or expected to live) with a registered sex offender, due to authorized notification. (§ 4120(m))
- **Law enforcement/public safety agencies** by receiving more specific residence-related information involving children. (§ 4120(d)(2), § 4120(f)(1))
