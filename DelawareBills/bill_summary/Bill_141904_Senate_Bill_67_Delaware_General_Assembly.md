## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16, §1145 to (1) codify how long a Background Check Center (BCC) criminal history report remains valid for home-care employment and (2) require Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS) regulations using the “Rap Back System” to notify employers of subsequent criminal history.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Sets that an applicant’s criminal history report obtained through the BCC criminal history screening process is valid for **3 years** from the date of the applicant’s **last fingerprinting**.  
- Applies validity to **“any prospective employer”** (not limited to the employer that originally requested the report).  
- Maintains the rule that an employer may not employ an applicant for work in a private residence **before obtaining a criminal history**.  
- Requires DHSS to promulgate regulations addressing multiple items, including fingerprinting frequency and how criminal histories are prepared, reviewed, and updated.  
- Specifically requires regulations to use the **Rap Back System** (as defined in **11 Del. C. § 8502**) so **any subsequent criminal history of an employee** is provided to **current and future employers**.  
- Fiscal note identifies **one-time technology costs of $55,500 in FY 2026** to implement BCC system changes; ongoing costs are **indeterminable**.  
- Delaware State Police State Bureau of Identification (SBI) expects potential programming/record-related changes, but costs are **unknown** at the time of projection.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduced repeat fingerprinting/background check burden**: A BCC criminal history report remains valid for **3 years**, usable by **any prospective employer**, potentially reducing duplicative screenings within that time window (bill text §1145(c)).  
- **Ongoing monitoring for new offenses**: Required use of the **Rap Back System** to provide **subsequent criminal history** to **current and future employers** may improve timeliness of updates compared to relying only on periodic re-checks (bill text §1145(n)(3)).  
- **Regulatory clarity**: DHSS must promulgate regulations covering suitability criteria, notification methods, administrative review, and other implementation provisions (bill text §1145(n)(1)-(7)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Implementation costs**: Requires BCC system technology changes with **one-time FY 2026 cost of $55,500** (fiscal note).  
- **Unknown/indeterminable additional costs**: SBI anticipates potential programming/record-responsibility changes; costs are **unknown**, and ongoing fiscal impacts are **indeterminable** (fiscal note).  
- **Longer reuse period may rely more heavily on Rap Back implementation**: The report is valid for **3 years**, which may increase the importance of the Rap Back notification system functioning as intended (requirement is in bill text; performance implications are not quantified in source text).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Validity period codified**: A BCC criminal history report is valid for **3 years** from the date of the applicant’s **last fingerprinting**, and is valid for **any prospective employer** (amends §1145(c)).  
- **Rap Back definition added**: Adds definition of “Rap Back System” by reference to **11 Del. C. § 8502** (amends §1145(b)).  
- **Regulatory requirement for Rap Back notifications**: Requires DHSS regulations to ensure the Rap Back System provides **subsequent criminal history** to **current and future employers** (amends §1145(n)(3)).  
- **Regulatory framework affirmed/expanded**: Requires DHSS to promulgate regulations on suitability criteria, frequency of review/updating, fingerprint frequency, notice methods, and administrative review process (amends §1145(n)(1)-(7)).

## Who Benefits
- **Applicants/employees** who can use a BCC criminal history report for **3 years** with **any prospective employer** (bill text §1145(c)).  
- **Employers/home-care agencies** who can rely on an existing BCC report for up to **3 years** and receive subsequent criminal history through Rap Back-based processes (bill text §1145(c), §1145(n)(3)).  
- **Individuals receiving services in private residences** (and authorized representatives) insofar as employers must obtain a criminal history before employment and regulations support ongoing updates (bill text §1145(c), §1145(n)).
