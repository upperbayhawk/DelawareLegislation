## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To strengthen enforcement of Delaware’s worker misclassification law (Chapter 35, Title 19) by making general contractors responsible for subcontractors’ violations, and by allowing the Department of Labor to take registration action against contractors who use unregistered subcontractors on public works contracts.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Makes a **general contractor responsible for a subcontractor’s violations** of Delaware’s employee misclassification law (Title 19, Ch. 35).  
- Establishes that a general contractor is **jointly and severally liable** for a subcontractor’s **penalties and restitution** assessed under **§ 3505**.  
- Clarifies that a general contractor’s liability for a subcontractor’s **penalties does not include “debarment”** under **§ 3505(h)(1)**.  
- Retains/clarifies the **debarment trigger**: an employer found in violation **more than twice in a 2-year period** may be subject to debarment under **§ 3505(h)(1)**.  
- Requires the Department of Labor to **file a list of employers subject to debarment** with specified state offices/agencies (OMB; Revenue; UI; Insurance; Workers’ Compensation; Attorney General).  
- Provides that debarment applies to certain **successor entities** that share **1 or more of the same principals or officers** and are engaged in the **same or equivalent trade/activity**.  
- Allows the Department to **deny, suspend, or revoke** a contractor’s **certificate of registration** if the contractor **contracts with or uses an unregistered subcontractor** on a **public works contract**.  
- **Effective as law on January 28, 2026**, enacted **without the Governor’s approval** under **Delaware Constitution, Art. III, § 18**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved enforceability** against misclassification when the violating party is an **unregistered subcontractor**, by shifting responsibility to the general contractor (Synopsis; § 3503(e)(2), § 3505(j)).  
- **Greater compliance incentives** for general contractors to vet subcontractors’ registration and classification practices, due to **joint and several liability** for restitution and penalties (§ 3505(j)).  
- **Protects workers and compliant businesses** by addressing misclassification that affects **tax withholding, unemployment insurance, wage laws, and workers’ compensation** (Synopsis).  
- **Public-sector contracting integrity** strengthened by enabling registration sanctions for using **unregistered subcontractors** on **public works contracts** (§ 3607(a)(5)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased liability exposure for general contractors**: they can be held **jointly and severally liable** for a subcontractor’s penalties and restitution, even if the subcontractor is the direct violator (§ 3505(j)(1); § 3503(e)(2)).  
- **Additional compliance/administrative burden** on general contractors to verify subcontractor registration and monitor practices to avoid downstream penalties and restitution (§ 3607(a)(5); § 3505(j)).  
- **Potential contract cost impacts** where general contractors seek to mitigate new liability through tighter subcontracting terms or risk pricing (liability structure stated in § 3505(j); cost effects not quantified in text).

## Key Policy Changes
- **New responsibility rule:** “Notwithstanding” the general prohibition on aiding/abetting violations, **a general contractor is responsible for a subcontractor’s violations** of Chapter 35 (§ 3503(e)(2)).  
- **Joint and several liability:** A general contractor is **jointly and severally liable** for a subcontractor’s **penalties and restitution** under § 3505 (§ 3505(j)(1)).  
- **Debarment carve-out:** A general contractor’s liability for a subcontractor’s **penalties** does **not include debarment** under § 3505(h)(1) (§ 3505(j)(2)).  
- **Debarment threshold cross-reference/technical correction:** An employer with violations **more than twice in a 2-year period** may be subject to debarment **under § 3505(h)(1)** (§ 3510(c)(1)).  
- **Debarment list distribution:** Department must file the debarment list with **OMB, Division of Revenue, Division of Unemployment Insurance, Department of Insurance, Office of Workers’ Compensation, and Office of the Attorney General** (§ 3510(c)(2)).  
- **Successor debarment applicability:** Debarment applies to successor entities that have **1+ of the same principals/officers** and the **same or equivalent trade/activity** (§ 3510(c)(3)).  
- **Registration sanction trigger on public works:** Department may deny/suspend/revoke registration if contractor **contracts with/uses an unregistered subcontractor** on a **public works contract** (§ 3607(a)(5)).
