## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Propose a Delaware Constitution amendment (Article I) to create an explicit, fundamental right for employees to organize and bargain collectively, and to prohibit laws that interfere with or diminish those rights, including laws/ordinances that bar certain union-security agreements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds a new **Article I, § 22 “Workers’ rights”** to the Delaware Constitution.
- Establishes that **employees have a “fundamental right”** to **organize** and **bargain collectively** through representatives of their choosing.
- Specifies bargaining purposes include **negotiating wages, hours, working conditions**, and protecting **economic welfare and safety at work**.
- Prohibits passage of any law that **“interferes with, negates, or diminishes”** employees’ right to organize and bargain over **wages, hours, other terms and conditions of employment, and workplace safety**.
- Explicitly includes a prohibition on any law/ordinance that would **prohibit execution or application of agreements** requiring **union membership as a condition of employment** (i.e., bans on certain union-security agreements).
- As a constitutional amendment, requires **two-thirds vote** in each chamber and must be passed again by the **subsequent General Assembly** to be finally adopted (Del. Const. art. XVI, § 1).
- Fiscal note: potential impacts on state labor relations are **indeterminable but potentially significant**, with changes assumed not implemented until **FY 2028**; **FY 2027: N/A**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Constitutional protection of collective bargaining:** The bill elevates organizing and bargaining rights to a **constitutional “fundamental right”** (new Art. I, § 22), increasing durability against future statutory changes.
- **Broader subject protection:** It bars laws diminishing bargaining over **“other terms and conditions of employment and workplace safety”** (bill text), potentially strengthening employees’ ability to address workplace safety and conditions.
- **Prevents bans on union-security agreements:** It prohibits laws/ordinances that would bar agreements requiring **membership as a condition of employment** (bill text), preserving the legal ability to negotiate such provisions.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potentially significant but uncertain cost exposure:** Fiscal note states costs to **salary and benefit programs** and administrative costs for **Department of Human Resources** and **Office of Management and Budget** are **“indeterminable, but potentially significant”** if existing provisions become subject to collective bargaining.
- **May constrain future legislative flexibility:** The bill’s ban on laws that “interfere with, negate, or diminish” bargaining rights (bill text) could limit future statutory options related to labor policy (constraint described in the constitutional language itself).
- **Potential expansion of bargaining scope for state merit employees:** Fiscal note notes current bargaining scope excludes items like **position classification, health care and other benefit programs (Title 29, Chs. 52 and 96), overtime, workers compensation, disability programs, and pension programs**; if the amendment subjects these to bargaining, it could materially change state employment administration and costs (fiscal note).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Delaware Constitution Article I, § 22 (Workers’ rights)** establishing:
  - A **fundamental right** for employees to **organize** and **bargain collectively** through chosen representatives.
  - Covered bargaining purposes: **wages, hours, working conditions**, and protection of **economic welfare and safety at work**.
- **Prohibits** any law from being passed that **interferes with, negates, or diminishes** the right to bargain over:
  - **Wages, hours**
  - **Other terms and conditions of employment**
  - **Workplace safety**
- **Specifically bars** laws/ordinances that **prohibit execution or application of agreements** between employers and labor organizations that require **union membership as a condition of employment**.
