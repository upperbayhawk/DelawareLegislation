## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Establish an independent, nonpartisan Office of Inspector General (OIG) in Title 29 to prevent and investigate fraud, waste, mismanagement, corruption, abuse of governmental resources, and related conduct harming the public interest; increase oversight, accountability, and transparency in Delaware State government.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates an **independent, nonpartisan Office of Inspector General** to investigate fraud, waste, mismanagement, corruption, and abuse of governmental resources in **state agencies**. (§ 9003E(a))
- Defines “state agency” broadly to include **departments, boards, commissions, committees, and school districts**; includes the **General Assembly only for management of resources and routine administrative operations** (not legislative functions/disciplinary authority). (§ 9002E(5))
- Establishes an **Inspector General appointment process** using an **Inspector General Selection Panel** that submits **3 names** to the Governor; Governor nominates **1** for **Senate confirmation** (majority of elected Senators); IG serves a **5-year term** and may be reappointed. (§ 9010E; § 9004E(a))
- Requires complaint intake methods (including a **statewide toll-free number** and other channels) and sets investigative limits (generally not investigating employee employment-relationship complaints unless directly tied to fraud/waste/etc.). (§ 9005E(a)(3); § 9006E(a)(2))
- Requires a **public decision** at the conclusion of each complaint investigation, with confidentiality guardrails (e.g., cannot name an employee/contractor when **no wrongdoing** is found without **written permission**). (§ 9006E(g)(1)-(2))
- Grants strong investigative tools: **access to agency and contractor/grantee records**, **subpoena authority**, and a requirement agencies provide requested documentation within **5 business days** of a written demand. (§ 9007E(c)-(d))
- Makes investigative records **confidential and FOIA-exempt**, with specified exceptions; requires an **annual report by March 1** posted on the OIG website and sent to specified officials/entities. (§ 9008E; § 9009E(a))
- Fiscal note projects new OIG costs: **FY2026 $590,769** (includes **$100,000** one-time start-up), **FY2027 $1,398,468**, **FY2028 $1,503,561**, with personnel costs assumed to grow **2% annually**. (Fiscal Note #6-7)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Creates a dedicated statewide mechanism to investigate and deter **fraud, waste, mismanagement, corruption, and abuse**, improving oversight and accountability. (§ 9003E(a); § 9005E(a)(1))
- Requires **public decisions** after complaint investigations, increasing public-facing transparency while protecting individuals not found to have committed wrongdoing. (§ 9006E(g)(1)-(2))
- Improves investigative effectiveness through **record-access deadlines (5 business days)** and **subpoena power**, including court enforcement. (§ 9007E(c)-(d))
- Clarifies coordination/avoid-duplication expectations through required collaboration (including MOUs) with DOJ, Auditor of Accounts, the State Public Integrity Commission, and others. (§ 9005E(b))
- Annual reporting by **March 1** supports ongoing accountability and tracking of complaints, investigations, and unresolved recommendations. (§ 9009E(a))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Creates new and ongoing State operating costs (projected **$590,769 in FY2026**, rising to **$1,503,561 in FY2028**). (Fiscal Note #6)
- Investigative records are **confidential and FOIA-exempt**, which may limit public access to underlying investigative materials. (§ 9008E(a))
- Agencies, contractors, and grantees face compliance burdens, including producing documents within **5 business days** and responding to subpoenas/interviews. (§ 9007E(c)-(d))
- Public decisions must identify individuals when wrongful acts/omissions are found, which may create reputational impacts for named persons. (§ 9006E(g)(4)b)

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates Title 29 **Chapter 90E** establishing the **Office of Inspector General** as independent and nonpartisan. (§ 9003E(a))
- Defines “state agency” to include **school districts** and limits General Assembly coverage to **resource management and routine administrative operations** (excluding legislative functions/discipline). (§ 9002E(5))
- Sets IG eligibility restriction: certain former officials cannot be nominated until **3 years** after leaving specified roles. (§ 9003E(c)(2))
- Sets IG term at **5 years**; requires IG to notify the Secretary of State **at least 6 months** before term end regarding intent to seek reappointment. (§ 9004E(a)(1)-(2))
- Establishes complaint process rules: any person/entity may complain; IG must remain neutral; must notify complainant when declining to investigate and provide reason; no complaint fee. (§ 9006E(a), (b)(2), (d)-(e))
- Requires a **public decision** at the end of each complaint investigation; restricts naming when no wrongdoing is found without written permission; requires consultation opportunity that cannot delay announcement. (§ 9006E(g))
- Grants investigatory powers: access to records (including contractor/grantee records), inspection/copying with or without notice, interviews, subpoenas, oath administration, and court enforcement; agencies must respond within **5 business days**. (§ 9007E)
- Makes investigative records **confidential and FOIA-exempt**, with enumerated exceptions and required confidentiality procedures. (§ 9008E)
- Requires DOJ referrals for evidence of crimes and for significant problems; allows IG to bring a **civil action on behalf of the State** if DOJ does not. (§ 9006E(i)-(j); § 9005E(a)(5))
- Requires annual reporting **no later than March 1**, published on the OIG website and distributed to specified recipients. (§ 9009E(a))
- Establishes/updates Selection Panel process: panel submits **3** names; Governor selects **1** for Senate confirmation; panel is a FOIA public body; Secretary of State supports panel operations. (§ 9010E)
- Provides for JLOSC review beginning **July 1, 2032**. (Section 5(1), as described)
- Adds a distribution requirement to transmit an unspecified document referenced in “paragraph (2)” to multiple recipients including the Governor, General Assembly, Division of Legislative Services leadership, and the Delaware Public Archives. (Chunk 5)

## Who Benefits
- Delaware residents and taxpayers, through increased oversight, accountability, and transparency in State government operations. (§ 9003E(a); § 9001E findings as described)
- Complainants and the public, via required complaint intake, declination notices, public decisions, and annual reporting. (§ 9006E(b)(2), (g); § 9009E)
- Individuals investigated where **no wrongdoing** is found, due to naming protections absent **written permission**. (§ 9006E(g)(2))
- State integrity/oversight entities (DOJ, Auditor of Accounts, State Public Integrity Commission), through mandated coordination and referrals. (§ 9005E(b); § 9006E(i))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- State agencies (including school districts), contractors, and grantees subject to investigations, subpoenas, and rapid document production requirements (**5 business days**). (§ 9007E(c)-(d))
- Individuals publicly identified in OIG decisions when wrongful acts/omissions are found. (§ 9006E(g)(4)b)
- Certain former officials restricted from IG nomination for **3 years** after leaving specified positions. (§ 9003E(c)(2))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Agency cooperation mandate:** agencies must provide requested records within **5 business days** of a written demand and assist with releases for restricted/privileged documents. (§ 9007E(b)-(c))
- **Subpoena enforcement:** IG may petition a court to compel compliance if a person refuses to obey a subpoena. (§ 9007E(d))
- **Mandatory crime reporting:** IG must report evidence of a crime to DOJ and appropriate law enforcement. (§ 9006E(i))
- **Civil enforcement backstop:** if DOJ does not file a civil action to address a reported problem, IG may pursue a civil action for violation of law or policy. (§ 9006E(j))
- **Confidentiality compliance:** investigative records are confidential and FOIA-exempt; IG must maintain confidentiality of specified identities and establish confidentiality rules/procedures. (§ 9008E)
- **IG conduct/tenure compliance:** mandatory removal upon certain convictions; for-cause removal for violating Code of Conduct requires Governor recommendation and **two-thirds vote of each house**; resignation deemed upon candidacy actions (filing/forming committee/announcement). (§ 9004E(c)-(d))
- **No-fee rule and neutrality:** IG may not charge a fee for complaint submission/investigation and must remain neutral (not an advocate for complainant or agency). (§ 9006E(d)-(e))
