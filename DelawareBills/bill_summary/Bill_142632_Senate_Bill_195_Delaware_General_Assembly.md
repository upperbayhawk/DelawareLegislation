## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend the Town of Delmar charter to (1) revise municipal election/candidate filing timing and voter-list language, and (2) increase the Town’s authority to issue bonds/obligations without following certain charter procedures, by raising the outstanding cap from **$1,000,000** to **$10,000,000**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes Delmar municipal election wording from **“biannually”** to **“biennially,”** clarifying elections occur every **2 years**.
- Extends the candidate notice deadline: candidates must notify the Town Manager **not less than 30 days** before the election (was **10 days**).
- Confirms the Town uses the **State’s Voter Registration System** as its registered-voter list source (per **Title 15** of the Delaware Code).
- Revises when Delmar may deny a voter from the State system: denial is tied to whether the voter is **no longer a resident of the Town on the day of the election** or is **otherwise disqualified under the Charter**.
- Expands the Town Council’s bonding authority under Charter **Section 24(b)** by increasing the maximum aggregate amount of bonds/obligations that may be **outstanding at any time** from **$1,000,000** to **$10,000,000**.
- Allows issuance up to the **$10,000,000** cap **without regard to requirements** in **Section 24(a)(1)-(7)**, if approved by Town Council resolution meeting stated requirements.
- Act is enacted as **Chapter 53, Volume 85** and was **approved June 30, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More time for election administration and public notice:** Moving candidate notice from **10 to 30 days** provides additional lead time for preparing ballots/notices and publishing or posting candidate lists (list must still be printed in a newspaper or posted in **at least five (5) public places** **at least five (5) days** before the election).
- **Clearer election frequency:** Replacing “biannually” with **“biennially”** reduces ambiguity about whether elections occur twice a year or every two years.
- **Modernized voter-list reference:** Using the **State’s Voter Registration System** (Title 15) provides a standardized voter source.
- **Greater municipal financing capacity:** Raising the outstanding cap to **$10,000,000** can enable larger or multiple municipal projects to be financed through bonds/obligations, subject to Town Council resolution requirements.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher potential debt exposure:** Increasing the bonding/obligation limit from **$1,000,000 to $10,000,000 outstanding at any time** increases the Town’s maximum authorized indebtedness under this pathway.
- **Reduced procedural constraints for certain borrowing:** Bonds/obligations under Section 24(b) may be issued **without regard to** the requirements in **Section 24(a)(1)-(7)** (though other Section 24 provisions still apply), potentially reducing charter-level procedural steps otherwise applicable.
- **Tighter candidacy timeline for late-deciding candidates:** Requiring filing **30 days** in advance may make it harder for candidates who decide to run closer to election day.

## Key Policy Changes
1. **Election frequency wording**
   - Changes “biannually” to **“biennially”** for the municipal election held on the **first Monday in October**, **7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m.**
2. **Candidate filing/notice deadline**
   - Candidate must notify the Town Manager **in writing** of candidacy **not less than 30 days prior to the election** (previously **10 days**).
3. **Voter registration list source**
   - Establishes use/adoption of the **State’s Voter Registration System** as the Town’s registered voter list (Title 15).
4. **Voter denial standard (as tied to the State system list)**
   - Updates language to allow denial of a voter registered in the State system if the voter **is no longer a resident of the Town on the day of the election** or is **otherwise disqualified from voting under the Charter**.
5. **Borrowing/bonding limit**
   - In Section 24(b), increases the maximum aggregate amount of bonds/other obligations that may be **outstanding at any time** under that subsection from **$1,000,000** to **$10,000,000**, and confirms issuance may occur **without regard to Section 24(a)(1)-(7)** if conditions are met.

## Who Benefits
- **Town of Delmar government/Town Council:** Increased flexibility and capacity to finance “proper municipal purpose” needs through bonds/obligations up to **$10,000,000 outstanding**.
- **Voters/residents (administrative clarity):** Clearer election frequency language and reliance on the State’s registration system as the voter list source.
- **Election administrators:** More lead time (30 days) to finalize candidate lists and meet publication/posting requirements.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Potential candidates:** Individuals who decide to run within **30 days** of the election may be unable to meet the filing/notice deadline.
- **Town residents/taxpayers (potentially):** Increased authorized outstanding debt capacity (up to **$10,000,000**) could increase financial obligations if the Town issues more debt (the bill does not specify repayment sources or tax effects).
- **Voters affected by residency/disqualification determinations:** Individuals registered in the State system who are determined to be **non-residents on election day** or otherwise disqualified under the Charter may be denied voting in Town elections.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approval date:** **June 30, 2025** (Act approved).
- **Municipal election schedule (as charter text):** First Monday in October, **7:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m.**, held **biennially**.
- **Candidate filing deadline:** **Not less than 30 days prior to the election** (changed from 10 days).
- **Candidate list publication/posting deadline:** **At least 5 days prior** to the municipal election.
- **Bond cap condition:** Up to **$10,000,000 outstanding at any time** under Section 24(b).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Election compliance:** Candidates must file written notice with the Town Manager during regular business hours **≥ 30 days** before the election; Town Manager must publish/post candidate list **≥ 5 days** before election (publication in a newspaper of general circulation in the Town, or posting in **at least five (5) public places** designated by Town Council).
- **Voter eligibility enforcement:** Town may deny a voter registered in the State’s system if the voter is **not a Town resident on election day** or is **otherwise disqualified under the Charter**.
- **Borrowing/bond issuance procedural requirements under Section 24(b):**
  - Requires Town Council **Resolution** adopted by **at least a majority of all members** approving issuance for a **proper municipal purpose**.
  - Resolution must state: **amount**, **purpose**, **manner of securing**, and that total bonds/obligations issued under 24(b) do not exceed **$10,000,000**, plus other pertinent facts in Town Council’s possession.
  - A subsequent resolution may establish bond details and must be adopted by **at least a majority of a quorum** (as established under Section 10).
