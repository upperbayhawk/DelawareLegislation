## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Increase the maximum “senior property tax credit” available to eligible homeowners age 65+ by raising the dollar cap on the credit.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Raises the senior property tax credit cap from **$500 to $1,000**, while keeping the cap at **the lesser of 50% of the school tax levied or the dollar cap**.  
- Applies the **$500 cap for all years prior to January 1, 2026**, and the **$1,000 cap for all years beginning on or after January 1, 2026**.  
- The credit remains contingent on **local school board authorization by majority vote** (per 29 Del. C. § 6102(q)(3)).  
- The credit is applied by the **receiver of taxes and county treasurer**, after accounting for certain exemptions/reductions and any change to the current expense tax rate.  
- Fiscal note estimates added State cost of **$15.3 million per year** in **FY 2026, FY 2027, and FY 2028**.  
- The State reimburses districts for issued credits through an annual operating budget appropriation; for **FY 2025** that appropriation is **$29.8 million**.  
- Fiscal note reports **71,537** recipients in **FY 2025** with an **average credit of $429**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Larger tax relief for eligible seniors:** Increases the maximum credit from **$500 to $1,000** (still limited to **≤ 50%** of the applicable school tax), potentially reducing school property tax bills for qualifying homeowners age **65 or over**.
- **State-funded reimbursement to districts:** The fiscal note states the **State reimburses local school districts** for the cost of senior property tax credits via annual appropriation, which may help avoid shifting the credit cost onto district budgets (subject to appropriations).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increases State spending:** Fiscal note projects **$15.3 million** additional cost annually in **FY 2026–FY 2028**.
- **Uneven/local availability:** The credit depends on whether a **local school board authorizes** it by majority vote; benefits may vary by district based on local decisions.
- **Assessment/reassessment uncertainty in estimates:** Fiscal note indicates costs for **New Castle and Sussex** are based on historical assessed values because reassessments are not complete, which may affect cost accuracy.
