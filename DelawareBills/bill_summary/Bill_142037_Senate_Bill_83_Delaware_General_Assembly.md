## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To strengthen Delaware’s long-term care resident “bill of rights” by (1) expressly recognizing domestic partners for visitation privacy and room-sharing opportunities, (2) adding an explicit nondiscrimination right tied to protected classes in Chapter 45 of Title 6, and (3) requiring timely updates to resident-rights notices and staff training after any revisions to the resident-rights statute.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Extends resident visitation privacy and (where feasible) room-sharing opportunity to a resident’s **domestic partner**, not only a spouse, when both are residents, unless **medically contraindicated**.  
- Adds a new resident right to be **free from discrimination** in care, treatment, or services on any basis protected under **Chapter 45 of Title 6**.  
- Requires the Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS) to update the official notice that contains **§ 1121 in its entirety** **within 30 days** of any revisions to § 1121.  
- Requires each long-term care facility to provide staff training addressing revisions to § 1121 **within 60 days** of any revision.  
- Requires the resident-rights notice to be available in a **language and format accessible** to each resident or the resident’s authorized representative.  
- Creates compliance timelines tied to **future changes** to § 1121 (not only the changes made by this Act).  
- Approved **September 3, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer protections for domestic partners:** Explicitly includes “domestic partner” for visit privacy and potential room-sharing, supporting recognition of domestic partnerships (including those entered in other states, per synopsis).  
- **Explicit nondiscrimination right in long-term care settings:** Establishes a resident right to be free from discrimination in care, treatment, or services, referencing protected classes under **Title 6, Chapter 45**.  
- **Improved awareness of rights:** Mandates DHSS to update resident-rights notices within **30 days** of § 1121 revisions and requires facilities to train staff within **60 days**, supporting more consistent implementation.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative workload for DHSS:** DHSS must update and make available an updated § 1121 notice within **30 days** after any revision to § 1121.  
- **Training burden for facilities:** Facilities must deliver staff training within **60 days** of any § 1121 revision, which may require scheduling, materials, and tracking compliance.  
- **Operational constraints may limit room-sharing:** The room-sharing opportunity is only “**where feasible**” and may be denied if “**medically contraindicated**,” potentially limiting practical effect in some facilities.

- **§ 1123(a):** Requires DHSS to update the § 1121 notice **within 30 days** of any revisions to § 1121; notice must be accessible in language/format to the resident or authorized representative.
