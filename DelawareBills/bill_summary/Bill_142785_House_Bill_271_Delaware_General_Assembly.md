## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 4, § 1354(e) of the Delaware Code to clarify that marijuana establishment spacing requirements apply **only to retail marijuana licenses**, not to non-retail marijuana operations (e.g., cultivation, manufacturing, testing).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Limits the statutory **distance/spacing restriction** in **§ 1354(e)** to **retail marijuana licenses**.
- Requires the Commissioner to **refuse** a **retail** marijuana license if another licensed **retail** establishment of the same type is within:
  - **1,200 feet** (by accessible public road/street) in an **incorporated city or town**, or
  - **1 mile** (by accessible public road/street) in an **unincorporated or rural area**.
- Creates/maintains a discretionary approval window: in unincorporated/rural areas, if an existing retail establishment is **less than 1 mile but more than 9/10 of 1 mile**, the Commissioner **may** grant the license.
- Preserves existing exceptions for **existing retail licenses**, including **sale/transfer/renewal** and certain **moves/relocations** (e.g., within **500 feet**, or within the same shopping center/mall).
- By clarifying scope, permits **co-location** of **non-retail** marijuana operations (cultivation, manufacturing, testing) without being blocked by retail spacing rules.
- States intent to align marijuana spacing policy with Delaware’s **liquor-control** approach (spacing focused on retail outlets).
- Bill release date shown on text: **01/20/2026 (11:47 AM)**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces siting constraints for non-retail facilities**: The synopsis states the Act “permits the co-location of non-retail marijuana operations, including cultivation, manufacturing, and testing facilities.”
- **May reduce development costs and duplicative infrastructure/zoning**: The bill’s findings state clarification will “reduce unnecessary development costs” and “avoid duplicative zoning and infrastructure” by focusing spacing on consumer-facing retail locations.
- **Maintains retail-focused community/public safety buffer**: The bill retains the **1,200 feet / 1 mile** spacing rule for retail establishments, which the findings tie to “community-impact and public-safety concerns.”
- **Preserves regulatory authority**: The synopsis states the change preserves the Commissioner’s “full regulatory authority over licensing, inspection, and enforcement.”

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Clarifies § 1354(e) applies to retail only**: Amends the refusal ground so the Commissioner “shall refuse to grant a **retail marijuana license**” when spacing thresholds are violated.
- **Retains core retail spacing thresholds**:
  - **1,200 feet** in incorporated city/town.
  - **1 mile** in unincorporated/rural areas.
  - Discretion to grant if **< 1 mile but > 9/10 of 1 mile** in unincorporated/rural areas.
- **Retains existing retail exceptions**:
  - Does not apply to “existing retail license” or “sale, transfer of ownership, or renewal.”
  - Allows certain relocations, including moving within **500 feet** by accessible public road/street, or moving within the same shopping center/mall.

## Who Benefits
- **Non-retail marijuana businesses** (cultivation, manufacturing, testing) that may seek **co-location** or siting flexibility without being constrained by retail spacing rules (as stated in the synopsis).
- **Applicants/developers** of non-retail facilities who may avoid “unnecessary development costs” and “duplicative zoning and infrastructure” (bill findings).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected

- **Potential new retail applicants in constrained geographies**: Retail spacing refusal rules remain in place at **1,200 feet / 1 mile**, which may continue to limit new retail license approvals near existing retail establishments.
