## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 11 to expand Delaware courts’ authority to waive, suspend, modify, or not impose criminal-case fines and fees based on inability to pay, create a defined “hardship waiver” process (including presumptions of hardship), and require related reporting.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new **§ 4104A “Hardship waiver”** process covering criminal-case (and motor vehicle offense) monetary obligations **except those originating in the Voluntary Assessment Center (VAC)**.
- Requires the sentencing court to **advise defendants on the record and in writing** about the hardship waiver; **defense counsel must also advise** defendants about the waiver and required evidence.
- Establishes a **presumption** that a defendant qualifies for a hardship waiver if the defendant submits evidence of specified conditions (e.g., **receipt of listed public benefits**, **representation by Office of Defense Services**, **>3 days pretrial detention in the prior 6 months**, or **a sentence of incarceration**).
- Even with a presumption, the court may still impose fines/fees **only if it finds doing so (in whole or part) is “in the interest of justice,”** and must **document reasons** in the sentencing order when overriding the presumption.
- Authorizes courts, **notwithstanding any law or court rule to the contrary**, to **waive/suspend/modify** fines or fees **including those otherwise deemed mandatory or not subject to waiver**.
- Creates/clarifies **post-sentencing access**: a defendant denied a waiver at sentencing may **apply later** under court rules; courts may promulgate rules for evidence/standards/procedure and must provide rules to defendants if promulgated.
- Modifies restitution judgment treatment: judgments that include restitution plus other amounts are exempt from Title 10 judgment-expiration rules **only for the restitution portion**.
- Effective date: **180 days after enactment**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces financial burden for people unable to pay:** Courts may waive fines/fees in whole or part based on evidence of hardship, and presumptions apply for specific indicators (e.g., Medicaid, SNAP, SSI/SSDI, TANF, veterans’ benefits; ODS representation; incarceration-related criteria). (§ 4104A(c))
- **Improves procedural clarity and access:** Mandatory advisements by the court (record + writing) and defense counsel increase the likelihood eligible defendants learn about relief. (§ 4104A(b))
- **Creates a mechanism for already-sentenced individuals:** Allows post-sentencing submission of evidence and later application if waiver not granted at sentencing. (§ 4104A(c)(1), (e))
- **Transparency when court overrides presumption:** Requires documentation in the sentencing order when the court imposes monetary penalties despite presumptive hardship. (§ 4104A(d))
- **Data reporting:** Annual report must include number of hardship waivers granted and amounts waived/not imposed. (§ 4107(b)(4))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential revenue reductions:** Fiscal note states revenue loss to the **General Fund** (criminal fines) and to **various statutory fees/costs/assessments** is **indeterminable** for FY2026–FY2028 due to unknown eligibility and grant rates. (Fiscal Note, Cost table)
- **Administrative workload increase:** Courts must administer a waiver process, conduct hardship hearings (including post-sentencing), provide advisements and possibly provide promulgated rules, and document reasons when overriding presumptions. (§ 4104A(b), (c), (d), (g))
- **Reduced collections from certain cases may be substantial:** Fiscal note estimates **40%** of FY2024 collections may qualify for hardship waiver consideration (excluding VAC collections). (Fiscal Note, Assumption 3(b))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Interest of justice” standard not defined** for when a court may still impose fines/fees despite presumptive hardship. (§ 4104A(c)(2))
- **Evidence requirements and procedures** are left to court rules (“may promulgate rules”); the statute does not specify timelines for promulgation or uniform standards. (§ 4104A(g))
- **Scope of “fee” includes “any other monetary obligation”** related to a criminal case or motor vehicle offense (excluding VAC), which could raise questions about which specific assessments fall within/outside the waiver. (§ 4104A(a))
- **No numeric threshold for “income too low”** or what constitutes “undue hardship.” (§ 4104A(c)(3)(d)–(e))

## Key Policy Changes
- **§ 4101(a):** Defendant must pay costs imposed by a court **unless the defendant qualifies for a hardship waiver under § 4104A**.
- **§ 4101(b)(5):** Clarifies that when a judgment includes **restitution plus other fines/fees**, the Title 10 § 4711 exemption applies **only to the restitution portion** (not the entire judgment).
- **§ 4104(a)(1)a.:** Court may order payment at sentencing **where the court finds ability to pay** (adds ability-to-pay condition).
- **Creates new § 4104A “Hardship waiver”:**
  - Defines covered “fee” broadly but **excludes VAC-originating obligations**.
  - Requires court and defense counsel advisements about waiver and evidence.
  - Establishes presumptive hardship triggers (public benefits list; ODS representation; **>3 days** pretrial detention in prior **6 months**; incarceration sentence).
  - Allows courts to consider additional hardship factors if no presumption applies (incarceration history; residential mental health facility in prior **6 months**; homelessness in prior **6 months**; low income/undue hardship; other persuasive hardship).
  - Requires written reasons if overriding presumption.
  - Allows later applications under court rules.
  - Grants courts broad authority to waive/suspend/modify **including “mandatory” amounts**, notwithstanding other law/rule.
  - Authorizes courts to promulgate procedural rules and provide them to defendants if promulgated.
- **§ 4105(d):** Repeals prior language allowing an additional imprisonment sentence (up to **30 days**) in lieu of payment where a defendant sentenced to imprisonment is ordered to pay a fine/costs/restitution. (Marked “[Repealed.]”)
- **§ 4107(b)(4):** Annual report must include waiver counts and amounts waived/not imposed under § 4104A.

## Who Benefits
- Defendants who demonstrate inability to pay, including those who submit evidence that they:
  - receive listed public assistance benefits (e.g., **Medicaid**, **SNAP**, **TANF**, **SSI**, **SSDI**, **LIHEAP**, veterans’ pension/disability, subsidized housing/daycare, etc.); or
  - were represented by the **Office of Defense Services**; or
  - were detained pretrial in Delaware **more than 3 days** in the **6 months** before sentencing; or
  - were sentenced to incarceration in the case. (§ 4104A(c)(2))
- Individuals previously sentenced to pay fines/fees who seek post-sentencing relief. (§ 4104A(c)(1), (e))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- State and special funds receiving fine/fee revenues, to the extent courts waive or do not impose amounts:
  - **General Fund** (criminal fines collections).
  - Statutory fee/assessment recipients identified in the fiscal note (e.g., Court Security fee, Victim’s Rights fee, Victim Compensation Fund, Transportation Trust Fund fee, Voluntary Ambulance Fund fee, Videophone fee, Fund to Combat Violent Crime), to the extent within the Act’s scope. (Fiscal Note)
- Court administrative resources may be strained due to increased hearings/process requirements. (§ 4104A; § 4107)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** “This Act takes effect **180 days after its enactment**.” (Section 2)
- **Pretrial detention lookback period for presumption:** detention **>3 days** in the **6 months prior to sentencing**. (§ 4104A(c)(2)c)
- **Additional hardship factor lookback periods:** residential mental health facility and homelessness criteria reference the **preceding 6 months**. (§ 4104A(c)(3)(b)–(c))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Mandatory advisements:** Sentencing court must advise the defendant **on the record and in writing** of waiver availability; defense counsel must also advise defendant regarding waiver and evidence. (§ 4104A(b))
- **Documentation requirement:** If court imposes fines/fees despite presumptive hardship, court must **document the reason in its sentencing order**. (§ 4104A(d))
- **Rule provision requirement (conditional):** If courts promulgate hardship-hearing rules, a copy **must be given to a defendant at or before sentencing**. (§ 4104A(g))
- **Reporting requirement:** Annual report must include number of waivers granted and amounts waived/not imposed. (§ 4107(b)(4))
