## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 10, Chapter 43 of the Delaware Code to create a statutory evidentiary privilege for confidential communications between a “victim” and a qualified “victim advocate,” with limited exceptions (imminent harm prevention and mandatory child-abuse reporting).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes a **“victim advocate privilege”** covering **confidential communications** and certain **records** created while providing victim services.  
- Defines **“victim”** broadly to include persons identified as a crime victim in charging documents/police reports and persons seeking help for **domestic violence, abuse, sexual offenses, caregiver abuse, human trafficking, or stalking** (even outside the criminal justice system).  
- Limits who qualifies as a **“victim advocate”** to an employee (or supervised volunteer) of a victim services agency who completed at least **20 hours of advocacy training** (documented by the agency).  
- States the privilege **does not extend to victim services employees of a law-enforcement agency**.  
- Allows the privilege to be claimed by the **victim**, certain **parents/guardians/custodians** (with conflict/defendant exclusions), and the **victim advocate**.  
- Provides that the privilege **continues if the victim is unreachable** and ends only upon **written waiver** or the **victim’s death**.  
- Creates exceptions where the advocate may disclose to prevent **imminent death or serious physical injury**, and where disclosure is required by **16 Del. C. § 903** (mandatory reporting).  
- Grants **immunity from liability** for good-faith disclosures made under the exceptions, with **good faith presumed**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Encourages victims to seek services and speak candidly** by making communications and related records privileged and generally not revealable. (Bill text: prohibits revealing confidential communications/records, § 4319A(b).)
- **Clear, defined exceptions** allow disclosure to prevent imminent serious harm and to comply with mandatory reporting under **16 Del. C. § 903**. (§ 4319A(e).)
- **Defines minimum training threshold (20 hours)** for advocates, potentially supporting more consistent professional standards for privileged communications. (§ 4319A(a)(4).)
- **Protects advocates from liability** when they disclose in good faith under the exceptions; presumption of good faith may reduce legal risk in close calls. (§ 4319A(f).)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Restricts access to evidence** by making certain communications and records privileged and generally non-disclosable, which could limit information available in legal proceedings. (§ 4319A(b).)
- **Excludes law-enforcement-based victim services employees** from the privilege, potentially creating unequal confidentiality protections depending on where the victim seeks services. (§ 4319A(b).)
- **Privilege termination upon victim death** could allow disclosure after death (unless otherwise protected), potentially affecting posthumous privacy. (§ 4319A(d).)

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill states advocates may not “divulge records kept during the course of providing victim services” (§ 4319A(b)), but it does not detail **which records** are covered (e.g., third-party documents, emails, text messages) beyond the general phrase.
- The scope of “**victim services employees of a law-enforcement agency**” excluded from privilege is not further defined (e.g., contractors housed within an agency, grant-funded positions). (§ 4319A(b).)
- The bill references mandatory reporting under **16 Del. C. § 903** but does not restate what that statute covers; interpretation relies on external law. (§ 4319A(e)(2).)
- The standard “reasonably believes” disclosure is “necessary” to prevent harm is included, but **no procedure** is specified for how courts evaluate necessity or what must be disclosed. (§ 4319A(e)(1).)

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates new/updated **10 Del. C. § 4319A (Victim advocate privilege)** establishing:
  - Definitions for **confidential communication**, **crisis services**, **victim**, **victim advocate**, **victim services**, and **victim services agency**. (§ 4319A(a).)
  - A rule that, unless otherwise required by law, a victim advocate **may not reveal confidential communications** or **divulge records** created/kept while providing victim services. (§ 4319A(b).)
  - An explicit limitation: the privilege **does not extend** to victim services employees of a **law-enforcement agency**. (§ 4319A(b).)
  - Who may claim the privilege: **victim**, certain **parent/guardian/custodian** (with exclusions if defendant or conflicts), and **victim advocate**. (§ 4319A(c).)
  - Duration/termination: continues even if victim unreachable; ends upon **written waiver** or **death of the victim**. (§ 4319A(d).)
  - Exceptions: disclosure permitted/required for **imminent death or serious physical injury prevention** and for reports required under **16 Del. C. § 903**. (§ 4319A(e).)
  - **Immunity** for good-faith disclosures under exceptions; **good faith presumed**. (§ 4319A(f).)
- Sets a qualification threshold: victim advocate must have at least **20 hours of training** in an advocacy course of instruction (agency-documented). (§ 4319A(a)(4).)

## Who Benefits
- **Victims** seeking victim services for domestic violence, abuse, sexual offenses, caregiver abuse, human trafficking, or stalking, and those identified as victims in criminal justice documents. (§ 4319A(a)(3).)
- **Victim advocates and victim services agencies** (non-law-enforcement) whose communications/records are protected and who receive immunity for good-faith disclosures under the exceptions. (§ 4319A(b), (f).)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Parties seeking disclosure** of victim–advocate communications/records in legal proceedings (e.g., litigants or others pursuing evidence), due to the creation of a privilege. (§ 4319A(b).)
- **Victim services employees of law-enforcement agencies**, because the statute states the privilege does not extend to them. (§ 4319A(b).)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Confidentiality requirement:** A victim advocate “may not reveal” confidential communications or “divulge records” kept during victim services, unless otherwise required by law. (§ 4319A(b).)
- **Exceptions/authorized disclosures:** Allowed/required disclosures for imminent harm prevention and mandatory reporting under **16 Del. C. § 903**. (§ 4319A(e).)
- **Immunity standard:** Any victim advocate who discloses under subsection (e) **in good faith** is immune from liability; **good faith is presumed** in related proceedings. (§ 4319A(f).)
- **Training threshold (qualification):** Defines “victim advocate” to require **minimum 20 hours training**, documented by the agency. (§ 4319A(a)(4).)
