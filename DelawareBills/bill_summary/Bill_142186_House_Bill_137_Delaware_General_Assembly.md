## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 29 Del. C. § 5955 to clarify that the Merit Employee Relations Board (MERB) does not have jurisdiction to interpret or apply the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) as it applies to state personnel, and to confirm the Secretary of the Department of Human Resources’ authority to implement FLSA compliance without MERB appeal.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Reaffirms that the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (29 U.S.C. § 201 et seq.) supersedes conflicting Delaware state personnel laws and MERB merit system rules, but only to the extent of conflict and only while the FLSA is deemed applicable to state government personnel practices.  
- Changes statutory language to state the Secretary of the Department of Human Resources “has” (not “shall have”) authority to implement FLSA compliance, including identifying conflicts and resolving them via rulings and regulations.  
- Explicitly bars appeals to the MERB of the Secretary’s determinations “regarding the Fair Labor Standards Act as applied to state personnel.”  
- Clarifies (per the Synopsis) that MERB does not have jurisdiction to interpret or apply the FLSA.  
- Directs (per the Synopsis) that disputes about the Secretary’s interpretation of the FLSA for state personnel must be determined “as outlined in” Title 29 of the United States Code (i.e., under federal law pathways).  
- Makes technical drafting corrections to align with the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (per the Synopsis).  
- No dollar amounts, penalties, or appropriations are stated in the bill text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer forum/jurisdiction rules:** The bill expressly states the Secretary’s FLSA determinations “may not be appealed to the Merit Employee Relations Board,” reducing uncertainty about whether MERB can review FLSA-related disputes.  
- **More centralized compliance administration:** Confirms the Secretary’s authority to identify conflicts between state law/merit rules and the FLSA and to resolve conflicts by rulings and regulations (29 Del. C. § 5955 as amended).  
- **Alignment with federal framework:** The Synopsis states disputes must be determined as outlined in Title 29 of the U.S. Code, potentially reducing inconsistent interpretations between state administrative bodies and federal law.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced state-level appeal avenue:** State personnel cannot appeal the Secretary’s FLSA determinations to MERB (explicitly stated), which may limit administrative review options within the merit system framework.  
- **Greater discretion concentrated in one office:** The Secretary is empowered to determine conflicts and resolve them through rulings/regulations; the bill removes MERB as an appeal forum for those determinations (explicitly stated).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **What specific federal dispute pathway applies:** The bill (via Synopsis) says disputes must be determined as outlined in Title 29 of the U.S. Code, but the Delaware statute text does not specify the precise procedural steps (e.g., agency complaint vs. court action).  
- **Scope of “determination regarding the FLSA as applied to state personnel”:** The statute bars MERB appeals of this determination, but does not define the outer boundary of what counts as an FLSA “determination” versus other personnel actions.
