## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 31 to create the **Affordable Rental Housing Program (ARHP)** as a **revolving fund within the Housing Development Fund**, administered by the Delaware State Housing Authority (DSHA), to support and finance affordable rental housing.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates the **Affordable Rental Housing Program (ARHP)** within the **Housing Development Fund** as a **revolving fund** to support affordable rental housing.  
- Targets housing for **very low-income (<50% of AMI)**, **low-income (50%–80% of AMI)**, and **moderate-income (≤ $5,500 above 80% of AMI)** families, plus **elderly individuals** and **individuals with disabilities**.  
- Authorizes **long-term loans** for: **(1) purchasing buildings/land**, **(2) construction/renovation**, and **(3) necessary facilities (e.g., water and waste disposal systems)**.  
- Sets loan interest rate as the **lower** of the rate at **loan approval** or **loan closing**, and requires DSHA to provide **interest credit** to reach a **1% payment rate**.  
- Sets loan structure limits: **amortization not to exceed 50 years**; **term not to exceed 30 years from the date of the loan**; DSHA may limit **prepayment**.  
- Establishes rent-setting rules during the loan term: tenant rent is the **lesser of basic rent** or **30% of tenant adjusted income**; projects must accept **all tenant-based rent subsidies**.  
- Requires projects to have **2+ units per building** and comply with **building codes**, the **Model Energy Code**, and **HUD physical standards**.  
- Requires DSHA to adopt implementing policies/procedures via the **Administrative Procedures Act** and to **annually publish AMI** by region in the **Register of Regulations** and on the **DSHA website**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Creates a dedicated financing mechanism** (a revolving fund) to support affordable rental housing, potentially increasing supply by providing **long-term loans** for acquisition, construction, and renovation.  
- **Improves affordability predictability for tenants** by capping tenant rent at the **lesser of basic rent or 30% of adjusted income** during the loan term.  
- **Reduces financing costs** through required DSHA **interest credit** to a **1% payment rate**, which may help projects pencil out financially.  
- **Facilitates use of rental assistance** by requiring housing projects to **accept all tenant-based rent subsidies**.  
- **Encourages minimum quality standards** by requiring compliance with **HUD physical standards**, applicable **building codes**, and the **Model Energy Code**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative workload** is increased because DSHA must promulgate regulations and procedures under the Administrative Procedures Act and must **annually publish AMI** by region.  
- **Limits on prepayment** may be imposed by DSHA, which could reduce flexibility for borrowers/projects (the bill authorizes DSHA to set such conditions).  
- **Rent-setting uniformity requirement** (“same rate for all similar units”) could reduce an owner’s ability to use differential pricing among similar units within a project during the loan term.

## Key Policy Changes
- Establishes **§ 4036** creating the **Affordable Rental Housing Program (ARHP)** within the Housing Development Fund as a **revolving fund**.  
- Defines income categories tied to **Area Median Income (AMI)**:  
  - **Very low-income:** below **50% of AMI**.  
  - **Low-income:** **50% to 80% of AMI**.  
  - **Moderate-income:** no more than **$5,500 above 80% of AMI**.  
- Authorizes **long-term ARHP loans** for acquisition, construction/rehab, and certain utility-type facilities (water/waste disposal).  
- Requires DSHA **interest credit** so borrowers’ **payment rate is 1%**.  
- Sets loan constraints: **≤50-year amortization**; **≤30-year term** from loan date; DSHA may restrict **prepayment**.  
- Sets tenant rent during loan term as the **lesser of basic rent or 30% of adjusted income**; mandates acceptance of **tenant-based rent subsidies**.  
- Allows DSHA to require **first priority** to applicants living in **substandard housing**.  
- Requires compliance with **building codes**, **Model Energy Code**, and **HUD physical standards**; minimum **2 units per building**.  
- Requires DSHA to promulgate implementing rules and **annually publish AMI** by region.
