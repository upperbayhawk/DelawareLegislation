## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 30 of the Delaware Code to increase Delaware’s Child and Dependent Care Expense Tax Credit to match the federal credit at **100%** (up from **50%**) for the same tax year.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Increases Delaware’s child and dependent care expense tax credit from **50% to 100%** of the **federal** child and dependent care expense credit for the same tax year.  
- Applies to **resident individuals** claiming the credit under **30 Del. C. § 1114(a)**.  
- Maintains the existing cap: the Delaware credit **cannot exceed the taxpayer’s Delaware tax otherwise due**.  
- Keeps the existing rule for certain married taxpayers: if spouses file a **joint federal return** but calculate Delaware taxes **separately**, the credit is limited in how it may be applied.  
- Does not specify a new credit amount in dollars; the value depends on the taxpayer’s **allowable federal credit**.  
- The bill text provided does **not** state an effective date.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Larger tax relief for eligible filers:** By moving from a **50%** to **100%** match of the federal credit, eligible resident taxpayers may receive a **higher Delaware tax credit** (subject to the tax-liability cap in § 1114(a)).  
- **Simplifies alignment with federal credit:** Pegging the state credit to **100% of the federal credit** keeps the state calculation directly tied to the already-determined federal amount for the same tax year.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **No benefit beyond tax liability:** The credit still **cannot exceed Delaware tax otherwise due**, so taxpayers with low or zero Delaware income tax liability may not realize the full increase.  
- **Continued limitation for certain married filers:** For spouses filing a **joint federal return** but computing Delaware taxes separately, the credit may only be applied against the spouse with the **lower taxable income** and cannot exceed that spouse’s tax (per § 1114(b) as referenced in the bill).

- **Refundability not addressed:** The bill retains the “shall not exceed the tax otherwise due” limitation but does not state whether any portion is refundable.
