## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s child care and dependent care expense tax credit to increase the credit for certain lower-income residents and apply refundability as provided in the amended statute text.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Increases the Delaware child care and dependent care expense credit for **resident individuals with taxable income under $60,000** from **50% to 100%** of the **federal** allowable credit for the same tax year.  
- Keeps the credit for **resident individuals with taxable income of $60,000 or more** at **50%** of the federal allowable credit.  
- The under-$60,000 credit remains described in statute as **refundable**.  
- For both income groups, the credit is capped: it **may not exceed the tax otherwise due** under Delaware income tax.  
- Renumbers an existing subsection regarding spouses filing jointly federally but separately for Delaware (from **(b)** to **(c)**) and retains its limitation.  
- Applies starting with **tax years beginning after December 31, 2025** (i.e., **tax year 2026** and later).  
- Credit amount continues to be tied to the **child and dependent care expense credit allowable for federal income tax purposes**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Larger tax relief for eligible lower-income filers:** Under-$60,000 residents can claim **100% (up from 50%)** of the federal allowable credit amount for the same tax year.  
- **Maintains targeted approach:** The enhanced percentage applies only below the **$60,000 taxable income** threshold; the **$60,000+** group is unchanged at **50%**.  
- **Statutory refundability retained for under-$60,000 group:** The credit is described as a **refundable credit** for eligible residents with taxable income under $60,000.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **No increase for taxpayers at or above $60,000 taxable income:** The bill explicitly leaves that group’s credit at **50%** of the federal credit.  
- **Credit cannot exceed Delaware tax due:** For under-$60,000 and $60,000+ filers, the bill retains language that the credit **shall not exceed the tax otherwise due**, which may limit benefit for taxpayers with low or no Delaware tax liability.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Interaction between “refundable” label and the cap:** Subsection (a) calls the credit “refundable,” but also states it cannot exceed the **tax otherwise due**; the bill text does not clarify how refundability operates given this limitation.  
- **Definition/measurement of “taxable income”:** The bill uses “taxable income” and the **$60,000** threshold but does not restate how taxable income is calculated (relies on existing Title 30 definitions).  
- **Administrative guidance timing:** No deadlines are provided for updated forms, instructions, or guidance.

## Key Policy Changes
- **§ 1114(a) change (under $60,000 taxable income):**  
  - Changes the credit percentage from **50% to 100%** of the **federal child and dependent care expense credit** allowable for the same tax year.  
  - Retains: credit described as **refundable** and **cannot exceed Delaware tax otherwise due**.
- **§ 1114(b) (at least $60,000 taxable income):**  
  - Retains credit at **50%** of the federal allowable credit.  
  - Retains cap: **cannot exceed tax otherwise due**.
- **Renumbering of spouse provision:** Existing spouse allocation rule is renumbered from **(b)** to **(c)**, continuing to limit application of the (refundable) credit when spouses file jointly federally but separately for Delaware.

## Who Benefits
- **Resident individuals with Delaware taxable income under $60,000** who have eligible child care and dependent care expenses and qualify for the **federal** credit; their Delaware credit percentage increases to **100%** of the federal allowable credit.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Resident individuals with taxable income of $60,000 or more** do not receive an increased credit (remains **50%** of the federal credit).  
- **Couples filing jointly federally but separately for Delaware** may be limited because the credit “may only be applied against the tax imposed on the spouse with the lower taxable income” and cannot exceed that spouse’s tax.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025** (applies starting **tax year 2026**).
