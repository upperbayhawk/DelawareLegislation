## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To update Delaware Code provisions governing the Delaware Institute for Dental Education and Research (DIDER) Board of Directors, including board composition, voting status, terms/term limits, officer selection procedures, required diversity training, and clarifications to DIDER’s purposes and duties—while reaffirming support for hospital-based dental residency programs tied to licensure requirements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Reaffirms legislative findings that DIDER has functioned primarily as a **funding conduit** supporting **hospital-based residency training programs** required under **Chapter 11, Title 24**, and declares continued support should be perpetuated.  
- Changes the DIDER Board structure to **12 total members**: **9 voting** and **3 non-voting (ex officio)**.  
- Revises the **Governor-appointed** voting membership and adds a voting seat for the **Christiana Care General Practice Residency Program**.  
- Adds **non-voting ex officio** members from **Delaware State University (Wesley College of Health and Behavioral Sciences)** and the **University of Delaware (Center for Health Profession Studies)**, plus the **State Dental Director (or designee)**.  
- Updates board member **term rules** to **3-year terms** (non–ex officio), and removes prior language allowing reappointment up to **3 times** and a **9-year** maximum service limit.  
- Establishes new procedures for selecting **Chair** and **Vice Chair**, including a **nominating committee** process and **3-year officer terms**, with **no more than 3 consecutive terms**.  
- Requires all board members, **before serving**, to complete training on the **importance of diversity in healthcare** and its role in reducing disparities and increasing access to care.  
- Clarifies that invited university/committee **consultants may attend** relevant meetings but **do not vote**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer governance structure:** Specifies **12 members** with defined **voting vs. non-voting** roles, which may improve board operations and clarity of authority.  
- **Broader institutional input (non-voting):** Adds ex officio representation from **DSU** and **UD**, potentially strengthening coordination on health professions pathways (explicitly non-voting in text).  
- **Residency program alignment:** Adds a voting seat from the **Christiana Care General Practice Residency Program**, potentially improving alignment with Delaware’s hospital-based residency training needs referenced in the findings and purposes.  
- **Diversity training requirement:** Mandates board-member training focused on diversity in healthcare and how it reduces disparities and improves access, potentially shaping policy choices consistent with that goal.  
- **More structured leadership selection:** Requires a **nominating committee** review for Chair candidates and sets term parameters for Chair/Vice Chair, potentially improving leadership continuity and accountability.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Removal of explicit board member term limits:** The prior explicit limit (“reappointed 3 times” and “no longer than 9 years”) is deleted and replaced with 3-year terms without an explicit cap, potentially allowing longer tenure (change is explicit in amended text).  
- **Potential reduced direct representation of certain roles previously listed:** The replaced membership list removes an explicitly listed voting member from the **Delaware Higher Education Office** and a specified relationship to **Christiana Care Health Services** within the Delaware State Dental Society appointees (the new structure instead includes DSU/UD as non-voting and Christiana Care GPR as voting).  
- **Additional compliance step for members:** Mandatory diversity training is an added prerequisite for serving (time/administrative burden not quantified in the bill).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- How “selected in concert with DIDER” applies to the **Chair of the Delaware Health Care Commission (or designee)** serving as an ex officio voting member—process and documentation are not specified.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Findings updated:** States DIDER has been limited to supporting hospital-based residency training programs required under **Chapter 11, Title 24**, and that continued support should be perpetuated.  
- **Board composition rewritten:**  
  - **12 members total** = **9 voting** + **3 non-voting**.  
  - **Voting members (9):**  
    - **8 Governor appointees:** public representative; federally-qualified health center representative; **3** Delaware State Dental Society reps (in consultation with the Society); **1** State Board of Dentistry and Dental Hygiene rep (in consultation); **1** dental hygienist (in consultation with Delaware Dental Hygienist Association); **1** member from the **Christiana Care General Practice Residency Program**.  
    - **Chair of the Delaware Health Care Commission (or designee)** as an ex officio voting member (selected in concert with DIDER).  
  - **Non-voting members (3):** ex officio from **DSU Wesley College of Health and Behavioral Sciences**; ex officio from **UD Center for Health Profession Studies**; and the **Delaware State Dental Director (or designee)**.  
- **Terms revised:** Non–ex officio members serve **3-year terms**; vacancy appointments fill the remainder; prior staggered initial terms and the explicit **9-year maximum** are removed.  
- **Chair/Vice Chair process:** Chair candidates reviewed by a **nominating committee**; Chair term is **3 years** with **no more than 3 consecutive terms**; Vice Chair selected similarly with same term rules.  
- **Mandatory training:** Board member must complete **diversity in healthcare** training **before** becoming a member.  
- **Consultant participation:** Temple admissions/UD HPEC/similar DSU committee consultant may be invited to relevant meetings and **does not vote**.  
- **Purposes/duties edits:** Updates language to emphasize CoDA-accredited hospital residencies, dental hygiene/assisting education expansion, incentives such as loan repayment (as approved by the Delaware Health Care Commission), and access for underserved populations; allows affiliation agreements subject to Commission approval/signature.

## Who Benefits
