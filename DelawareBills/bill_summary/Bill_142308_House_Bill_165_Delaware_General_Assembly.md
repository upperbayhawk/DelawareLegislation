## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 24 of the Delaware Code to authorize **physician assistants**, **certified nurse practitioners**, and **occupational therapists** to perform **dry needling**, while establishing **referral**, **advertising**, and **board rulemaking** requirements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes **physician assistants (PAs)** to perform dry needling in **Chapter 17, Title 24**, subject to restrictions.  
- Authorizes **certified nurse practitioners (NPs)** to perform dry needling in **Chapter 19, Title 24**, subject to restrictions.  
- Clarifies that **occupational therapy services** may include **dry needling** in **Chapter 20, Title 24**, and authorizes occupational therapists to perform it, subject to restrictions.  
- Defines “dry needling” as use of a **thin filiform needle** to treat **neuromusculoskeletal pain and movement impairments**, based on **Western medical concepts**, and requiring an exam and diagnosis by the practitioner (PA/NP/OT as applicable).  
- Requires a **written physician referral specific to dry needling** before the procedure for PAs, NPs, and OTs; referral may be transmitted **digitally**.  
- Prohibits PAs, NPs, and OTs from advertising or holding themselves out as an **acupuncturist** unless they are a **licensed acupuncturist**.  
- Requires the relevant **Board** to establish **training and practice standards** for dry needling by rule and regulation.  
- Effective **6 months after enactment into law**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expands authorized provider types** who may perform dry needling (PAs, NPs, OTs), which may increase availability of the service (explicitly authorized by the Act’s synopsis and code amendments).
- **Patient protection measures**: requires a **written physician referral specific to dry needling** before treatment (Pages 1–3: §§ 1774F(a)(1), 1939(a)(1), 2022(a)(1)).
- **Standards setting**: requires the Board to adopt **training and practice standards** via regulation (Pages 1–3: §§ 1774F(b), 1939(b), 2022(b)).
- **Truth-in-advertising safeguard**: bars practitioners from representing themselves as **acupuncturists** unless licensed as such (Pages 1–3: §§ 1774F(a)(2), 1939(a)(2), 2022(a)(2)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added access step**: dry needling cannot be performed without a **written physician referral specific to dry needling**, potentially creating an additional administrative hurdle for patients and practitioners (Pages 1–3: §§ 1774F(a)(1), 1939(a)(1), 2022(a)(1)).
- **Implementation burden on boards**: the Board “must establish” training and practice standards by regulation, which may require additional regulatory work (Pages 1–3: §§ 1774F(b), 1939(b), 2022(b)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill states “The **Board** must establish” standards, but does not specify in the provided text **which board(s)** for each profession beyond the general reference (e.g., PA Board, Nursing Board, OT Board).  
- The bill requires a **written physician referral specific to dry needling**, but does not define:
  - duration/expiration of a referral,
  - whether standing orders are allowed,
  - whether the referring physician must have examined the patient.  
- Training requirements are deferred to regulation; the bill text does not specify **minimum hours**, competencies, or supervision requirements.
- “Requires a [profession] exam and diagnosis” is included in the definition of dry needling for each profession, but the bill does not clarify whether this is intended to restrict use only after a **fresh exam/diagnosis** for each treatment episode.
