## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 to update Delaware’s statewide standards and processes for K–3 reading screening and literacy intervention, add a mechanism for district/charter approval of alternative reading screeners, and require the Department of Education (DOE) to identify and support schools with persistently low 3rd-grade reading proficiency.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires K–3 students to be screened **3 times per year** using a **DOE-approved universal reading screener** (existing requirement continued/clarified).  
- Requires districts/charters to provide **1 or more** DOE-aligned literacy interventions for students identified with a **potential reading deficiency** (existing requirement continued).  
- Starting **December 1, 2026**, DOE must **review and update** the lists of approved screeners and interventions **at least every 3 years**.  
- Specifies literacy intervention approaches must include explicit instruction in **phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, and comprehension**, include **decodable/phonetic text strategies**, and may include **multisensory approaches/technology**.  
- Creates an application process for districts/charters to seek DOE approval of an **alternative reading screener**; applications due **by March 15** before the school year of use; approved tools must be added to DOE’s list.  
- Beginning **February 28, 2026**, DOE must annually identify schools with a **significant level** of students **below proficient** on the state **3rd grade reading assessment** for **2 consecutive years** and provide supports.  
- DOE must provide **professional learning at no cost** and **during the contractual day**.  
- Fiscal note estimates **FY2026 one-time costs $375,000–$625,000**; and **FY2027–FY2028 ongoing costs $50,000–$100,000 per year** (vendor review of alternative screeners).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More defined intervention standards**: Statute requires interventions to incorporate explicit, systematic components (e.g., phonemic awareness, phonics, decodable text), potentially improving consistency of literacy practices statewide.  
- **Flexibility with oversight**: Districts/charters may use an **alternative screener** if DOE approves it, allowing local choice while retaining statewide minimum requirements for screeners.  
- **Targeted support for struggling schools**: DOE must identify schools below proficient for **2 consecutive years** and provide supports (coaching, professional development, monitoring), potentially focusing resources where performance is persistently low.  
- **Transparency and reporting**: Annual district/charter reporting (by **October 31**) and DOE public reporting (by **December 31**) continue, supporting public accountability.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added administrative workload for DOE**: Fiscal note states DOE lacks in-house capacity and anticipates contracting for (1) stakeholder facilitation and tool evaluation (**$250,000–$500,000 one-time**) and (2) annual review of alternative assessments (**$50,000–$100,000 ongoing**).  
- **Added process steps for districts/charters seeking alternative screeners**: Requires an application by **March 15** preceding the year of use, which may limit adoption timelines.  
- **Undefined identification threshold**: DOE must identify schools with a “**significant level**” below proficient; the term is not quantified in the bill text, which may create uncertainty about which schools are flagged.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What numerical threshold constitutes a “**significant level** of students below proficient” under the **3rd grade reading assessment** (the bill does not define a percentage or cutoff).  
- What specific criteria, documentation, and timeline DOE will use to **approve alternative screeners** beyond “meets the requirements of this section.”  
- Whether DOE supports for identified schools are mandatory in a particular form or intensity; the bill lists supports that “**may include**” various actions.

## Key Policy Changes
- **DOE list updates**: Starting **December 1, 2026**, DOE must review/update the screener and intervention lists **at least every 3 years** (in consultation with LEA curriculum/special education supervisors and elementary teachers).  
- **Intervention approach requirements**: Literacy intervention approaches must:  
  - Use explicit, systematic, sequential instruction in **phonemic awareness, phonics, vocabulary, fluency, comprehension**;  
  - Incorporate **decodable/phonetic text** instructional strategies;  
  - Include (as needed) **individual instruction, multisensory approaches, tutoring, mentoring, technology**;  
  - Include strategies/materials connecting access to **Tier 1** instruction, including **knowledge-building texts** and **vocabulary building**.  
- **Alternative screener pathway**: DOE must provide a process for districts/charters to apply for approval of an alternative screener; **applications due March 15** preceding the school year of use; approved screeners must be added to DOE’s list.  
- **Low proficiency identification/support**: Beginning **February 28, 2026**, DOE must annually identify schools with a significant level of students below proficient on the 3rd-grade reading assessment for **2 consecutive years**, provide supports, and establish monitoring procedures.

## Who Benefits
- K–3 students identified as at risk for reading difficulties (including characteristics of dyslexia) who receive aligned interventions.  
- Families/guardians receiving written communication about screening results and interventions.  
- Schools receiving DOE supports (coaching, professional development, improvement plan support) after being identified under the 3rd-grade proficiency criteria.  
- Districts/charters seeking flexibility to use an alternative screener that meets statutory requirements and receives DOE approval.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- DOE, due to increased workload and need for vendor support/costs to implement consultation, approvals, and school support/monitoring.  
- Districts/charters and educators, due to administrative responsibilities tied to screening, data entry/compilation, and required communications (though the bill also requires contractual-day time and coverage for these tasks).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon signature by the Governor** (fiscal note assumption).  
- **March 15** (annually): deadline for districts/charters to submit applications to DOE to use an alternative reading screener in the following school year.  
- **October 31** (annually): districts/charters must report to DOE the number/percentage of students identified with potential reading deficiency (disaggregated) and interventions provided.  
- **December 31** (annually): DOE must compile district/charter reports and deliver a comprehensive report to specified officials and publish it on DOE’s website.  
- **February 28, 2026** (and annually thereafter): DOE must identify certain schools below proficient for **2 consecutive years** and provide supports/monitoring.  
- **December 1, 2026** (and at least every 3 years thereafter): DOE must review and update approved screener and intervention lists.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- Compliance requirements include:  
  - District/charter screening of K–3 students **3 times per year** with a DOE-approved universal screener; written parent/guardian communication; provision of educator contractual-day time and coverage for required tasks.

  - District/charter annual reporting to DOE by **October 31**.  
  - DOE annual public reporting by **December 31**.  
  - DOE establishment of procedures to monitor implementation of literacy supports for identified schools (calls, meetings, site visits, document review).
