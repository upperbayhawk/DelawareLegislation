## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends the City of Milford Charter to (1) add/clarify City Council powers (including eminent domain and proclamations), (2) clarify the Mayor’s committee-creation authority (with Council consent), (3) change terms of office from 2 years to 3 years on a phased schedule, (4) adjust the annual organizational meeting time, and (5) set eligibility/disqualification rules tied to owing delinquent City debts.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes Milford City Council to exercise **eminent domain** for property acquisition under **Title 29, Chapter 95**, **excluding land used solely for recreational purposes**.  
- Authorizes City Council, by **majority vote**, to issue **proclamations** recognizing achievements, historical events, special days/months for civic/social purposes, and other matters Council deems appropriate.  
- Clarifies the Mayor may establish committees, appoint members, and designate chairs **with the consent of City Council**.  
- Changes the **Mayor’s term** from **2 years to 3 years** for any newly elected/appointed Mayor **after January 1, 2026** (beginning with the **2026 election** and each election thereafter).  
- Changes **Councilmember terms** from **2 years to 3 years** for any newly elected/appointed Councilmember **after January 1, 2027** (for the **2027 and 2028 elections**, and each election thereafter).  
- Changes the annual organizational meeting time to **7:00 p.m.** (from 6:00 p.m.) on the **second Monday following the annual election**.  
- Makes people who are **in arrears** and owe the City certain debts (and the debts are **no longer appealable**) **ineligible** to be elected Mayor or Councilperson.  
- Requires removal: a sitting Mayor/Councilperson who becomes **more than 2 months** in arrears on covered City debts (and **no longer appealable**) is **disqualified**, and the seat is **declared vacant** per Charter **Article 4.05**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expands municipal acquisition tools:** Expressly allows eminent domain for property acquisition under **Title 29, Ch. 95**, potentially supporting City projects that require property acquisition (except **recreation-only** land).  
- **Governance clarity:** Explicitly ties the Mayor’s committee authority to **City Council consent**, reducing ambiguity about who must approve committee creation.  
- **Administrative continuity:** Moving from **2-year to 3-year terms** may reduce election frequency and provide longer continuity in leadership (Mayor beginning **2026**, Council beginning **2027/2028**).  
- **Financial accountability for officials:** Establishes clear eligibility and removal rules for officials who are **delinquent** in City obligations (taxes, special assessments, utility bills, and other fees/costs) once **no longer appealable**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Broader use of eminent domain:** Granting explicit eminent domain power (even with the recreation-only exception) may increase the City’s ability to compel property acquisition under **Title 29, Ch. 95**, affecting property owners subject to takings.  
- **Reduced electoral frequency:** Extending terms from **2 years to 3 years** delays voters’ next opportunity to change leadership compared with the prior schedule.  
- **Automatic disqualification risk:** Officials who become **more than 2 months** in arrears on covered debts (and no longer appealable) must be **disqualified** and their seat vacated, which could lead to abrupt vacancies.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill excludes eminent domain for “land to be used **solely for recreational purposes**,” but does not define “solely,” potentially raising questions for mixed-use projects.  
- “Persons in arrears… and **no longer appealable**” is not further defined in the bill text (e.g., what City process determines when a debt is no longer appealable).  
- The Charter language includes “City Council shall consist of **not more than 8** members” while also describing ward-based seats of **2 per ward** (8 total); the bill does not explain how “not more than 8” interacts with the ward allocation if boundaries or structure change.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Eminent domain authority:** Adds Charter authority for City Council to exercise eminent domain for property acquisition under **Title 29, Ch. 95**, **excluding** land used solely for recreational purposes.  
- **Council proclamations:** Allows proclamations by **majority vote** for civic/social recognitions and other matters deemed appropriate.  
- **Mayor committees:** Clarifies the Mayor may establish committees, appoint members, and designate chairs **with City Council consent**.  
- **Term length changes:**  
  - Mayor: **3-year terms** for newly elected/appointed Mayors **after January 1, 2026** (starting with **2026 election** and thereafter).  
  - Councilmembers: **3-year terms** for newly elected/appointed Councilmembers **after January 1, 2027** (for **2027 and 2028 elections**, and thereafter).  
- **Organizational meeting time:** Changes from **6:00 p.m.** to **7:00 p.m.** on the second Monday after the annual election.  
- **Eligibility/removal for delinquent debts:**  
  - Ineligible to be elected if in arrears owing City funds (taxes, special assessments, utility bills, other fees/costs) and **no longer appealable**.  
  - Sitting officials: if **more than 2 months** in arrears on such debts (no longer appealable), they are **disqualified** and the seat is declared vacant per **Article 4.05**.

## Who Benefits
- **City of Milford government/City Council:** Gains explicit Charter authority for eminent domain (with limitation) and for issuing proclamations; clearer governance rules on committees.  
- **Residents seeking continuity:** Potentially benefit from longer **3-year** terms for elected officials (phased in starting **2026** for Mayor and **2027/2028** for Council).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Property owners** whose property could be targeted for acquisition through eminent domain under **Title 29, Ch. 95** (except recreation-only land).  
- **Potential candidates and sitting officials** who are delinquent in City debts:  
  - Candidates who are in arrears (and debts no longer appealable) become **ineligible**.  
  - Sitting officials who become **>2 months** in arrears (and no longer appealable) must vacate office.
