## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Juvenile Offender Civil Citation Program to (1) require peace officers to consciously consider issuing civil citations for qualified juveniles for specified delinquency acts, (2) require documentation in DELJIS when citations are not issued for certain offenses, (3) require annual agency reporting on citation usage to the Police Officer Standards and Training Commission, and (4) add Juvenile Civil Citation Program training to mandatory police training standards.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires peace officers to **consciously consider** issuing a juvenile civil citation for **qualified juvenile offenders** for delinquency acts covered by 10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(2).
- For **12 specified offenses**, if a qualified juvenile is **charged instead of cited**, the officer **must document the reason** in the **Delaware Criminal Justice Information System (DELJIS)**.
- For other covered acts, if the officer charges rather than cites, the officer **may** document the reason (optional).
- Requires **every law-enforcement agency** (as defined in 11 Del. C. § 1913) to submit an **annual report** to the **Police Officer Standards and Training Commission** describing use of juvenile civil citations, including **all instances** where a qualified juvenile was charged instead of cited.
- Adds **training on the Juvenile Civil Citation Offender Program** to police in-service/continued training standards set by the Commission (11 Del. C. § 8404).
- Fiscal note anticipates **one-time DELJIS system changes** costing **$468,176 in FY 2026**.
- **Effective date:** **18 months after enactment into law**; the act was **approved August 20, 2025**, making the effective date **February 20, 2027**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Increased use/consideration of diversion:** Officers must “consciously consider” citations for qualified juveniles, which may increase referrals to the civil citation program for eligible cases (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)a).
- **Improved accountability and transparency:** Mandatory DELJIS documentation for specified offenses and annual reporting by agencies creates traceability of when/why citations were not used (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)b; § 1004A(l)).
- **More consistent statewide practice:** Required training on the Juvenile Civil Citation Offender Program may promote more uniform understanding and use of the program across agencies (11 Del. C. § 8404(a)(5)b).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added administrative workload for officers:** For the listed offenses, officers must enter reasons in DELJIS when charging a qualified juvenile instead of citing (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)b).
- **Added agency reporting burden:** All law-enforcement agencies must compile and submit an annual report including all instances where qualified juveniles were charged instead of cited (10 Del. C. § 1004A(l)).
- **Technology implementation cost and complexity:** DELJIS must build new functionality and reporting mechanisms; fiscal note indicates “significant programming and system design changes” (Fiscal Note assumptions #3).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Mandatory consideration:** Peace officers must “consciously consider” issuing a civil citation for qualified juvenile offenders for covered delinquency acts (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)a).
- **Mandatory documentation in DELJIS for certain offenses when charging instead of citing:** For the offenses listed below, documentation of the reason **must** be entered into **DELJIS** if a qualified juvenile is charged rather than cited (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)b):
  1. Criminal mischief (11 Del. C. § 811)  
  2. Graffiti/possession of graffiti implements (11 Del. C. § 812)  
  3. Criminal trespass 3rd degree (11 Del. C. § 821)  
  4. Criminal trespass 2nd degree (11 Del. C. § 822)  
  5. Shoplifting (11 Del. C. § 840)  
  6. Theft (11 Del. C. § 841)  
  7. Receiving stolen property (11 Del. C. § 851)  
  8. Selling stolen property (11 Del. C. § 852A)  
  9. Unlawful use of payment card (11 Del. C. § 903)  
  10. Disorderly conduct (11 Del. C. § 1301)  
  11. Harassment (11 Del. C. § 1311)  
  12. Loitering (11 Del. C. § 1321)
- **Optional documentation for other covered acts:** For other acts under § 1004A(b)(2), officers **may** document the reason for not issuing a citation when charging instead (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)b).
- **Annual reporting requirement:** Every law-enforcement agency must annually report to the Police Officer Standards and Training Commission on civil citation use, including all instances where qualified juveniles were charged instead of cited (10 Del. C. § 1004A(l)).
- **Training standards updated:** The Commission’s in-service/continued training standards must include training on the Juvenile Civil Citation Offender Program (11 Del. C. § 8404(a)(5)b).

## Who Benefits
- **Qualified juvenile offenders** (as defined): juveniles with **no prior adjudication of delinquency** and **no prior diversion referral within the last 1 year** (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(3)).
- **Police Officer Standards and Training Commission**: receives standardized annual reporting on citation usage (10 Del. C. § 1004A(l)).
- **State oversight stakeholders** (through DELJIS data/reporting): improved tracking of citation decisions for specified offenses (Fiscal Note; 10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)b).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Peace officers**: increased documentation requirements in DELJIS for specified offenses when charging qualified juveniles instead of citing (10 Del. C. § 1004A(b)(4)b).
- **Law-enforcement agencies**: added requirement to produce annual reports including specific instance-level listings (10 Del. C. § 1004A(l)).
- **DELJIS**: required system redesign and implementation workload (Fiscal Note assumptions #3).
