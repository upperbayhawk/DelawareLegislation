## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 29, § 6960 to require prevailing-wage provisions in specifications for certain public works contracts (and aggregates of contracts) above stated dollar thresholds when the project is paid in whole or part with public funds and involves mechanics and/or laborers.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **every public works contract (or aggregate of contracts for a project)** to include a **minimum wage (“prevailing wage”) provision** if it meets specified thresholds and funding/worker criteria.  
- Applies to projects **paid in whole or in part with public funds** where the State or a subdivision is a party and the State appropriated any part of the funds.  
- Thresholds: **over $500,000** for **new construction** (including painting/decorating) and **$45,000** for **alteration, repair, renovation, rehabilitation, demolition, or reconstruction** (including painting/decorating).  
- Prevailing wage is based on **Delaware Department of Labor, Division of Industrial Affairs** determinations for the **county where the work is performed**.  
- The statute text includes methodology tied to **collective bargaining agreement (CBA) rates** prevailing for **4 consecutive years**, otherwise using the **highest rate of the 4 years**, or **Consumer Price Index-Construction** adjustments when CBA rates do not prevail.  
- If prevailing wage cannot be determined due to lack of qualifying agreements, the Department uses its **annual prevailing wage survey**.  
- Provides for a **1-time challenge of the prevailing wage rate per cycle**, as in Department regulations.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardizes wage floors** on covered public works by requiring contract specifications to include **prevailing wage minimums** (explicit requirement in §6960(a)).  
- **County-specific wage setting** may better align wages with local labor markets (wages “prevailing in the county in which the work is to be performed”).  
- **Predictable annual adjustments** are specified (CBA-based adjustments where applicable; otherwise **CPI-Construction**), which may reduce uncertainty in wage updates.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Could **increase labor costs** for covered projects because contractors must pay at least the **prevailing wage** (the bill requires minimum wages in contract specifications for covered projects).  
- May **increase administrative compliance work** for agencies and contractors to ensure specifications include correct prevailing wage provisions and rates set by the Department of Labor (rate determination and incorporation are required elements).

## Key Policy Changes
- Mandates that specifications for covered **public works contracts or aggregated contracts** must include a provision stating **minimum prevailing wages**.  
- Sets coverage thresholds:
  - **New construction** (including painting and decorating): **in excess of $500,000**.  
  - **Alteration/repair/renovation/rehabilitation/demolition/reconstruction** (including painting and decorating): **$45,000**.  
- Confirms prevailing wage determination is made by **Delaware Department of Labor, Division of Industrial Affairs** based on the **county of performance**.  
- Uses CBA-based methodology when CBA rates prevail and meet a **4 consecutive years** condition; otherwise uses the **highest rate of the 4 years** and **CPI-Construction** for annual adjustments when CBA rates do not prevail.  
- Allows a **1-time challenge** to the prevailing wage rate **per cycle** per Department regulations.
