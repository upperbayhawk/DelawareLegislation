## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 21 to tighten Division of Motor Vehicles (DMV) privacy rules—especially limiting disclosure of citizenship/immigration-status-related data and limiting when personal identifiable information (PII) tied to driving privilege cards may be shared, including with federal agencies.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits the DMV (and its officers, employees, and contractors) from knowingly disclosing personal information from motor vehicle records except as specifically allowed.  
- Limits routine disclosure access primarily to **driver license and driver performance records that are “3 years old or less,”** with specified exceptions that may access records **over 3 years old** being retained by DMV.  
- Creates a specific non-disclosure rule for **citizenship or immigration status** and related sensitive identifiers: **Social Security number**, **taxpayer identification number**, and documents that reveal those items generally may not be disclosed.  
- Allows sharing of the above sensitive items only in limited circumstances: **(1) voter registration purposes; (2) with specific approval of the Delaware Attorney General; (3) with a valid court order; (4) where specifically authorized by this section or other state law.**  
- Adds contract requirements for certain entities accessing DMV data, including an **agreement not to use or disclose DMV information for civil immigration enforcement or related purposes** (with stated exemptions).  
- Tightens driving privilege card confidentiality by prohibiting sharing PII with **any federal agency or federal law enforcement** unless there is **specific Delaware Attorney General approval or a valid court order**.  
- Keeps existing penalties and civil remedies for unlawful obtaining/disclosure, including **Class A misdemeanor** exposure and civil damages including **liquidated damages of not less than $2,500**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces exposure of immigration-status-related data**: Explicitly restricts disclosure of “citizenship or immigration status” and related identifiers (SSN, taxpayer ID), limiting risk of misuse.  
- **Stronger protections for driving privilege card applicants/holders**: Bars sharing PII with federal agencies absent **AG approval or court order**, which may increase confidence in using the program.  
- **Adds a contractual compliance hook**: Requires certain requesters who access records electronically/continually to contractually agree to data protection terms, including a **non-use for civil immigration enforcement** clause.  
- **Maintains enforcement tools**: Unlawful disclosures remain subject to **Class A misdemeanor** penalties and civil liability with **minimum $2,500 liquidated damages**, plus possible punitive damages and attorney’s fees.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **More administrative steps to disclose certain data**: Requiring **Delaware Attorney General specific approval** or a **valid court order** for certain releases may slow responses to requests involving restricted information (as written).  
- **Compliance burden for some data users**: Entities accessing records electronically/continually must sign contracts and comply with restrictions on redisclosure and use (including the civil immigration enforcement restriction), which may require process changes.  
- **Potentially narrower availability of older driver records**: The bill emphasizes disclosure authorization generally applying to **records 3 years old or less**, which could limit access to older records except for listed categories.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “civil immigration enforcement or related purposes”**: The bill requires certain contracts to include a non-use/non-disclosure clause for these purposes, but does not define the phrase.  
- **Who is exempt from the contract provisions**: The text states exemptions for “Law enforcement, the courts, other motor vehicle jurisdictions and those governmental agencies and businesses designated by the Secretary of Transportation,” but does not list which agencies/businesses are designated.  
- **Interaction between the “3 years old or less” limitation and other subsections**: The bill revises language about what records are available to “authorized persons or agencies,” but how that limitation applies across all permissible uses may require interpretation.  
- **Operational meaning of “voter registration purposes”**: The bill authorizes disclosure of certain restricted items for voter registration purposes, without specifying which entities or workflows qualify.

## Key Policy Changes
- **General nondisclosure rule**: DMV and its staff/contractors may not knowingly disclose personal information from motor vehicle records except as authorized.  
- **Age of records limitation**: Disclosure authorization generally applies only to **driver license and driver performance records “3 years old or less,”** with specified entities (e.g., persons requesting their own records; law enforcement; courts; other motor vehicle jurisdictions) also having access to older retained records and to title/registration information as described.  
- **New explicit sensitive-data categories** that “may not be disclosed” by DMV:
  - **Social Security number (or lack thereof)**
  - **Taxpayer identification number (or lack thereof)**
  - **Citizenship or immigration status**
  - Documents/reproductions revealing the above  
- **Limited exceptions for disclosing the sensitive categories**: allowed only for **voter registration purposes**, **with specific AG approval**, **with a valid court order**, or **where specifically authorized by this section or other state law**.  
- **Contract requirements expanded/clarified** for continual/electronic access requesters: contracts must specify legal basis, purposes, data elements, nondisclosure commitments, and include an **agreement not to use/disclose for civil immigration enforcement or related purposes** (with stated exemptions).  
- **Driving privilege card confidentiality strengthened** (§ 2711(d)):
  - PII collected must be kept confidential except for purposes/procedures equally applicable to other applicants, and only where release does not differentiate whether someone applied for/received a driving privilege card versus a driver license.
  - **Department may not share PII relating to issuance of a driving privilege card with any federal agency or federal law enforcement** without **specific Delaware AG approval or a valid court order**.

## Who Benefits
- **Applicants for and holders of driving privilege cards/permits** (confidentiality and limits on sharing with federal agencies).  
- **All DMV record subjects** whose citizenship/immigration status, SSN, or taxpayer ID information is held by DMV (reduced disclosure pathways).  
- Individuals who have filed **notarized affidavits** under § 305(c) seeking additional confidentiality (reinforced notification rules upon ordered disclosure).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Businesses/agencies that rely on DMV data** via electronic/continual access may face added contractual limits, including restrictions related to civil immigration enforcement purposes.  
- **Requesters seeking citizenship/immigration status or SSN/taxpayer ID-related information** may be denied unless they meet one of the specified exceptions (court order, AG approval, voter registration purpose, or explicit statutory authorization).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Record age threshold:** disclosure authorization generally applies to driver license and driver performance records **“3 years old or less.”**  
- **Records retention for redisclosure:** authorized recipients who resell/redisclose must maintain records for **not less than 5 years** and provide them for inspection upon request.  
- **Appeal fee:** de novo appeal to Justice of the Peace Court requires an **application fee of $10**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Criminal penalty:** Violations (misrepresenting identity, false statements to obtain restricted info, or knowingly violating the chapter) are a **Class A misdemeanor**; **Superior Court** has jurisdiction.  
- **Civil liability:** A person who knowingly obtains/discloses/uses DMV personal information for a non-permitted purpose is liable; court may award:
  - **Actual damages, but not less than $2,500 liquidated damages**
  - **Punitive damages** upon proof of willful or reckless disregard
  - **Reasonable attorney’s fees and litigation costs**
  - **Preliminary/equitable relief** as appropriate  
- **Contractual compliance:** Certain authorized electronic/continual access users must sign contracts including specified terms (legal basis, purposes, data elements, nondisclosure, and **no civil immigration enforcement use/disclosure**), subject to stated exemptions.  
- **Notification requirements:** When an administrative hearing officer or Justice of the Peace Court orders release of information involving individuals protected under § 305(c), DMV must provide **United States mail notification** identifying what will be released and to whom.
