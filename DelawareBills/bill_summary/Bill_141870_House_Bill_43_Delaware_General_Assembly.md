## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 10, § 147 to adopt an “inmate mailbox rule” for **pro se inmates** appealing **criminal** Superior Court judgments to the Delaware Supreme Court, so the filing date is based on when the inmate places the appeal in the institution’s internal mail system (with required documentation), rather than when the courts receive it.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a specific timeliness rule for **appeals filed by a pro se inmate confined in an institution**.  
- Keeps the existing general rule: criminal appeals must be filed within **30 days after the date of the judgment or decree**.  
- For covered inmates, an appeal is timely if it is placed in the institution’s internal mail system **on or before the last day for filing** (i.e., within the 30-day window).  
- Requires **first-class postage to be prepaid** for the mailbox rule to apply.  
- Requires the appeal to be accompanied by a **staff receipt verifying the date and time** it was placed in the internal mail system.  
- Requires institution staff to provide the inmate a receipt copy containing: **staff name**, **date/time**, and **case number**.  
- Law is enacted as **Chapter 23, Volume 85**, and was **approved May 22, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces late-filing risk for incarcerated pro se appellants** by treating the filing date as the date of deposit in the institution mail system (if conditions are met), aligning with the bill’s stated intent to be “similar to the federal inmate mailbox rule.”  
- **Creates documentation for disputes about timeliness** (receipt with staff name, date/time, and case number), which may improve clarity in contested filing-date situations.  
- **Standardizes institutional handling** of outgoing appeal filings by requiring staff to provide a defined receipt.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Adds administrative steps for institutions** (staff must generate/provide receipts with specific required information).  
- **Creates additional compliance conditions for inmates** (must prepay first-class postage and obtain/attach the required receipt), which could still lead to dismissal if any required element is missing.  
- **Potential for litigation over compliance** with the receipt/postage requirements (e.g., whether a receipt “verifies” the deposit date/time), though the bill provides limited procedural detail.

## Key Policy Changes
- Amends **10 Del. C. § 147** to add a new subsection **(b)** establishing an inmate mailbox rule for certain appeals.  
- For **pro se inmates confined in an institution**, an appeal is “timely filed” if all are met:  
  1) The praecipe/notice of appeal is placed in the institution’s internal mail system **on or before the last day for filing**;  
  2) **First-class postage is prepaid**; and  
  3) The filing is accompanied by a **receipt from institution staff** verifying the **date and time** the document was placed in the internal mail system.  
- Requires institution staff to give the inmate a receipt copy that includes:  
  - **Staff member name** who received the filing;  
  - **Date and time** of placement in the internal mail system;  
  - **Case number**.  
- Maintains the general 30-day rule in **§ 147(a)**: appeals must be filed within **30 days after the date of the judgment or decree**, except as provided in new subsection (b).

## Who Benefits
- **Pro se inmates confined in an institution** who file criminal appeals from Superior Court to the Delaware Supreme Court within the **30-day** appeal period and comply with the mailbox-rule requirements.  
- **Courts and parties** may benefit from clearer documentation of when an inmate transmitted an appeal (receipt with date/time and case number).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Institutions and staff** responsible for processing inmate legal mail, due to new receipt-generation and documentation requirements.  
- **Pro se inmates** who cannot satisfy the conditions (e.g., cannot prepay first-class postage or cannot obtain/attach the required receipt) may still face timeliness issues.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approved May 22, 2025** (Chapter 23, Volume 85).  
- The underlying appeal deadline remains **30 days after the date of the judgment or decree** for criminal appeals, with the mailbox-rule timeliness measured by deposit in the internal mail system **on or before the last day for filing**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements for pro se inmates seeking mailbox-rule protection**:  
  - Must place the appeal in the institution’s internal mail system **on or before the filing deadline**;  
  - Must have **first-class postage prepaid**;  
  - Must include a **staff receipt verifying date/time of deposit**.  
- **Compliance requirements for institution staff**: must provide the inmate a receipt copy containing **staff name**, **date/time**, and **case number**.  
- No new criminal or civil penalties are specified in the source text; the practical consequence of noncompliance is that an appeal may not be treated as timely under § 147(b).
