## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Clarify and standardize how background checks are completed and reviewed for individuals appointed to fill vacancies on local school boards by requiring completion of the background check and a qualification determination by the Commissioner of Elections before appointment.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires that a person **may not be appointed** to fill a school board vacancy unless they **complete a background check** required under **14 Del. C. § 209** and meet other seat qualifications.
- Assigns the **Commissioner of Elections** the duty to **determine eligibility/qualification** for an **appointment to fill a school board vacancy**, under **14 Del. C. §§ 209 and 1052**, **before** the appointment occurs.
- Clarifies that, for school board-related background checks, the **State Bureau of Identification (SBI)** and **DSCYF** must provide results **within 15 days** of a request.
- Expands the “recipient” list for background-check results to include the **Commissioner of Elections** not only for candidates for election, but also for individuals **seeking appointment** to fill a vacancy until the next school board election.
- Keeps the existing vacancy process: remaining board members fill the vacancy for the **remainder of the fiscal year**, and the seat is then filled by election at the **next regular school board election** for the unexpired term.
- Enacted as **Chapter 185, Volume 85**; **approved September 2, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer accountability and process:** The bill states who must review appointment eligibility—**the Commissioner of Elections**—addressing statutory ambiguity noted in the synopsis.
- **Uniform screening standard:** Aligns appointees with candidates by requiring the Commissioner to determine qualification “in the same manner” as for candidates (as described in the synopsis; implemented via code changes referencing §§ 209 and 1052).
- **Timeliness requirement:** Mandates that SBI and DSCYF provide relevant background check information **within 15 days** of a request, which may reduce delays in seating qualified board members.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added procedural step before appointment:** The appointment cannot occur until the background check is completed and the Commissioner of Elections determines the person is qualified, which could delay filling vacancies (the bill creates a “before… may be appointed” requirement).
- **Administrative workload shift/increase:** The Commissioner of Elections must make qualification determinations for vacancy appointees in addition to election candidates (explicitly required by the amended text).

## Key Policy Changes
- **14 Del. C. § 1054(b)(2):** Changes appointment requirements for filling school board vacancies by requiring:
  - The appointee **must** meet qualifications to be elected to the seat, including **completion of a background check** required by **14 Del. C. § 209**; and
  - The **Commissioner of Elections must determine** the individual is qualified under **14 Del. C. §§ 209 and 1052** **before** the individual may be appointed.
- **31 Del. C. § 309(e)(5)a.2:** Adds/clarifies that background check results must be provided to the **Commissioner of Elections** for:
  - an individual seeking to be a **candidate** for election to a school board; **or**
  - an individual seeking to be **appointed to fill a vacancy** on a school board until the next school board election.
- Retains other recipients and the **15-day** turnaround requirement for school board-related checks:
  - Governor (State Board of Education or vocational-technical school district),
  - Commissioner of Elections (candidates/appointees),
  - superintendent (serving district board member),
  - charter school board (charter board members/prospective members).
