## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29, § 5527 to change how the **minimum pension** for **members of the Delaware General Assembly** is calculated, **based on whether they were elected before or on/after February 28, 2025**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes the **minimum pension calculation** for a General Assembly member **elected on or after February 28, 2025** to be computed under **29 Del. C. § 5527(a)(1)**, instead of using a “highest-paid retiree” based minimum formula.
- **Keeps the prior minimum pension formula** for General Assembly members **elected before February 28, 2025** (minimum tied to the **highest rate of payment being paid to any retired member**).
- Continues to require that elected-official pensions under this subsection be computed based on **compensation as an elected official**, separate from any other credited service.
- Does **not** change the age/service eligibility tiers already in § 5527(d)(1) for elected officials elected **prior to January 1, 2012** versus **on/after January 1, 2012**.
- Maintains that any pension for **non–elected official credited service** is calculated separately under the rest of Chapter 55.
- The synopsis states the Act implements a recommendation of the **Delaware Compensation Commission** (report dated **January 6, 2025**).
- The enacted act (Chapter 121, Volume 85) is **approved July 30, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces linkage to the “highest-paid retiree” benchmark for future legislators:** For members elected **on/after February 28, 2025**, pensions are no longer guaranteed to use a minimum calculated from the **highest rate of payment being paid to any retired member of the General Assembly** (Synopsis and amended text).
- **Creates a clearer forward-looking rule for new entrants:** A bright-line election date (**February 28, 2025**) determines which calculation applies.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Different pension treatment for similarly situated members depending on election date:** Members elected **before** February 28, 2025 keep the prior minimum formula, while those elected **on/after** that date do not (text in § 5527(d)(1)).
- **Potentially lower minimum pensions for future members:** Because the “highest-paid retiree” minimum method no longer applies to members elected on/after February 28, 2025, the minimum benefit level may be lower than it would have been under the prior method (change described explicitly in Synopsis). *(The bill text does not quantify the magnitude.)*

## Key Policy Changes
- **Minimum pension rule split by election date (General Assembly members):**
  - **Elected before February 28, 2025:** Minimum pension continues to be computed by **years of service × the highest rate of payment being paid to any retired member of the General Assembly**, where the “rate” is computed by **(monthly pension paid to that retired member) ÷ (that retired member’s years of service as an elected member of the General Assembly)**.
  - **Elected on or after February 28, 2025:** Pension is computed under **§ 5527(a)(1)** (instead of the “highest-paid retiree” minimum approach).
- **No change to eligibility ages/years-of-service thresholds stated in § 5527(d)(1):**
  - **Elected prior to January 1, 2012:** eligible at **age 60 with ≥ 5 years** of service, or **age 55 with ≥ 10 years**.
  - **Elected on/after January 1, 2012:** eligible at **age 60 with ≥ 20 years**, or **age 65 with ≥ 10 years**.
- Confirms separate treatment of other service: “Any pension for credited service other than as an elected official shall be determined…as a **separate pension**.”

## Who Benefits
- **Members of the General Assembly elected before February 28, 2025**, who retain the existing minimum pension calculation tied to the **highest rate of payment being paid to any retired member** (as described in § 5527(d)(1)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Members of the General Assembly elected on or after February 28, 2025**, whose pension will be computed under **§ 5527(a)(1)** rather than being subject to the prior minimum formula tied to the highest-paid retiree benchmark.
