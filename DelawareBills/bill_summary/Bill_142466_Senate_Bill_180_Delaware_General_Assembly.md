## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Modernize Delaware’s Pharmacy Practice Act (Chapter 25, Title 24) to reflect current pharmacy practice and create a more objective, consistent licensure and discipline framework for pharmacists, pharmacies (including nonresident pharmacies), and other regulated drug-related establishments, with strengthened accountability mechanisms.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Rewrites and expands statutory definitions to reflect modern practice (e.g., “compounding” must comply with applicable USP standards; adds “outsourcing facility,” “key personnel,” and other updated terms).  
- Revises Board governance: Board member service expands to up to **three consecutive 3-year terms**; officers identified as **President and Vice President**; Board must meet at least **6 times per calendar year**.  
- Clarifies objective, rule-based licensure criteria for pharmacists and interns, including required **passing scores on NAPLEX** and a **jurisprudence exam**, and requires **fingerprints/criminal history checks** for applicants.  
- Strengthens renewal/reactivation/reinstatement controls: for certain lapses/inactive periods, the licensee must undergo **criminal background checks** and **NABP clearinghouse discipline review**; if inactive **>4 years**, must **reapply and re-take/pass MPJE**.  
- Expands discipline tools for pharmacists and regulated entities, including **permanent revocation** and substantially higher administrative monetary penalties (pharmacists: up to **$10,000** first violation; **$50,000** subsequent).  
- Establishes/updates disciplinary frameworks for **pharmacies, nonresident pharmacies, and pharmaceutical establishments**, including potential penalties up to **$250,000 per violation** and up to **$250,000 per day** for continuing violations (for entity license discipline).  
- Increases penalty for operating a **nonresident pharmacy without a Delaware license**: up to **$10,000 per day** (previously $50/day).  
- Adds an emergency exemption (effective immediately) allowing an out-of-state pharmacist to provide pharmacy services in Delaware during **emergency circumstances or a declared disaster**, with required notice to the Board.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger enforcement deterrence:** Raises maximum administrative penalties for pharmacist misconduct (up to **$10,000** first violation; **$50,000** subsequent) and for entity violations (up to **$250,000 per violation** and up to **$250,000 per day** for continuing violations).  
- **More robust vetting for public safety:** Requires **fingerprints** and **state/FBI criminal history record** checks for pharmacist/intern applicants and for **key personnel** of certain establishments, and adds **NABP clearinghouse** discipline reviews for reinstatement/reactivation scenarios.  
- **Clearer licensure standards:** Codifies objective requirements (e.g., **NAPLEX** and **jurisprudence exam** passing scores) which can improve consistency in licensing decisions.  
- **Improved accountability for out-of-state dispensing into Delaware:** Nonresident pharmacies must obtain a Delaware license and face a higher fine (up to **$10,000/day**) for delivering prescription drugs into Delaware without a license.  
- **Emergency preparedness:** Allows licensed pharmacists from other U.S. jurisdictions to provide pharmacy services in Delaware during emergencies/disasters for a period determined by the Board and Secretary of State, if they notify the Board and comply with Delaware law.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher compliance exposure/cost risk for regulated entities:** The statute authorizes very large penalties for pharmacies, nonresident pharmacies, and pharmaceutical establishments (up to **$250,000 per violation** and **$250,000 per day** for continuing violations).  
- **Additional administrative burden for applicants and licensees:** Fingerprinting/criminal background checks are required in multiple circumstances (initial licensure; reciprocity; reinstatement/reactivation after specified timeframes; key personnel checks for establishments), and applicants are responsible for “the required fee, if any” for obtaining records.  
- **Potential operational impacts on staffing:** Limits a pharmacist-in-charge to serving as pharmacist-in-charge of **only 1 pharmacy**, and each pharmacy may only have **1 pharmacist-in-charge**.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Emergency circumstances” scope and duration:** The emergency pharmacist practice exemption applies during “emergency circumstances” as determined by the Board in coordination with the Secretary of State, or a declared disaster, **for a period as determined** by them; the criteria and duration are not set in statute.  
- **Fee amounts not specified:** The bill states fees must approximate costs and are set by the Division at the beginning of each licensure biennium, but **does not specify dollar amounts** for licensing/renewal/late fees or for background checks.  
- **Many operational standards deferred to regulation:** The Act repeatedly relies on “Board’s rules and regulations” (e.g., prescription label content requirements, pharmacy license type designations, and emergency refills under §2552), leaving key implementation details outside the statutory text.

## Health Impact
- States that the Board’s primary objective is protection of public health and safety; strengthens discipline and compliance mechanisms for pharmacies and drug distribution entities.  
- Requires reporting of vaccine administrations to **DelVAX within 72 hours** (existing language retained in the practice-of-pharmacy definition).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Board governance changes**
  - Board members: eligible to serve up to **three consecutive 3-year terms**.  
  - Officers: President and Vice President elected annually; officer term limits addressed in statute.  
  - Meeting minimum: at least **6** regular business meetings per calendar year.  

- **Licensure and background checks**
  - Pharmacist licensure requires objective criteria including passing **NAPLEX** and a **jurisprudence exam**.  
  - Fingerprinting/criminal history checks (State Bureau of Identification + FBI) required for applicants and in multiple reentry scenarios; applicants pay required fees, if any.  
  - Reciprocity/transfer: requires good standing in all jurisdictions and adds criminal background check requirement.

- **Renewal, lapse, inactive status**
  - Lapsed **>1 year but <4 years**: late fee + CE proof + fingerprints/background check + **NABP clearinghouse** discipline review.  
  - Lapsed **>4 years**: above + proof of re-taking and passing **MPJE**.  
  - Inactive status up to **4 years**; if not reactivated within **4 years**, must reapply and **re-take/pass MPJE**.

- **Discipline and penalties**
  - Pharmacist discipline: adds violation of Board’s **Code of Ethics** as a ground; authorizes **permanent revocation**.  
  - Pharmacist administrative penalties increased to **up to $10,000** per first-time violation; **up to $50,000** per subsequent violation.  
  - Entity discipline (pharmacies, nonresident pharmacies, pharmaceutical establishments other than pharmacies): monetary penalties up to **$250,000 per violation** and up to **$250,000 per day** for a continuing violation.  
  - Nonresident pharmacy unlicensed delivery into Delaware: fine up to **$10,000 per day**.

- **Pharmacy operations and records**
  - Permanent closing notice: **14 days prior**; temporary closing > **7 consecutive business days**: **5 days prior** notice to the Board.  
  - Pharmacy must provide requested pharmacy records in hard copy (unless another form agreed) no later than **15 business days** after request (unless emergency/medical condition dictates immediate production).  
  - Prescription records retention: at least **3 years**.

- **Emergency out-of-state pharmacist practice (added by amendment)**
  - New exemption allowing out-of-state pharmacists to practice in Delaware during certain emergencies/disasters, with required notice to the Board.

## Who Benefits
- **Patients/consumers in Delaware:** Enhanced oversight, stronger penalties for unsafe or unlawful conduct, requirements for pharmacy record access within **15 business days**, and clearer accountability for medication safety issues.  
- **Delaware regulators (Board/Division):** Expanded tools (e.g., higher penalties; random CE audits; enhanced licensing structures) to enforce standards.  
- **Delaware-licensed pharmacies facing out-of-state competition:** Stronger licensing requirements and enforcement for **nonresident pharmacies** delivering into Delaware.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Pharmacists and applicants:** Additional compliance steps and costs (fingerprinting/background checks; exam/reentry requirements such as re-taking **MPJE** after certain lapse/inactive thresholds).  
- **Pharmacies, nonresident pharmacies, and other drug establishments:** Increased enforcement exposure and potential financial liability due to higher maximum penalties (up to **$250,000 per violation**; up to **$250,000 per day** continuing).  
- **Nonresident pharmacies shipping into Delaware:** Risk of fines up to **$10,000/day** for operating without a Delaware nonresident pharmacy license.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Section 1 (major Chapter 25 overhaul):** Takes effect **1 year after enactment into law**. (Approved **June 30, 2025** → effective **June 30, 2026**.)  
- **Section 2 (emergency out-of-state pharmacist exemption):** Takes effect **upon enactment into law** (Approved **June 30, 2025** → effective **June 30, 2025**).  
- **Key statutory deadlines/intervals created or retained**
  - Report vaccine administrations to **DelVAX within 72 hours** of administration.  
  - Notify Board of permanent pharmacy closing: **14 days prior**; temporary closing > **7 consecutive business days**: **5 days prior**.  
  - Provide requested pharmacy records within **15 business days** (unless emergency/medical condition dictates immediate).  
  - Nonresident pharmacy corporate officer/pharmacist changes: update Board **within 30 days** (as part of nonresident pharmacy conditions section; disciplinary framework also references timeliness requirements).  
  - Nonresident pharmacy must provide inspection reports to Board **within 60 days** after receipt from home-state regulator.  
  - Biological product substitution: communicate to prescriber **within 10 days** following dispensing (unless exception applies).  
  - Temporary pharmacist license: **90 days**, with **one 90-day extension**.  
  - Temporary pharmacy permit: **60 days**, with **one 60-day extension**.  
  - Temporary suspension pending hearing: order generally may remain effective **no longer than 60 days** (unless continuance requested); expedited hearing request must be made within **5 calendar days** of notification.  
  - License inactivity limit: up to **4 years**; if not reactivated within **4 years**, must reapply and **re-take/pass MPJE**.  
  - Emergency PrEP dispensing: **30- to 60-day** supply if conditions met; negative HIV test must be within prior **7 days**.  
  - PEP: exposure must be within prior **72 hours**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Unlicensed practice of pharmacy (§2507):**  
  - First conviction: **$500–$1,000** fine.  
  - Second or subsequent conviction: **$1,000–$2,000** fine.

- **Pharmacist discipline (§2516):**  
  - Sanctions include reprimand, censure, probation, suspension, **permanent revocation**.  
  - Administrative penalties: up to **$10,000** for each first-time violation; up to **$50,000** for each subsequent violation.

- **Pharmacy discipline (§2531A):**  
  - Monetary penalty up to **$250,000 per violation** and up to **$250,000 per day** for a continuing violation; plus reprimand/probation/suspension/**permanent revocation**.

- **Nonresident pharmacy discipline (§2538):**  
  - Monetary penalty up to **$250,000 per violation** and up to **$250,000 per day** for a continuing violation; plus reprimand/probation/suspension/**permanent revocation**.  
  - Operating a nonresident pharmacy delivering into Delaware without a license: fine up to **$10,000 per day**.

- **Pharmaceutical establishment (other than pharmacy) discipline (§2546):**  
  - Monetary penalty up to **$250,000 per violation** and up to **$250,000 per day** for a continuing violation; plus reprimand/probation/suspension/**permanent revocation**.

- **Hindering inspections (§2547):** fine up to **$500 per incident**.

- **Compliance requirements emphasized in text**
  - Fingerprinting/background checks for applicants and key personnel as specified.  
  - Record retention for prescriptions: **≥3 years**; access to Board inspection.  
  - Required notices and reporting timelines (DelVAX 72 hours; closing notices; inspection report submissions; biological product communication within 10 days).  
  - Emergency out-of-state pharmacist practice: must **notify the Board** of intent to practice and abide by Delaware laws/rules/regulations.
