## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To prohibit “citizen’s arrest” in Delaware by barring arrest or detention by private persons unless they have explicit statutory authority, while allowing limited assistance to peace officers in specified circumstances. The bill also repeals outdated Title 11 provisions referencing arrests by private detectives.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits arrest or detention by any person **not specifically authorized by statute** to effect an arrest or detention. (11 Del. C. § 1914(a))
- Creates a narrow exception allowing a private citizen to **assist a peace officer** if **requested** or if the officer is **incapacitated**. (11 Del. C. § 1914(b))
- Expressly aims to eliminate doubt that **“citizen’s arrest” is not permitted** in Delaware (Synopsis).
- Repeals two outdated statutes in Chapter 65: **§ 6563** (incarceration upon arrest by private detective) and **§ 6564** (penalties for violating § 6563).
- Notes that various actors already have limited statutory detention authority, including merchants for suspected shoplifting (**11 Del. C. § 840**), motion picture theater staff for suspected unlawful recording (**§ 858**), and video lottery agents for suspected video lottery machine offenses (**§ 1474**). (Synopsis)
- Signed/approved **July 14, 2025** (attachment: Laws of Delaware, Vol. 85, Ch. 74).
- The repealed § 6564 had provided for a fine of **$100–$500** or, upon nonpayment, imprisonment of **6 months–1 year**; repeal removes that specific penalty provision.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clarifies legality and reduces ambiguity**: The statute expressly prohibits private arrests/detentions absent statutory authority, addressing uncertainty about “citizen’s arrest.” (Synopsis; § 1914)
- **Limits potentially unsafe civilian interventions**: By prohibiting unauthorized arrests/detentions, the law narrows private use of coercive detention powers to those explicitly authorized by law. (§ 1914(a))
- **Preserves limited cooperation with law enforcement**: Allows civilians to assist when a peace officer requests help or is incapacitated. (§ 1914(b))
- **Modernizes the code**: Repeals outdated private-detective arrest provisions that the synopsis indicates are inconsistent with current practice (private investigators “do not have authority to make arrests”). (Synopsis; repeal of §§ 6563–6564)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces private actors’ ability to physically stop suspected offenders** unless a separate statute authorizes detention: the general prohibition may constrain civilians who previously believed they could perform a “citizen’s arrest.” (§ 1914(a); Synopsis)
- **Removes a specific penalty provision tied to private-detective arrest incarceration rules**: repeal of § 6564 eliminates the explicit fine (**$100–$500**) and default imprisonment (**6 months–1 year**) that applied to violations of repealed § 6563. (Repeal of § 6564)

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Penalties for violating § 1914**: The bill text prohibits unauthorized arrest/detention but does not state within § 1914 what criminal charge, civil liability, or specific penalty applies for violations. (No penalty language in § 1914 as shown)
- **Scope of “assist”**: The statute allows assistance when requested or if the officer is incapacitated, but does not define the permissible level of force or specific actions encompassed by “assist.” (§ 1914(b))
- **Interaction with existing detention statutes**: The synopsis references limited detention authorities (e.g., § 840, § 858, § 1474), but the bill does not list them in statutory text or clarify how conflicts are resolved beyond “specifically authorized by statute.” (§ 1914; Synopsis)

- **Adds a limited exception for assisting law enforcement**:

## Who Benefits
- **General public**, through clearer rules that “citizen’s arrest” is not permitted absent statutory authorization. (Synopsis; § 1914)
- **Peace officers**, by having a defined, narrow basis for requesting or receiving civilian assistance during an arrest/detention (including if incapacitated). (§ 1914(b))
- **Individuals subject to arrest/detention**, by limiting arrest/detention authority to those explicitly authorized by statute. (§ 1914(a))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Private citizens** who previously believed they could conduct a “citizen’s arrest,” as such actions are prohibited unless independently authorized by statute. (Synopsis; § 1914(a))
- **Private security/private investigators**, to the extent any relied on prior code language suggesting arrest-related procedures for private detectives (even though synopsis states they currently lack arrest authority). (Synopsis; repeal of §§ 6563–6564)
