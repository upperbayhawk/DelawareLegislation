## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16 to update the Behavioral Health Consortium and the Prescription Opioid Settlement Distribution Commission (Commission), including (1) expanding the Consortium’s charge beyond substance use disorder, (2) changing membership and adding term limits, and (3) revising governance and authority for distributing money from the Prescription Opioid Settlement Fund and Prescription Opioid Impact Fund.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands the Behavioral Health Consortium’s scope to include **all aspects of behavioral health care**, not only substance use disorder (Synopsis; §5195(b)(1)).
- Changes the Consortium’s composition from **27 members to 25 members** and adjusts specific represented groups (e.g., adds representation tied to the **Maternal and Child Death Review** body and a **veteran** impacted by behavioral health) (§5195A(a); §5195A(a)(2)i; Synopsis).
- Imposes term limits for Governor-appointed Consortium members: **2-year terms**, **no more than 3 terms / 6 total years** (§5195A(d)).
- Restructures the Commission’s leadership so the **Governor (or designee) is chair** (instead of co-chairs including the Attorney General) (§5196A(d)).
- Shifts distribution authority so the **Commission distributes** settlement/impact funds (rather than the Consortium distributing based on Commission recommendations), and the Commission can distribute via grants, DHSS contracts, or allocations to agencies/local governments (§5196B(a)).
- Keeps multi-signature authorization for disbursements by the State Treasurer, but updates the signatory from the Consortium chair to the **Commission chair** (§5196B(b)).
- Continues/clarifies annual public reporting on fund receipts and awards/disbursements, and requires reports be provided to specified state entities and the Public Archives (§5196(d); §5195(c); §5196A(c)(5)).
- Removes the Behavioral Health Commission’s approval authority over the Commission’s granting/contracting processes (Synopsis).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader oversight of behavioral health system:** The Consortium is expressly charged with oversight of entities affecting **behavioral health-care and substance use treatment** to ensure quality care (§5195(b)(1); Synopsis).
- **Improved governance clarity for opioid funds:** Establishes clearer responsibility for distributions by placing distribution authority in the Commission and setting explicit voting/quorum rules for official action including distributing funds (§5196A(e)).
- **Enhanced accountability and transparency:** Requires an **annual report** with specific required disclosures (amounts/sources received; contracts/grants with recipients, amounts, terms; disbursements including administrative expenses and local government block grants) and publication on the Commission website (§5196(d)(1); §5195(c)(2)).
- **Regularized turnover/limits for appointees:** Term limits for Governor-appointed Consortium members may promote periodic refresh of representation (**max 6 years**) (§5195A(d)).
- **Representation updates:** Membership changes add representation tied to maternal/child mortality review and **Delaware’s veteran community** (§5195A(a)(1)b; §5195A(a)(2)i; Synopsis).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced role for the broader Consortium in fund distributions:** The bill shifts from “Consortium distributes based on Commission recommendations” to “**Commission shall distribute**” (§5196B(a)), which may reduce checks formerly embedded in the “recommendation → adoption” structure reflected in prior text.
- **Concentration of Commission leadership:** Replacing co-chairs (Attorney General and Governor/designees) with a single chair (Governor/designee) centralizes leadership authority (§5196A(d)).
- **Membership reductions/changes:** Consortium membership decreases from **27 to 25** (§5195A(a)), and certain entities are removed as “nonoperational organizations” (Synopsis), potentially reducing breadth of perspectives (specific removed entities are not fully enumerated in the provided text excerpts).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Consortium scope expanded** to include all aspects of behavioral health care (Synopsis; §5195(b)(1)).
- **Consortium membership adjusted and reduced** from **27 to 25** members; adds representation associated with maternal/child death review and a veteran impacted by behavioral health (§5195A(a); §5195A(a)(2)i; Synopsis).
- **Term limits added** for Governor-appointed Consortium members: **2-year terms**, max **3 terms / 6 years**, consecutive or nonconsecutive; ability to appoint shorter terms for staggering (§5195A(d)).
- **Commission leadership changed**: Governor/designee becomes **chair**; Commission may elect a vice chair (§5196A(d)).
- **Fund distribution authority updated**: Commission (not Consortium) distributes money from the Settlement Fund and Impact Fund; distribution mechanisms include grants, DHSS contracts, and transfers to agencies/local governments (§5196B(a)).
- **Treasurer disbursement authorizations updated**: requires authorization by the **Commission chair**, OMB Director, Controller General, and Joint Finance Committee co-chairs (§5196B(b)).
- **Annual reporting requirements maintained/clarified**, including publication on Commission website and distribution to specified state entities and the Public Archives (§5196(d); §5195(c); §5196A(c)(5)).
