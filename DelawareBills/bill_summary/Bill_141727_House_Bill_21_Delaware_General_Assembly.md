## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s controlled substances law (Title 16) to classify **tianeptine** as a **Schedule I controlled substance** and **ban its sale/manufacture/delivery**, while **explicitly preventing any civil/criminal penalties or asset forfeiture for simple possession** of tianeptine by an individual.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds **tianeptine** (including **salts, isomers, and salts of isomers**) to **Schedule I** under **16 Del. C. § 4714(h)**.
- Covers **“any quantity”** of tianeptine (no minimum threshold).
- Creates a new rule that **no civil or criminal penalty** may be imposed for **possession** of tianeptine by an individual, and **no civil/criminal asset forfeiture** may be pursued based on such possession (**16 Del. C. § 4761A(a)**, as amended/repurposed).
- States that **manufacturing, selling, or delivery** of tianeptine **remains unlawful** and is subject to existing controlled substance penalties (**§ 4761A(b)**).
- The bill synopsis describes tianeptine as not FDA-approved and being sold as a “dietary supplement” in retail settings, and frames the Act as banning its sale by scheduling it.
- The bill notes it requires **two-thirds approval** because it expands an existing misdemeanor (per synopsis).
- **Approved July 21, 2025** (Chapter 87, Volume 85).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Enables enforcement against commercial distribution**: By placing tianeptine in **Schedule I**, the bill supports prosecution/penalties for **manufacturing, selling, or delivering** tianeptine under Title 16 controlled substance provisions (explicitly preserved in **§ 4761A(b)**).
- **Reduces punitive impact on users for possession**: The bill **prohibits civil/criminal penalties and forfeiture** for **possession by an individual** (**§ 4761A(a)**), limiting criminalization for simple possession.
- **Clear breadth of covered substances**: Inclusion of **salts, isomers, and salts of isomers** and **“any quantity”** reduces loopholes in chemical form or small-quantity claims (**§ 4714(h)**).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Possession is non-penalized, potentially complicating enforcement goals focused on supply reduction**: The bill removes any civil/criminal penalty and forfeiture for possession (**§ 4761A(a)**), while relying on enforcement against manufacturing/sales/delivery (**§ 4761A(b)**).
- **Compliance burden on retailers and suppliers**: Entities selling products containing **any quantity** of tianeptine face illegality once scheduled (**§ 4714(h)** plus unlawful sale/manufacture/delivery under **§ 4761A(b)** and existing chapter penalties).
- **Scope may sweep broadly**: Because it covers **any quantity** in any “material, compound, mixture, or preparation,” products with trace amounts (if any exist) would be covered (**§ 4714(h)**).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Schedules tianeptine as Schedule I**: Adds **§ 4714(h)** to include “any material, compound, mixture, or preparation that contains any quantity of tianeptine including its salts, isomers and salts of isomers…”.
- **Decriminalizes possession (no penalties; no forfeiture)**: Creates/repurposes **§ 4761A** to state:
  - **No civil or criminal penalty** may be imposed for **possession of tianeptine by an individual**.
  - **No civil or criminal asset forfeiture** may be pursued for such possession.
- **Keeps supply-side conduct illegal**: Confirms that **manufacturing, selling, or delivery** remains unlawful and subject to existing penalties in the chapter (**§ 4761A(b)**).
