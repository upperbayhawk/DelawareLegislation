## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To require statewide reciprocity/recognition of Board-issued plumbing and HVACR licenses by all Delaware counties, cities, and municipalities, preventing local jurisdictions from adding additional licensing requirements, and to clarify related enforcement provisions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates two new defined terms: **“State HVACR License”** and **“State Plumbing License”** (licenses issued by the Board to individuals to provide services in Delaware).
- Requires that a **State Plumbing License** be recognized by **any county, city, and municipality** in Delaware **“without reservations or additional requirements”** to work in those jurisdictions.
- Requires that a **State HVACR License** be recognized by **any county, city, and municipality** in Delaware **“without reservations or additional requirements”** to work in those jurisdictions.
- Leaves in place the **fine structure** for violating the license-required provisions: **$1,500–$3,000** (first offense) and **$3,000–$6,000** (second or subsequent offense).
- Confirms **Justice of the Peace Court** jurisdiction over violations of these sections.
- Synopsis states the Act **limits the ability to set professional standards and licensing requirements** for plumbing/HVACR licensees **to the Board**.
- Synopsis states the Act **requires a 2/3 vote** because it **indirectly impacts a municipal charter**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Uniform statewide licensing access:** Explicitly prevents counties/cities/municipalities from imposing **additional local requirements** beyond the Board’s license (“without reservations or additional requirements”).
- **Reduced duplicative local licensing barriers:** A licensee meeting state Board requirements can work across Delaware jurisdictions without obtaining separate local approvals (as stated in the recognition requirement).
- **Clarity and consistency for contractors and consumers:** Definitions of the state licenses and mandatory recognition may reduce confusion about where a license is valid.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced local control:** Local governments are prohibited from adding **any additional requirements** for licensed plumbing/HVACR work, potentially limiting local tailoring of licensing-related standards (per synopsis and mandatory recognition language).
- **Potential implementation friction:** Municipalities that currently require additional licensing steps may need to revise processes to comply (necessitated by “must be recognized…without…additional requirements”).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds definitions (Title 24, Ch. 18, §1802):**
  - “**State HVACR License**” = a license issued by the Board to an individual to provide heating, ventilation, and air conditioning services in Delaware.
  - “**State Plumbing License**” = a license issued by the Board to an individual to provide plumbing services in Delaware.
- **Mandates local recognition of state licenses:**
  - **Plumbing (§1807(d))**: State Plumbing License **must be recognized** by any county, city, municipality **without reservations or additional requirements**.
  - **HVACR (§1820(d))**: State HVACR License **must be recognized** by any county, city, municipality **without reservations or additional requirements**.
- **Penalties and jurisdiction (renumbered subsections):**
  - **First offense:** fine **not less than $1,500** and **not more than $3,000**.
  - **Second or subsequent offense:** fine **not less than $3,000** and **not more than $6,000**.
  - **Justice of the Peace Court** has jurisdiction over violations.
