## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Delaware’s workers’ compensation health-care payment system (Title 19, § 2322B) to authorize a **permanent one-time 3% increase in aggregate workers’ compensation medical expenses** to correct **Evaluation and Management (E&M) code** reimbursements that the bill states have fallen below **Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS)** reimbursement rates, while leaving existing Medicare-based caps and CPI-based annual adjustments in place.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a **permanent one-time +3% increase** in **aggregate workers’ compensation medical expenses** to correct **E&M code** reimbursement levels (stated to be below CMS rates).
- Keeps the rule that maximum allowable payment remains **the lesser of the provider’s actual charges or the fee schedule amount**.
- Retains procedure-specific reimbursement caps tied to Medicare: generally **≤ 200% of Medicare**, with exceptions of **≤ 250% for radiology** and **≤ 300% for surgery** (HCPCS Level I or II codes).
- Preserves annual payment-system updates based on **CPI-U (Consumer Price Index—Urban, U.S. City Average, All Items)** beginning **January 1, 2018**, subject to the Medicare-based caps.
- Leaves in statute earlier cost-reduction targets and timelines (e.g., **20% by January 31, 2015**; additional reductions by **January 31, 2016** and **January 31, 2017**) as existing policy language.
- Continues oversight mechanisms: the **Workers’ Compensation Oversight Panel** role and **independent actuary** verification for compliance (as written in § 2322B).
- **Effective date:** the Act takes effect **January 31, 2026** (law approved **June 30, 2025**).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **May improve payment adequacy for E&M services:** The bill explicitly increases aggregate medical expenses by **3%** to correct E&M reimbursements that have “fallen below” **CMS** rates (Synopsis; § 2322B(a)(3)c).
- **May support provider participation in workers’ compensation cases:** The Synopsis states Delaware is in “critical need” of physicians to accept and treat workers’ compensation patients and frames the E&M adjustment as addressing that issue.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Raises overall workers’ compensation medical spending:** The Act authorizes a **+3% increase in aggregate workers’ compensation medical expenses** (even though characterized as correcting E&M reimbursements).
- **No detailed allocation mechanism in the text:** The bill references correcting **E&M code reimbursements** but does not specify within the statutory language how the 3% aggregate increase is targeted, distributed, or limited to E&M codes.
