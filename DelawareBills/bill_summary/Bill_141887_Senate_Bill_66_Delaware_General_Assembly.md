## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s sex offender community notification law (Title 11, § 4121) to remove the requirement that schools, school districts, and licensed child care providers maintain physical binders of community notification notices, and instead require them to provide a standardized “school and child care notification” directing the public to the Delaware State Police Sex Offender Central Registry website and other official access points.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Eliminates the longstanding requirement (in place since 2002) that schools, school districts, and licensed child care providers keep a **binder** of sex offender community notifications in an administrative office.  
- Requires schools/school districts/licensed child care providers to provide a **“school and child care notification”** containing: (1) where to obtain searchable records (online and in-person), (2) how to register for updates, and (3) the Registry website **URL**.  
- Sets distribution methods for the “school and child care notification”: **post on a website** (if the entity has one) or **send in writing at least annually** to staff and parents/guardians (if no website), and **provide upon request** when asked for registry information.  
- Clarifies in statute that **sex offender registry searchable records must be available on the Internet** via the Delaware State Police Sex Offender Central Registry website.  
- Adjusts what information is included/excluded in “searchable records available to the public,” including limiting victim age searches to **age ranges** and stating the offender’s relationship to the victim **may not** be included.  
- Requires searchable records be updated **as often as practicable, but not less than every 3 months** (while the bill’s findings note the website is updated every Friday).  
- Maintains the Tier-based notification framework: Tier II must have searchable public records (community notification optional); Tier III must have searchable records **and** community notification; and schools must still receive notice when an offender plans to attend.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces administrative burden** on schools/child care providers by removing the obligation to maintain and manage a physical binder of community notifications. (Bill findings and synopsis cite “financial, administrative, and emotional burdens.”)  
- **Improves currency and accessibility of information** by directing the public to the centralized Registry, which the bill notes is searchable by offender or geography and updated regularly (findings state “updated every Friday”).  
- **Clarifies appropriate point-of-contact** by directing questions to SBI/Delaware State Police Sex Offender Unit rather than school staff (findings cite schools lack expertise to address offense/risk questions).  
- **Standardizes communications** through a defined “school and child care notification” and specified delivery methods (website posting, annual written notice if no website, and upon-request provision).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Less on-site, immediate access** at schools/child care locations because the bill eliminates the binder that was “available to view upon request,” replacing it with notice directing people elsewhere (online or police/SBI in-person).  
- **Access depends on external systems** (Registry website, email alerts, police/SBI access). The bill emphasizes these exist, but the school/child care location no longer maintains local records.  
- **Ongoing communication obligation remains** for entities without websites, which must send written notification **at least annually** to staff and parents/guardians.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The text requires searchable records be updated “**not less than every 3 months**,” while legislative findings say the Registry website is updated “**every Friday**.” The bill does not explain how these two statements are intended to align in practice.  
- “School and child care notification” must include the Registry website **URL**, but the statute does not specify the exact URL text to be used (presumably maintained/updated by the provider).  
- The bill uses “may not” / “may be searchable only” language regarding victim-related fields; it is not fully clear from the amendment text whether these are intended as strict prohibitions/limits in all cases or technical drafting adjustments (though the operative text reads as requirements).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Repeals/ends binder requirement:** Removes the mandate that if a school/school district/licensed child care provider receives community notification, it must be “placed in a binder,” retained, and made available for viewing, and that parents/staff be frequently informed of binder availability/location.  
- **Creates “school and child care notification” requirement:** Defines required content:
  1) Searchable records can be obtained **online** at the Delaware State Police Sex Offender Central Registry website or **in-person** at a Delaware State Police troop or SBI.  
  2) The public can **register to receive community notifications** through the Registry website.  
  3) The Registry website **URL**.  
- **Sets delivery rules for the notification:**
  - **Post on website** if the provider has a website.  
  - If no website, **send in writing at least annually** to faculty/staff and a parent/guardian of an enrolled child.  
  - Provide to staff/faculty/parent/guardian **upon request** for sex offender registry information.  
- **Internet availability clarified:** Registry “searchable records available to the public” must be available via “police agencies … and the Internet” and “maintained by the Delaware State Police Sex Offender Central Registry website.”  
- **Public record content changes/clarifications:**
  - Relationship to victim **may not** be included in searchable records.  
  - Victim age may be searchable only by these **age ranges**: **birth to 11**, **12 to 15**, **16 to 17**, and **18 years and above**.  
  - Searchable records must not include: victim identities, offender Social Security number, and arrests not resulting in conviction.  
  - Searchable records must include a warning against unlawful use and note potential civil/criminal penalties.  
  - Update frequency: “as often as practicable, but not less than every **3 months**.”

## Who Benefits
- **Schools, school districts, and licensed child care providers** (reduced binder maintenance responsibilities; clearer, more limited role).  
- **School staff/administrators** (reduced burden cited in findings as financial/administrative/emotional).  
- **Members of the public** seeking information (clear direction to a centralized, searchable, internet-based Registry and an alert/registration option).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Individuals who relied on in-person binder review at schools/child care providers** as a primary access method may have reduced on-site access because the binder requirement is eliminated.  
- **Schools/child care providers without websites** must perform **at least annual** written distribution to staff and parents/guardians, which may be an ongoing administrative task (though replacing binder obligations).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (schools/child care):**
  - Provide “school and child care notification” by website posting (if available), or written notice **at least annually** (if no website), and provide upon request when registry information is requested.  
- **Compliance requirements (Delaware State Police/SBI):**
  - Maintain public searchable records on the Registry website and update them at least every **3 months**.  
- **Warning requirement:** Public searchable records must include a warning that misuse to unlawfully injure/harass/commit crimes could lead to **civil or criminal penalties** (the bill references penalties generally but does not set new penalty amounts or specific offenses).  
- **Immunity provision restated/retained:** Elected officials/public employees/agencies have civil immunity for discretionary release of relevant information absent **gross negligence or bad faith**.
