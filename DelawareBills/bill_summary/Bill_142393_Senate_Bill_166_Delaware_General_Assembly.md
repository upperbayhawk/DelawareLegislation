## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 to require schools and other State-approved or State-funded educational programs to provide specified excused absences and reasonable accommodations for pregnant and parenting students, and to prohibit discrimination based on pregnancy, childbirth, lactation, or parenting/guardianship status.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits excluding or discriminating against a person in any education program/activity receiving State approval or financial assistance based on pregnancy, childbirth, related conditions (including lactation), or parenting/guardianship status.  
- Requires excused absences for labor, delivery, and **prenatal/postnatal medical appointments** that a physician states in writing are medically necessary.  
- Requires up to **5 days** of excused health-related absences for pregnancy-related health needs/illness, with additional days excused if supported by a health-care provider note; student must get **at least 30 days** to obtain the note and cannot be penalized while obtaining it.  
- Requires **6 weeks** of excused absences for the student giving birth, starting immediately after birth and taken consecutively; only due date/delivery date note may be required, and student gets **at least 30 days** to obtain it without penalty.  
- Requires excused absences for a child’s illness/medical appointment, including up to **4 days per school year** with **no provider note required**.  
- Requires reasonable accommodations including a compliant lactation room (not a restroom) and refrigerator access, modified transportation/equipment access, schedule/coursework modifications, academic supports, and emergency childcare breaks.  
- Bars academic penalties for using accommodations and requires make-up work opportunities (time to make up work must be “reasonable” and **not less than the number of days absent**).  
- Directs the Department of Education to promulgate regulations to implement and enforce the section.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Establishes clear statewide minimum protections (excused absences and accommodations) for pregnant and parenting students in any program receiving State approval or financial assistance.  
- Provides specific, enforceable accommodation standards for lactation space (private, clean, secure; power source; chair; table; lighting/ventilation; door security; proximity; refrigerator access).  
- Limits documentation burdens: schools generally may not require documentation unless expressly allowed; for certain absences the bill sets specific thresholds (e.g., **4 days/year** for child appointments with no note).  
- Protects academic standing by prohibiting academic penalties tied to use of accommodations and guaranteeing make-up work opportunities.

## Educational Impact
- Requires excused absences for specified pregnancy/parenting-related events and appointments, including **6 weeks** postpartum for the student giving birth.  
- Requires academic accommodations (schedule/course load changes, remote learning/online access, extensions, test rescheduling and testing accommodations, tutoring/supplemental instruction/learning support/counseling).  
- Prohibits academic penalties for using accommodations and requires opportunities to make up missed work with a minimum make-up period **not less than the number of days absent**, and with the same options as illness-related absences.

## Health Impact
- Supports access to prenatal/postnatal care by requiring excused absences for appointments deemed medically necessary in writing by a physician.  
- Supports postpartum recovery via **6 weeks** excused absence immediately following birth (with additional days possible with provider note).  
- Supports lactation needs by requiring a compliant lactation room and refrigerator access for expressed milk storage.  
- Provides up to **5 days** excused pregnancy-related health absences without immediate documentation, with **30 days** to obtain a note if needed for additional days.

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates/updates § 2708 to:  
  - Prohibit discrimination in State-approved or State-funded education programs based on pregnancy, childbirth, related conditions (including lactation), or parenting/guardian status (including parent-child relationship cross-referenced to **Title 13, §§ 8-201(a)(1) and 8-201(b)(1-3)**).  
  - Mandate excused absences for:
    - Labor and delivery.  
    - Prenatal/postnatal medical appointments medically necessary per physician’s written statement.  
    - Up to **5 days** for pregnancy-related health need/illness (more with provider note; **≥30 days** to obtain; no penalty while obtaining).  
    - **6 weeks** postpartum excused absence taken consecutively (more with provider note; **≥30 days** to obtain; no penalty while obtaining; only due date/delivery date note may be required).  
    - A student’s child’s illness/medical appointment, including up to **4 days per school year** with no provider note.  
    - Court appearances/related needs in adoption, foster care, custody, visitation, child support, domestic violence, dating violence, sexual assault, stalking involving a pregnant/parenting student.  
  - Mandate reasonable accommodations, including specified lactation room standards, modified transportation/equipment access, schedule/coursework changes, academic supports, and emergency childcare breaks.  
  - Provide that students cannot be forced to accept accommodations.  
  - Bar academic penalties tied to accommodation use; require make-up work opportunities.  
  - Limit documentation requirements except where explicitly authorized.  
  - Require DOE regulations for implementation/enforcement.
