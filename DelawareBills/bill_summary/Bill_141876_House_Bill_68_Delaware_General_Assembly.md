## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To amend Title 1 of the Delaware Code to designate **March 9** as **“6888th Central Postal Directory Battalion Day”** and encourage statewide commemoration honoring the battalion and recognizing postal employees.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes **March 9** as **6888th Central Postal Directory Battalion Day** in Delaware law (Title 1, Chapter 6, **§ 610**).
- Directs that the day **“should be commemorated in this State by appropriate ceremonies”** honoring members of the **6888th Central Postal Directory Battalion**.
- Encourages citizens, as part of commemoration, to **write and mail letters** to family, friends, **military personnel, and veterans**.
- Frames the commemoration as also recognizing **postal employees** and their role “in connecting us to each other.”
- The legislative findings highlight the battalion’s WWII service, including **855** African-American women (**824 enlisted**, **31 officers**) and their mission to clear mail backlogs affecting **seven million people**.
- Notes the battalion’s operational output (historical context): **3** separate **8-hour** shifts, **7 days/week**, averaging **65,000 parcels per shift** (**195,000 daily**).
- Act was **approved August 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Formal recognition and public education:** Creates an official day to honor a historically significant unit—the **only all African-American, all-female battalion deployed to the European Theater** in WWII (as stated in findings).
- **Encourages civic participation:** Explicitly encourages residents to engage in a concrete activity—**writing and mailing letters**—as part of the commemoration.
- **Recognition of public service:** The statute ties the day to recognizing **postal employees** and the “important service” they provide.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **No defined standards for “appropriate ceremonies”:** The bill provides a commemoration directive but does not specify who organizes ceremonies, what qualifies, or whether any state resources are expected.
- **Potential administrative expectations without funding:** By stating the day “should be commemorated…by appropriate ceremonies,” agencies or communities may feel pressure to plan events, but the bill does not provide appropriations or implementation guidance.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- Whether there is any expectation of **state-sponsored** events versus purely **voluntary/community-led** observance.
- Whether Delaware intends to produce **official materials** (proclamations, educational resources) to support the commemoration.
