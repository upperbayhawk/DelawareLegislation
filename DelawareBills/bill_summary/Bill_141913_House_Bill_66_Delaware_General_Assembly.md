## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Expand Delaware’s Free School Meals Program so that all attending students in participating public schools can receive free breakfast and free lunch, and require the Department of Education (DOE) to reimburse schools for meal costs not covered by federal reimbursements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires public schools participating in the **federal School Breakfast Program** to provide **free breakfast to all attending students, regardless of household income** (14 Del. C. § 4137A(b), as amended).
- Requires public schools participating in the **federal National School Lunch Program** to provide **free lunch to all attending students, regardless of household income** (new § 4137A(c)).
- Maintains a limit of **“a maximum of 1 free meal for each meal service period”** (renumbered § 4137A(d)).
- Requires DOE to reimburse participating public schools for the **gap between federal reimbursement received and the federal “free” reimbursement rate applied to eligible meals** (renumbered § 4137A(e)).
- Adds/updates definitions (including “Breakfast” per **7 C.F.R. § 220.2**, “Lunch” per **7 C.F.R. § 210.2**, and “Eligible meal”).
- Effective date is **not immediate** in the bill text: the Act takes effect **30 days after publication in the Register of Regulations** of a notice from the **Controller General** that **funds have been appropriated** (Section 2).
- Fiscal note estimates State costs of **$45,538,434 (FY 2026)**, **$47,202,855 (FY 2027)**, and **$48,931,013 (FY 2028)**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Universal access to meals:** The bill requires participating public schools to make **free breakfast and lunch available to all attending students** (regardless of income), expanding access beyond students eligible for reduced-price meals.
- **State reimbursement structure:** Schools are to be reimbursed by DOE for costs not covered by federal reimbursements (new § 4137A(e)), which may reduce local financial burden for implementing universal meals (to the extent funding is appropriated).
- **Consistent meal standards via federal definitions:** By defining breakfast and lunch via federal regulations (**7 C.F.R. § 220.2** and **7 C.F.R. § 210.2**), the bill ties meals to existing federal program requirements.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Significant State cost:** Fiscal note projects State costs of **$45,538,434 in FY 2026**, increasing to **$48,931,013 by FY 2028**.
- **Implementation depends on appropriations and notice:** The bill’s effective date depends on an appropriation and publication of notice by the Controller General (Section 2), which could delay benefits to students.
- **Meal limit may not cover additional needs:** Students may receive only **1 free meal per meal service period** (§ 4137A(d)), which may limit access for students needing additional food beyond standard service.

## Key Policy Changes
- Expands free meal availability from reduced-price eligible students to **all attending students** in participating public schools:
  - **Free breakfast for all attending students** at schools participating in the School Breakfast Program (§ 4137A(b)).
  - **Free lunch for all attending students** at schools participating in the National School Lunch Program (§ 4137A(c)).
- Sets/retains a participation limit: **maximum of 1 free meal for each meal service period** (§ 4137A(d)).
- Requires DOE reimbursement to schools equal to:  
  **(federal free reimbursement rate × total eligible meals served)** minus **(total federal reimbursements actually received for those meals)** (§ 4137A(e)).
- Adds/updates definitions including:
  - “Breakfast” (7 C.F.R. § 220.2)
  - “Lunch” (7 C.F.R. § 210.2)
  - “Eligible meal” (breakfast or lunch satisfying Program requirements)
- Changes effective date structure to a **funding-contingent** trigger (Section 2).
