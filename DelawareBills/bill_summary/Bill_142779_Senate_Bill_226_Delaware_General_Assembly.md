## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s medical marijuana law (Title 16) to require certain licensed health care facilities to allow **terminally ill medical marijuana cardholders** to use medical marijuana on the facility’s premises, subject to specified restrictions and facility policies, with limited exceptions. The Act is titled **“Ryan’s Law.”**

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires a **“health care facility”** (licensed under Title 16, Ch. 10 or 11) to **permit medical marijuana use** for a **cardholder with a terminal illness**, with conditions.  
- Patient (or designated caregiver) must provide a **copy of the registry identification card** and the facility must **document use in the patient’s medical record**.  
- Facilities must **prohibit smoking or vaping** as methods of medical marijuana use on premises.  
- Facilities must ensure staff **do not administer** or **retrieve marijuana from storage** (except for disposal under facility policy); the **patient/caregiver is responsible** for acquiring, administering, retrieving, and removing marijuana.  
- Marijuana must be **stored securely in a locked container** (in the patient’s room, another designated area, or with the designated caregiver).  
- A facility **may prohibit** use if its medical professionals determine marijuana use **may adversely impact care** or is **contraindicated**.  
- A facility **may suspend** permitting use if **federal agencies/US DOJ/CMS** initiate specified actions or issue rules/guidance expressly prohibiting such use, until notified it may resume.  
- Effective **1 year after enactment into law**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expands access for terminally ill patients in facilities:** Facilities “**must permit**” medical marijuana use by a **cardholder who has a terminal illness** (new §4904A(c)(1)).  
- **Creates clear operational guardrails:** Requires documentation in medical records, locked storage, and written facility policies and procedures (new §4904A(c)(1)(b), (f), (h)).  
- **Limits professional risk for good-faith compliance:** Provides protection from civil/criminal liability and professional discipline for good-faith compliance, except for **gross negligence, recklessness, or intentional misconduct** (§4904A(c)(5)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative burden on facilities:** Requires developing and disseminating **written policies and procedures**, maintaining records, and managing storage/disposal processes (§4904A(c)(1)(b), (g), (h)).  
- **May be unavailable in some circumstances:** Facilities may prohibit use if deemed adverse/contraindicated (§4904A(c)(2)) and may suspend use due to federal enforcement actions or federal guidance/rules (§4904A(c)(3)).  
- **No smoking/vaping may limit patient options:** Facilities must prohibit smoking or vaping as methods of use (§4904A(c)(1)(c)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill requires facilities to permit use for a “**cardholder who has a terminal illness**,” but does not define **“terminal illness”** in the amended text.  
- The bill requires marijuana to be stored in a locked container in the patient’s room, other designated area, or with the caregiver, but does not specify **facility standards** for locked storage beyond “securely” and “locked container” (§4904A(c)(1)(f)).  
- The bill references “health care facility” via licensing under Chapter 10 or 11 (§4902A(7)) but does not list specific facility types in this text.  
- The bill permits suspension upon certain federal actions or inquiries, but does not specify timelines or documentation requirements for suspensions beyond “until” federal notification allows resumption (§4904A(c)(3)).

## Health Impact
- Allows terminally ill medical marijuana cardholders to use medical marijuana in qualifying facilities, subject to restrictions (§4904A(c)(1)).  
- Requires integration into the patient’s medical record (§4904A(c)(1)(b)) and allows prohibition if contraindicated or adverse to treatment (§4904A(c)(2)).  
- Excludes application to emergency department contexts while the patient is receiving or waiting for emergency medical services (§4904A(c)(4)(a)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds/creates health care facility exceptions** to general limitations on medical marijuana possession/use by requiring facilities to permit use by terminally ill cardholders with specific conditions (§4904A(c)(1)).  
- **Prohibits smoking/vaping** of medical marijuana in health care facilities under the exception (§4904A(c)(1)(c)).  
- **Assigns responsibility** to patient/caregiver for acquisition, administration, retrieval, and removal; restricts staff involvement to disposal only (§4904A(c)(1)(d)-(e)).  
- **Requires locked storage** and discharge/removal procedures, with facility storage until disposal if not removed (§4904A(c)(1)(f)-(g)).  
- **Allows facility prohibition** when contraindicated/adversely impacts care (§4904A(c)(2)).  
- **Allows facility suspension** based on specified federal enforcement actions, inquiries, or federal rules/guidance (§4904A(c)(3)).  
- **Limits liability/discipline** for good-faith compliance, except gross negligence, recklessness, or intentional misconduct (§4904A(c)(5)).  
- States compliance **may not be a condition** of obtaining/retaining/renewing a facility license (§4904A(c)(6)).  
- Names the Act **“Ryan’s Law”** (Section 4).

## Who Benefits
- **Terminally ill patients** who are medical marijuana **cardholders** seeking to use medical marijuana while staying in qualifying health care facilities (§4904A(c)(1)).  
- **Designated caregivers** assisting terminally ill patients (responsibility and role specified; §4904A(c)(1)(e)).  
- **Health care professionals and facility staff** receive liability/discipline protections for good-faith compliance (with exceptions) (§4904A(c)(5)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Health care facilities** may face new compliance and administrative requirements (medical record documentation, policies/procedures, storage rules) (§4904A(c)(1)(b), (f), (h)).  
- **Terminally ill patients** may be denied access where the facility determines use is contraindicated or adverse to care (§4904A(c)(2)) or where the facility suspends use due to federal actions/guidance (§4904A(c)(3)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Facility must permit** medical marijuana use for terminally ill cardholders and must implement listed requirements (card verification; documentation; no smoking/vaping; staff administration limits; patient/caregiver responsibility; locked storage; discharge/removal; written policies) (§4904A(c)(1)(a)-(h)).  
- **Facility may prohibit** use if medically contraindicated or adverse to care (§4904A(c)(2)).  
- **Facility may suspend** permitting use under specified federal enforcement actions, inquiries, or prohibitive federal rules/guidance, until notified it may resume (§4904A(c)(3)).  
- **Scope limits:** Does not apply in emergency medical services/emergency department waiting/receiving EMS (§4904A(c)(4)(a)); applies only within Delaware’s physical boundaries (§4904A(c)(4)(b); also §4904A(b) and (c)(4)(b) reference Delaware boundaries).  
- **Liability/discipline shield:** No civil/criminal liability or unprofessional conduct discipline for good-faith compliance, except for **gross negligence, recklessness, or intentional misconduct** (§4904A(c)(5)).  
- **Licensing:** Compliance cannot be required as a condition of facility licensure renewal/retention (§4904A(c)(6)).
