## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16, Chapter 48B to ensure Delaware’s statutory framework for opioid settlement agreements and released claims explicitly covers not only opioid-related “entities” but also “individuals” (and entities controlled by such individuals), aligning the law with potential non-bankruptcy settlements (including with members of the Sackler family) and preserving the statewide settlement structure tied to the Prescription Opioid Impact Fund.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands the definition of “statewide opioid settlement agreement” to expressly include agreements with opioid-related **individuals** and **entities controlled by such individuals** (16 Del. C. § 4802B(10)).  
- Clarifies that the statutory bar on post-release litigation applies to released claims against **entities, individuals, or other persons** released in a statewide opioid settlement agreement (16 Del. C. § 4809B).  
- Maintains the existing cutoff date: **no government entity may assert released claims after February 28, 2021** against covered released parties.  
- Confirms the Attorney General/Department of Justice is the State representative for these agreements, consistent with the statewide settlement process.  
- The bill text itself does **not** set settlement amounts or allocation rules; it changes who can be covered by qualifying statewide settlement agreements and who receives litigation protection after release.  
- Approved date in the enrolled act: **July 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Closes a statutory gap for non-bankruptcy settlements:** By adding “related individuals” and “any entities controlled by such individuals” to the definition of covered settlement agreements, the State can structure statewide opioid settlements that include individuals, not just entities (amended § 4802B(10)).  
- **Strengthens “global peace”/release protections for settling parties:** Extending the released-claims litigation bar to “individuals” may make it easier to finalize statewide settlements that require broad releases (amended § 4809B).  
- **Supports statewide management of settlement funds:** The changes are expressly tied (in the synopsis) to the statewide structure created in 2022 for managing and distributing opioid settlement monies through Chapter 48B.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits future litigation options by government entities:** The bill reinforces that, after **February 28, 2021**, government entities cannot assert **released claims** against parties released in a statewide opioid settlement agreement, now explicitly including **individuals** (amended § 4809B).  
- **May reduce leverage for subdivisions after releases are granted:** Once the Department of Justice releases claims in a qualifying statewide opioid settlement agreement, the statute bars other government entities from pursuing those released claims after the stated date (amended § 4809B).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “released claims”:** The bill does not define in this text what specific claims are “released,” beyond referencing those released by DOJ in a statewide opioid settlement agreement (amended § 4809B).  
- **Which “government entities” are covered:** The term “government entity” is used, but the bill text provided does not enumerate which state/local bodies fall within it.  
- **Interaction with future settlements:** The bill does not specify how or when a particular individual (or entity controlled by an individual) becomes covered—this appears to depend on the terms of each “statewide opioid settlement agreement.”

## Key Policy Changes
- **Expanded settlement definition (16 Del. C. § 4802B(10)):**  
  - Adds/clarifies that a “statewide opioid settlement agreement” may be between DOJ (for the State) and opioid-related parties including:  
    - manufacturers, distributors, dispensers, consultants, chain pharmacies, related entities, **related individuals**, **any entities controlled by such individuals**, and subdivisions of the State.  
- **Expanded litigation bar categories (16 Del. C. § 4809B):**  
  - Clarifies that after **February 28, 2021**, no government entity may assert released claims against **entities, individuals, or other persons** released by DOJ in a qualifying statewide opioid settlement agreement executed by the Attorney General and the released party.

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware Department of Justice / Attorney General:** Clearer statutory authority to structure statewide opioid settlement agreements that include **individuals** as released parties.  
- **Settling individuals and related controlled entities:** Gain clearer statutory protection from post-release government-entity litigation on released claims after **February 28, 2021**, when included in a statewide opioid settlement agreement release.  
- **Statewide opioid settlement framework (including the Prescription Opioid Impact Fund structure):** Potentially improved ability to finalize and implement statewide settlements that require releases covering individuals.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Other government entities (state subdivisions/local governments) seeking to sue released parties:** Their ability to assert **released claims** after **February 28, 2021** is barred against released **individuals** as well as entities (amended § 4809B).
