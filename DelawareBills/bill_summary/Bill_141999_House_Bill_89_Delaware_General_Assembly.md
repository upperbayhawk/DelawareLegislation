## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Create a Home Improvement Dispute Resolution Process administered by the Delaware Department of Justice (DOJ), Division of Consumer Protection, and require contractors to participate in good faith; tie nonparticipation to Consumer Fraud Act violations and contractor registration consequences.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes a **Home Improvement Dispute Resolution Process** within the DOJ’s **Division of Consumer Protection** and requires the Division to **post the process on its website**. (Title 29, new Subchapter VI)
- Sets **eligibility steps for buyers**, including sending a **written request** to the contractor and giving the contractor **20 days** to respond, before applying to the Division. (§ 2562(b))
- Requires the Division, upon a completed application, to **notify the contractor** and give the contractor **20 days** to respond to the Division with the contractor’s position. (§ 2562(c))
- Makes contractor participation **mandatory** and requires participation **“in good faith”**; failure to participate in good faith or failure to respond is a **Consumer Fraud Act** violation. (§§ 2562(e), 2566)
- Provides a framework for assessing “good faith” (e.g., timely responses, written responses, reasonable settlement offers, mitigation/repairs), and states that **refusing a settlement is not, by itself, evidence of bad faith**. (§ 2563)
- Requires the Division to **notify the Department of Labor** when a contractor fails to respond or fails to participate in good faith. (§ 2565)
- Expands civil remedies: allows recovery of **actual damages, court costs, and reasonable attorney fees**, and authorizes **treble damages (3× compensatory damages)** if specified pre-suit and proof conditions are met (including a **10-business-day** settlement offer timeline). (§ 2567)
- Effective **immediately upon enactment**, but must be **implemented 6 months from the date of enactment**. (Section 3; Fiscal Note assumption: effective upon Governor’s signature)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Structured dispute pathway for homeowners/buyers:** Buyers can initiate a DOJ-assisted process after giving the contractor **20 days** to respond to a written request, potentially resolving disputes without immediate litigation. (§ 2562(b), (d))
- **Stronger contractor accountability:** Failure to respond or participate in good faith is explicitly an **unlawful practice under the Consumer Fraud Act**, presumed **wilful** (unless good cause shown). (§ 2566)
- **Enhanced consumer remedies:** Buyers may recover **attorney fees and costs** and may qualify for **treble damages** when procedural steps are followed and the court awards more than the buyer’s settlement offer. (§ 2567)
- **Registration leverage:** Contractor failure to participate in good faith becomes relevant to **registration application disclosures** and may support **denial/suspension/revocation** of registration. (Title 19 §§ 3605(b)(4)d, 3607(a)(8))
- **Interagency escalation:** Required notice to the **Department of Labor** when contractors fail to respond/participate may increase compliance pressure. (§ 2565)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative and staffing costs to DOJ:** The fiscal note estimates **FY2026 recurring costs of $299,388** and **one-time FY2026 costs of $20,010**, including staffing and a case management system. (Fiscal Note)
- **Potential increased liability exposure for contractors:** Nonparticipation is presumed **wilful**, and qualifying cases may result in **3× compensatory damages** plus attorney fees and costs. (§§ 2566(b), 2567)
- **Mandatory participation burden:** Contractors must engage with Division correspondence and process requirements on statutory timelines (**20 days** to respond to Division notice). (§ 2562(c), (e))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Process details not fully specified in statute:** The bill requires the Division to “establish” and describe procedures in notices, but does not detail mediation format, timelines beyond the 20-day response windows, or resolution outcomes. (§ 2562(a), (c)(4))
- **“Good faith” determination mechanics:** The bill references a “finder of fact” and allows additional indicia via regulations, but does not specify who initially determines good faith within the process versus in later enforcement/litigation. (§ 2563)
- **Scope of “home improvement” and “home improvement contract”:** Defined by cross-reference to **11 Del. C. § 916**, not restated in the bill text. (§ 2561(7)-(8))
- **Confidentiality limits depend on court discretion:** Communications are generally treated as compromise negotiations but may be admitted for limited purposes (good faith) “in the discretion of the court.” (§ 2564)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Title 29, Chapter 25, Subchapter VI** (“Home Improvement Dispute Resolution”), including:
  - Definitions (buyer, contractor, home, etc.). (§ 2561)
  - Buyer eligibility prerequisites, including a **written request** using a Director-approved form, signed **under penalty of perjury**, delivered through specified methods, and giving the contractor **20 days** to respond. (§ 2562(b)(1))
  - Buyer application requirements to the Division, signed **under penalty of perjury**, including the request, contractor response or sworn nonresponse statement after **20 days**, the contract, and other requested information. (§ 2562(b)(2))
  - Division notice to contractor initiating the process; contractor given **20 days** to respond to the Division. (§ 2562(c))
  - Mandatory contractor participation **in good faith**. (§ 2562(e))
  - “Good faith” factors and rule that settlement agreement acceptance is not determinative. (§ 2563)
  - Confidentiality/evidence treatment of communications; possible court admission for the limited purpose of assessing good faith. (§ 2564)
  - Division notice to Department of Labor upon nonresponse or lack of good faith. (§ 2565)
  - Makes nonresponse/non-good-faith participation an unlawful practice and **presumed wilful** (rebuttable for good cause). (§ 2566)
  - Civil cause of action: **actual damages + costs + reasonable attorney fees**; **treble damages (3×)** available if conditions met, including **written settlement offer at least 10 business days** before suit and contractor rejection (including deemed rejection by not responding within **10 business days**). (§ 2567)
- **Amends Title 19 contractor registration provisions**:
  - Registration application must disclose if contractor failed to participate in good faith in the new process. (19 Del. C. § 3605(b)(4)d)
  - Department may deny/suspend/revoke registration for failing to participate in good faith. (19 Del. C. § 3607(a)(8))
  - Expands basis to include violations related to **consumer protection**. (19 Del. C. § 3607(a)(7))

## Who Benefits
- **Buyers/homeowners** purchasing home improvement services or merchandise who have disputes tied to home improvement contracts (access to DOJ-assisted process; potential fee-shifting and treble damages under specified conditions). (§§ 2562, 2567)
- DOJ/State enforcement posture (clear statutory linkage between nonparticipation and Consumer Fraud Act enforcement tools). (§ 2566)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Home improvement contractors** subject to mandatory participation requirements, potential Consumer Fraud Act exposure (presumed wilful violations), potential treble damages and attorney-fee liability, and potential registration denial/suspension/revocation. (§§ 2562(e), 2566, 2567; 19 Del. C. §§ 3605, 3607)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective immediately** upon enactment (and fiscal note assumes effective upon **Governor’s signature**). (Section 3; Fiscal Note assumption)
- **Implementation required 6 months from the date of enactment.** (Section 3)
- Key statutory deadlines within the process:
  - Contractor gets **20 days** to respond to the buyer’s written request (buyer must include this statement). (§ 2562(b)(1)d)
  - If no response within **20 days**, buyer may attest to nonresponse and apply to the Division. (§ 2562(b)(2)b.2)
  - Contractor gets **20 days** to respond to the Division’s initiation notice. (§ 2562(c)(3))
  - For treble damages eligibility: buyer must make a written settlement offer **at least 10 business days** before filing suit; contractor nonresponse within **10 business days** counts as rejection. (§ 2567(b)(2)-(3))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Mandatory contractor participation** in the dispute resolution process **in good faith**. (§ 2562(e))
- **Consumer Fraud Act violation:** Failure to respond to Division notice or failure to participate in good faith is an unlawful practice under **6 Del. C. § 2513** and a violation of the Consumer Fraud Act; violation is **presumed wilful**, rebuttable by showing **good cause**. (§ 2566)
- **Department of Labor notification:** Division must notify DOL of contractor nonresponse or lack of good faith, and must send a copy of the notice to the contractor. (§ 2565)
- **Civil remedies:** Actual damages, court costs, reasonable attorney fees; **treble damages (3×)** available if statutory prerequisites are met (including eligibility steps, pre-suit settlement offer timing, rejection, damages exceeding offer, and wilful violation). (§ 2567)
- **Contractor registration consequences:** Failure to participate in good faith may be grounds for **denial, suspension, or revocation** of registration. (19 Del. C. § 3607(a)(8))
