## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 of the Delaware Code to require **more frequent reporting (three times per year instead of annually)** by school districts and charter schools to the Delaware Department of Education (the “Department”) on (1) reading screening results and interventions and (2) evidence-based reading curricula and related professional development implementation.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **each school district and charter school** to report to the Department **three times a year** rather than annually.
- Sets three reporting deadlines each year: **October 31, March 1, and June 30**.
- Expands §158(d) reporting cadence for reading screening data: **number and percentage of students** identified with a **potential reading deficiency**, including **characteristics of dyslexia**, **disaggregated by grade and individual school**, plus **literacy intervention approaches being provided**.
- Expands §4145(c) reporting cadence for evidence-based reading curricula and professional development implementation for inclusion in the Department’s annual report.
- Requires reporting of: **(1) curricula adopted**, **(2) identified individuals and responsibilities** for approving/providing professional development, and **(3) how educators will access and successfully complete required professional development**.
- Changes statutory language from permissive/annual framing to **mandatory** and **tri-annual** reporting (“shall must”; “annually must report” on the three dates).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More timely visibility** into reading screening results and interventions by requiring reporting on **October 31, March 1, and June 30**, instead of once per year (Sections 1 and 2).
- **Improved monitoring of early literacy needs**, including students showing **characteristics of dyslexia**, by requiring repeated reporting of **counts and percentages** disaggregated by **grade** and **individual school** (§158(d)).
- **More frequent accountability** for implementation of evidence-based reading curricula and professional development planning, since curricula and PD-related items must be reported on each of the three annual dates (§4145(c)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased administrative workload** for districts and charter schools due to moving from **annual** reporting to **three reports per year** for both reading screening/intervention reporting (§158(d)) and curriculum/PD reporting (§4145(c)).
- **Compressed reporting timelines** may require additional data management capacity to meet fixed deadlines (**Oct 31, Mar 1, Jun 30**) each year.

## Key Policy Changes
1. **Title 14 §158(d) (Reading screening reporting):**
   - Changes reporting from **annual** to **three times per year**, due **on or before October 31, March 1, and June 30**.
   - Requires reporting of the **number and percentage of students** identified with a **potential reading deficiency**, including **characteristics of dyslexia**, disaggregated by **grade** and **individual school**, and the **literacy intervention approaches being provided**.
2. **Title 14 §4145(c) (Evidence-based reading curricula reporting):**
   - Changes reporting from **beginning October 31, 2023 (annual reporting)** to reporting **on or before October 31, March 1, and June 30 of each year**.
   - Requires reporting to the Department for the Department’s annual report of:
     - **Curricula adopted** under §4145.
     - **Individuals identified** under §4145(b)(3) and each individual’s responsibilities for approving/providing professional development under §4145(b)(2) and §4145(b)(4).
     - The district/charter plan to ensure educators have access to and successfully complete the professional development required under §4145(b)(4).
