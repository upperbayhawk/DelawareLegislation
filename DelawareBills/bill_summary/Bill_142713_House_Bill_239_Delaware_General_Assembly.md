## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Propose a constitutional amendment to **Section 9 of Article II of the Delaware Constitution** to require a **three-fifths vote of all members elected** to a chamber to **suspend that chamber’s rules**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Amends **Article II, Section 9** (Legislative rules of proceedings) of the **Delaware Constitution**.
- Adds a requirement that **no rule or rules may be suspended** without a vote of **three-fifths of all members elected** to the chamber seeking to suspend rules.
- Applies separately to **each House** (House of Representatives and Senate) when suspending its own rules.
- Leaves unchanged existing powers for each House to determine its rules, punish members for disorderly behavior, and expel a member.
- Retains the existing expulsion threshold: **two-thirds of all members elected** to expel a member.
- Labeled in the synopsis as the **“First Leg Constitutional Amendment”** (i.e., a constitutional amendment proposal).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Raises the threshold for suspending rules** to **three-fifths of all members elected**, which may increase procedural stability and make ad-hoc rule suspensions harder. (Text: “no rule or rules may be suspended without the concurrence of three-fifths of all members elected…”)
- Creates a **uniform constitutional standard** for rule suspension across chambers, rather than relying solely on internal rules. (Text amends Constitution §9)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces flexibility** to suspend rules quickly unless **three-fifths of all members elected** concur, which could make it harder to expedite legislative action. (Text establishes a higher vote requirement for suspensions)
- Could make rule suspensions more difficult in situations with **vacancies or absences**, because the threshold is based on **“all members elected”** rather than members present. (Text: “three-fifths of all members elected”)
