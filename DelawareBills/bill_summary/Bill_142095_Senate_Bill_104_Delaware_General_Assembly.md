## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Require certain high-occupancy and public-facing buildings in Delaware to maintain access to trauma kits to provide immediate emergency care to people experiencing trauma. (16 Del. C. Ch. 30R, § 3001R)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines “trauma kit” contents and specifies required instructional documents; also states a trauma kit **must not contain a chest seal**. (§ 3002R)
- Applies trauma-kit requirements to a broad list of buildings (e.g., grocery stores, restaurants, schools, hotels, apartments/condos, sports arenas, public buildings, courthouses, health-care facilities, and “work areas” with **50+ people routinely on site**). (§ 3004R; § 3002R(3))
- Requires the responsible managing person/entity to **acquire and place at least 6 trauma kits** per covered building/facility in a **clearly visible, centrally located, and easily accessible** area. (§ 3004R(c)(1))
- Requires **annual inspection** of all kits (once every year from installation date) for expired/missing items and replacement as needed. (§ 3004R(c)(2))
- Requires **restocking after each use** when the manager is aware or reasonably should be aware a kit was used. (§ 3004R(c)(3))
- Requires **at least annual tenant notification** of kit locations and provision of **training contact information** (may point to recognized “Stop the Bleed”/similar sources). (§ 3004R(c)(4))
- Creates an administrative penalty: **$100 for a first violation** and **not less than $250 for each subsequent violation**. (§ 3007R)
- Effective **180 days after enactment into law**. (Section 3)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Faster access to bleeding-control supplies in many public and high-occupancy settings by requiring **at least 6 trauma kits** in covered buildings. (§ 3004R(c)(1))
- Ongoing readiness via **annual inspection** and **post-use restocking** requirements. (§ 3004R(c)(2)–(3))
- Improves awareness and potential training uptake by requiring **annual notification** to tenants with training contact information. (§ 3004R(c)(4))
- Provides a civil-liability protection framework for compliant entities by stating compliant entities are **not liable for civil damages** resulting from acts/omissions in rendering emergency care using a trauma kit. (§ 3004R(b)); also ties trauma-kit use to “emergency care or treatment” under Title 16 §§ 6801–6802. (Section 2; § 6804)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Compliance burden on managing entities to purchase, place, inspect, and restock **at least 6 kits** per covered building/facility and to send annual notifications. (§ 3004R(c)(1)–(4))
- Potential administrative penalties for noncompliance: **$100 first violation** and **≥ $250** for subsequent violations. (§ 3007R)
- Broad coverage (including **apartment buildings/condominiums** and many businesses) could impose obligations on a wide range of property managers and operators. (§ 3004R(a))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- Whether “**at least 6 trauma kits**” applies per building, per facility, per tenant area, or scales with size/occupancy beyond the minimum is not further specified. (§ 3004R(c)(1))
- The bill does not specify which agency enforces penalties across non-workplace settings, beyond directing rulemaking to the Department of Labor and the Department of Health and Social Services. (§ 3006R; § 3007R)
- No explicit standards for what qualifies as a “**clearly visible, centrally located, and easily accessible** area,” beyond those phrases. (§ 3004R(c)(1))
- The statement that a kit “**must not contain a chest seal within the kit**” is unusual and the rationale is not specified in the bill text. (§ 3002R(1))
- The term “**tenants**” is used broadly; how it applies to settings like schools, restaurants, museums, or courthouses is not defined. (§ 3004R(c)(4))

## Educational Impact
- Applies to **public and nonpublic schools** and **educational and vocational institutions**, requiring placement of **at least 6 trauma kits**, annual inspection, restocking after use, and annual notification about kit locations and training contacts. (§ 3004R(a)(20)–(21); § 3004R(c))

## Health Impact
- Establishes statewide access to bleeding-control trauma kits in many public settings and workplaces, with required contents (tourniquet, bleeding control bandage, gloves/marker, scissors, and instructional documents). (§ 3002R(2); § 3004R)
- Clarifies that use of a trauma kit is considered “emergency care or treatment” for purposes of Good Samaritan protections under Title 16 §§ 6801 and 6802. (Section 2; § 6804)

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates a new Chapter 30R in Title 16 establishing trauma-kit requirements, definitions, and duties. (§§ 3001R–3007R)
- Defines “work area” as a place of employment where **50 or more people** are routinely on site. (§ 3002R(3))
- Defines required trauma-kit contents and required instruction sources; prohibits including a **chest seal** in the kit. (§ 3002R(1)–(2))
- Requires covered buildings to have **at least 6 trauma kits** placed in accessible locations, with annual inspections and restocking after use. (§ 3004R(c)(1)–(3))
- Requires **annual notification** to tenants about kit locations and training contact information. (§ 3004R(c)(4))
- Exempts **private homes, private residences, and private vehicles**. (§ 3005R)
- Directs Department of Labor and DHSS to adopt implementing regulations. (§ 3006R)
- Establishes administrative penalties (**$100** first; **≥ $250** subsequent). (§ 3007R)
- Amends Title 16, Chapter 68 to treat trauma-kit use as emergency care for Good Samaritan purposes. (Section 2; § 6804)

## Who Benefits
- Members of the public, employees, tenants, students, and visitors in covered buildings who may need immediate bleeding-control aid. (§ 3001R; § 3004R)
- Building/facility managing entities that comply, due to specified civil-liability protection for damages arising from emergency care acts/omissions using a trauma kit. (§ 3004R(b))
- Individuals providing emergency assistance, through the bill’s linkage of trauma-kit use to emergency care under Title 16 §§ 6801–6802. (Section 2; § 6804)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Managing entities/property managers and employers responsible for covered buildings due to required purchase/placement/inspection/restocking and tenant-notification duties, and exposure to administrative penalties for violations. (§ 3004R(c); § 3007R)
- Covered facilities with multiple buildings or large footprints, to the extent the bill’s “at least 6” minimum may require multiple sets depending on implementation rules (specific scaling not stated). (§ 3004R(c)(1); § 3006R)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** **180 days upon enactment into law**. (Section 3)
- **Inspection deadline/frequency:** Inspect all trauma kits **once every year from the date of installation**. (§ 3004R(c)(2))
- **Restocking deadline:** Restock **after each use** when aware or reasonably should be aware of use. (§ 3004R(c)(3))
- **Notification frequency:** Notify tenants of kit locations and provide training contact information **at least once per year**. (§ 3004R(c)(4))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (covered buildings):**
  - Place **at least 6 trauma kits** in a **clearly visible, centrally located, and easily accessible** area. (§ 3004R(c)(1))
  - **Annual inspection** and replacement of expired/missing items. (§ 3004R(c)(2))
  - **Restock after each use** as needed. (§ 3004R(c)(3))
  - **Annual tenant notification** of kit locations and training contact information (at least one training source). (§ 3004R(c)(4))
  - Suppliers must provide acquirers with information governing **use and maintenance** of the trauma kit. (§ 3003R)
- **Penalties:** Administrative fine of **$100** for a first violation; **not less than $250** for each subsequent violation. (§ 3007R)
- **Rulemaking:** Department of Labor (workplace-related) and Department of Health and Social Services (non-workplace) must adopt implementing rules/regulations. (§ 3006R)
