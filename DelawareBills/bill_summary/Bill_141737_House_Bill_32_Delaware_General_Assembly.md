## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 14 to create a statewide “school officer unit” funding structure so school districts and charter schools can employ school officers—defined as either school resource officers (SROs) or constables—with at least one unit per school and additional units for schools over 1,000 full-time equivalent (FTE) students.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates “school officer units” for **all school districts and charter schools** to fund employment of **school officers (constables or SROs)**.  
- Allocates units by enrollment: **1 unit per school**, plus **1 additional unit for every 1,000 FTE students over the first 1,000**, plus **fractional units** for partial amounts over 1,000.  
- Requires cost-sharing: the **State pays up to 70%** of annual salary and other employment costs; **local funds pay the remainder**.  
- Requires recipients to use appropriated unit funds to **employ a school officer**; fractional units can be **combined** or converted to a **cash option up to 30% of a unit** for specified uses under **§ 4140(b)**.  
- Allows a district/charter to **decline** unit funding.  
- SROs funded under the section must comply with **training requirements under § 4112F(d)**.  
- DOE may adopt regulations (with State Board approval) to implement/enforce.  
- Fiscal note estimates **241.84 units in FY 2026**, growing **2.0% annually**, with total costs depending on mix of constables vs. sworn law enforcement officers.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Baseline coverage statewide:** Funds **at least 1 school officer in each school** (school districts) and **each charter school**, regardless of enrollment (Synopsis; § 1716I(b)).  
- **Scales with school size:** Provides **additional units above 1,000 FTE**, including **fractional units**, aligning funding with enrollment (§ 1716I(b)(1)-(2)).  
- **Local flexibility in staffing:** “School officer” includes **constables or SROs**, allowing districts/charters to choose the type of personnel (§ 1716I(a)(4); fiscal note Assumption #3).  
- **Dedicated funding use requirement:** Funds appropriated for a unit must be used to **employ a school officer**, preventing diversion of those unit funds (§ 1716I(e)(1)).  
- **Option for fractional-unit cash (limited):** Cash option for fractional units **up to 30% of a unit** for items authorized under **§ 4140(b)** (§ 1716I(e)(2)b).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Creates/raises local cost obligations:** Local governments must pay the **portion not covered by the State (up to 30%+)** of salary and other employment costs (§ 1716I(d)). Fiscal note shows local shares (e.g., FY 2026 local unit cost estimated at **$7,026,311** under a mixed staffing assumption).  
- **Potentially large state and total costs:** Fiscal note estimates total costs ranging from **$23,692,912 (FY 2026 minimum scenario: all constables)** to **$71,607,886 (FY 2026 maximum scenario: all sworn law enforcement officers)** (Fiscal note #9).  
- **Equipment/vehicle costs assumed state-funded in fiscal note:** Fiscal note assumes equipment and maintenance costs are **fully funded through state funds** (Assumptions #7c, #8c), which increases state cost exposure.  
- **May be challenging to staff:** Bill requires unit funds be used to employ a school officer if accepted (§ 1716I(e)(1)), which could be difficult if hiring shortages exist (no alternative compliance stated other than declining funding (§ 1716I(f))).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates new § 1716I “School officer unit”** in Title 14, Chapter 17.  
- **Defines “school officer”** as **a constable or an SRO** (§ 1716I(a)(4)).  
- **Allocates units**:  
  - School districts: **1 unit per school** + additional **1 per 1,000 FTE over 1,000** + **fractional unit** for remaining percentage over 1,000 (§ 1716I(b)(1)).  
  - Charter schools: **1 unit per charter school** + additional **1 per 1,000 FTE over 1,000** + **fractional unit** (§ 1716I(b)(2)).  
- **Prevents double counting:** Each student may be counted **only once** in establishing units (§ 1716I(c)).  
- **Cost-sharing rule:** State pays **maximum 70%**; local pays remainder (§ 1716I(d)).  
- **Use-of-funds requirement:** Unit funds must be used to **employ a school officer** (§ 1716I(e)(1)).  
- **Fractional unit options:** Combine fractions to fund an officer, or take a **cash option up to 30% of a unit** for § 4140(b) items (§ 1716I(e)(2)).  
- **Opt-out allowed:** A district/charter may **decline** unit funding (§ 1716I(f)).  
- **Training requirement:** An SRO funded must comply with **§ 4112F(d)** training (§ 1716I(g)).  
- **Regulatory authority:** DOE rulemaking with State Board approval (§ 1716I(h)).

## Who Benefits
- **School districts and charter schools** receiving state-shared funding for school officers (§ 1716I(b), (d)).  
- **Schools/students** in schools that gain funded officer positions, including schools under **1,000 students** that still receive **1 unit** (§ 1716I(b); Synopsis).  
- **Constables and SROs** who may be employed using unit funding (§ 1716I(a)(4), (e)(1)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Local taxpayers/local education agencies’ budgets** due to required **local share** of salary and other employment costs (State pays up to **70%**, local pays remainder) (§ 1716I(d); fiscal note local unit cost estimates).
