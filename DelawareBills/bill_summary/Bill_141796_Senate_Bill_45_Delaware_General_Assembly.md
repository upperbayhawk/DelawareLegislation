## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 11 (Crimes and Criminal Procedure) to create a specific criminal offense for **fraudulent statements made in connection with applying for or renewing insurance**, including statements made to obtain **fees/commissions/other benefits** from insurers or intermediaries.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new criminal offense: **“application insurance fraud”** in **11 Del. C. § 913B**.  
- Covers **false, incomplete, or misleading** written or oral statements (including **computer-generated documents**) that are **material** to an application to **obtain or renew insurance**.  
- Criminalizes not only making such statements, but also **assisting/abetting/soliciting/conspiring** to prepare or make them.  
- Separately criminalizes making false statements on/relative to an application to obtain a **fee, commission, money, or other benefit** from an **insurer, agent, broker, or individual**.  
- Sets penalty level by dollar threshold: **Class G felony** if the aggregate value wrongfully obtained or sought is **at least $1,000**; otherwise **Class A misdemeanor**.  
- Treats **each act** as a separate offense; allows **aggregation of 5 or more separate acts** to establish liability/threshold.  
- Provides that signing/initialing an application **may give rise to an inference** the person read and reviewed it.  
- Expands definitions so “insurer” and “insurance” include certain **health coverage entities/benefits** (health service corporations, HMOs, managed care organizations).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clarifies and expands criminal enforcement** against fraud occurring at the application/renewal stage (new § 913B).  
- **Deterrence** may improve application integrity by making material misstatements explicitly criminal (false/incomplete/misleading, material facts).  
- Allows prosecution where conduct involves **collaboration** (assists/abets/solicits/conspires).  
- Establishes a **clear felony threshold** at **$1,000** in aggregate wrongfully obtained or sought.  
- Applies explicitly to certain **health coverage arrangements** by defining “insurer” and “insurance” to include HSCs/HMOs/MCOs and subscriber benefits.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- The statute criminalizes **“false, incomplete, or misleading”** statements that are “material,” which could increase exposure for applicants who make **omissions** that are later deemed material (standard set in text; no additional safe harbor described).  
- **Each act** can be charged separately, including multiple acts within a **single application/document**, potentially increasing charging counts and associated penalties.  
- Signing/initialing an application **may allow an inference** of review, which could make defense harder for signers who claim they did not read/understand the contents.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What precisely qualifies as **“material”** to an application is **not defined** in the bill text.  
- How “**aggregate value**” is calculated (e.g., premium savings, commissions, benefits valuation) is **not specified** beyond “property, services, or other benefit.”  
- The mechanism and limits for **aggregating “5 or more separate acts”** (time period, relatedness, same insurer vs. multiple) are **not specified**.  
- The bill does not specify whether the offense requires successful obtainment of insurance/benefit versus merely **“sought to be obtained”** (it includes both, but details are not further clarified).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds **11 Del. C. § 913B** establishing the crime of **application insurance fraud**.  
- Prohibited conduct includes:
  - Presenting/causing presentation of **written or oral statements** (including computer-generated documents) in support of an application to obtain/renew insurance, **knowing** it contains **false, incomplete, or misleading** material information.  
  - **Assisting/abetting/soliciting/conspiring** with another to prepare or make such statements for submission.  
  - Making false/fraudulent statements on/relative to an application to obtain a **fee, commission, money, or other benefit** from an insurer, agent, broker, or individual.  
- Penalty structure:
  - **Class G felony** if aggregate value wrongfully obtained or sought is **≥ $1,000**.  
  - Otherwise **Class A misdemeanor**.  
- Offense counting/aggregation:
  - **Each act** is a separate offense.  
  - **5 or more separate acts may be aggregated** to establish liability under the section.  
  - **Multiple acts within a single application/document** each count as separate offenses.  
- Evidence inference:
  - Signing/initialing an application or supporting document **may give rise to an inference** that the person read/reviewed it.  
- Definitions expanded for health coverage entities/benefits.

## Who Benefits

- **Insurance markets/policyholders** to the extent enforcement reduces fraudulent underwriting and improper commissions (benefit not quantified in text).
