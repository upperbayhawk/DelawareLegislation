## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29, Chapter 63 of the Delaware Code to (1) codify eligibility and usage rules for General Assembly Grants-in-Aid and (2) create a 6-member Grant-in-Aid Subcommittee to review new applicants and make annual recommendations to the Joint Finance Committee (JFC).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Codifies **minimum eligibility requirements** for organizations seeking Grants-in-Aid (e.g., nonprofit or government status, **2+ years in operation**, governance, financial documentation, and revenue diversification).
- Prohibits Grant-in-Aid funds from being used for specified purposes, including **child daycare**, **capital equipment**, **building relocation/rehab/renovation/purchase**, **elected officials’ compensation**, **political campaigns/partisan purposes**, **lobbyists/lobbying**, and **benefits for non-Delaware residents**.
- Creates a **Grant-in-Aid Subcommittee** with **3 Senators and 3 Representatives**, with **party-balance limits** (no more than **2 per chamber** from the same political party).
- Subcommittee members **may not also serve on the Joint Finance Committee**.
- Subcommittee must **annually review** applications from organizations **new** to Grants-in-Aid and recommend approvals and funding levels to the JFC.
- Subcommittee **may** review current recipients for **program efficacy** and **compliance** and may request additional documentation and hold public hearings.
- Requires a **final report and recommendations by the third Monday of May each year**.
- Effective **January 1 following enactment**; Subcommittee leadership must develop review rules/procedures by **February 1 following enactment**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardizes eligibility statewide**: Sets clear statutory criteria (e.g., **2-year operating history**, governance requirements, accounting/budget procedures, and financial statements within **past 3 years**), which may improve consistency in who can apply.  
- **Stronger fiscal controls**: Limits use of funds for certain categories (e.g., **no capital equipment**, **no building purchase/renovation**, **no lobbying**), which may reduce risk of funds being used outside intended operating/program purposes.
- **Added legislative oversight structure**: Establishes a dedicated **6-member** body to review **new** applicants annually and optionally review existing recipients for **efficacy/compliance**.
- **Transparency/record-development tools**: Authority to require additional information and to hold **public hearings** can create a clearer record for JFC decisions.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Restricts allowable uses**: The bill prohibits Grant-in-Aid funding for several categories (including **child daycare**, **capital equipment**, and **building-related costs**), potentially limiting support for organizations whose needs fall in those areas.
- **Eligibility barriers for smaller/newer organizations**: Requires **at least 2 years** of operation and the ability to fund **at least half of operations** from non–Grant-in-Aid sources, which may exclude early-stage or financially constrained nonprofits.
- **Additional administrative workload**: Requires staff support from the Office of the Controller General (OCG) and ongoing annual review/reporting, with associated costs (see Budget Impact).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The statute requires programs be “**unduplicated by other state-supported agencies**” and “**satisfy unmet needs**,” but does not define how duplication or unmet need is measured.  
- The prohibition on “**providing child daycare**” is not further defined (e.g., whether it includes after-school care, mixed-service programs, or partial daycare components).
- “**Capital equipment**” is not defined (e.g., thresholds for capitalization, vehicles, technology, or durable goods).
- The bill allows applicants unable to provide audited/reviewed/compiled statements to submit an explanation and other documents, but does not specify **minimum acceptable substitutes** or standards for approval.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Eligibility codified** for Grants-in-Aid applicants; to be eligible an organization must, at minimum:
  - Be either:
    - an **incorporated nonprofit** with **IRS recognizable exempt status** (or operating under a qualifying nonprofit “umbrella”), **or**
    - a **state, county, or local government organization**.
  - Have been in operation **at least 2 years** before applying.
  - If a nonprofit under the IRS-exempt category: have an **active, community-represented, volunteer Board of Directors** that sets policies/goals/objectives and maintains meeting minutes.
  - Offer programs “**unduplicated**” by other state-supported agencies and meeting “**unmet needs**.”
  - Have **accounting and budget procedures**.
  - Have an **audit, review, or compilation** of financial statements completed within the **past 3 years** by a CPA or public accountant, or provide an explanation and other supporting financial documentation if unable.
  - Demonstrate ability to fund **at least half (50%)** of operations from **non–Grant-in-Aid** revenue sources.
- **Permitted/Prohibited uses codified**: Grant-in-Aid funding may **not** be used for:
  1) **Providing child daycare**  
  2) **Purchase of capital equipment**  
  3) **Relocation, rehabilitation, renovation, or purchase of buildings**  
  4) Paying any part of an **elected official’s salary or benefits**  
  5) **Political campaigns** or **partisan political purposes**  
  6) Hiring **lobbyists** or other **lobbying services**  
  7) Activities/programs/services benefiting individuals who **do not reside in Delaware**
- Establishes **§ 6352 Grant-in-Aid Subcommittee**:
  - **6 members** total: **3 Senate** (appointed by President Pro Tempore) and **3 House** (appointed by Speaker).
  - Party limitation: **no more than 2** appointees per chamber from the same political party.
  - Chair/vice chair rotation: **odd-numbered years** chair is a **Senator**; **even-numbered years** chair is a **Representative**.
  - Members **cannot** also be members of the **Joint Finance Committee**.
  - Must provide annual report to JFC by the **third Monday of May**.
  - Members compensated at same rate as the **Joint Legislative Oversight and Sunset Committee**.

## Who Benefits
- The General Assembly/JFC, through a structured review process and annual recommendations for **new** Grant-in-Aid applicants.
- Applicants and the public, through clearer statutory eligibility standards and restrictions on allowable spending.
- Delaware residents specifically, because funds may not be used for activities/services benefiting individuals who **do not reside in Delaware**.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Organizations operating for **less than 2 years** (ineligible).
- Organizations unable to fund **at least 50%** of operations from non–Grant-in-Aid sources (ineligible).
- Organizations seeking Grant-in-Aid support for prohibited uses (e.g., **child daycare**, **capital equipment**, or **building** purchase/renovation/relocation).
- Existing recipients who may be subject to additional review for **efficacy** and **compliance**.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** **January 1 following enactment into law** (Section 2).  
- **Rules/procedures deadline:** By **February 1 following enactment**, the Subcommittee Chair and Vice Chair, in collaboration with the Controller General, must develop rules and procedures (Section 3).
- **Annual reporting deadline:** Subcommittee must deliver a final report and recommendations to the JFC each year by the **third Monday of May** (§ 6352(e)).
