## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 19 of the Delaware Code to prohibit employers from requiring employees to attend employer-sponsored meetings or communications primarily intended to convey the employer’s opinion on political or religious matters, and to prohibit retaliation for refusing to attend or for reporting violations.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Makes it an unlawful employment practice to **require** an employee to attend/participate in an employer-sponsored meeting or communication when the **primary purpose** is communicating the employer’s opinion about **political matters** or **religious matters**.  
- Defines **“political matters”** broadly to include political parties, legislation, regulation, public policy, election campaigns, or decisions to join/support political, civic, community, fraternal, or labor organizations.  
- Defines **“religious matters”** to include religious belief, affiliation, practice, and membership in or support of religious organizations/associations/groups.  
- Creates exceptions where the meeting/communication is **required by law**, **wholly voluntary**, or **necessary to perform lawfully required job duties**.  
- Provides a specific exception for **religious employers** when the meeting/communication is **religious**.  
- Prohibits employers from **threatening, discharging, disciplining, or taking adverse employment action** because an employee refuses to attend/participate or makes a **good-faith report** of a violation or suspected violation.  
- Authorizes civil fines in Department actions for retaliation-related violations: **$1,000 to $5,000 per violation**, in addition to damages.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Employee protection from compelled participation:** Employees gain statutory protection from being forced to attend meetings primarily focused on employer opinions about political or religious matters.  
- **Anti-retaliation safeguard:** Explicitly bars adverse actions for refusing to participate or for making a **good-faith report** of violations or suspected violations.  
- **Clear monetary deterrent:** Establishes a defined civil penalty range (**$1,000–$5,000 per violation**) in specified Department-brought actions, which may deter prohibited conduct.  
- **Operational flexibility preserved via exceptions:** Allows required-by-law, voluntary, and job-duty-necessary communications, reducing conflicts with legitimate business needs.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Compliance and training burden:** Employers may need to assess whether the “primary purpose” of communications is political/religious and adjust policies and manager practices accordingly (standard implied by the bill text).  
- **Potential disputes over “primary purpose” and “voluntary”:** The bill turns legality on whether the meeting’s primary purpose is covered speech and whether attendance is “wholly voluntary,” which could increase workplace complaints or litigation risk (based on the standards created).  
- **Penalty exposure:** Employers may face civil fines of **$1,000–$5,000 per violation** (in addition to damages) in covered Department actions.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Primary purpose” standard:** The bill does not specify how to determine when the “primary purpose” of a meeting/communication is to convey the employer’s opinion on political or religious matters.  
- **Scope of “employer-sponsored meeting or communication”:** The bill does not define whether this includes emails, intranet posts, mandatory trainings, one-on-one meetings, or recorded video messages.  
- **Meaning of “wholly voluntary”:** The bill does not specify how to assess voluntariness (e.g., subtle pressure, attendance tracking, or implied consequences).  
- **Religious employer exception boundaries:** The bill does not define “religious employers” or how to determine when communications are “religious.”

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds/clarifies that it is an unlawful employment practice to **require** employees to attend/participate in employer-sponsored meetings/communications primarily intended to communicate the employer’s opinion on **political** or **religious** matters.  
- Establishes exceptions for meetings/communications that are:
  - **required by law**, or
  - **wholly voluntary**, or
  - **necessary for the employee to perform the employee’s lawfully required job duties**.  
- Creates an exception for **religious employers** when the employer-sponsored meetings/communications are **religious**.  
- Prohibits adverse employment actions when:
  - an employee does not attend/participate in a covered meeting/communication, or
  - an employee (or someone acting on the employee’s behalf) makes a **good-faith report** of a violation or suspected violation.  
- Amends judicial remedies/civil penalties so that, in Department actions for violation of specified retaliation-related provisions, the Court must fine the employer **not less than $1,000 nor more than $5,000 for each violation**, in addition to damages liability.

## Who Benefits
- **Employees** who do not want to attend employer-sponsored meetings/communications primarily about the employer’s political or religious opinions.  
- **Employees who report violations** (or suspected violations) in good faith, due to anti-retaliation protection.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Employers** who currently require attendance at meetings/communications primarily focused on political or religious opinions, due to expanded unlawful employment practice exposure and potential fines (**$1,000–$5,000 per violation**) plus damages.  
- **Managers/HR staff** responsible for organizing communications, due to the need to ensure meetings are not mandatory when primarily political/religious (impact derived from compliance obligations in the bill text).
