## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To increase required health insurance coverage for medically necessary hearing aids (and related professional services and earmolds) for individuals **younger than 24 years old** who are covered **as dependents**, by replacing the prior **$1,000 per hearing aid, per ear, every 3 years** minimum with broader coverage requirements. Applies to **individual**, **group/blanket**, and the **State employee health plan**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires insurers and the State employee health plan to cover the **entire cost** of **medically necessary hearing aids** for dependents **under age 24** (not just up to **$1,000**).  
- Requires coverage of **hearing care professional services** related to **prescribing, fitting, or dispensing** the hearing aid (and references services related to earmolds in the synopsis).  
- Sets minimum replacement frequency: **at least 1 hearing aid per ear every 3 years**, or sooner if a hearing care professional determines a new hearing aid is **medically necessary**.  
- For hearing aids with earmolds: requires coverage of **at least 1 earmold per ear annually**, or sooner if medically necessary.  
- Allows insurers to require a **prescription or other documentation** to prove need.  
- Preserves insurers’ ability to apply **deductibles, coinsurance, allowable charge limits, coordination of benefits, and provider network/licensing requirements**, and to conduct **managed care/medical necessity/utilization review**.  
- Does **not** apply to enumerated **limited benefit** policies (e.g., hospital confinement indemnity, disability income, accident-only, long-term care, Medicare supplement, etc.).  
- Effective upon Governor’s signature, but applies only to policies **issued/renewed/modified/etc. after December 31, 2026**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces out-of-pocket costs** for families by moving from a **$1,000 cap** to coverage of the **entire cost** of medically necessary hearing aids for eligible dependents. (Synopsis notes pediatric hearing aids can cost **$3,000 to $5,000** out-of-pocket.)  
- **Improves access and continuity** by guaranteeing replacement at least every **3 years** (or sooner if medically necessary) and earmolds at least **annually** (or sooner if medically necessary).  
- **Covers related professional services** (prescribing, fitting, dispensing), which can be integral to effective use of hearing aids.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increases plan costs** for the State employee Group Health Insurance Plan (GHIP): fiscal note estimates **$265,980 (FY2026)**, **$279,279 (FY2027)**, **$293,242 (FY2028)**.  
- Potential for **additional State fiscal impact** if the mandate is interpreted as exceeding an ACA **Essential Health Benefit (EHB)**, triggering State **defrayal** obligations under **45 CFR 155.170** (fiscal note).  
- Coverage remains subject to **deductibles/coinsurance/allowable charge limitations** and **provider restrictions**, which may limit practical access or leave some cost-sharing in place (expressly preserved in the bill text).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill states coverage is “medically necessary hearing aids” and includes professional services, but **does not specify** a numeric dollar cap, reimbursement rate, or a definition/standard for **“medical necessity”** beyond determinations by a “hearing care professional.”  
- The synopsis references covering services related to dispensing the hearing aid **or earmold**, but the operative text in Title 18 explicitly includes professional services related to dispensing **the hearing aid**; the precise scope of services tied specifically to **earmolds** is **not fully detailed** in the cited operative clauses.  
- The fiscal note flags potential ACA EHB issues but **does not specify** whether Delaware will treat this as an EHB or how defrayal would be calculated.

## Health Impact
- Expands coverage for hearing aids and related services for dependents **under 24**, supporting earlier and more consistent access to hearing assistance and related clinical services (as described in synopsis and coverage provisions).  
- Establishes minimum intervals for replacement (**3 years**) and earmolds (**annually**) with earlier replacement when **medically necessary**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Raises required coverage** from **up to $1,000 per hearing aid, per ear, every 3 years** to coverage of **medically necessary hearing aids** (i.e., no $1,000 cap stated) for dependents **younger than 24**.  
- Requires coverage of:  
  - **At least 1 hearing aid per ear every 3 years**, or sooner if medically necessary (Title 18 §§3357, 3571A; Title 29 §5223).  
  - For hearing aids with earmolds: **at least 1 earmold per ear annually**, or sooner if medically necessary (Title 18 §§3357, 3571A; Title 29 §5223).  
  - **Hearing care professional services** for prescribing, fitting, or dispensing the hearing aid (and included similarly for the State plan).  
- Updates the definition of “hearing aid” to include **related parts, attachments, or accessories, including earmolds** (and removes prior exclusion language for certain items in the amended text).  
- Maintains insurer tools/limits: documentation requirements, managed care/utilization review, and standard cost-sharing/features (deductibles, coinsurance, allowable charge limits, COB, provider restrictions).  
- Applies these requirements across:  
  1) **Individual policies** (Title 18, Ch. 33)  
  2) **Group and blanket policies** (Title 18, Ch. 35)  
  3) **State employee health plan** (Title 29, Ch. 52)
