## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29, Chapter 70 (state-owned property disposal) to clarify and reorganize rules on when and how state agencies—including public schools—may reallocate, trade in, sell, or otherwise dispose of state-owned “materiel,” including computer technology, and to move definitions into the chapter definitions section.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Clarifies that **all equipment, supplies, and “materiel” purchased in whole or part with state-appropriated funds are assets of the State**, not of the holding/using agency. (§ 7002(a)(1))
- Requires an agency that determines materiel is **excess to its needs** to **report it by memorandum** to **OMB Government Support Services** for reallocation or disposal. (§ 7002(a)(2))
- Specifies that **materiel being “replaced in kind”** (including for scenarios covered under the vehicles and computer-technology provisions) **is not “excess”** and may be **traded in or sold**, with proceeds credited as required by **§ 6102(c)**. (§ 7002(a)(3))
- Maintains a **2-week written notice** process by Government Support Services to state agencies and specified volunteer emergency organizations for non-vehicle, non-computer technology materiel with useful life. (§ 7002(b)(1)a)
- Provides that **vehicles** may be offered to agencies at **fair market value**, and if not purchased by agencies, to eligible **26 U.S.C. § 501(c)** tax-exempt organizations headquartered in Delaware, also at **fair market value**. (§ 7002(b)(2)a)
- Requires that **surplus/obsolete computer technology** be **offered first** to the **Department of Education’s Partners in Technology Group**; if accepted, that group **picks up at no charge**. (§ 7002(b)(3))
- If no requests are received within the **2-week** period, Government Support Services may **transfer to storage**, then make items available to political subdivisions, nonprofits, or the public at **reasonable cost**, and may arrange **public auction** or sale when appropriate. (§ 7002(c))
- Makes largely **technical/organizational changes**: moves definitions into § 7001, relocates the trade-in/sale provision from former § 7002(e) to § 7002(a)(3), and updates drafting. (Synopsis)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer authority for trade-ins/sales** when items are “replaced in kind,” expressly including agencies and **schools**, which may simplify procurement replacement cycles. (§ 7002(a)(3); § 7001(4))
- **Prioritizes educational reuse of computer technology** by requiring first offer to DOE’s **Partners in Technology Group** and providing **no-charge pickup**, which can reduce disposal burden on agencies and facilitate reuse. (§ 7002(b)(3))
- **Structured redistribution process** (2-week notice and written requests) promotes reuse by state agencies and certain volunteer emergency services before storage/auction. (§ 7002(b)(1))
- **Defined pathway for vehicles** to reach agencies and then eligible Delaware-headquartered **501(c)** organizations at **fair market value**. (§ 7002(b)(2)a)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Agencies must follow a **memorandum reporting process** for excess materiel and written request procedures, which may add administrative steps. (§ 7002(a)(2); § 7002(b)(1)b; § 7002(b)(2)b)
- For computer technology, Government Support Services must **offer first to DOE Partners in Technology** before other disposition, which could add a required step before disposal. (§ 7002(b)(3))
- “Fair market value” determinations for vehicles are assigned to Government Support Services, which may require valuation effort and could be contested, but the bill does not provide additional standards. (§ 7002(b)(2)a)

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **How “fair market value” is determined** for vehicles (method, appraisal standards, dispute resolution) is **not specified**. (§ 7002(b)(2)a)
- The bill references crediting receipts as required by **§ 6102(c)**, but does not restate **what accounts/funds** receive proceeds in these transactions. (§ 7002(a)(3))
- “Reasonable cost” for sale/availability to political subdivisions, nonprofits, or the general public is not defined. (§ 7002(c)(1))
- Criteria for determining “additional useful life” (non-vehicle, non-computer technology materiel) are not specified. (§ 7002(b)(1)a)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Moves definitions** (including “Agency,” “Materiel,” “Computer technology,” “School,” and “Government Support Services”) into **§ 7001 (Definitions)**. (§ 7001)
- Codifies/clarifies that **materiel being replaced in kind is not “excess”** and may be **traded in or sold**, with receipts credited under **§ 6102(c)**; applies to “an agency, including a school,” and references situations covered by the vehicles and computer-technology subsections. (§ 7002(a)(3))
- Keeps and clarifies the **2-week written notice** and **written request** process for redistribution of usable materiel (excluding vehicles and computer technology from that specific process). (§ 7002(b)(1))
- Continues vehicle disposition rules: agencies first at **fair market value**, then eligible Delaware-headquartered **501(c)** organizations at **fair market value**. (§ 7002(b)(2))
- Continues and clarifies computer technology priority: offer first to DOE Partners in Technology; if refused, dispose under subsection (c). (§ 7002(b)(3))
- Clarifies post-notice disposition: storage transfer, availability at **reasonable cost**, and when public auction or sale may be used. (§ 7002(c))
- Technical revisions to reduce repetitive language and conform to Delaware drafting standards. (Synopsis)

## Who Benefits
- **State agencies and public schools/charter schools** that can more clearly **trade in or sell** replaced-in-kind materiel (other than vehicles) and apply receipt-crediting rules. (§ 7002(a)(3); § 7001(4))
- **Department of Education’s Partners in Technology Group**, which receives first opportunity to acquire surplus computer technology with **no-charge pickup**. (§ 7002(b)(3))
- **Volunteer ambulance, rescue, and fire organizations** that receive notice and opportunity to request certain available materiel. (§ 7002(b)(1)a)
- Eligible **Delaware-headquartered 501(c) organizations** that may purchase vehicles not bought by agencies at **fair market value**. (§ 7002(b)(2)a)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Agencies may face added process requirements (memoranda, coordination) when disposing of excess materiel. (§ 7002(a)(2))
- Potential purchasers of surplus property other than the prioritized groups (e.g., general public) may have to wait until after internal/state and specified-organization processes, and after any DOE Partners in Technology review for computer technology. (§ 7002(b); § 7002(c))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **2 weeks’ written notice** required for availability of certain materiel with additional useful life (excluding vehicles and computer technology). (§ 7002(b)(1)a)
- **2-week period**: if no requests are received within the 2-week period under subsection (b), Government Support Services may transfer materiel to storage and proceed with broader availability/sale/auction options. (§ 7002(c)(1))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- Agencies must **report excess materiel by memorandum** to Government Support Services. (§ 7002(a)(2))
- Requests for materiel (and vehicles by eligible nonprofits) **must be submitted in writing** to Government Support Services. (§ 7002(b)(1)b; § 7002(b)(2)b)
- For computer technology deemed surplus/obsolete/no longer suitable, Government Support Services must **offer first** to DOE Partners in Technology before other disposal, and if refused, dispose under subsection (c). (§ 7002(b)(3))
