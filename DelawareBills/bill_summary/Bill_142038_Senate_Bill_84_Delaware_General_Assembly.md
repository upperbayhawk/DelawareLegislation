## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Strengthen Delaware’s “Right to Farm” nuisance protections by expanding when agricultural operations qualify for protection and clarifying how those protections interact with compliance, negligence, and government environmental enforcement. (See Synopsis; amendments to 10 Del. C. § 8141 and 3 Del. C. § 1401.)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands the statutory definition of “agricultural operation” for nuisance-suit limitation purposes to include a detailed list of activities (e.g., cultivation, livestock, equine activity for profit, certain farm stands/carts, and transportation to/from markets and certain agricultural infrastructure).  
- Provides that 10 Del. C. § 8141 nuisance protections do **not** apply where the operation fails to conform to **federal, state, or local** health or zoning requirements, is conducted negligently/improperly, or where an agency is enforcing environmental standards (air, water quality, etc.).  
- Establishes a nuisance-suit limitation after an operation has been in operation **more than 1 year**, barring nuisance claims based on “changed conditions” in the locality, subject to listed exceptions.  
- Removes (in § 8141(e)) prior language that expressly voided local ordinances treating protected farm activities as nuisances **but** preserved exceptions for failure to follow “good agricultural practice” and “significant change” in the operation; the subsection is revised so that language is struck and the subsection ends with “void.”  
- Adds/clarifies in 3 Del. C. § 1401 that if an operation is compliant with applicable laws, regulations, and permits, it is **presumed** to be conducted consistent with “good agricultural practice.”  
- Prohibits state or local law enforcement agencies from bringing **criminal or civil** actions against an agricultural operation for an activity that is compliant with all applicable state and federal laws, regulations, and permits (3 Del. C. § 1401).  
- Creates a cross-reference: an operation meeting 10 Del. C. § 8141(a) “may not be deemed a nuisance” unless excluded under § 8141(b).  
- Allows attorney’s fees and costs to be awarded to a prevailing agricultural defendant if the court finds the nuisance claim **frivolous** (10 Del. C. § 8141(f)).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduced exposure to nuisance litigation for farms:** The bill strengthens statutory defenses/limitations against nuisance suits for qualifying agricultural operations (Synopsis; 10 Del. C. § 8141; 3 Del. C. § 1401).  
- **Clearer protected activities list:** Enumerating activities (including transportation to/from markets and certain infrastructure) may reduce uncertainty about what counts as an “agricultural operation” for nuisance protection (10 Del. C. § 8141(a)(1)–(12)).  
- **Deters frivolous nuisance claims:** Fee-shifting (court costs + reasonable attorney fees) applies when the farm defendant prevails and the claim is deemed frivolous (10 Del. C. § 8141(f)).  
- **Presumption of good practice when compliant:** Compliance with applicable laws/permits triggers a presumption the operation is consistent with good agricultural practice (3 Del. C. § 1401).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced recourse for neighbors in some disputes:** Expanded nuisance protections and a compliance-based presumption may make it harder for nearby residents to succeed in nuisance actions where the operation is legally compliant (3 Del. C. § 1401; 10 Del. C. § 8141(c)).  
- **Potential chilling effect from fee-shifting:** Plaintiffs may be discouraged from filing nuisance claims due to the risk of paying the farm’s court costs and attorney fees if the claim is found frivolous (10 Del. C. § 8141(f)).  
- **Changed treatment of local ordinances:** The bill revises § 8141(e) by striking language that previously addressed local ordinances and included carve-outs tied to “good agricultural practice” and “significant change”; this may change how local nuisance-abatement ordinances interact with the statute (10 Del. C. § 8141(e) as amended).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Expands/clarifies what qualifies as an “agricultural operation”:** Adds a structured list in 10 Del. C. § 8141(a)(1)–(12), including:
  - Roadside farmer’s stands or carts where **not less than 50%** of products sold are directly from the operation (§ 8141(a)(10)).  
  - Transportation of agricultural products to/from a market storage area or to/from listed agricultural-related infrastructure (§ 8141(a)(12)).  
- **Defines exclusions from nuisance protections:** Protections do not apply if:
  - The operation does not conform to **federal, state, or local** health or zoning requirements (§ 8141(b)(1));  
  - A government agency is enforcing air, water quality, or other environmental standards (§ 8141(b)(2));  
  - The operation is conducted negligently or improperly (§ 8141(b)(3)).  
- **Nuisance limitation tied to “changed conditions” after >1 year:** An operation in place **more than 1 year** generally cannot become a nuisance due to changed local conditions, unless an exclusion applies (§ 8141(c)).  
- **Fee shifting for frivolous claims:** Prevailing agricultural defendant may recover court costs and reasonable attorney fees if the court finds the nuisance claim frivolous (§ 8141(f)).  
- **Presumption of good agricultural practice based on compliance:** If compliant with applicable laws/permits, the operation is presumed consistent with good agricultural practice (3 Del. C. § 1401).  
- **Limits state/local law-enforcement actions for compliant activities:** Bars criminal or civil actions by state/local law-enforcement agencies against compliant agricultural operations for compliant activity (3 Del. C. § 1401).  
- **Cross-reference between Title 3 and Title 10 nuisance standards:** An operation meeting § 8141(a) may not be deemed a nuisance unless excluded under § 8141(b) (3 Del. C. § 1401).

## Who Benefits
- **Agricultural operators and related entities** (including those engaged in the enumerated activities like livestock, poultry, equine for profit, farm stands/carts meeting the **50%** threshold, and transportation tied to agricultural markets/infrastructure) through expanded nuisance protection and potential attorney-fee recovery for frivolous claims.  
- **Employees/principals of agricultural operations** via an “absolute defense” framework in 3 Del. C. § 1401 for nuisance actions when conditions are met.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Neighbors and community members** who seek to bring nuisance claims against agricultural operations may face higher legal barriers and potential fee exposure if claims are deemed frivolous (10 Del. C. § 8141(f); 3 Del. C. § 1401).  
- **Local governments** to the extent local nuisance ordinances conflict with the amended state nuisance protections (implicated by 10 Del. C. § 8141(e) changes).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance conditions for protections:** To qualify for protection, agricultural operations must conform to applicable **federal, state, and local** health/zoning requirements and avoid negligent or improper conduct (10 Del. C. § 8141(b)).  
- **Environmental enforcement preserved:** The protections cannot be used against federal, state, or local agencies enforcing air, water quality, or other environmental standards (10 Del. C. § 8141(b)(2); Synopsis).  
- **Fee shifting (civil):** If a defendant agricultural operation prevails and the court finds the nuisance claim frivolous, the plaintiff must pay court costs and reasonable attorney fees (10 Del. C. § 8141(f)).  
- **Restriction on state/local law-enforcement actions:** State or local law-enforcement agencies may not bring criminal or civil actions for activities that are compliant with all applicable state and federal laws, regulations, and permits (3 Del. C. § 1401).
