## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Titles 22, 29, and 30 of the Delaware Code to revise how Downtown Development Districts (DDDs) and newly defined Downtown Development Corridors are defined, applied for, designated, renewed, reviewed/terminated, and incentivized— including changes to local incentive obligations, DSHA-administered grant eligibility/administration, state planning roles, and historic preservation tax credit allocation pools.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines a “Downtown Development Corridor” as an area within a municipality with population **30,000+ (2020 federal census)** designated under the Act; “Downtown” is determined locally under Office of State Planning Coordination (OSPC) guidelines.
- Requires designation criteria to include “need and impact” factors (including **income, unemployment rate, homeownership rate, vacant/abandoned housing**) that must count for **at least 50%** of application consideration.
- Establishes application review by the **Cabinet Committee on State Planning Issues** (recommendation to Governor); the Governor may designate immediately, designate effective **1 year** later, or deny.
- Sets designation limits and terms: **no more than 15 districts** at any one time; **no more than 1 corridor per municipality**; initial designation term **10 years**, with up to **two 5-year** renewals.
- Makes local incentives proposed in an approved application **binding upon designation** (and must be constitutionally consistent); boundary/incentive amendments require Committee approval and revised incentives must be **“equal or superior.”**
- Creates a structured formal review/termination process with notice/opportunity to be heard and requiring **3/5 affirmative Committee vote**; after termination, **no new incentives** may be authorized after the termination date (though previously authorized incentives may continue for a reasonable period if allowed by regulation).
- Provides DSHA-administered investor grants: a qualified investor may receive a grant of up to **20%** of **qualified real property investment** **in excess of** a DSHA-set minimum qualified investment threshold; grant availability is **subject to annual appropriation**.
- Updates historic preservation tax credit allocation pools (annual cap **$5,000,000**), including reservations for projects in DDDs **and Corridors**, with unused pool balances available to any eligible project on **April 1** each year.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Targets designations toward higher-need areas by requiring “need and impact” factors to represent **≥ 50%** of application scoring/consideration.
- Improves program predictability by setting a standard designation term (**10 years**) with defined renewal options (**two 5-year renewals**).
- Creates/clarifies a statewide investment incentive: grants up to **20%** of qualified investment **above** a minimum threshold (threshold set by DSHA).
- Adds procedural protections for participating areas before adverse action (notice and opportunity to be heard; **3/5** Committee vote required for formal review/termination decisions).
- Adds stability constraints on DSHA rule changes: DSHA may not change the minimum investment threshold or other grant limitations/conditions **more often than once per year**.
- Uses reserved historic tax credit pools to support smaller-credit projects (credits **≤ $300,000**) and projects located in districts/corridors.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Participation caps may exclude otherwise eligible communities/projects: **≤ 15 districts statewide at one time** and **≤ 1 corridor per municipality**.
- Corridor eligibility is limited to municipalities with **30,000+ population (2020 census)**, excluding smaller municipalities from corridor designation.
- Local governments are locked into their proposed incentives once designated (local incentives become **binding upon designation**), reducing flexibility to revise commitments later.
- Investments do **not** qualify for grants unless performed in accordance with the applicable corridor/district plan, excluding nonconforming projects.
- DSHA discretion over investment inclusions/exclusions, verification procedures, and grant caps/criteria may create uncertainty until DSHA rules are applied; grant availability depends on annual appropriations.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/clarified corridor definition:** “Downtown Development Corridor” tied to municipality population **≥ 30,000 (2020 federal census)**.
- **Designation criteria weighting:** “Need and impact” factors must account for **at least 50%** of application consideration (including income, unemployment, homeownership, vacant/abandoned housing).
- **Decision process:** Cabinet Committee evaluates and recommends; Governor may designate immediately, designate effective **1 year** later, or deny.
- **Program caps:** **≤ 15 districts** may exist at any one time; **≤ 1 corridor per municipality**.
- **Designation term/renewal:** initial **10-year** designation; renewals up to **two 5-year** periods (based on Committee recommendation).
- **Binding local incentives:** local incentives in the approved application become **binding upon designation**; may include regulatory flexibility proposals (e.g., permit process reforms, zoning changes, exemptions from local ordinances), subject to constitutional constraints.
- **Amendments:** boundary/incentive changes require Committee approval; revised incentives must be **equal or superior** to prior commitments.
- **Formal review/termination framework:**
  - Generally no formal review recommendation for **2 years** after initial designation.
  - Generally no termination recommendation for **1 year** after placement on formal review (exception referenced for failure to provide required local incentives).
  - Notice and opportunity to be heard required.
  - Committee approval of Office recommendation requires **3/5 affirmative vote**.
  - After termination, **no new incentive** may be authorized after the termination date (continuation of previously authorized incentives may be allowed for a reasonable period by regulation).
- **Grant program (DSHA-administered):**
  - Grant up to **20%** of qualified real property investment **above** the minimum qualified investment threshold.
  - Minimum qualified investment threshold is set by DSHA; DSHA may change it **not more often than once per year**; historic example: **$25,000** for FY ending **June 30, 2015** for a single residential or mixed-use building/facility.
  - Qualified investor includes owners or tenants; tenant investments are proportional to leased space; common-interest community unit owners count only their titled proportion (not common elements).
  - Investment must be performed **in accordance with the corridor/district plan** to qualify.
  - Grant availability is **subject to annual appropriation**; DSHA may impose caps/limits and additional criteria and may coordinate caps with other incentives (including historic preservation tax credits), but DSHA may not change such conditions **more often than once per year**.
- **Historic preservation tax credit pool updates (Title 30, §1816):** within the **$5,000,000** annual cap:
  - **$100,000** reserved each fiscal year for qualified resident curators (unused carries to the next fiscal year for specified awards).
  - **$1,500,000** reserved each year for projects receiving a credit of **not more than $300,000**.
  - **$1,500,000** reserved each year for projects located in DDDs **or Downtown Development Corridors**, of which **$500,000** is reserved for corridor/district projects receiving a credit of **not more than $300,000**.
  - On **April 1** each year, unused pool balances become available to any eligible project.

## Who Benefits
- Property owners and tenants investing in designated corridors/districts who meet DSHA rules (including plan compliance) and qualify for grants up to **20%** of qualified investment above the threshold.
- Municipalities/unincorporated areas that secure designation and leverage binding local incentives plus a state-administered incentive framework.
- Applicants for historic preservation tax credits whose projects fit reserved pools, especially corridor/district projects and projects with credits **≤ $300,000**.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Smaller municipalities (population **< 30,000**, 2020 census) seeking corridor designation (ineligible for corridor designation under the definition).
- Municipalities/counties that become designated but later face constraints from **binding** incentive commitments and reporting/program obligations.
- Applicants whose work is not consistent with the corridor/district plan (ineligible as “qualified real property investment” for grants).
- Applicants potentially constrained by DSHA-imposed caps/limits or additional criteria, including limits on combining grants with other incentives.
