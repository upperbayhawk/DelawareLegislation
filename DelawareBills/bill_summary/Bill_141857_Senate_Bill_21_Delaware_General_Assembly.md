## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s General Corporation Law (Title 8) to (1) expand and clarify statutory “safe harbor” protections for conflicted transactions involving directors, officers, controlling stockholders, and control groups (DGCL §144), and (2) define and limit what corporate “books and records” stockholders may inspect and under what conditions (DGCL §220).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates DGCL §144 so certain interested-director/officer transactions are not void/voidable and generally are insulated from equitable relief or damages **if** approved via specified “safe harbor” pathways (disinterested director approval, disinterested stockholder approval, or fairness).  
- Creates explicit statutory safe harbors for **“controlling stockholder transactions,”** including separate treatment for **“going private transactions.”**
- Defines key terms in §144, including **“controlling stockholder”** (majority voting power, or at least **one-third voting power plus managerial authority**), **“control group,”** **“disinterested director/stockholder,”** **“material interest/relationship,”** and **“fair as to the corporation.”**
- Adds a presumption that directors of exchange-listed companies deemed independent under exchange rules are **presumed disinterested**, rebuttable only by **“substantial and particularized facts.”**
- Limits monetary damages exposure for controlling stockholders/control groups (in that capacity) to loyalty, bad faith/intentional misconduct/knowing law violations, or improper personal benefit (i.e., **no monetary damages for duty of care** in that capacity).
- Amends DGCL §220 to define “books and records” (including specified categories like board minutes/materials, annual financial statements for **3 years**, and independence questionnaires) and imposes conditions for inspection (good faith/proper purpose; reasonable particularity; specific relevance).
- Allows corporations to impose reasonable confidentiality/use restrictions, require an agreement that produced information is **incorporated by reference** into related stockholder complaints, and to **redact** irrelevant portions.
- Limits Court of Chancery in §220 actions: generally may not order production beyond the defined categories, with a narrow “functional equivalent” exception when the corporation lacks certain specified records.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Greater clarity and predictability for conflicted transactions:** §144 provides explicit statutory pathways (disinterested director/stockholder approvals and fairness standards) that, if satisfied, protect transactions from being void/voidable and from certain remedies (text: §144(a)–(c)).  
- **Structured process for controlling-stockholder deals:** Creates defined review/approval mechanisms for “controlling stockholder transactions,” including heightened requirements for “going private transactions” (text: §144(b)–(c)).  
- **Defined standards and presumptions for independence:** Exchange-listed director independence determinations receive a strengthened presumption, rebuttable only by “substantial and particularized facts” (text: §144(d)(2)).  
- **More defined stockholder inspection scope:** §220 enumerates “books and records” categories, potentially reducing disputes over what must be produced (text: §220(a)(1)).  
- **Confidentiality and litigation-shaping tools:** Corporations may impose confidentiality/use restrictions, require incorporation-by-reference into complaints, and redact non-essential portions (text: §220(b)(3)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced litigation remedies in covered conflict situations:** Covered transactions “may not be the subject of equitable relief” and may not give rise to damages/sanctions if safe harbor conditions are met (text: §144(a)–(c)), potentially limiting stockholder challenge leverage.  
- **Limits court-ordered production in §220 actions:** Court of Chancery generally may not order production beyond the defined list of records (text: §220(e)), which may narrow access compared to broader requests.  
- **Incorporation-by-reference requirement may deter inspections:** Corporations may condition production on an agreement that produced information is deemed incorporated by reference into a related complaint (text: §220(b)(3)).  
- **Broader ability to redact:** Corporations may redact portions not “specifically related” to the purpose (text: §220(b)(3)), potentially increasing disputes about relevance.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- How “reasonable restrictions” on confidentiality, use, or distribution will be evaluated in practice is **not further specified** (text provides authority but not standards) (§220(b)(3)).  
- The thresholds for what constitutes “managerial authority” and “functionally equivalent” control under the **one-third voting power** test are defined at a high level but may require judicial application to facts (§144(e)(2)b.).  
- The precise contours of “fair as to the corporation” are defined, but application remains fact-dependent (definition includes “beneficial,” “fair dealing,” and arm’s-length comparability) (§144(e)(6)).

## Key Policy Changes
1. **DGCL §144 safe harbor expansion and remedies limitation**
   - Interested director/officer acts or transactions are protected from being void/voidable and from equitable relief/damages/sanctions **if**:
     - Approved in good faith by **majority of disinterested directors** after disclosure/knowledge of material facts (even if less than quorum), or
     - Approved/ratified in good faith by **uncoerced majority of votes cast by disinterested stockholders** after disclosure/knowledge, or
     - The act/transaction is **fair as to the corporation** (§144(a)(1)–(3)).
2. **New statutory framework for controlling stockholder transactions**
   - For controlling stockholder transactions (not going private): safe harbor if approved/recommended by a properly constituted committee (majority disinterested; excluding controlling stockholder) **or** approved/ratified by **uncoerced majority of votes cast by disinterested stockholders**, **or** fair (§144(b)(1)–(3)).
   - For going private transactions: requires **both** committee approval/recommendation **and** disinterested stockholder approval/ratification, **or** fairness (§144(c)(1)–(2)).
3. **Definitions added to §144**
   - “Controlling stockholder”: (a) **majority voting power**, or (b) at least **one-third voting power** plus power to elect directors holding a **majority of board voting power** and power to exercise managerial authority (§144(e)(2)).
   - “Control group,” “controlling stockholder transaction,” “going private transaction,” “material interest,” “material relationship,” “disinterested director/stockholder,” “fair as to the corporation” (§144(e)(1)–(9)).
4. **Director independence presumption for exchange-listed corporations**
   - If board determines a director is independent under national exchange rules, director is presumed disinterested; rebuttal requires **“substantial and particularized facts”** (§144(d)(2)).
5. **Limits on controlling stockholder/control group monetary damages (in that capacity)**
   - No monetary damages for breach of fiduciary duty **other than**: loyalty breach, bad faith/intentional misconduct/knowing violation of law, or improper personal benefit (§144(d)(5)).
6. **DGCL §220 inspection rights restructured**
   - Defines “books and records” categories, including (non-exhaustive list per statute):  
     - Stockholder minutes/consents for **3 years**; stockholder communications for **3 years**; board/committee minutes and action records; board/committee materials; annual financial statements for **3 years**; §122(18) agreements; director/officer independence questionnaires (§220(a)(1)c.–i.).
   - Adds inspection conditions: good faith + proper purpose; reasonable particularity; and records must be specifically related (§220(b)(2)).
   - Permits confidentiality/use/distribution restrictions; incorporation-by-reference condition; and redactions of portions not specifically related (§220(b)(3)).
   - Court limitation: in stockholder §220 actions, court generally may not order records beyond §220(a)(1) list (§220(e)), but may order “functional equivalent” records if the corporation lacks certain specified items (including certain minutes/actions/financial statements, and for exchange-listed companies, independence questionnaires), only as necessary and essential to the proper purpose (§220(f)).

## Who Benefits
- **Corporations, directors, officers, controlling stockholders, and control groups** seeking clearer statutory protections and defined processes for conflicted transactions (§144).  
- **Corporations** seeking to manage and constrain §220 inspections through defined record categories, confidentiality conditions, incorporation-by-reference requirements, and redactions (§220).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Stockholders (especially in governance disputes)** who seek broader inspection or litigation remedies may face narrower court-ordered production in §220 actions and expanded safe harbor protections limiting remedies for conflicted transactions (§144; §220(e), §220(b)(3)).  
- **Dissident or investigative stockholders** may be constrained by the requirement to describe requests with reasonable particularity and show specific relationship to purpose (§220(b)(2)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Safe harbor compliance pathways (transaction-approval requirements):**
  - Disclosure/knowledge of material facts; good-faith approval by disinterested directors or disinterested stockholders; additional committee + stockholder vote requirements for going-private controlling stockholder transactions (§144(a)–(c)).  
- **Inspection demand requirements (§220):**
  - Written demand **under oath** stating the purpose; must be in good faith and for a proper purpose; must describe purpose and records sought with reasonable particularity; records must be specifically related (§220(b)(1)–(2)).  
  - If requester is a beneficial owner (not record holder), demand must include documentary evidence of beneficial ownership and a true/correct-copy statement (§220(b)(5)).  
  - If made by attorney/agent, must include authorization (power of attorney or other writing) (§220(b)(6)).  
  - Demand must be directed to the corporation’s registered office in Delaware or principal place of business (§220(b)(7)).  
- **Court of Chancery authority and limits:**
  - Exclusive jurisdiction to determine director inspection entitlement; court may set conditions (§220(d)).  
  - In stockholder inspection proceedings, production is generally limited to §220(a)(1) categories, with a narrow functional-equivalent exception (§220(e)–(f)).  
- **Legislative passage threshold:**
  - Requires **two-thirds** affirmative vote of members elected to each house to amend the general corporation law (Del. Const. Art. IX, §1) (stated in Synopsis).
