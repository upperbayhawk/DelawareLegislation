## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 16, Chapter 50, § 5003 (voluntary admission procedure) to allow the Director or Deputy Director of the Division of Family Services (DFS) to use a **designee** to **consent to** and **sign** voluntary psychiatric admissions for youth **under age 18** who are in **DSCYF/DFS custody** under **Chapter 25 of Title 13**, and to clarify related discharge request provisions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Allows a **DFS Director/Deputy Director designee** to provide **consent to treatment** for a minor’s **voluntary admission** to a designated psychiatric treatment facility or hospital when the minor is in **DSCYF custody** (Title 13, Ch. 25).
- Allows the same **designee** to **sign the request for admission** for such youth (previously limited to Director/Deputy Director).
- Confirms that a voluntary patient (or specified adult/DFS officials for minors in custody) may make a **written request for discharge at any time** to the attending psychiatrist.
- Provides that discharge requests are governed by existing **§ 5003(e)** processes, but a minor’s discharge **may be conditioned on consent** of the parent/guardian or DFS Director/Deputy Director/**designee** (when in custody).
- If discharge is requested **against the advice of the treatment team and facility administrator**, the facility **may initiate involuntary treatment procedures** under the chapter, treating the request as if made by the patient.
- Applies only to applicants **under 18** and specifically addresses those in **DSCYF/DFS custody**.
- Enacted as **Chapter 95, Volume 85**, and **approved July 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Operational flexibility / timeliness:** Explicitly authorizes a DFS **designee** to consent and sign for admission, which may reduce delays when the Director/Deputy Director is unavailable (bill synopsis states this “reflects best practices” and matches current surgical consent processes for DFS custody youth).
- **Continuity with existing policy framework:** Codifies designee authority consistent with the described DFS approach (synopsis: designee acts with “direct approval” of the Director or Deputy Director).
- **Clarifies decision-maker list:** Makes statutory text more explicit that a designee may act for DFS leadership in both admission consent/signature and discharge-related provisions for youth in custody.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Broader delegation of authority:** Expands who may consent/sign for psychiatric admission for youth in DFS custody from only the Director/Deputy Director to include a **designee**, potentially raising concerns about how delegated authority is exercised (the bill text does not specify training/qualification standards for designees).
- **Discharge constraints remain possible:** For minors, discharge “may be conditioned” on consent of the parent/guardian or DFS leadership/designee, which could limit a youth’s ability to leave a facility even after a discharge request is made (as stated in § 5003(f)(2)a as amended).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Who qualifies as a “designee”:** The statute does not define selection criteria, credentialing, scope limits, or documentation requirements for a designee.
- **Process for “direct approval”:** The synopsis references that designee action occurs only with “direct approval” of the Director/Deputy Director, but this approval requirement is **not detailed in the statutory language** included.
- **Interaction with existing § 5003(e):** The bill references subsection (e) for discharge procedures but does not restate it; impacts depend on the content of § 5003(e) (not included in the source text provided).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 16, § 5003(f)(1):** For applicants **under 18** in **DSCYF custody** (Title 13, Ch. 25), consent to treatment for voluntary admission may be given by the **DFS Director or Deputy Director** **or the Director’s/Deputy Director’s designee**.
- **Title 16, § 5003(f)(1):** The **request for admission** must be signed by the parent/guardian or, for youth in custody, the **DFS Director/Deputy Director or designee**.
- **Title 16, § 5003(f)(2) and (f)(2)a:** Adds/clarifies that a DFS **designee** is included among those who may request discharge and among those whose **consent may condition discharge** for a minor in custody.
- **Title 16, § 5003(f)(2)b:** If discharge is requested against clinical/administrative advice, the facility may initiate **involuntary treatment procedures**, and this applies as if the patient requested discharge.

## Who Benefits
- **Youth under 18 in DSCYF/DFS custody** needing timely voluntary psychiatric admission.
- **DSCYF/DFS leadership and staff**, by allowing delegated authority for admissions paperwork and consent.
- **Psychiatric facilities/hospitals**, by clarifying who can legally consent and sign for admission for youth in DFS custody.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Youth in DSCYF/DFS custody** if broader delegation results in decisions being made by a designee rather than the Director/Deputy Director personally (the bill expands authorized actors).
- **Parents/guardians (in non-custody contexts)** are not directly changed, but the bill maintains structures where discharge for minors may be conditioned on adult/official consent.
