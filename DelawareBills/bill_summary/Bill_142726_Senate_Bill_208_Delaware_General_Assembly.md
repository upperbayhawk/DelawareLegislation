## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 24, Chapter 35 (psychology) to add and regulate 2 additional psychology professional categories—**licensed psychology associate** and **master’s-level psychological assistant**—and to rename the existing “psychological assistant” category as **doctoral-level psychological assistant**, along with related supervision, licensure/registration, discipline, and fee authority updates. (Sections 1, 8–10, 25; Synopsis)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new **license**: **licensed psychology associate** (master’s degree level) with authority to **independently practice** within defined limits. (New § 3508A; § 3502(5), (6))
- Creates a new **registration**: **master’s-level psychological assistant**, who may perform only Board-approved functions **under direct supervision** of a psychologist or supervising licensed psychology associate. (New § 3509A; § 3502(7), (13)–(15))
- Renames Delaware’s existing “psychological assistant” as **doctoral-level psychological assistant** and treats existing registrants as doctoral-level assistants upon implementation. (Amended § 3502(3); § 3509(e); Synopsis)
- Requires applicants for licensure/registration (psychologist, licensed psychology associate, doctoral-level assistant, master’s-level assistant) to submit **fingerprints** and obtain **state and federal criminal history reports**, at the applicant’s expense; limits licensure/registration when convictions are “substantially related,” unless a waiver applies under 29 Del. C. § 8735(x)(3). (Amended § 3508(b); new § 3508A(b); amended § 3509(c); new § 3509A(c))
- Updates Board authority to set education/experience standards, continuing education standards, and supervision limits (including number of assistants a supervisor may oversee). (Amended § 3506(a)(5), (16); § 3509(d); § 3509A(d))
- Maintains/clarifies discipline tools including **civil discipline** (up to **$500 per violation**) and **criminal penalties** for unlicensed practice (misdemeanor with fines **$500–$1,000** first offense; **$1,000–$2,000** subsequent; possible **up to 1 year imprisonment**). (Amended §§ 3516(a)(6), 3520(b))
- Establishes an **implementation trigger**: effective upon enactment, but implemented on the **earlier** of **12 months after enactment** or Board notice in the **Register of Regulations** that final implementing regulations are adopted. (Section 25)
- Fee impact: authorizes licensing/registration fees for new categories; amounts **not specified** and must be approved and cost-based. (Fee Impact statement; amended § 3512)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expands regulated workforce categories** by adding a master’s-level independent practitioner (licensed psychology associate) and a supervised master’s-level assistant, which could increase availability of regulated psychological services within defined scopes. (New § 3508A; new § 3509A; Synopsis)
- **Clarifies role distinctions** by renaming the existing psychological assistant to “doctoral-level psychological assistant,” differentiating education levels in statute. (§ 3502(3); § 3509(e); Synopsis)
- **Standardizes public-protection requirements** across new license/registration types by applying criminal background check requirements and subjecting new categories to disciplinary standards. (New § 3508A(b); § 3509A(c); amended § 3514; Synopsis)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **New/expanded fee exposure**: the Division of Professional Regulation may impose **new licensing fees** for the added license/registration types; exact amounts are unknown and subject to Secretary of State approval. (Fee Impact statement; § 3512)
- **Compliance burden on applicants**: fingerprints and state/FBI background checks are required **at the applicant’s expense** for licensure/registration. (Amended § 3508(b); new § 3508A(b); amended § 3509(c); new § 3509A(c))
- **Practice limitations for licensed psychology associates** may restrict certain high-stakes testing/evaluations (e.g., forensic, neuropsychological, child custody) to doctoral-level practitioners, limiting services they can provide. (New § 3508A(e)(2))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Fee amounts and schedule** for the new categories are not stated; fees must “reasonably reflect” costs and require Secretary of State approval. (Fee Impact statement; § 3512)
- The **specific functions** master’s-level psychological assistants may perform are not enumerated in statute; they depend on the supervisor’s statement and Board regulations. (New § 3509A(a)(1); § 3502(7))
- The **implementation date** depends on (a) enactment + 12 months or (b) publication notice of final regulations—no calendar date is provided in the bill text. (Section 25)
- The statute references a bracketed phrase **“[the implementation date of this Act]”** for conversion of titles for existing assistants, but does not define a single fixed date beyond Section 25’s trigger. (§ 3509(e); Section 25)

## Health Impact
- Establishes a new master’s-level independent practitioner (**licensed psychology associate**) with defined scope and prohibitions on certain specialized evaluations. (New § 3508A(e))
- Adds a new supervised master’s-level role (**master’s-level psychological assistant**) requiring direct supervision and function-limited practice. (New § 3509A; § 3502(7))
- Reinforces patient-record access and continuity rules for licensees, including:
  - **At least 30 days’ notice** before discontinuing a practice (if not transferring records) or terminating a patient relationship. (§ 3521(a)(1))
  - Record request fulfillment within **45 days** (unless faster medically necessary). (§ 3521(f))
  - Record retention/disposal permitted **7 years** after specified events. (§ 3521(g))
- Requires reporting to the Division within **30 days** of becoming aware of reportable conduct/inability to practice. (§ 3518A(d))

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds license category: Licensed psychology associate** (master’s degree; minimum **60 graduate semester hours**), with internship, **2 years** supervised experience after degree, and passing **EPPP** score. (New § 3508A(a)(1)–(4))
- **Defines licensed psychology associate scope**:
  - May **independently** practice “at the master’s-level” within training, except barred from certain comprehensive specialty/subspecialty services requiring doctoral-level education. (New § 3508A(e)(1)–(2))
  - Explicit prohibitions include neuropsychological evaluations, forensic evaluations (including capacity/competency; local/national security risk), medical/health capacity evaluations (including pre-surgical), personality/projective evaluations as final determinations, child custody/parental fitness, and public-safety employment/fitness-for-duty evaluations. (New § 3508A(e)(2)(a)–(f))
  - May not hold out as a licensed psychologist (including “neuropsychologist” or “forensic psychologist”). (New § 3508A(f))
- **Adds registration category: Master’s-level psychological assistant**:
  - Supervisor eligibility: psychologist or licensed psychology associate with **3 years** practice. (New § 3509A(a))
  - Must work only in delineated functions, from supervisor’s office, under supervision plan with face-to-face consultation as required. (New § 3509A(a)(1)–(2))
- **Renames and grandfathers existing assistants**:
  - “Psychological assistant” becomes “doctoral-level psychological assistant.” (§ 3502(3); § 3509(e))
  - Individuals registered as “psychological assistant” before implementation are “considered” doctoral-level assistants and may use the new title, as approved by the Board. (§ 3509(e))
- **Criminal background checks moved/clarified** as qualification requirements for each license/registration type; all are **at the applicant/individual’s expense** and include state and FBI reports. (Amended § 3508(b); new § 3508A(b); amended § 3509(c); new § 3509A(c))
- **Unlicensed practice penalties** updated/clarified:
  - First offense: fine **$500–$1,000** per offense; may include imprisonment up to **1 year**.
  - Subsequent offenses: fine **$1,000–$2,000** per offense. (§ 3520(b))
- **Discipline**: monetary penalty not to exceed **$500 per violation**; permanent revocation required for conviction of a felony sexual offense. (§ 3516(a)(6)–(7))

## Who Benefits
- **Master’s-level psychology professionals** seeking an independent, regulated practice pathway as a **licensed psychology associate**. (New § 3508A; Synopsis)
- Individuals pursuing further licensure who can register as a **master’s-level psychological assistant** while completing additional requirements. (New § 3509A(a)(2)(b); Synopsis)
- The **Board/Division** through clarified authority and potential new fee-supported licensure/registration categories. (§§ 3506, 3512–3513; Fee Impact statement)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Applicants/registrants** who must pay for fingerprinting and state/FBI background checks and meet new/clarified qualification standards. (§§ 3508(b), 3508A(b), 3509(c), 3509A(c))
- **Licensed psychology associates** limited from providing specified specialized evaluations and may face enforcement risk if practicing beyond the statutory limits. (New § 3508A(e)(2), (f))
- Individuals practicing without required licensure/registration face misdemeanor penalties and fines. (§ 3520)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective:** “on enactment.” (Section 25)
- **Implementation:** earlier of:
  1) **12 months from enactment**, or  
  2) Board notice published in the **Register of Regulations** that final implementing regulations have been adopted. (Section 25)
- **Reporting deadline:** required reports to the Division must be filed within **30 days** of the licensee becoming aware of reportable information. (§ 3518A(d))
- **Board meetings:** at least **once each quarter**. (§ 3504(a))
- **Appeal deadline:** appeal Board decision to Superior Court within **30 days** of service or postmarked mailing date. (§ 3517(c))
- **Temporary suspension notice:** at least **24 hours** written/oral notice before temporary suspension order; temporary suspension order generally up to **60 days** unless continuance requested. (§ 3516(c))
- **Patient notice:** at least **30 days** prior to discontinuing practice (without record transfer) or terminating patient relationship. (§ 3521(a)(1))
- **Patient record request fulfillment:** within **45 days** from closure/assembly of complete record (unless faster medically necessary). (§ 3521(f))
- **Record disposal:** allowed **7 years** after specified events; notice publications after death/custodian appointment are required **at least 1 time per month for 3 months**. (§§ 3521(g), 3521(b)(1)a; 3522(b)(1)a)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Licensure/registration required** to practice psychology or to act/hold out as a licensed psychology associate, doctoral-level psychological assistant, or master’s-level psychological assistant. (§ 3507; § 3520)
- **Criminal penalties (misdemeanor)** for unlicensed/unregistered practice or improper title use:
  - First offense fine **$500–$1,000** per offense; possible imprisonment up to **1 year**.
  - Second/subsequent offense fine **$1,000–$2,000** per offense. (§ 3520(b))
- **Disciplinary sanctions** for licensees/registrants (after hearing) include reprimand, censure, probation, suspension, revocation, and **monetary penalty up to $500 per violation**; permanent revocation required for felony sexual offense convictions. (§ 3516(a))
- **Mandatory reporting**: licensees must report certain conduct/inability to practice to the Division **in writing within 30 days**; good-faith reporters have immunity (absent gross/wanton negligence). (§ 3518A(d))
- **Supervision compliance**: supervising psychologists/supervising licensed psychology associates can be disciplined for failures in supervision and for acts committed by supervised assistants under their direction/control. (§ 3514(a)(9))
- **Cease and desist**: Board may apply to the Attorney General for a cease-and-desist order after formal warning when an unlicensed person is practicing/using titles. (§ 3515(b))
