## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To enact Delaware’s participation in the **Interstate Compact for School Psychologists** to facilitate interstate practice of school psychology in educational/school settings and **increase the availability of school psychological services** by creating a pathway for school psychologists to obtain **equivalent licenses** in other Compact member states. (§ 8242)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Enacts the **School Psychologist Interstate Licensure Compact** in Delaware (Title 14, Chapter 82, Subchapter IV). (§ 8241)
- Creates an interstate system to allow qualified school psychologists to obtain an **“equivalent license”** in other **member states** without “burdensome and duplicative requirements.” (§ 8242(b)(1); § 8244(b))
- Requires home-state licensure standards for Compact participation, including **passing a qualifying national exam** and completing a **minimum 1,200-hour supervised internship**, with **at least 600 hours in a school**. (§ 8244(a)(6))
- Requires applicants for an equivalent license to undergo a **criminal background check** in the state where the equivalent license is sought. (§ 8245(a)(5))
- Establishes an interstate governing body, the **School Psychologist Interstate Licensure Compact Commission**, with **one delegate per member state** and at least **one Commission meeting each calendar year**. (§ 8248(b)(1), (8))
- Allows the Commission to fund operations through **annual assessments on member states** and **fees on licensees** practicing under equivalent licenses (amounts set by Commission rule/formula). (§ 8248(g)(3))
- Provides enforcement tools for member-state noncompliance, including default notices and possible **termination by supermajority vote** of member-state delegates. (§ 8251(b)-(c))
- **Delaware’s Act takes effect only when the Compact is enacted into law in the 7th member state**; Delaware DOE must then publish notice in the **Register of Regulations** that 7 states have adopted the Compact. (Section 2; § 8252(a))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded access to services:** Compact is explicitly intended to “improve the availability” and “public accessibility” of school psychological services by increasing availability of qualified, licensed school psychologists. (§ 8242(a), (b)(3))
- **Workforce mobility to address shortages:** Promotes mobility “to address workforce shortages” across member states. (§ 8242(b)(2))
- **Reduced duplicative licensure barriers:** Enables qualifying psychologists to practice in other member states “without first satisfying burdensome and duplicative requirements.” (§ 8242(b)(1))
- **Support for military families:** Facilitates relocation of active military members and spouses by deeming them to hold a home state license in specified locations (permanent residence, primary state of practice, or PCS relocation state). (§ 8246)
- **Public protection safeguards included:** Requires compliance with the **remote state’s scope of practice** and preserves each state’s authority to investigate and discipline. (§ 8242(b)(5); § 8247(a))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential new state costs/assessments:** The Commission may levy an **annual assessment** on each member state to cover Commission operations (allocated by formula set by Commission rule). (§ 8248(g)(3))
- **Additional administrative workload for Delaware’s licensing authority:** Delaware must participate in **information sharing**, maintain a list of **equivalent licenses**, have complaint mechanisms, and provide notice to the Commission of adverse actions/investigations. (§ 8244(a)(2)-(5); § 8249)
- **Background check requirement may add cost/time for applicants:** Equivalent-license applicants must undergo a **criminal background check** in the state where they seek the equivalent license. (§ 8245(a)(5))
- **Commission governance and rulemaking may create compliance complexity:** Commission rules become binding as specified, with notice procedures and emergency rules possible on **48 hours’ notice** (with later full process within **90 days**). (§ 8250(e)-(f))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Fee amounts not stated:** Member states “may set and collect a fee” for granting an equivalent license, but no dollar amounts are provided. (§ 8244(c))
- **Assessment formula not stated:** The Commission’s **annual assessment** total and the allocation formula are to be set by Commission rule; no amounts are in the bill text. (§ 8248(g)(3))
- **Which Delaware license counts as the “equivalent license”:** Delaware must “identify and maintain” a list of equivalent licenses, but the specific credential(s) are not enumerated in the Compact text. (§ 8244(a)(3))
- **Which national exam qualifies:** A “qualifying national exam” is endorsed by NASP and/or approved by Commission rule; the specific test(s) are not named in statute. (§ 8243(19); § 8244(a)(6)a.)
- **Which agency is Delaware’s “state licensing authority”:** The Compact uses this term but does not specify which Delaware body serves this role. (§ 8243(29))

## Educational Impact
- Increases pathways for qualified school psychologists to practice in Delaware schools by allowing interstate equivalent licensing, intended to improve availability of **academic, mental, and behavioral health services** in school settings. (§ 8242(a); § 8243(23))

## Health Impact
- The Compact’s definition of “school psychological services” includes **mental and behavioral health services** (assessment, prevention, intervention, evaluation, consultation/collaboration). (§ 8243(23))
- Includes safeguards for public health/safety by limiting practice to qualified licensed professionals and preserving state discipline authority. (§ 8242(b)(4); § 8247)

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds Delaware law adopting the **Interstate Compact for School Psychologists**. (§ 8241)
- Requires member states to ensure home-state licensure standards include:
  - Passing a **qualifying national exam**. (§ 8244(a)(6)a)
  - Completing **1,200 hours** supervised internship, including **600 hours in a school**. (§ 8244(a)(6)b)
  - Graduation from a qualifying school psychologist education program. (§ 8244(a)(6)c)
- Requires equivalent-license applicants to complete a **criminal background check** in the state where they seek the equivalent license. (§ 8245(a)(5))
- Establishes the interstate **Commission** with broad administrative and fiscal powers, including assessing fees/assessments, adopting rules, and overseeing compliance. (§ 8248)
- Establishes enforcement and dispute resolution processes among member states and between a member state and the Commission, including potential litigation in specified federal courts. (§ 8251)

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware students and school communities** who rely on school psychological services (improved availability/access is an express purpose). (§ 8242(a), (b)(3))
- **School psychologists** seeking to practice across state lines via an equivalent license pathway. (§ 8242(b)(1); § 8244(b))
- **Military members and their spouses** who are licensed school psychologists and relocate due to PCS or other circumstances. (§ 8246)
- **Delaware school systems/employers** seeking a larger pool of qualified school psychologists. (Purpose provisions emphasizing workforce mobility and shortages: § 8242(b)(2))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Delaware state licensing authority/administration** due to required information sharing, complaint processes, reporting, and Commission participation. (§ 8244(a)(2)-(5); § 8249)
- **Delaware (state government)** if subject to Commission **annual assessments** to fund Compact operations. (§ 8248(g)(3))
- **Applicants for equivalent licenses** who must complete criminal background checks in the state of sought licensure, which may add steps/costs. (§ 8245(a)(5); § 8245(b))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Delaware Act effective date:** Takes effect **upon enactment of the Compact by the seventh state**. (Section 2; § 8252(a))
- **DOE notice requirement:** Upon enactment by the seventh state, the **Secretary of the Department of Education** must publish notice in the **Register of Regulations** that the Compact has been adopted by **7 states**. (Section 2)
- **Commission existence:** Commission comes into existence **on or after the Compact effective date**. (§ 8248(a); § 8252(a))
- **Delegate vacancy deadline:** A member state must fill a Commission delegate vacancy within **60 days**. (§ 8248(b)(5))
- **Commission meeting frequency:** At least **once each calendar year**. (§ 8248(b)(8))
- **Rulemaking notice:** Proposed rulemaking notice at least **30 days** before the meeting where it will be voted on. (§ 8250(e))
- **Emergency rules:** May be adopted with **48 hours’ notice**; standard procedures must be applied **no later than 90 days** after the emergency rule’s effective date. (§ 8250(f))
- **Member state withdrawal effective date:** Withdrawal takes effect **180 days** after enactment of the repealing statute. (§ 8252(b))
- **Post-withdrawal/termination license recognition:** A withdrawing or terminated state must continue to recognize Compact-granted licenses for **at least 6 months after the date of notice** to licensees. (§ 8252(d); § 8251(f))
- **Bill approval date (as enacted act):** Approved **September 2, 2025**. (Chapter 188 act text)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Member state compliance requirements:** Must share information, maintain equivalent license list, investigate complaints, and report adverse actions/investigative information to the Commission. (§ 8244(a)(2)-(5); § 8249)
- **Licensee compliance requirements:**
  - Maintain an active home state license. (§ 8245(a)(1))
  - Meet state-specific requirements after an equivalent license is granted. (§ 8245(a)(2))
  - Complete Commission administrative/application requirements and pay associated fees. (§ 8245(a)(3))
  - Complete home-state renewal requirements, including continuing professional education, if applicable. (§ 8245(a)(4))
  - Complete criminal background checks when applying for an equivalent license (and for renewals in remote states). (§ 8245(a)(5); § 8245(b))
- **Discipline:** Compact does not limit a member state’s authority to investigate or discipline under its laws; states may exchange investigatory/disciplinary files with confidentiality protections. (§ 8247)
- **State default/termination process:**
  - Written notice of default with proposed cure and technical assistance. (§ 8251(b)(1))
  - Possible termination for failure to cure by **supermajority vote** of member-state delegates. (§ 8251(c))
  - Terminated state remains responsible for assessments/obligations incurred through termination date. (§ 8251(e))
- **Enforcement litigation:** Commission may sue a defaulting state in U.S. District Court (D.C. or where Commission has principal offices); prevailing party awarded costs and reasonable attorney’s fees. (§ 8251(j)(1))
