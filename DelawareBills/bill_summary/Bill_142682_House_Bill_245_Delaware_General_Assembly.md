## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 to remove the statutory allowance for certain public school districts to increase school property tax revenue by up to **10%** following a **general reassessment**, and to require reassessment-related rate calculations to be **revenue-neutral** (no increase in actual revenue) unless voters approve an increase by referendum.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Eliminates the provision in **14 Del. C. § 1916(b)** that previously allowed school districts to realize **up to a 10% increase in actual revenue** after a countywide general reassessment.
- Requires the post-reassessment “new real estate tax rate” to **realize no increase in actual revenue** over the **fiscal year immediately preceding** the reassessment-driven valuation change (revenue-neutral calculation).
- Preserves voter control by stating that, after a rate is set under this subsection, it **may only be increased by an election of qualified voters** under **§ 1903** (or after a subsequent reassessment under § 1916).
- Makes conforming changes for cross-county districts in **§ 1916(c)(2)** by removing reference to the prior **10% increase** authorization.
- The Act **takes effect July 1, 2025** and applies to **all public school tax rates after July 1, 2025**; the fiscal note assumes effectiveness upon the Governor’s signature and **retroactive to July 1, 2025**.
- Fiscal note projects **local school revenue losses** totaling **$37,778,881** per year for **FY 2026–FY 2028**.
- Projected annual losses are concentrated in certain districts, including **Christina ($18,200,000)** and **Colonial ($5,373,448)**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Limits automatic tax-driven revenue growth after reassessment:** By removing the up-to-**10%** post-reassessment revenue increase authority, the bill reduces the ability to increase school property tax revenue without a voter-approved referendum (per changes to **§ 1916(b)** and reference to **§ 1903**).
- **Promotes revenue neutrality after reassessment:** The amended language requires the recalculated rate to realize **no increase in actual revenue** compared with the immediately preceding fiscal year’s real estate tax revenue.
- **Greater predictability for taxpayers:** Ties reassessment-related tax rate recalculation to a revenue-neutral standard rather than allowing built-in growth of up to **10%**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces local school revenue:** Fiscal note estimates annual local revenue loss of **$37,778,881** in **FY 2026, FY 2027, and FY 2028**.
- **May constrain district budgeting after reassessment:** By prohibiting any revenue growth from the reassessment-driven rate recalculation (absent voter approval), districts lose a mechanism previously available to address costs without referendum.
- **Large projected impacts in some districts:** For example, fiscal note lists annual projected losses of **$18,200,000 (Christina)** and **$5,000,000 (Appoquinimink)**.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date mechanics:** The bill text states it “takes effect **July 1, 2025**,” while the fiscal note assumes it becomes effective “upon signature of the Governor” and is **retroactive to July 1, 2025**. The controlling effective-date language may require clarification if enacted with different timing.
- **Interaction with “projected revenue loss” language:** Prior statutory language referenced a district’s ability to increase the rate up to **10%** to address reassessment-driven losses; the amended text’s final structure and remaining references may need interpretation for how districts respond to reassessment-caused decreases while still remaining revenue-neutral.
- **Scope of applicability:** The bill states it applies to “all public school tax rates after **July 1, 2025**,” but the text does not further define treatment for rates set before that date but billed/collected after.

## Key Policy Changes
- **14 Del. C. § 1916(b):** Removes the allowance for a reassessment-driven tax rate to “realize no more than **10%**” additional actual revenue; replaces with a requirement that the recalculated rate **“realizes no increase in actual revenue”** over the prior fiscal year’s real estate tax revenue.
- **Rate increases constrained to referenda:** Provides that after the rate is increased/achieved under this subsection, it **may only be increased** by an election of qualified voters under **§ 1903** (or after a subsequent general reassessment under § 1916).
- **14 Del. C. § 1916(c)(2):** Removes reference to the “**10% increase** as authorized by subsection (b)” in the cross-county reassessment tax levy provision.
- **Applicability clause:** Applies to **all public school tax rates after July 1, 2025**.

## Who Benefits
- **Property taxpayers in affected school districts:** Benefit from removal of the statutory mechanism that could allow up to a **10%** increase in actual school property tax revenue after reassessment without a new referendum.
- **Voters/qualified electors:** Increased practical control over tax rate increases through reliance on **§ 1903** referenda rather than post-reassessment statutory latitude.
