## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To codify Delaware’s sexual assault kit (SAK) testing policy and expand victims’ rights related to the location, testing status, results, and preservation of biological evidence, while updating DNA databank procedures and definitions in Titles 11 and 29.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires law-enforcement agencies (LEAs) to submit **all SAKs** to the Division of Forensic Science (DFS) for analysis, unless the case is determined to involve **no crime** (Title 29 § 4713(g)(1)b.; Title 11 § 9403B(a)).  
- Sets SAK handling deadlines: LEAs must submit a SAK to DFS **no more than 30 days** after contacting DFS to discuss evidence; DFS must complete DNA analysis **no more than 90 days after receipt** and maintain an annual average turnaround of **≤ 90 days** (Title 29 § 4713A(a), (b)).  
- If DFS cannot meet SAK testing deadlines, SAK testing must be performed by a **federal or accredited private crime laboratory at the State’s expense** (Title 29 § 4713A(e)).  
- Establishes victim rights to receive information (upon request) about **where evidence is located**, **testing status**, whether a **DNA profile was obtained**, whether it was entered into **SDIS/NDIS**, and whether there is a **match** (with limits if disclosure would impede an ongoing investigation) (Title 11 § 9403B(b), (d)).  
- Requires LEAs to respond to victim requests **as soon as possible, but no more than 7 calendar days** after the request (Title 11 § 9403B(c)).  
- Limits destruction of biological samples in **unsolved sexual assault cases**: cannot destroy/dispose until **20 years after collection**, or if victim was **under 18** at the time, not before the victim is **40 years old** (Title 29 § 4713(m)(1)).  
- Requires **written notice at least 20 days** before destruction/disposal of biological samples in unsolved sexual assault cases, and requires preservation if the victim requests it (Title 11 § 9403B(e)).  
- Fiscal note projects DFS may receive **~20 additional SAKs/year** at **$1,050 per SAK** (7 specimens × $150), for **$21,000 annually** in **FY 2026, FY 2027, and FY 2028**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Faster and more predictable SAK processing:** Statutory deadlines of **30 days** for LEA submission (after contacting DFS) and **90 days** for DFS completion may reduce delays in testing (Title 29 § 4713A(a), (b)).  
- **Backstop to prevent deadline slippage:** If DFS cannot meet deadlines, testing must shift to a **federal or accredited private lab** at **State expense** (Title 29 § 4713A(e)).  
- **Expanded victim access to information:** Victims can receive updates on evidence location, testing status/results, SDIS/NDIS entry, and matches, with LEA response required within **7 calendar days** (Title 11 § 9403B(b)-(d)).  
- **Stronger evidence preservation rules:** Biological samples from unsolved sexual assault cases must be retained for **20 years**, or until a minor victim reaches **age 40**, helping preserve evidence for future investigation/prosecution (Title 29 § 4713(m)(1)).  
- **Victim control over preservation:** Requires **20-day written notice** before disposal and mandates preservation upon victim request (Title 11 § 9403B(e)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added operational and cost pressures on DFS/State:** Fiscal note anticipates **$21,000 per year** (FY26–FY28) for additional SAK testing; costs could increase further if DFS must outsource testing “at the state’s expense” when deadlines cannot be met (Fiscal Note Assumption/Cost; Title 29 § 4713A(e)).  
- **Administrative workload for LEAs:** LEAs must coordinate with DFS, submit additional items/standards “as soon as possible,” update LEISS with dates/status/results, and respond to victim information requests within **7 calendar days** (Title 29 § 4713A(a)(2)-(3), (c)(2); Title 11 § 9403B(c)).  
- **Long-term evidence storage obligations:** Mandatory retention periods (**20 years** or until victim **age 40**) may increase storage and evidence management burdens for DFS and LEAs (Title 29 § 4713(m)(1)).  
- **Limited legal consequences for missed deadlines:** The statute states failure to meet time requirements is **not** grounds to challenge evidence, exclude evidence, dismiss cases, set aside convictions, or claim damages (Title 29 § 4713A(f)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The total number of currently unsubmitted SAKs stored in LEA evidence lockers statewide is **unknown** (Fiscal Note Assumption #3).  
- The bill requires LEAs to submit a SAK within **30 days after the LEA contacts DFS** to discuss evidence, but does not specify how soon after receiving a SAK an LEA must make that initial DFS contact (Title 29 § 4713A(a)(1); Title 11 § 9403B(a)(2) says “as soon as possible”).  
- The bill requires outsourcing if DFS cannot meet deadlines, but does not specify procurement process, capacity limits, or selection criteria beyond “federal or accredited, private crime laboratory” (Title 29 § 4713A(e)).  
- Victim right to be informed of a DNA match applies only if disclosure “would not impede or compromise an ongoing investigation,” but the bill does not define that standard (Title 11 § 9403B(d)(3)).

## Budget Impact

- **DFS:** Must meet **90-day** completion deadline and annual average **≤ 90 days** for SAKs; may need to outsource testing if unable to comply (Title 29 § 4713A(b), (e)).  
- **LEAs:** Must submit SAKs within **30 days** after contacting DFS; submit suspect/elimination standards and associated evidence “if available”/“as soon as possible;” update **LEISS** with testing dates/status/results; respond to victim requests within **7 calendar days** (Title 29 § 4713A(a), (c); Title 11 § 9403B(c)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **SAK submission requirement:** LEAs must submit **all sexual assault kits** to DFS for analysis, unless the “no crime occurred” exception applies (Title 11 § 9403B(a)(1); Title 29 § 4713(g)(1)b.).  
- **Submission deadline:** LEA must submit SAK to DFS **no more than 30 days** after contacting DFS to discuss what evidence will be submitted (Title 29 § 4713A(a)(1)).  
- **Testing deadlines:** DFS must complete SAK DNA analysis **within 90 days of receipt**; annual average turnaround must not exceed **90 days** (Title 29 § 4713A(b)(2)-(3)).  
- **Outsourcing requirement:** If DFS cannot meet deadlines, untested SAKs must be tested by a **federal or accredited private lab at State expense** (Title 29 § 4713A(e)).  
- **Victim information rights & response time:** Upon request, victims/designees may obtain evidence location/testing status; victims have rights to be told profile obtained, entered into SDIS/NDIS, and match info (with investigation safeguard); LEA must respond within **7 calendar days** (Title 11 § 9403B(b)-(d)).  
- **Preservation & destruction limits:** No destruction/disposal of biological samples from unsolved sexual assault cases until **20 years after collection**, or for minor victims until victim is **40**; requires **≥ 20 days** written notice before destruction and mandatory preservation upon victim request (Title 29 § 4713(m)(1); Title 11 § 9403B(e)).  
- **Notification methods:** Victim-required information under the Victims’ Bill of Rights may be provided **verbally, electronically, or as printed material**; victims must keep contact info updated and may select a preferred method (including no contact) (Title 11 § 9414).  
- **Confidentiality/FOIA:** Biological sample records held by DFS may be used only for law enforcement purposes and are exempt from FOIA (Title 29 § 4713(h)).  
- **Criminal penalties retained/clarified:** Tampering with a biological sample/container is a **Class D felony**; unauthorized use/dissemination of database information is a **Class A misdemeanor** (Title 29 § 4713(d), (l)).

## Who Benefits
- **Victims of sexual assault**, who gain explicit rights to timely information about evidence location, testing status/results, database entry, and (when not compromising investigations) match information, plus notice/preservation rights (Title 11 § 9403B).  
- **Law enforcement and prosecutors**, through standardized submission/testing processes and broader, timely DNA analysis of SAKs and associated evidence (Title 29 § 4713A).  
- **Statewide investigative systems (SDIS/CODIS/NDIS workflows):** clearer statutory alignment with NDIS policies and updated references to DELJIS/LEISS/DIAC (Title 29 § 4713; § 4713A).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **DFS and state budget administrators**, due to projected recurring costs (**$21,000/year FY26–FY28**) and potential additional costs for outsourced testing at State expense (Fiscal Note; Title 29 § 4713A(e)).  
- **Local law-enforcement agencies**, due to added mandated timelines, evidence submission coordination, LEISS updates, and victim-request response obligations (Title 29 § 4713A; Title 11 § 9403B(c)).  
- **Evidence storage custodians (DFS/LEAs)**, due to extended mandatory preservation periods (Title 29 § 4713(m)(1)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** Effective upon signature by the Governor (Fiscal Note Assumption #1). The enrolled act shows: **Approved August 20, 2025**.  
- **Deadlines/Timeframes created by the Act:**
  - LEA must submit SAK to DFS **≤ 30 days** after contacting DFS to discuss evidence (Title 29 § 4713A(a)(1)).  
  - DFS must complete SAK DNA analysis **≤ 90 days after receipt** (Title 29 § 4713A(b)(2)).  
  - DFS annual average SAK turnaround must be **≤ 90 days** (Title 29 § 4713A(b)(3)).  
  - LEA must respond to victim request for location/status information **as soon as possible, but ≤ 7 calendar days** (Title 11 § 9403B(c)).  
  - Written notice of intent to destroy/dispose unsolved sexual assault biological samples must be provided **≥ 20 days** before destruction/disposal (Title 11 § 9403B(e)(1)).  
  - Preservation minimums for unsolved sexual assault biological samples: **20 years after collection**, or if victim was **under 18**, not before victim is **40 years old** (Title 29 § 4713(m)(1)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (procedural):**
  - LEA SAK submission and evidence/standard submission timing requirements (Title 29 § 4713A(a); Title 11 § 9403B(a)).  
  - DFS SAK testing deadlines and annual average requirement; outsourcing mandate if DFS cannot meet deadlines (Title 29 § 4713A(b), (e)).  
  - LEISS update requirement for investigating LEA (Title 29 § 4713A(c)(2)).  
  - Victim information request response deadline (**7 calendar days**) (Title 11 § 9403B(c)).  
  - Pre-destruction notice (**20 days**) and mandatory preservation if requested (Title 11 § 9403B(e)); retention limits for unsolved cases (Title 29 § 4713(m)(1)).
- **Penalties:**
  - Tampering/attempted tampering with a biological sample/container collected under the statute: **Class D felony** (Title 29 § 4713(d)).  
  - Knowingly disseminating/receiving/using DNA database information for an unauthorized purpose: **Class A misdemeanor** (Title 29 § 4713(l)).
- **Limit on remedies for missed deadlines:** Failure to comply with SAK time requirements does **not** allow challenges to DNA evidence, exclusion of evidence, dismissal/set-aside, or damages claims (Title 29 § 4713A(f)).
