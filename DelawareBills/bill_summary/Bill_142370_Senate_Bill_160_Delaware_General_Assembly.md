## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 of the Delaware Code to require statewide posting and school distribution of **existing** informational materials about **Type 1 diabetes** to parents/guardians.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the **Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS)**, **in consultation with the Department of Education (DOE)**, to **post existing appropriate Type 1 diabetes informational materials** on the **DHSS website**.  
- Requires **each local education agency (LEA)** to **make those informational materials available** to **parents or guardians** of enrolled students.  
- Specifies minimum required content for the materials: description, risk factors/warning signs, screening recommendation, screening process/results implications, and post-diagnosis treatment-plan recommendation.  
- The bill focuses on **information dissemination**, not new medical services, screenings, or mandates for students.  
- Effective-date rule is tied to **August 1** and the bill’s enactment timing relative to **August 1, 2025**.  
- The act was **approved July 21, 2025** (as reflected in the enrolled law text).  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved parental awareness**: Materials must include **warning signs and risk factors** and a recommendation to consult a primary care provider for possible **immediate screening** when warning signs appear (§ 4150(b)(2)-(3)).  
- **Standardized baseline information statewide**: DHSS posts materials and LEAs distribute them, creating a consistent information source across districts (§ 4150(a)).  
- **Supports informed follow-up after diagnosis**: Materials must recommend consulting a primary care provider to develop a treatment plan and may include referral to a specialist such as an **endocrinologist** (§ 4150(b)(5)).  

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative workload for LEAs**: Each LEA must “make the informational materials available” to parents/guardians (§ 4150(a)), which may require communication processes and staff time.  
- **No specified delivery method or frequency**: The bill requires availability but does not define how/when distribution occurs (§ 4150(a)).  
- **Relies on “existing” materials**: The bill requires posting “existing appropriate informational materials,” which may limit updates or tailoring unless already available (§ 4150(a)).  

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What qualifies as **“existing appropriate informational materials”** and who determines appropriateness (DHSS alone or DHSS/DOE jointly)? (§ 4150(a)).  
- What does it mean for an LEA to **“make the informational materials available”** (e.g., enrollment packet, annual notice, website link, email, paper copies on request)? (§ 4150(a)).  
- No timeline is stated for how quickly DHSS must post materials after the effective date, or how quickly LEAs must begin making them available.  
- No mechanism is specified for updating materials over time or ensuring accessibility in multiple languages or accessible formats.
