## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware law (Title 11, § 1913) to limit when law enforcement may release or publish the **name and/or photograph (including mugshots)** of individuals suspected of or charged with crimes, extending restrictions beyond juveniles to also cover adults.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands § 1913 from applying only to **juveniles** to also restricting release/publication for **adults** suspected of or charged with crimes.
- Keeps the existing juvenile rule: law enforcement may release/publish a juvenile’s name or photograph only if **(1) charged with a “violent felony”** and **(2) release/publication is necessary to protect public safety**.
- Creates a new adult rule: law enforcement may release/publish an adult’s name or photograph only if **(1) suspected of or charged with a felony** and **(2) release/publication is necessary to protect public safety**.
- Broadens/clarifies definitions: “publish” includes posting on **publicly maintained social media pages or websites**.
- Defines “photograph” to include **mugshots** (face or profile).
- Uses a combined concept of “name of juvenile”/“photograph” to cover individuals arrested or suspected of committing a **crime or act of delinquency** (for juvenile context).
- No explicit penalties, enforcement mechanism, or effective date are stated in the provided text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces public dissemination of mugshots and names** for adults unless the case involves a **felony** and publication is **necessary for public safety** (new subsection (c)).
- **Protects juveniles’ privacy** by retaining the current higher threshold (must be **charged with a violent felony** and publication must be **necessary** for safety) (subsection (b)).
- Creates a clearer rule for modern communications by explicitly including **public law-enforcement social media/websites** within “publish” (definition of “Publish”).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Limits law-enforcement ability to publicly identify suspects in **non-felony (misdemeanor) cases**, even if public identification could be useful, because adult publication is conditioned on **felony suspicion/charge** plus **public safety necessity** (subsection (c)(1)-(2)).
- Requires law enforcement to apply a **“necessary to protect public safety”** standard before releasing information, which may slow communications or create uncertainty in fast-moving situations (subsections (b)(2) and (c)(2)).
- The bill does not state a penalty or remedy for violations, potentially reducing clarity about consequences for noncompliance (no penalty language in text).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Extends publication/release limits to adults**:
  - A law-enforcement agency **may not release or publish** the **name or photograph** of an adult suspected of or charged with a crime unless:
    1) The individual is **suspected of or charged with a felony**; and  
    2) Release/publication is **necessary to protect the public’s safety**.  *(New § 1913(c)(1)-(2))*
- **Maintains juvenile restriction** (existing structure retained):
  - No release/publication of a juvenile’s name or photograph unless:
    1) Juvenile is **charged with a violent felony**; and  
    2) Release/publication is **necessary to protect the public’s safety**.  *(§ 1913(b)(1)-(2))*
- **Clarifies definitions**:
  - “Publish” includes display on **any publicly-maintained social media page or website**.  
  - “Photograph” includes **mugshots**.  
  - “Juvenile” remains **17 years of age or younger**.
  - “Violent felony” is as defined in **11 Del. C. § 4201**.

## Who Benefits
- **Adults suspected of or charged with non-felony offenses** (misdemeanors), because law enforcement publication would be prohibited unless felony threshold and public-safety necessity are met.
- **Individuals (adults and juveniles) whose cases do not meet the bill’s thresholds**, by limiting public release of names and photos, including mugshots.
- **Juveniles (17 or younger)** continue to receive heightened protection unless charged with a **violent felony** and publication is necessary for safety.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Law-enforcement agencies** that currently publicize names/photos for reasons other than the bill’s criteria (e.g., routine arrest posts), because they would face new restrictions for adults and continued restrictions for juveniles.
- **Media outlets and the public** may receive less information directly from law enforcement in **non-felony adult cases** or where agencies cannot conclude publication is “necessary” for public safety.
