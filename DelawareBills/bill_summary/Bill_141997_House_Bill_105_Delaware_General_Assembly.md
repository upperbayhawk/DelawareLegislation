## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 19 to require pay range and benefits transparency in job opportunity postings, require certain employment records retention, and establish Department of Labor enforcement for these requirements. Applies only to employers with more than 10 employees.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires employers to announce/post/make known **each job opportunity to all employees**. (§709C(b)(1))
- Requires internal and external job postings to disclose: **(1) hourly or salary compensation (or range)** and **(2) a general description of benefits/other compensation**. (§709C(c)(1))
- Defines “hourly or salary compensation range” as a **minimum and maximum wage** set **in good faith** based on specified references (e.g., pay scale, previously determined range, actual range of others in equivalent roles, or budgeted amount). (§709C(a)(2))
- If no posting has been made available to an applicant, employer must provide compensation (or range) and benefits description **prior to any offer or discussion of compensation** and **at any time at the applicant’s request**. (§709C(b)(2))
- Commission-based job opportunity postings must disclose that the role is commission-based and **do not have to disclose compensation/range**; tipped roles must disclose tipped status and **base wage or range of base wages**. (§709C(c)(2))
- Requires retention of **job descriptions and salary/wage rate history** for each employee **during employment plus 3 years after employment ends**, and production to DOL upon request. (§709C(d))
- Enforcement by Department of Labor via administrative proceedings; first offense gets a **written warning**, subsequent offenses carry **$2,000–$10,000** civil penalties per violation; retaliation also penalized **$2,000–$10,000** per act. (§709C(e))
- Exempts employers with **10 or fewer employees** and exempts certain third-party reposting activity from liability. (§709C(g)-(h))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Applicants may receive pay range and benefits information in postings and, if not posted, **before any offer or compensation discussion**, supporting more informed decision-making. (§709C(b)(2), (c)(1))
- Employees gain visibility into internal job opportunities because employers must make **each job opportunity known to all employees** (with limited immediate-hire exceptions). (§709C(b)(1), (b)(3))
- Standardizes baseline disclosure (pay/range + benefits description) across internal and external postings, potentially improving transparency in hiring practices. (§709C(c)(1))
- Recordkeeping requirements (job descriptions and wage rate history maintained through **employment + 3 years**) may support clearer documentation of pay practices. (§709C(d))
- Establishes an enforcement mechanism with defined penalties and an appeal path to Superior Court within **30 days** of notice of a final order. (§709C(e)(1)b)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Imposes new compliance duties on covered employers, including posting content requirements and long-term record retention (**employment duration + 3 years**). (§709C(c), (d))
- Civil penalties for repeat violations may be significant (**$2,000 to $10,000 per violation**), increasing risk/cost for noncompliance. (§709C(e)(2))
- Commission-based postings are allowed to omit compensation/range (must only disclose commission basis), which reduces transparency for those roles compared to hourly/salary roles. (§709C(c)(2)a)
- Temporary/interim/acting roles needing immediate hire are exempt from the internal notice requirement, potentially limiting employee awareness for those opportunities. (§709C(b)(3))

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds/updates pay transparency requirements for job opportunities, including:
  - Internal notice requirement for each job opportunity to all employees (with immediate-hire exception). (§709C(b)(1), (b)(3))
  - Mandatory inclusion in internal and external postings of compensation (or range) and general benefits/other compensation description. (§709C(c)(1))
  - Applicant right to receive compensation (or range) and benefits description when no posting was made available, **prior to any offer or compensation discussion**, and upon request. (§709C(b)(2))
  - Special posting rules:
    - Commission roles: disclose commission basis; no compensation/range required. (§709C(c)(2)a)
    - Tipped roles: disclose tipped basis and base wage or base wage range. (§709C(c)(2)b)
  - Record retention: job descriptions + wage rate history kept through employment **plus 3 years**. (§709C(d))
  - Enforcement regime: written warning for first offense; **$2,000–$10,000** civil penalties for second/subsequent offenses; retaliation penalties **$2,000–$10,000**; 30-day appeal window. (§709C(e))
  - Exemptions: employers with **10 or fewer employees**; third parties who repost job opportunity notifications are not liable. (§709C(g)-(h))

## Who Benefits
- Job applicants who receive compensation/range and benefits information in postings or, in some cases, before compensation discussions. (§709C(b)(2), (c)(1))
- Current employees who must be informed of job opportunities internally (except certain immediate-hire roles). (§709C(b)(1), (b)(3))
- Employers and regulators seeking standardized documentation through mandatory retention of job descriptions and wage histories. (§709C(d))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Covered employers (more than 10 employees) facing added compliance, recordkeeping, and penalty exposure. (§709C(c), (d), (e), (h))
- Employers using broad pay ranges may face scrutiny because “breadth” is a factor in assessing good-faith compliance. (§709C(c)(1)a)
- Job seekers for commission-based roles may receive less compensation transparency because ranges are not required. (§709C(c)(2)a)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- The Act takes effect **1 year after enactment into law**. (Section 2)
- Employers must retain required records for **the duration of employment plus 3 years after the end of employment**. (§709C(d))
- Appeals of a Secretary of Labor final order must be filed in Superior Court **within 30 days from the date notice was sent**. (§709C(e)(1)b)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- Enforcement: Department of Labor may investigate and adjudicate violations through administrative proceedings; must adopt regulations establishing procedures; Secretary must issue a **written final order** when imposing a penalty and provide prompt notice. (§709C(e)(1)a)
- Appeals: aggrieved party may appeal to Superior Court within **30 days**; appeal is on the record (no trial de novo); court review limited to substantial evidence absent fraud; may remand for further record proceedings. (§709C(e)(1)b)
- Collection: if penalties unpaid after appeal or appeal period, DOL may seek execution in Superior Court as if the final order were a court judgment. (§709C(e)(1)c)
- Penalties:
  - First offense: **written warning**. (§709C(e)(2))
  - Second or subsequent offense: civil penalty **$2,000–$10,000 per violation**. (§709C(e)(2))
  - A failure to comply for **1 job opportunity = 1 violation** regardless of number of postings. (§709C(e)(2))
  - Retaliation/discrimination for complaint/information/proceedings/testimony: civil penalty **$2,000–$10,000 per discharge or act**. (§709C(e)(3))
- Compliance requirements (core):
  - Post/make known each job opportunity internally (with immediate-hire exception). (§709C(b)(1), (b)(3))
  - Include pay/range and benefits description in internal/external postings (with commission/tipped rules). (§709C(c))
  - Provide pay/range and benefits description to applicants in the circumstances described and upon request. (§709C(b)(2))
  - Maintain job description and wage history records through employment + **3 years**, and provide to DOL upon request. (§709C(d))
