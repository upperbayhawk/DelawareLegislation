## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Adopts the Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act in Delaware to allow an individual to transfer Delaware real estate to one or more beneficiaries at death by recording a **revocable “transfer on death (TOD) deed,”** allowing the property to pass **outside probate**. (New Title 25, Ch. 2; Synopsis)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes a **revocable, nontestamentary** TOD deed that transfers a transferor’s Delaware real property interest **effective at death**, if **recorded before death** in the county where the property is located. (§§ 205, 206, 207, 209)
- Clarifies that a TOD deed **controls over a contrary instruction in a will** (stated in the optional form; also described in Synopsis item (1)). (§ 216; Synopsis)
- Provides detailed rules on **revocation**: only by a later acknowledged and recorded instrument (e.g., revocation form, new TOD deed, or inter vivos deed that expressly revokes), and **not by a “revocatory act”** on the deed itself. (§ 211)
- Establishes that during the transferor’s life, the TOD deed does **not** affect the transferor’s rights, creditors’ rights, public assistance eligibility, or create a beneficiary property interest. (§ 212)
- At death, the beneficiary takes the property **subject to existing liens/encumbrances**, and estate creditor claims/allowances can be enforced against TOD-transferred property if probate assets are insufficient, with a strict **8-month** filing window. (§§ 213(b), 215, 215(c))
- Creates optional statutory forms for a **TOD deed** and a **revocation** to standardize recording. (§§ 216, 217)
- Adds a property insurance continuity rule: TOD beneficiaries are treated as insureds for **60 days** after the transferor’s death (with a **30-day/expiration** rule if the policy expires earlier). (§ 4132, Title 18)
- Exempts TOD deeds from certain transfer-tax filing requirements at recording and excludes TOD deeds from the definition of “document” for the relevant realty transfer tax provisions. (Title 30 §§ 5401, 5402)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Avoids probate for real estate transfers** by enabling automatic transfer at death through a recorded TOD deed. (§ 205; optional form text “Probate is not required”; Synopsis)
- **Retains full lifetime control** for the owner: TOD deed does not limit the owner’s ability to sell, encumber, or otherwise transfer the property during life. (§ 212(1); optional form Q&A)
- **Standardized forms and recording rules** may reduce errors and increase clarity for recorders and the public. (§§ 216–217; Synopsis item (2))
- **Temporary insurance continuity** for beneficiaries for up to **60 days** after death may prevent coverage gaps while the beneficiary arranges a new policy. (Title 18 § 4132(c)–(e); Synopsis item (8))
- Clarifies administrative handling by excluding TOD-transferred real estate from probate inventory filings and ensuring reporting to county assessment boards includes TOD transfers. (Title 12 §§ 1905, 2508)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Creditor/allowance exposure after death**: if the probate estate is insufficient, creditor claims and statutory allowances may be enforced against TOD-transferred property, potentially affecting beneficiaries’ expectations. (§ 215(a))
- **Time-limited estate recovery process** requires action within **8 months after death**, which may create procedural pressure for estates/creditors and uncertainty for beneficiaries during that period. (§ 215(c))
- **Beneficiary receives no warranty of title**, even if the deed says otherwise. (§ 213(d))
- **Recording before death is mandatory** for effectiveness; failure to record timely makes the TOD deed ineffective. (§ 209(3); § 216 form notice: “must be recorded before your death”)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Title 25, Chapter 2** (Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act) authorizing TOD deeds for Delaware real property. (§§ 201–220)
- TOD deed must be a **properly recordable deed**, state transfer occurs at death, and be **recorded before death** in the county where the property is located. (§ 209)
- TOD deeds are **revocable** and **nontestamentary**; capacity aligns with will capacity. (§§ 206–208)
- **Revocation mechanics**: only by a later acknowledged and recorded instrument; joint-owner TOD deed revoked only if revoked by **all living joint owners**; no revocation by “revocatory act” on the deed. (§ 211)
- **No lifetime beneficiary interest** and no effect on transferor/creditors/public assistance eligibility during life. (§ 212)
- **Transfer rules at death**: beneficiary must survive; lapsed shares pass to remaining designated beneficiaries proportionally; joint ownership survivorship supersedes TOD deed unless transferor was last surviving joint owner. (§ 213(a)–(c))
- **Estate recovery**: TOD-transferred property can be reached for allowed claims/statutory allowances if probate estate insufficient; action must be filed within **8 months** of death. (§ 215)
- **Insurance continuity**: TOD beneficiary treated as insured for **60 days** after death (with 30-day/expiration minimum), unless beneficiary obtains their own policy. (Title 18 § 4132(c)–(e))
- **Transfer tax administration**: TOD deeds excluded from “document” definition for the relevant transfer tax subchapter; no transfer-tax affidavit, estimated income tax form, or other local forms required for recording/effectiveness of a TOD deed. (Title 30 §§ 5401(1)a, 5402(g))
- **Probate administration adjustments**: TOD-transferred real estate excluded from probate inventory/appraisal; Registers must report TOD transfers to Boards of Assessment. (Title 12 §§ 1905, 2508)

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware real property owners** who want a simple, revocable way to transfer real estate at death without creating a revocable trust or using probate for that property. (§ 205; Synopsis)
- **Designated beneficiaries** who receive property automatically at death and receive short-term insurance continuity rights. (Title 25 § 213; Title 18 § 4132)
- **County recording/assessment administration** (Recorders of Deeds and Boards of Assessment) through standardized forms and clarified reporting. (§§ 216–217; Title 12 § 2508; Synopsis item (2))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Heirs or devisees under a will** whose instructions conflict with a TOD deed, because the TOD deed is intended to take precedence for the same property. (§ 216 form statement; Synopsis item (1))
- **Beneficiaries** may be affected by estate creditor/allowance enforcement against TOD property when probate assets are insufficient. (§ 215)
- **Secured parties/encumbrance holders are not negatively affected** by the transfer; the bill explicitly preserves encumbrances (beneficiaries take subject to them), but beneficiaries may experience the burden of existing liens. (§ 213(b))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date of Act**: **90 days after enactment into law.** (Section 8)
- **Applicability of TOD deed rules**: applies to TOD deeds made **before, on, or after** the effective date, **by a transferor dying on or after** the effective date. (§ 203)
- **Creditor/allowance enforcement deadline**: proceeding must be commenced **not later than 8 months after the transferor’s death.** (§ 215(c))
- **Insurance coverage period**: generally **60 days immediately following death**; if policy expires sooner than 60 days after death, coverage continues **30 days or until expiration, whichever is later**. (Title 18 § 4132(c)–(d))
- **Recording deadline for effectiveness**: TOD deed and revocation instruments must be **recorded before the transferor’s death** to be effective. (§ 209(3); § 211(a)(2); §§ 216–217 form notices)
- **Probate inventory timing (existing law referenced/retained)**: executor/administrator must file inventory within **3 months** after granting of letters; TOD real estate is excluded from the real estate description required there. (Title 12 § 1905(a))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (core):**
  - TOD deed must be **recorded before death** and meet recordable deed formalities and include the death-transfer statement. (§ 209)
  - Revocation must be by a specified **acknowledged and recorded** instrument **after** the TOD deed’s acknowledgment and **before death**. (§ 211(a))
  - Disclaimer of an interest in real property must be **recorded** in the county where the real property is located. (Title 12 § 612(f)(2))
- **No explicit penalties** specified in source text for noncompliance; the consequence is generally **ineffectiveness** of the TOD deed/revocation if requirements are not met. (§ 209(3); § 211(a)(2); forms)
