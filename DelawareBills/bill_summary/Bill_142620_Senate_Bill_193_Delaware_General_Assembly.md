## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s public works “craft training requirement” laws to eliminate the option for contractors/subcontractors to comply by paying into the Department of Labor’s Apprenticeship and Training Fund (“buy-out”), thereby requiring compliance through apprenticeship/craft training participation instead.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Removes the option for public works contractors/subcontractors to satisfy craft training requirements by **making payments** into the Apprenticeship and Training Fund. (Synopsis; Title 29 §6960A)
- Retains/relies on compliance pathways tied to **having active apprentices**, **recent completions (within 6 months)**, or **consortium membership** with an active apprentice. (Title 29 §6960A(b)(1)a.–c.)
- Applies when a public works contract **meets prevailing wage**, the contractor has **10+ total employees**, the project is **not a federal highway project** (with a stated exception), and the craft is on DOL’s list of approved/registered programs. (Title 29 §6960A(a)(1))
- Contractors must ensure **subcontractors** also provide craft training when the requirement applies to the subcontractor. (Title 29 §6960A(a)(2))
- Existing penalty language in §6960A references payment amounts and penalties up to **10%** of the payment; the bill text shows deletions related to payment compliance, but the penalty section language remains in the excerpt and appears tied to the payment mechanism. (Title 29 §6960A(d))
- Fiscal note projects reduced revenues to DOL/DOE previously funded by payment option collections averaging **$85,940/year** (DOL **$27,501**, DOE **$58,439**) and a **one-time $25,000** technology cost at DOL to remove the payment workflow. (Fiscal Note assumptions 3–5)
- DOL reports that in the last two fiscal years, **less than 10%** of public works contracts/projects used the payment option for compliance. (Fiscal Note assumption 2)
- Effective date assumed to be **upon signature by the Governor**. (Fiscal Note assumption 1)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Strengthens apprenticeship participation** by removing the compliance alternative of paying into the fund (“buy-out”), aligning compliance with active apprenticeship training pathways. (Synopsis; §6960A(b)(1)a.–c.)
- **Standardizes compliance** around training participation (active apprentice, recent completion, or consortium with active apprentice) for covered public works contracts. (§6960A(b)(1))
- Could increase the number of contractors directly engaging in approved/registered craft training programs because payment compliance is removed. (Synopsis describes buy-out as an avoidance mechanism)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces dedicated funding flows** associated with buy-out payments: average annual collections **$85,940**, with **$27,501** used by DOL and **$58,439** provided to DOE; fiscal note projects these as annual revenue losses. (Fiscal Note assumption 4)
- **One-time administrative/technology cost** to DOL of **$25,000** to remove the payment option and related workflows in the reporting system. (Fiscal Note assumption 5)
- Contractors that previously complied via payments must shift to apprenticeship/consortium participation, which may change compliance steps before contract execution. (§6960A(b)(1); Fiscal Note assumption 2 describes contractors using payment option)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Eliminates payment-based compliance (“buy-out”)** for craft training requirements on covered public works contracts by deleting §6960A(b)(1)(d) (“Making a payment under subsection (c)…”). (Bill text page 3)
- Maintains craft training compliance methods requiring:
  - **At least 1 active apprentice** in the craft program; or
  - **At least 1 active apprentice completing** the program **within 6 months** before contract execution; or
  - **Consortium membership** with regular contribution, access, and **at least 1 active apprentice**. (§6960A(b)(1)a.–c.)
- Keeps applicability thresholds for covered projects/contractors:
  - project meets **prevailing wage**;
  - contractor has **10+ total employees**;
  - not a **federal highway project** (except referenced exception);
  - craft has an approved/registered apprenticeship program on DOL’s list. (§6960A(a)(1))

## Who Benefits
- Apprenticeship and craft training programs and workers participating in “earn while you learn” systems, to the extent more contractors must comply via apprenticeship participation rather than payments. (Synopsis; §6960A(b)(1)a.–c.)
- Contractors already operating apprenticeship programs or participating in consortia, since the bill removes an alternative compliance route used by some competitors. (Synopsis)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Contractors/subcontractors that previously relied on the payment option to satisfy craft training requirements. (Fiscal Note assumption 2; §6960A(b)(1) change)
- Department of Labor and Department of Education funding streams supported by payment-option collections (average **$27,501/year** DOL and **$58,439/year** DOE). (Fiscal Note assumption 4)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** Assumed **upon signature by the Governor**. (Fiscal Note assumption 1)
- Existing annual list publication deadlines in Title 19 §204 (as shown in the bill text excerpt):
  - List of apprentice-appropriate classifications revised by **December 31** each year. (§204(b)(1))
  - DOL craft list published by **January 31** each year. (§204(b)(2)a.)
- Other timing thresholds retained in §6960A:
  - Apprentice completion within **6 months** before contract execution qualifies. (§6960A(b)(1)b.)
  - Penalty remission/refund requires compliance within **60 days** of notice; application must be filed within **1 year** of notice. (§6960A(d)(2))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance timing:** If required, contractor must satisfy craft training requirement **before the contract is executed** and must commit that subcontractors comply when applicable. (§6960A(a)(2); §6960A(b)(1))
- **Penalty provisions (as written in provided excerpt):**
  - Contract must include a penalty provision for failure to comply. (§6960A(d)(1))
  - Penalty provision requires payment of the amount required under subsection (c) plus an additional amount **not to exceed 10%** of that payment. (§6960A(d)(1)a.–b.)
  - Penalty may be remitted/refunded if compliance is established within **60 days** of notice; application must be filed within **1 year**. (§6960A(d)(2))
  - Unrefunded penalty amounts revert to the government entity under the contract. (§6960A(d)(3))
- **Note:** Because the bill removes the payment-based compliance option in §6960A(b)(1)(d), the continued operation of penalty language tied to payment amounts is unclear from the excerpt. (Bill text pages 2–3)
