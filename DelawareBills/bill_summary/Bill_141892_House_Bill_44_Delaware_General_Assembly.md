## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 14 of the Delaware Code to require Delaware to maintain a Migrant Education Program that meets migratory children’s educational needs, preserves the level of services in place (or required) as of **January 19, 2025**, and ensures continuity of services even if **federal funding is insufficient**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes/updates Delaware’s **Migrant Education Program** in **Title 14, Chapter 48** with stated purposes focused on addressing migratory children’s unique educational needs.
- Requires the **Delaware Department of Education (DOE)** to ensure migratory children’s educational needs are met.
- If **federal funding is insufficient**, DOE must **identify, transfer, or request appropriation of supplemental funding** from the State or other sources to avoid **interruption of services**.
- Sets a **service-maintenance baseline date: January 19, 2025**—the program must include all services provided by the State on that date, or required under **ESEA (20 U.S.C. § 6301 et seq.) as of that date**.
- Authorizes DOE to **promulgate regulations** to implement Chapter 48.
- Fiscal note reports **262 migrant children** currently served in Delaware public schools.
- Federal funding cited for **FY 2025**: **$595,508** (Title I, Part C) + **$122,251** (Consortium Incentive Grant) = **$717,759** total.
- Fiscal impact for **FY 2026–FY 2028: Indeterminable** (future federal funding unknown).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Continuity of services:** Explicit requirement to ensure **“no interruption in services”** if federal funds are insufficient (§ 4802).
- **Service floor anchored to a date certain:** Maintains services that existed or were required as of **January 19, 2025** (§ 4803), which may protect against program reductions.
- **Clear assignment of responsibility:** Places the duty on DOE to ensure migratory children’s needs are met and to pursue supplemental funding if needed (§ 4802).
- **Regulatory authority:** Allows DOE to adopt regulations to implement the program (§ 4804), supporting administrative clarity.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential state fiscal exposure:** If federal support declines, DOE must seek supplemental funding (identify/transfer/request appropriation), which could shift costs to the State (§ 4802; fiscal note: impact indeterminable).
- **Baseline rigidity:** Tying required services to what existed/was required as of **January 19, 2025** may limit flexibility to redesign services if circumstances change (§ 4803).
- **Uncertain cost planning:** Fiscal note states future federal funding is unknown and fiscal impact is **indeterminable** for **FY 2026–FY 2028**, complicating budgeting.
