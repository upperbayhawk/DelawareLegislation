## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Establish Delaware’s participation in the **Athletic Trainer Compact** (new **Chapter 26A, Title 24**), enabling **interstate practice mobility** for qualified licensed athletic trainers through a **Compact Privilege**, while preserving each state’s authority to protect public health and safety through licensure and discipline.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates the **Athletic Trainer Compact** to allow Delaware-licensed athletic trainers meeting uniform requirements to practice in other **Member States** via a **Compact Privilege** (equivalent to a license in a Remote State).  
- Requires Member States (including Delaware, if participating) to **participate in a Commission Data System** and **share licensure, adverse action, and significant investigative information**.  
- Sets eligibility for a Compact Privilege, including a **criminal background check** and professional qualification pathways (e.g., **active BOC certification**, or specified education + BOC exam).  
- Establishes that athletic trainers must follow the **Scope of Practice of the state where the patient is located** and are subject to the **Remote State’s regulatory authority**.  
- Provides for **automatic deactivation** of Compact Privileges in all Member States when an **Adverse Action** is imposed against the Compact Qualifying License during the pendency of the order.  
- Creates the **Athletic Trainer Licensure Compact Commission**, including governance, rulemaking, assessments/fees, reporting, auditing, and enforcement mechanisms.  
- Sets key deadlines: each Member State selects **one Commissioner within 60 days** of that state’s effective date; licensees must report certain actions from non-Member States within **30 days**.  
- Compact becomes effective only when enacted into law by the **7th Member State**; withdrawal by a state takes effect **180 days** after repeal.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved access and continuity of care:** The Compact’s stated objectives include increasing access to athletic training and enhancing continuity of care through mutual recognition of licenses and allowing **telehealth** to facilitate access.  
- **Streamlined multistate practice:** Eliminates the necessity for licenses in multiple states for eligible practitioners by creating **Compact Privileges** (Section §2601A objectives; §2604A).  
- **Workforce mobility and military family support:** The Compact explicitly supports **relocating Active Military Members and spouses**, including a provision that they **shall not be required to pay a fee to the Commission** for a Compact Privilege (§2606A).  
- **Stronger cross-state discipline visibility:** Requires reporting of **Adverse Actions** and **Current Significant Investigative Information** through a shared Data System (§2603A(a)(7); §2609A), and deactivates Compact Privileges across states during pending adverse actions (§2604A(e)).  
- **Preserves state control of practice standards:** Requires compliance with the **Remote State’s Scope of Practice** and confirms Remote State authority to remove privileges, impose fines, and take protective actions (§2604A(c)-(d)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative and compliance burden on the state licensing authority:** Member States must fully participate in the Data System, report adverse actions/investigations, and implement criminal background check procedures (including FBI checks) within rule-established timeframes (§2603A(a)(6)-(8); §2609A).  
- **Potential costs to Delaware from Commission assessments/fees:** The Compact Commission may **levy an annual assessment** on Member States and impose fees on licensees to fund operations (§2608A(g)(3)).  
- **Increased multistate regulatory coordination demands:** Delaware must comply with Compact Commission **Rules that have the force of law** in Member States (subject to certain Scope of Practice conflict limits) (§2610A(b)).  
- **Confidentiality/public records complexity:** Some investigative information is available only to Member States; states may designate information not shareable with the public without permission (§2609A(d), (f)), which may require process changes.

## Educational Impact
- Compact Privilege eligibility references education standards where BOC certification pathway is not met, including at least a **bachelor’s degree** with an athletic training major from a **CAATE-accredited** program (or foreign equivalent, or substantial equivalent set by Rule) (§2604A(a)(1)(b)).  
- Continuing competence is required for member-state eligibility; evidence of **active BOC certification may satisfy** continuing competence for Compact purposes (§2602A(12); §2603A(a)(3)).  

## Health Impact
- The Compact is expressly intended to **improve public access** to athletic training services and supports **telehealth** to increase access (§2601A).  
- Patient protection mechanisms include:
  - Mandatory compliance with the **Scope of Practice** where the patient is located (§2604A(c)).  
  - Remote State authority to **remove Compact Privilege**, **impose fines**, or take other actions to protect health and safety (§2604A(d)).  
  - Cross-state reporting and deactivation of privileges during adverse actions (§2604A(e); §2609A).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new **Chapter 26A (Athletic Trainer Compact)** to Title 24, creating:
  - A **Compact Privilege** system allowing interstate practice based on a **Compact Qualifying License** (§2604A; §2605A).  
  - Uniform participation conditions for states, including data reporting and background check requirements (§2603A; §2609A).  
  - The **Athletic Trainer Licensure Compact Commission** with rulemaking power, governance structure, and funding authority (§2608A; §2610A).  
  - A multi-state discipline and information-sharing framework, including automatic privilege deactivation upon adverse action (§2607A; §2604A(e)).  
- Establishes licensure/discipline interoperability rules, including subpoenas, joint investigations, and cross-state information exchange (§2607A; §2609A).

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware-licensed athletic trainers** who meet Compact eligibility requirements and want to practice in other Member States (§2604A).  
- **Patients** seeking athletic training services, including via **telehealth**, due to increased provider mobility (§2601A).  
- **Active Military Members and their spouses**: no Commission fee for Compact Privilege; possible reduced/no state fee if Delaware chooses (§2606A).  
- **State regulators/public safety systems** through expanded data sharing on adverse actions and significant investigative information (§2609A).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State licensing authority/administration**: increased obligations for background checks, reporting, data submission, and compliance with Commission Rules (§2603A; §2609A; §2610A).  
- **Licensees subject to discipline**: adverse actions can trigger **deactivation of Compact Privileges in all Member States** during the pendency of the order (§2604A(e)).  
- **Member States (including Delaware)**: potential obligation to pay **annual assessments** to fund the Commission (§2608A(g)(3)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Compact effective date (overall):** Takes effect on the date the Compact statute is enacted into law in the **7th Member State** (§2612A(a)).  
- **Commissioner appointment deadline:** Each Member State must select **one Commissioner within 60 days** of that state’s effective date (§2608A(b)(1)).  
- **Commission vacancy fill deadline:** A vacancy must be filled within **60 days** (§2608A(b)(4)).  
- **Licensee reporting deadline:** Licensee must report to the Commission any adverse action/encumbrance/restriction taken by a **non-Member State within 30 days** from the date the action is taken (§2604A(a)(10)).  
- **Member State withdrawal effective date:** Withdrawal via repeal is effective **180 days after** enactment of the repealing statute (§2612A(b)(1)).  
- **Post-termination/withdrawal recognition period:** A terminated/withdrawing state must continue recognizing Compact Privileges for at least **180 days after the date of notice** of termination/withdrawal (§2611A(f); §2612A(b)(3)).  
- **Rule rejection window:** If a majority of Member State legislatures reject a Rule within **4 years** of adoption, it loses effect (§2610A(d)).  
- **Rulemaking notice:** At least **30 days** advance notice before public hearing on proposed rules (§2610A(f)).  
- **Emergency rule retroactive process deadline:** Usual rulemaking procedures must be applied **no later than 90 days** after an emergency rule’s effective date (§2610A(k)).  
- **Technical rule revision challenge period:** **30 days** after posting (§2610A(l)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Eligibility/compliance requirements for Compact Privilege** include:
  - Criminal background check prior to compact entry (§2604A(a)).  
  - No encumbrance within previous **2 years** (§2604A(a)(3)).  
  - Meeting Remote State **jurisprudence requirements** if imposed (§2604A(a)(9)).  
  - Pay applicable fees (§2604A(a)(6)); Active Military/spouse Commission fee waiver (§2606A).  
- **Remote State enforcement powers:** May remove Compact Privilege for a period of time, **impose fines**, or take other actions to protect health and safety (§2604A(d)).  
- **Automatic deactivation:** Adverse action against the Compact Qualifying License results in deactivation of Compact Privileges in all Member States during the order’s pendency (§2604A(e)).  
- **Investigations and subpoenas:** Remote States may issue subpoenas; states may conduct joint investigations and share investigative materials with confidentiality protections (§2607A(d), (g)).  
- **Member State compliance enforcement:** The Compact Commission may issue default notices, provide technical assistance, terminate states by majority vote, and by **2/3 vote** initiate legal action to enforce compliance; prevailing party may recover **costs and reasonable attorney’s fees** (§2611A(b)-(j)).  
- **Licensee cost recovery:** A Member State may recover from the licensee the costs of investigations and case dispositions resulting from adverse action, if permitted by state law (§2607A(f)).
