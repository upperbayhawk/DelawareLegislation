## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 29, § 2721 to expand allowable funding sources for the Delaware Volunteer Fire Service Revolving Loan Fund (the “Fund”) to include contributions/donations from counties and private entities, and require that county-contributed funds be used for loans benefiting fire companies within that county.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands Fund inflows beyond General Assembly appropriations to include money “received from any county of the State” and donations from “any person, corporation, or charitable organization.”  
- Requires county-specific contributions to be used for upgrading equipment and facilities “located within that county.”  
- Continues the Fund as a revolving loan account that can receive loan repayments and earn interest on deposits.  
- Preserves existing limitations: loans generally may not be used for equipment already ordered or facility/parking expansions already begun, with a limited exception if deemed necessary by the Delaware Council on Volunteer Fire Service.  
- Keeps administration within the Department of Finance, under guidelines and procedures developed by the Delaware Council on Volunteer Fire Service.  
- Reaffirms authorized purposes, including making loans, buying/refinancing debt, and allowing pledges of certain state funds as collateral.  
- Contains technical drafting corrections to conform to the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual.  
- Takes effect **90 days after enactment**; bill was **approved September 9, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More potential funding sources:** Allows the Fund to accept contributions from counties and donations from private parties (persons, corporations, charitable organizations), potentially increasing available loan capital beyond state appropriations.  
- **County funds targeted locally:** Requires that money received from a county be used for equipment/facility upgrades within that same county, improving geographic alignment of benefits.  
- **Supports readiness and response capacity:** Funds are used to “upgrade equipment and improve facilities” essential to “fire, rescue, emergency medical, and technical emergency response” services.  
- **Debt management flexibility:** Authorizes the Fund to “buy or refinance debt obligations” of eligible volunteer fire/rescue/EMS companies.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Uneven county participation could affect equity:** Because counties may contribute at different levels, the requirement that county funds stay within the contributing county could result in different access to loan support by county. (This follows directly from the county-restriction requirement.)  
- **Limits use for projects already underway:** Loans generally “must not be made available” for already-ordered equipment or expansions already begun, which can restrict help for ongoing projects unless the Council deems an exception necessary.
