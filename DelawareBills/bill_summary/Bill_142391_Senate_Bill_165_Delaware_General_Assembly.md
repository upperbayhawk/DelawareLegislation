## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 to require the Delaware Department of Education to pilot and then statewide-implement a **revised “student improvement component”** used in educator evaluation systems (Delaware Performance Appraisal System and Delaware Teacher Growth and Support System), with specified participation levels, “hold harmless” protections during pilots, and required consideration of certain student-related factors.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the Department of Education to run a **pilot of a revised student improvement component** in **no fewer than 3 local education agencies (LEAs)** in **academic year 2025-2026**.  
- The pilot must include **teachers, specialists, and administrators** and focus on **refining goal-setting**, **alignment with professional growth**, and **flexibility** in measures.  
- Pilot participation requires **consent of the LEA** and the **collective bargaining representative** (if applicable).  
- **Hold harmless** applies for participating LEAs in **2025-2026**: they still receive summative evaluations, but the **student improvement component does not factor into overall ratings**.  
- Student improvement measures must consider factors that may adversely affect evaluation, including **absence**, **mobility**, **chronic noncompliance with school rules**, and **chronic failure by parents to abide by the Parents’ Declaration of Responsibilities**, plus “other factors.”  
- If a student misses **more than 15% of class time**, that student’s performance **may be used only with the educator’s agreement**.  
- Pilot expands in **2026-2027** to **no fewer than 6 additional LEAs**, also **held harmless**.  
- Statewide implementation begins **2027-2028** for **all licensed and certified educators**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More context-sensitive evaluations:** Requires consideration of specific factors (e.g., **student absence, mobility, chronic noncompliance**) that “may adversely affect the evaluation,” potentially making the student improvement component more reflective of circumstances beyond an educator’s control.
- **Protection during testing/refinement:** “**Held harmless**” provisions in **2025-2026** and **2026-2027** ensure the student improvement component **does not affect overall ratings** while the system is refined.
- **Collaborative implementation:** Pilot requires **LEA consent** and **educator union consent (if applicable)**, which may support smoother implementation and buy-in.
- **Phased rollout:** A multi-year phase-in (**3+ LEAs in 2025-2026; 6+ additional in 2026-2027; statewide in 2027-2028**) allows time to refine processes before statewide adoption.
- **Attendance threshold safeguard:** For students missing **>15% of class time**, use of their performance requires **educator agreement**, limiting potential distortions from low attendance.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Delayed statewide use in overall ratings:** Because pilot participants are **held harmless** in **2025-2026** and **2026-2027**, statewide incorporation into overall ratings does not occur until **2027-2028**.
- **Potential inconsistency across LEAs during pilots:** Different LEAs may implement/refine measures differently during pilots, while the component is not used in overall ratings for pilot LEAs.
- **Additional implementation complexity:** The requirement to account for multiple factors (absence, mobility, compliance, parental responsibilities, “other factors”) may add administrative and methodological complexity to the evaluation process.
