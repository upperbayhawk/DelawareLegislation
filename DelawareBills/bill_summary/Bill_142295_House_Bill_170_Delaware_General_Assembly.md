## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s microbrewery licensing law (Title 4, § 512C) to allow a commonly owned microbrewery business to choose between operating **(a) 1 microbrewery + up to 2 brewpubs** or **(b) 2 microbreweries + 1 brewpub**, while keeping the **maximum total licenses at 3**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Amends **4 Del. C. § 512C(h)** (Microbreweries; license; alternating premises).
- Keeps the overall cap at **3 total licenses** under common ownership/control.
- Allows a microbrewery to sell its manufactured product for **off-premises consumption** at:
  - the microbrewery licensee, and either
  - **up to 2 brewery-pubs** (§ 512B), **or**
  - **1 additional microbrewery** (§ 512C) **and 1 brewery-pub** (§ 512B),
  - provided the locations are **all owned or controlled by the same person**.
- Changes prior structure described in the synopsis as allowing **1 microbrewery + up to 2 brewpubs**, by adding the new alternative: **2 microbreweries + 1 brewpub**.
- Does **not** increase the maximum number of establishments/licenses beyond **3**.
- Enacted as **Chapter 41, Volume 85** of the Laws of Delaware.
- **Approved June 24, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Business flexibility within the same 3-license cap:** A business can choose **2 microbreweries + 1 brewpub** instead of being limited (per synopsis) to **1 microbrewery + 2 brewpubs**.
- **Expanded off-premises sales points for the manufacturer’s own product (under common ownership):** The statute expressly allows sales for **off-premises consumption** at the additional affiliated locations described in § 512C(h).
- **No increase in total licenses allowed:** The synopsis states the **total number of licenses remains 3**, which may address concerns about overall expansion of license holdings.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Amends 4 Del. C. § 512C(h)** to allow off-premises sales of a microbrewery’s manufactured product at commonly owned/controlled affiliated locations under two alternative structures:
  1) **Up to 2 brewery-pubs** licensed under **§ 512B**, or  
  2) **1 additional microbrewery** licensed under **§ 512C** **and** **1 brewery-pub** licensed under **§ 512B**.  
- Confirms (per synopsis) that the **maximum total licenses a business could hold remains 3**, but changes the permissible mix to include **2 microbreweries + 1 brewpub**.
- Maintains that a microbrewery “**can also choose to operate just one establishment**” (synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- **Microbrewery businesses under common ownership/control** that want to expand using a structure of **2 microbreweries + 1 brewpub** (while staying within **3 total licenses**).
- **Affiliated brewery-pubs and microbreweries** (under the same ownership/control) that may sell the microbrewery’s product for **off-premises consumption** as authorized in § 512C(h).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Competing breweries/brewpubs not under the same ownership/control** may face stronger competition from operators able to run **2 microbreweries** plus a brewpub under the same ownership (as explicitly authorized).
- **Regulatory agencies/license administrators** may face added compliance review related to verifying “owned or controlled by the same person.” (Administrative burden implied by the new option and requirement.)
