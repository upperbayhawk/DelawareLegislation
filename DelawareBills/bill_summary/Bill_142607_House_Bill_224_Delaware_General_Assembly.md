## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To amend Delaware law to (1) authorize licensed Physician Assistants (PAs) to pronounce death and complete/sign the medical certification of death, (2) remove registered nurses (RNs) from the list of practitioners who may complete/sign the medical certification of death, and (3) require electronic preparation/certification/signature of required medical certifications of death.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes Delaware-licensed physician assistants (PAs) to **pronounce death**. (Title 16, §3123(g); Title 24, §1773(a)(2)g.)
- Authorizes PAs to **complete and sign the medical certification of death**. (Title 16, §3123(c); Title 24, §1773(a)(2)g.)
- **Removes registered nurses (RNs)** from the list of individuals who may **complete/sign** the medical certification of death. (Title 16, §3123(c); also described in Synopsis)
- Keeps (and clarifies) that **attending physicians and APRNs** may complete/sign medical certifications of death, and includes PAs. (Title 16, §3123(c))
- Retains that multiple professionals may **pronounce death**, including attending physician, medical examiner, RN/APRN, medical control physician, and PA. (Title 16, §3123(g))
- Requires that required **medical certifications of death must be electronically prepared, certified, and signed** by specified individuals. (Title 16, §3123(h))
- Maintains the **48-hour** timeframe: when no official death investigation is required, the medical certification must be completed/signed and returned to the funeral director **within 48 hours after death** (or as soon as possible in certain cases). (Title 16, §3123(c)(1))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expands the pool of authorized clinicians** who can pronounce death and complete/sign medical certifications by adding **physician assistants**, which could support timely completion within the **48-hour** requirement. (Title 16, §3123(c)(1); §3123(g); Title 24, §1773(a)(2)g.)
- **Aligns statutory authority with scope of practice** by removing RNs from completing/signing medical certifications of death where the bill states this conflicted with Title 24 scope of practice. (Synopsis; Title 16, §3123(c))
- **Standardizes electronic processing** by requiring electronic preparation/certification/signature for required medical certifications of death. (Title 16, §3123(h))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces RN authority**: registered nurses are removed from the list of practitioners who may complete/sign medical certifications of death, potentially concentrating that function among physicians/APRNs/PAs. (Title 16, §3123(c); Synopsis)
- **Electronic-only requirement** may create operational challenges for practitioners or facilities that are not equipped for electronic preparation/certification/signature, because the bill states certifications **must** be electronic. (Title 16, §3123(h))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill requires electronic preparation/certification/signature, but **does not describe exceptions** (e.g., outages, system downtime) or fallback procedures. (Title 16, §3123(h))
- The bill references deaths “under subsections (e) and (f)” for timing (“as soon as possible”), but this excerpt **does not include those subsections’ content**, limiting clarity on the affected scenarios. (Title 16, §3123(c)(1))
- The bill authorizes PAs to pronounce death and complete/sign certifications, but **does not detail training, supervision, or documentation standards** beyond referencing existing PA scope provisions. (Title 16, §3123; Title 24, §1773)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 16, §3123(c)** (medical certification completion/signature when no official death investigation is required):
  - Continues requirement to complete/sign and return to the funeral director **within 48 hours after death** (or as soon as possible in certain cases).
  - Specifies allowable certifiers as:
    - **Attending physician**
    - **Advanced practice registered nurse (APRN)** acting under **Title 24, §1902(s)**
    - **Physician assistant (PA)** acting under **Title 24, §1773**
  - **Removes registered nurses (RNs)** from the list of those who may complete/sign the medical certification of death.
- **Title 16, §3123(g)** (who may pronounce death):
  - Lists authorized individuals, including:
    - Attending physician
    - Medical examiner
    - **Registered nurse or APRN** acting under **Title 24, §1902(s)**
    - Medical control physician under **Title 24, §1760(b)**
    - **Physician assistant** acting under **Title 24, §1773**
- **Title 16, §3123(h)** (format and method):
  - Requires all required medical certifications of death to **be electronically prepared, certified, and signed** by designated individuals.
- **Title 24, §1773(a)(2)** (PA scope—medical acts):
  - Adds explicit authority for PAs: **“Pronouncing death and completing and signing a medical certification of death.”** (new item “g.”)

## Who Benefits
- **Physician assistants** (expanded statutory authority to pronounce death and complete/sign medical certifications). (Title 16, §3123(g); Title 24, §1773(a)(2)g.)
- **Funeral directors and families** may benefit from potentially more timely completion of required documentation within the **48-hour** timeframe due to added authorized certifiers (PAs). (Title 16, §3123(c)(1))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Registered nurses (RNs)**, who are removed from the list of practitioners who may complete/sign medical certifications of death. (Title 16, §3123(c); Synopsis)
- **Providers/facilities** that are not prepared to comply with electronic-only certification/signature requirements. (Title 16, §3123(h))
