## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 6 of the Delaware Code to require use of a specific merchant category code (MCC) for firearms and ammunition businesses in payment card transaction processing, and to create an enforcement mechanism (civil penalties and injunctive relief) administered exclusively by the Delaware Department of Justice (DOJ).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **payment card networks** to make available the **ISO “merchant category code for firearms and ammunition businesses”** (established **September 9, 2022**, or its successor) for use by merchant acquirers serving firearms merchants.  
- Requires **merchant acquirers** to **assign** that MCC to qualifying **firearms merchants** (as defined).  
- Defines “firearms merchant” as a **federal firearms licensee** whose **highest sales value** is (or is expected to be) from combined Delaware sales of **firearms, firearm accessories, or ammunition** (as stated to the acquirer).  
- Makes any **waiver or attempted waiver** of MCC requirements **void and unenforceable** as against public policy.  
- Grants the **Delaware DOJ exclusive enforcement authority** and authorizes DOJ to bring **civil actions** for violations.  
- Establishes a **notice-and-cure process**: DOJ must give **written notice at least 30 days** before filing, and no action may be brought if the recipient **cures within 30 days** and provides specified attestations and documentation.  
- Civil penalties upon a court finding of violation include **$10,000 per violation**, **injunctive relief**, and **reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs**.  
- Deadlines: networks must comply by **July 1, 2025**; most other provisions take effect **October 1, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardized transaction classification**: By requiring assignment of a dedicated MCC for firearms and ammunition businesses, the bill creates a consistent category for payment processing involving firearms merchants (6 Del. C. § 5002F(a)-(b)).  
- **Enforcement clarity**: Centralizes enforcement in the **Delaware DOJ** with explicit remedies, which may improve compliance consistency (6 Del. C. § 5003F(a), (d)).  
- **Opportunity to cure**: The **30-day cure** provision may reduce penalties for entities that promptly correct noncompliance and adopt internal policy changes (6 Del. C. § 5003F(b)-(c)).  
- **Deterrent effect**: A **$10,000 civil fine per violation** plus attorneys’ fees/costs and injunctive relief may incentivize compliance (6 Del. C. § 5003F(d)(2)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Compliance burden on payment ecosystem**: Payment card networks and merchant acquirers must implement and maintain MCC availability/assignment processes, with potential exposure to enforcement if misassigned or not assigned (6 Del. C. § 5002F; § 5003F).  
- **Legal and financial exposure**: Violations can lead to **$10,000 per violation** plus **attorneys’ fees and costs** and potential **injunctions**, which may be significant for entities processing many merchants/transactions (6 Del. C. § 5003F(d)(2)).  
- **No contractual flexibility**: Any waiver of the MCC requirements is **void** (6 Del. C. § 5002F(c)), limiting private contracting options.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “each violation”**: The statute sets **$10,000 for each violation** but does not define whether a “violation” is per merchant, per transaction, per day, or per failure event (6 Del. C. § 5003F(d)(2)a).  
- **Determining “highest sales value…expected to be”**: The definition relies on what the business states to its merchant acquirer “in the ordinary course of business,” but does not specify audit/verification standards or consequences for incorrect categorization statements (6 Del. C. § 5001F(4)).  
- **Interaction with successor MCCs**: The bill references the MCC established **September 9, 2022** “or the most recent successor” after that date, but does not specify how quickly updates must be adopted when successors are issued (6 Del. C. § 5001F(6)).  
- **Coverage of mixed merchants**: It is unclear how entities with diversified sales (where firearms-related products are not the “highest sales value”) should be treated for MCC assignment purposes (6 Del. C. § 5001F(4)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates new Chapter 50F (Title 6, Subpart II)** establishing definitions and obligations related to sales of **firearms, firearm accessories, and ammunition** in payment processing.  
- **Requires MCC availability**: Payment card networks must make the firearms/ammunition MCC available to merchant acquirers serving firearms merchants (6 Del. C. § 5002F(a)).  
- **Requires MCC assignment**: Merchant acquirers must assign the firearms/ammunition MCC to firearms merchants (6 Del. C. § 5002F(b)).  
- **Prohibits waiver**: Any waiver/attempted waiver of § 5002F requirements is void and unenforceable (6 Del. C. § 5002F(c)).  
- **Enforcement framework**: DOJ exclusive enforcement; mandatory **30-day pre-suit notice**; **30-day cure** option with documentation and policy-change attestation; civil remedies including **$10,000 per violation**, injunctions, and attorneys’ fees/costs (6 Del. C. § 5003F).

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware DOJ**: Receives explicit statutory authority and remedies to enforce MCC compliance statewide (6 Del. C. § 5003F).  
- **Payment system participants seeking uniform categorization**: Merchant acquirers and networks gain a mandated standard for classifying firearms and ammunition businesses (6 Del. C. § 5002F; § 5001F(6)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Payment card networks**: Must ensure MCC availability by the statutory deadline and face civil enforcement risk for noncompliance (6 Del. C. § 5002F(a); § 5003F).  
- **Merchant acquirers**: Must assign the MCC to firearms merchants and could face civil penalties and injunctive relief for violations (6 Del. C. § 5002F(b); § 5003F(d)).  
- **Firearms merchants (as defined)**: May experience operational/administrative impacts from MCC assignment requirements tied to their payment processing relationships (6 Del. C. § 5001F(4); § 5002F(b)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **By July 1, 2025**: Payment card networks must make available the firearms/ammunition MCC (6 Del. C. § 5002F(a); Section 2(1)).  
- **October 1, 2025**: “All other provisions” are effective (Section 2(2)), including the merchant acquirer assignment requirement and enforcement provisions.  
- **Enforcement notice/cure timing**: DOJ must provide written notice **at least 30 days** before filing suit; recipients have **30 days** from receipt to cure and provide required statements/documentation to avoid an action (6 Del. C. § 5003F(b)-(c)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements**
  - Payment card networks: must **make available** the required MCC for merchant acquirers serving firearms merchants (6 Del. C. § 5002F(a)).  
  - Merchant acquirers: must **assign** the required MCC to firearms merchants (6 Del. C. § 5002F(b)).  
  - Waivers are **void** (6 Del. C. § 5002F(c)).
- **Enforcement authority**
  - **Delaware DOJ has exclusive authority** to enforce the chapter (6 Del. C. § 5003F(a)).
- **Pre-suit process**
  - DOJ must provide **written notice at least 30 days** before bringing an action identifying violations (6 Del. C. § 5003F(b)).  
  - DOJ may not sue if the recipient **cures within 30 days** and provides: (i) cure attestation, (ii) supporting documentation, and (iii) attestation of internal policy changes to prevent recurrence (6 Del. C. § 5003F(c)).
- **Penalties/remedies upon court finding of violation**
  - **$10,000 civil fine for each violation** (6 Del. C. § 5003F(d)(2)a).  
  - **Injunctive relief** as the court deems necessary (6 Del. C. § 5003F(d)(2)b).  
  - **Reasonable attorneys’ fees and costs** incurred in investigating and bringing the action (6 Del. C. § 5003F(d)(2)c).
