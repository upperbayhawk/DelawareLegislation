## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Require counties (and, for local county school taxes, counties/school tax administrators) to refund or credit property tax overpayments that result from an assessment appeal, using a uniform threshold rule: mandatory refund at **$50 or more**, optional refund or credit when **under $50**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a clear rule for tax overpayments after a **real property assessment appeal** reduces assessed value for the **tax year the appeal was filed**. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- If the total overpayment of taxes collected by the county is **$50 or more**, the property owner **is entitled to a refund**. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- If the total overpayment is **less than $50**, the county **may** either **refund** the amount or **apply it as a credit** against a later tax bill for the same property. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- Aligns county “taxes paid in error” statute to state that overpayments found after an assessment appeal are handled under the **§8311** rule. (Title 9 §8618(b))
- Applies the same refund/credit rule to **local county school taxes** overpaid after an assessment appeal, by cross-referencing **Title 9 §8311**. (Title 14 §1921(b))
- Keeps existing procedures for refunds of taxes paid “through error or by mistake,” including written request requirements and **3-year** refund record retention. (Title 9 §8618(a); Title 14 §1921(a))
- Law enacted as **Chapter 133, Volume 85**, **approved August 12, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Predictable taxpayer remedy:** Establishes a clear entitlement to a refund when an assessment appeal produces an overpayment of **$50+**. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Administrative flexibility for small amounts:** For overpayments **< $50**, counties may choose refund or **credit**, potentially reducing transaction costs. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Consistency across county and school taxes:** Local county school tax overpayments after assessment appeals follow the same rule as county-collected taxes. (Title 14 §1921(b); Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Transparency/records:** Continues requirement to keep refund records for **at least 3 years** (county records subject to FOIA availability). (Title 9 §8618(a)(3); Title 14 §1921(a)(3))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential delay/deferral for small refunds:** When the overpayment is **less than $50**, the county may choose a **credit** instead of a refund, which may not provide immediate cash back to the taxpayer. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Reduced local revenues in affected years:** Requires refunds for qualifying overpayments (**$50+**) after successful assessment appeals, which can reduce county collections for that tax year. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Administrative processing burden:** Counties must process refunds/credits tied to assessment appeal outcomes and track them, including recordkeeping for **3 years**. (Title 9 §8311(d); Title 9 §8618(a)(3))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Timing of payment:** The bill specifies entitlement to refund/credit but does not state **when** the refund must be issued after the assessment appeal decision.  
- **Form of credit application:** For **< $50** overpayments, the bill allows a credit “to offset any subsequent tax obligation billed to that property,” but does not specify how long the credit remains valid or whether interest applies. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Scope of “taxes collected by the county”:** The bill links refunds to “any taxes collected by the county,” and separately addresses “local county school taxes,” but does not further define treatment for other pass-through levies if collected by the county. (Title 9 §8311(d); Title 14 §1921(b))

## Fiscal or Administrative Impact
- **Counties:** Must issue refunds for assessment-appeal overpayments of **$50 or more**; may issue refunds or apply credits for **< $50**. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Counties/school tax administration:** Must refund or credit **local county school taxes** overpaid after an assessment appeal under the same **$50** threshold rule. (Title 14 §1921(b))
- **Record retention:** Counties must keep refund records for **at least 3 years** and make them available under FOIA; school boards/receivers must keep refund records for **at least 3 years** open to public inspection during business hours. (Title 9 §8618(a)(3); Title 14 §1921(a)(3))

## Key Policy Changes
- **New threshold rule for assessment-appeal overpayments:**  
  - **$50 or more:** taxpayer **entitled to refund**.  
  - **Less than $50:** county **may** refund or **apply as credit** to future tax obligation for the property. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Conforming change for county refunds after assessment appeals:** County tax overpayments determined after an assessment appeal must be handled under **§8311**. (Title 9 §8618(b))
- **Conforming change for school taxes after assessment appeals:** Local county school tax overpayments determined after an assessment appeal must be handled under **§8311**. (Title 14 §1921(b))

## Who Benefits
- **Property owners/taxpayers** who successfully appeal an assessment and thereby **overpaid taxes** in the tax year the appeal was filed—especially those with overpayments of **$50 or more** (mandatory refund). (Title 9 §8311(d))
- Taxpayers with smaller overpayments (**< $50**) who may receive either a refund or a credit. (Title 9 §8311(d))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **County governments** (and potentially school tax recipients) due to required refunds for qualifying overpayments (**$50+**) following assessment appeals. (Title 9 §8311(d); Title 14 §1921(b))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Refund entitlement/obligation:** Counties must provide a refund for assessment-appeal overpayments of **$50 or more**; for **< $50**, counties must choose between refund or credit. (Title 9 §8311(d))
- **Refund request process (taxes paid in error/mistake):**
  - County: written request; approval by county tax collecting authority in writing; retain records **3 years**; FOIA availability. (Title 9 §8618(a))
