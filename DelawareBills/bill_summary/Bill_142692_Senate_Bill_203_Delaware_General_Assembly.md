## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To amend Title 9 of the Delaware Code to expressly authorize counties to set **different real property tax rates by reasonable property classification**, while requiring **uniform rates within each classification** consistent with **Delaware Constitution Article VIII, § 1**, and to clarify that this authority applies **retroactively and prospectively**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes a county governing body to adopt **different tax rates for real property** based on **county-adopted real property classifications** (9 Del. C. § 8002(a)(4)a.).
- Requires that any such classification-based rates be **uniform for all real property within the same classification** (9 Del. C. § 8002(a)(4)b.; Delaware Constitution Art. VIII, § 1).
- Defines “county tax rate” to include either a **single rate across all classes** or **classification-based rates** (9 Del. C. § 8002(a)(5)).
- Allows the **tax collection warrant form** to be modified to reflect **different rates for each class** when classification-based rates are used (9 Del. C. § 8005(a)(2)).
- Removes statutory wording implying **all real property must be taxed at the same rate** (amends 9 Del. C. § 8101(b)).
- Retains existing limits and procedures, including: **Kent County cap of $0.50 per $100 assessed value** (9 Del. C. § 8002(b)); post-reassessment revenue limit of **no more than 15%** above the prior year (excluding new construction) (9 Del. C. § 8002(c)); “rolled-back rate” computation (9 Del. C. § 8002(d)); and **15-day newspaper advertisement** requirement for increases over the rolled-back rate (9 Del. C. § 8002(e)).
- Applies to county tax rates adopted **before, on, or after** the Act’s effective date (Section 5).
- Approved **August 12, 2025** (Chapter 132, Volume 85).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Legal clarity/codification:** The Act codifies “longstanding common law recognition” that counties (and municipalities, per synopsis) may tax different classes of real property if classifications are reasonable and rates are uniform within each class (Synopsis; § 8002(a)(4)).
- **Administrative alignment:** Explicitly permits updating **tax collection warrant forms** to match multi-rate systems, supporting implementation if counties use classification-based rates (§ 8005(a)(2)).
- **Transparency retained after reassessment:** Keeps the requirement to disclose the **percent increase over the rolled-back rate** and to advertise it within **15 days** (§ 8002(e)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential for unequal tax burdens across classes:** By authorizing different rates by classification, the Act allows counties to shift tax burden among property classes (authority granted in § 8002(a)(4)a.), even though rates must be uniform within a class.
- **Retroactive application may affect expectations:** The Act applies to rates adopted **before** the effective date (Section 5), potentially impacting disputes or reliance on prior statutory wording (retroactivity stated explicitly).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **What classifications are permissible?** The Act allows “different real property classifications adopted by the county governing body” (§ 8002(a)(4)a.) but does not define classifications or specify standards for what is “reasonable” (reasonableness referenced in Synopsis, not codified as a detailed test).
- **Scope beyond counties:** The synopsis references “counties and municipalities,” but operative amendments are to **Title 9 (counties)**; municipal authority is not amended in this text.
- **Effective date text:** The bill text describes applicability relative to “the effective date of this Act” (Section 5) but does not itself state a standalone effective-date clause; the enrolled act shows approval date (Aug. 12, 2025).

## Key Policy Changes
- **New explicit authority for classification-based real property tax rates:** Counties may set **different real property tax rates** based on **county-adopted classifications** (§ 8002(a)(4)a.).
- **Uniformity requirement within each class:** Rates must be **uniform** for all property within the same classification (§ 8002(a)(4)b.).
- **Definition update:** “County tax rate” includes either a single rate or a classification-based rate structure (§ 8002(a)(5)).
- **Tax warrant form flexibility:** Warrant form may be changed to reflect different rates by real property class when used (§ 8005(a)(2)).
- **Conforming change to property-tax-rate language:** Removes language in § 8101(b) that “all [real] property … shall be taxed at the same rate,” aligning statute with classification-rate authority (§ 8101(b) as amended).
- **Applicability/retroactivity:** Applies to county tax rates adopted **before, on, or after** the Act’s effective date (Section 5).

## Who Benefits
- **County governments (governing bodies):** Gain explicit statutory authorization to use **different tax rates by real property classification** and to adjust collection documents accordingly (§ 8002(a)(4); § 8005(a)(2)).
- **Tax administration offices:** Clear authority to update **tax collection warrants** to match classification-based rate structures (§ 8005(a)(2)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Owners of property in classifications assigned higher rates:** If a county adopts classification-based rates, taxpayers in higher-taxed classes could pay more relative to other classes (enabled by § 8002(a)(4)a.; impacts depend on county rate-setting decisions).
- **Parties challenging past county tax rates:** Retroactive application to rates adopted **before** the effective date (Section 5) could disadvantage claims premised on prior statutory language.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approval date:** **August 12, 2025** (enacted as Chapter 132, Volume 85).
- **Retroactive/prospective applicability:** Applies to county tax rates adopted **before, on, or after** the Act’s effective date (Section 5).
- **Public notice deadline after reassessment:** Within **15 days of the meeting** at which the reassessment-related tax-rate ordinance is considered, the county must advertise the percentage increase in a newspaper of general circulation (§ 8002(e)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance—uniformity within classification:** If classification-based rates are adopted, they must be **uniform within each classification** (§ 8002(a)(4)b.).
- **Compliance—reassessment revenue limitation:** After a total reassessment becomes effective, the next fiscal year’s county property tax rate may not yield revenues **greater than 15%** above the prior year’s total county property taxes; **new construction initial assessments are excluded** from the calculation (§ 8002(c)).
- **Compliance—rolled-back rate disclosure and notice:** Upon total reassessment, the ordinance must state the **percent** the levied rate exceeds the **rolled-back rate**, and the county must advertise that percentage increase within **15 days** (§ 8002(d)-(e)).
