## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To incentivize construction and operation of up to three high-efficiency Combined-Cycle Gas Turbine (CCGT) electricity generation facilities in Delaware by creating a refundable, transferable per–megawatt-hour (MWh) corporate income tax credit (the Electricity Production Tax Credit, or EPTC), with additional bonus credits for certain siting and performance attributes.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new **Electricity Production Tax Credit (EPTC)** in **Title 30, Chapter 20, Subchapter XI** for qualifying **CCGT** facilities producing electricity.
- Credit applies only to facilities that **enter service between January 1, 2027, and January 1, 2038** and are **100 MW to 500 MW** in capacity.
- Establishes a **base refundable and transferable credit of $4 per MWh**, with up to **three $1 per MWh bonus credits** (brownfield/plant site, ≥55% efficiency, carbon capture).
- Eligibility requires **combined-cycle technology** and at least **50% efficiency**; a higher **55%** threshold triggers a bonus credit.
- Limits program to **no more than three facilities statewide** and caps credits at **$15 million per facility per tax year** (including value realized via sale/transfer).
- Credits are available for **10 years from commencing commercial operation**; unused credits may be **carried forward up to 10 years** and may be **sold/transferred for 10 years**.
- Requires **annual production and efficiency reports to the Public Service Commission (PSC)**; PSC, Division of Revenue, and DNREC must cooperate on oversight/compliance.
- Fiscal note: General Fund revenue loss is **indeterminable by year** (FY2027 and FY2028), but **maximum authorized credits** could reach **$45 million per year** statewide and **$450 million** over the program life (per fiscal note assumptions).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Encourages in-state dispatchable generation**: The bill’s synopsis states CCGT can provide “significant dispatchable power” and support “grid stability.”
- **Targets higher-efficiency facilities**: Requires **≥50% efficiency** and offers a **$1/MWh bonus at ≥55% efficiency**.
- **Incentivizes redevelopment of certain sites**: Provides **$1/MWh bonus** for facilities on an **industrial brownfield**, an **existing power plant site**, or a **former power plant site** that ceased operations **within 10 years** of construction start.
- **Promotes carbon capture integration**: Provides a **$1/MWh bonus** for integrating **carbon capture technology**.
- **Provides financing flexibility**: Credit is **refundable and transferable**, and unused credits can be **carried forward up to 10 years**, potentially increasing usability.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potentially significant General Fund revenue loss**: Fiscal note indicates maximum authorized credits of **$45 million per year** and up to **$450 million** over the life of the act (assuming credits are claimed for 10 years).
- **Benefits limited to a small number of projects**: Eligibility is restricted to **up to three** facilities, each **100–500 MW**, which may concentrate benefits among a few developers/operators.
- **Public subsidy for fossil-fueled generation may occur**: “Qualifying” facilities may be powered by **natural gas** (also hydrogen or landfill gas), and the bill explicitly includes natural gas combustion in the definition of qualifying facilities.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- **Definition/verification of “integrating carbon capture technology”**: The bill does not specify performance standards, capture rates, or verification criteria.
- **Measurement method for efficiency ratings**: The bill requires **minimum 50% efficiency** and a **55%** bonus threshold, but does not specify calculation methodology (e.g., HHV/LHV basis, testing protocol).
- **How “deliver electricity to Delaware consumers” is demonstrated**: Requirement is delivery “through the regional power grid or a microgrid,” but the bill does not state documentation standards.

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new **Electricity Production Tax Credit (EPTC)** subchapter in **Title 30**.
- Establishes a **$4 per MWh base credit** for qualifying electricity production from CCGT facilities.
- Creates three potential **$1 per MWh** bonus credits:
  1) Brownfield/plant-site siting (including a former plant site that ceased operations within **10 years** before construction start)  
  2) **≥55% efficiency**  
  3) **Carbon capture technology** integration
- Limits eligibility to **up to three** in-state CCGT facilities, **100–500 MW**, entering service **Jan 1, 2027–Jan 1, 2038**, meeting permitting/licensing requirements, delivering electricity to Delaware consumers, and operating at **≥50% efficiency**.
- Makes credits **refundable and transferable**, allows **10-year carryforward**, and provides credits for **10 years after commencing commercial operation**.
- Caps total credit value realized by an operator at **$15 million per tax year** per facility (including proceeds from sale/transfer).
- Requires **annual production and efficiency reports** to **PSC** and cross-agency oversight cooperation (PSC, Division of Revenue, DNREC).
