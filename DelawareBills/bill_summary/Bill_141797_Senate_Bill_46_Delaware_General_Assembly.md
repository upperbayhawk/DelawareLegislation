## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 21 to define key autonomous-vehicle terms and to require a **human safety operator** to be **physically present inside certain heavy autonomous vehicles** operating on Delaware highways for testing or commercial/passenger use, with fines for violations and a future DOT report requirement.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines **“autonomous vehicle”** as SAE J3016 **Levels 3, 4, or 5** (as existed **July 2023**), and distinguishes these from driver-assistance features (e.g., adaptive cruise control, lane keep assist) that do **not** qualify.  
- Defines **“human safety operator”** as an individual **physically inside** the vehicle with immediate access to steering/braking/acceleration controls, who must hold a **commercial driver license (CDL)** valid for the vehicle.  
- Prohibits operation on Delaware highways (for **testing**, **transporting goods**, or **transporting passengers**) of an autonomous vehicle that requires a **Class A CDL without an “O” restriction**, unless a **human safety operator is physically present** in the vehicle.  
- Establishes civil fines for violations: **$1,000–$3,000** for the first offense.  
- Increases penalties for repeat violations within **3 years**: **$3,000–$7,500** for each subsequent offense.  
- Creates a presumption that the **registered owner** is the violator subject to the fines.  
- Requires (by **January 1, 2030**, and **upon appropriation**) a Delaware DOT report evaluating performance and impacts on **public safety** and **transportation-sector employment**, including **disengagements** and **crashes**, and recommending whether to remove/modify/maintain the in-vehicle operator requirement.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Public safety safeguard for heavy vehicles:** Requires a trained, in-vehicle operator (with a CDL) for autonomous vehicles needing **Class A CDL without an O restriction**, potentially reducing risk during testing and operations (bill imposes presence requirement and DOT safety evaluation mandate).
- **Accountability via defined penalties:** Establishes clear fine ranges (**$1,000–$3,000**; repeat **$3,000–$7,500 within 3 years**) and presumes responsibility on the **registered owner**, which may support enforceability.
- **Future evidence-based policy review:** Mandates a DOT report by **01/01/2030** (if funded) including **disengagements** and **crashes**, plus consultation with safety, labor, infrastructure, and independent experts to inform whether the requirement should continue.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits driverless deployment for certain vehicles:** The bill prohibits operation without an in-vehicle human safety operator for autonomous vehicles requiring **Class A CDL without an O restriction**, which may constrain fully driverless testing or commercial operations for that category.
- **Potential compliance burden for operators/owners:** Requires staffing an in-vehicle CDL-qualified human safety operator for covered operations (testing, goods, passengers) on Delaware highways.
- **Registered-owner presumption may be contentious:** The person subject to fines is presumed to be the **registered owner**, which may penalize owners even when another party operated the vehicle (as written).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds/updates definitions in Title 21:
  - **Autonomous technology:** capability to drive without active physical control or monitoring by a human safety operator.
  - **Autonomous vehicle:** motor vehicle with integrated autonomous tech meeting **SAE J3016 Levels 3–5 (July 2023 version)**; excludes vehicles with only collision-avoidance/driver-assistance features listed in the bill.
  - **Human safety operator:** in-vehicle individual with immediate access to controls; must hold a **CDL valid for the vehicle**.
- Creates a new operational restriction (§ 4199D):
  - Covered autonomous vehicles (those that require a **Class A CDL without an O restriction**) may not be operated on Delaware highways for **testing**, **transporting goods**, or **transporting passengers** **without** an in-vehicle human safety operator.
- Establishes fines and repeat-offense structure:
  - First offense: **$1,000–$3,000**
  - Subsequent offense within **3 years**: **$3,000–$7,500**
  - Presumed violator for fines: **registered owner**
- Requires DOT report by **01/01/2030** (if appropriated), including consultations with:
  - Department of Safety and Homeland Security (traffic impacts; safety risks; impacts on motorists, bicyclists, pedestrians)
  - Department of Labor (job impacts; displacement; retraining shortcomings)
  - Independent experts (technology performance; public safety impacts)
  - Consideration of infrastructure needs/deficiencies
  - Recommendation to remove/modify/maintain the human safety operator requirement
