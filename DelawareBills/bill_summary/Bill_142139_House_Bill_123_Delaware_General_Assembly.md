## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To create a new Delaware criminal offense (Title 11) prohibiting knowingly or intentionally discharging a firearm **towards** certain protected locations—adding specific coverage for **schools** and **institutions of higher education**—and to classify the offense as a **Class E felony**, while preventing duplicative convictions for the same conduct under reckless endangering statutes.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates new crime in **Title 11, § 1460A**: **“Discharging a firearm towards a dwelling, place of worship, vehicle, place of business, school, or institution of higher learning.”**
- Prohibits **knowingly or intentionally** discharging a firearm **towards**: a **dwelling**, **place of worship**, **vehicle**, **place of business**, **school**, or **institution of higher education**.
- Defines **“dwelling”** by cross-reference to **11 Del. C. § 829**.
- Defines **“place of business”** as a **permanent physical structure** marked with **signage** where a legal enterprise operates.
- Explicitly **excludes shooting ranges** (and similar businesses whose purpose is lawful firearm practice/use) from the “place of business” definition.
- Sets penalty level: violation is a **Class E felony**.
- Bars dual convictions for the same conduct: a person may not be convicted of both this offense and **reckless endangering 1st degree** or **reckless endangering 2nd degree** for the same conduct.
- Act was **approved August 20, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Adds a specific felony offense** targeted at firearm discharge **towards** sensitive places (including **schools** and **institutions of higher education**), potentially improving prosecutorial charging options. (Creates § 1460A; adds items (5) and (6).)
- **Clarifies scope for businesses** and reduces unintended coverage by **excluding shooting ranges** and similar lawful-use venues. (§ 1460A(b)(2).)
- **Reduces duplicative punishment risk** by prohibiting convictions under both § 1460A and reckless endangering (1st or 2nd) for the same conduct. (§ 1460A(d).)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Felony expansion**: Creates a new **Class E felony** offense, which may increase felony prosecutions and related consequences (criminal records, incarceration exposure). (§ 1460A(c).)
- **No further statutory definitions provided** for “school” or “institution of higher education” within the text, which could increase reliance on interpretation or other code sections. (§ 1460A(a)(5)-(6) without definitions.)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates 11 Del. C. § 1460A** establishing a new crime for **knowingly or intentionally discharging a firearm towards**:
  1) a dwelling; 2) a place of worship; 3) a vehicle; 4) a place of business; **5) a school; 6) an institution of higher education**. (§ 1460A(a).)
- **Defines “place of business”** as a signed, permanent physical structure where a legal enterprise operates; **excludes shooting ranges** and similar lawful firearm-use venues. (§ 1460A(b)(2).)
- Sets the offense as a **Class E felony**. (§ 1460A(c).)
- **Anti-duplication rule**: prohibits conviction for both § 1460A and **reckless endangering 1st** or **reckless endangering 2nd** for the same conduct. (§ 1460A(d).)

## Who Benefits
- Residents and occupants of **dwellings** and patrons/participants at **places of worship**, and people associated with **schools** and **institutions of higher education**, to the extent the new felony offense is used to address firearm discharges directed toward these locations. (§ 1460A(a).)
- **Shooting ranges** and similar lawful firearm-use businesses, which are expressly excluded from the “place of business” category for this offense. (§ 1460A(b)(2).)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Individuals charged with or convicted of discharging a firearm **towards** covered locations, because the conduct is classified as a **Class E felony**. (§ 1460A(c).)
- Prosecutors may be constrained from obtaining multiple convictions for the same conduct due to the **bar on dual convictions** with reckless endangering (1st or 2nd). (§ 1460A(d).)
