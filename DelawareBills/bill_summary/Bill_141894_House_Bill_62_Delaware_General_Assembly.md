## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Titles 26 and 22 of the Delaware Code to expand and standardize protections for residential customers against termination of utility services (gas, electric, water, and wastewater in Title 26; municipal electric service in Title 22), including new weather-based shutoff restrictions, enhanced notice/contact requirements, medical certification protections, and civil penalty enforcement.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates defined **“heating season” (Nov. 1–Mar. 31)** and **“cooling season” (June 1–Sept. 30)** for termination rules.  
- Requires at least **72 hours’ notice** before terminating residential utility service for nonpayment (with additional notice requirements during heating/cooling seasons).  
- Limits shutoffs (absent a safety-related emergency) to **8:00 A.M.–4:00 P.M., Monday–Thursday**; also adjusts rules around weekends/holidays.  
- Prohibits gas/electric (and municipal electric) shutoffs for nonpayment when **8:00 A.M. temperature ≤ 35°F** (within **50 miles**) or when forecast indicates **Heat Index ≥ 95°F** (within **50 miles**).  
- During heating or cooling season, requires **14 calendar days’ written notice (First Class mail)** before termination and mandates specified content in the notice (payment plans, assistance, dispute rights, medical deferral).  
- Adds required customer contact attempts prior to shutoff: **3 documented attempts on separate days** during heating season (including **1 after 5:00 P.M.**) and **≥1 documented attempt** during cooling season (phone or customer-preferred method).  
- Bars heating-season shutoffs for nonpayment if an occupant receives **LIHEAP, TANF, SSI, or Purchase of Care (POC)** benefits.  
- Establishes/updates enforcement with **civil penalties up to $1,000 per violation per day** (PSC for jurisdictional utilities; municipalities for municipal electric companies) and requires municipalities to adopt ordinances/regulations to enforce.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces risk of dangerous shutoffs during extreme weather** by prohibiting termination at **≤35°F** and **Heat Index ≥95°F** (both based on National Weather Service, within **50 miles**).  
- **Improves due process and customer awareness** through **14-day First Class mail notice** with required disclosures (payment arrangements, dispute protections, and assistance referrals).  
- **Protects medically vulnerable residents** by preventing termination when illness is certified; certification lasts **120 days**, with renewals conditioned on a **“good faith effort to make payments.”**  
- **Protects low-income households in winter** by barring heating-season termination for households with occupants receiving **LIHEAP, TANF, SSI, or POC**.  
- **Extends protections to municipal electric customers** by applying similar termination rules to municipal electric companies and requiring municipal enforcement mechanisms.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **May increase utility/municipal administrative burden** due to mandated additional notices, documentation of contact attempts, deferral notices, and dispute processes (including continued service during disputes).  
- **May delay collections and increase arrearages** because shutoffs are restricted by: (1) time-of-day/day-of-week limits, (2) heating/cooling season procedures, (3) extreme-weather deferrals, (4) illness certifications, and (5) benefit-recipient protections.  
- **Potential compliance complexity** from multiple overlapping notice standards (e.g., **72-hour notice** plus **14-day written notice** during heating/cooling seasons) and differing enforcement bodies (PSC vs. municipalities).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Good faith effort to make payments”**: The bill allows the **Delaware Public Service Commission (PSC)** to define this term by regulation for PSC-jurisdiction utilities, but the text does not specify the standard itself; municipal electric provisions require a dispute process but do not define “good faith” criteria in detail.  
- **Dispute resolution forum for municipal electric/Title 22**: Title 26 allows PSC petition/review for PSC-jurisdiction utilities; Title 22 requires the electric company to create a dispute resolution process, but the scope/structure is not detailed.  
- **Interaction of weekend/holiday timing rules**: Title 26 retains a weekend-related provision and adds the Monday–Thursday 8 A.M.–4 P.M. limitation; how these provisions integrate in all scenarios is not fully clarified in the text.

## Health Impact
- Prohibits termination when an occupant is **so ill that termination would adversely affect health or recovery**, upon receipt of a signed statement from a **licensed physician, physician assistant, or advanced nurse practitioner**; statements are effective for **120 days**.  
- Limits shutoffs during **cold (≤35°F)** and **high-heat (Heat Index ≥95°F)** conditions, which directly relates to occupant health and safety.  
- Requires, **when possible**, advance notice to any known **case manager or coordinator** of an occupant in an affected dwelling unit.

## Key Policy Changes
1. **Season definitions added/standardized**:  
   - **Cooling season:** **June 1–September 30**  
   - **Heating season:** **November 1–March 31**
2. **Time-of-day/day-of-week shutoff limits (non-emergency)**:  
   - No suspension/turn-off **outside 8:00 A.M.–4:00 P.M., Monday–Thursday**; includes holiday substitution rules (e.g., if Monday is a holiday, substitute next business day; if Thursday/Friday holiday, substitute earlier business days as stated).
3. **Extreme weather shutoff prohibitions for gas/electric and municipal electric**:  
   - No termination if **8:00 A.M. temperature ≤ 35°F** (NWS, within **50 miles**).  
   - No termination if NWS forecast indicates **Heat Index may be ≥ 95°F** (within **50 miles**).  
   - If deferred, **deferral notice must be left** at the dwelling unit that day; for master-metered complexes, notice to **all occupants** in the affected building complex.
4. **Heating-season protections tied to benefits**:  
   - No heating-season termination for nonpayment if an occupant receives **LIHEAP, TANF, SSI, or POC**.
5. **Expanded notice and contact requirements during heating/cooling seasons (gas/electric and municipal electric)**:  
   - **14 calendar days’ written notice** to an adult occupant (and to both billing and service addresses if different).  
   - **Heating season:** **≥3 documented contact attempts** on separate days (one after **5:00 P.M.**).  
   - **Cooling season:** **≥1 documented contact attempt**.  
   - Notice must be **First Class mail** and include: earliest termination date (≥14 days from mailing), dispute protections, installment agreement/payment plan info (per Commission regulations for Title 26), assistance referrals (including LIHEAP), and medical deferral information.
6. **Medical certification and dispute protections**:  
   - Medical statements effective **120 days**; renewals require **good faith payment effort**.  
   - For PSC-jurisdiction utilities, disputes can be petitioned to the **PSC** and service must continue while pending final adjudication.  
   - For municipal electric, the electric company must create a **dispute resolution process** and continue service while the dispute is pending.
7. **Third-party notification programs**:  
   - Gas/electric utilities (Title 26) and all electric companies (Title 22) must maintain a **voluntary third-party notification program**; third parties are **not liable** by accepting that role.
8. **Civil penalties and municipal enforcement (Title 22)**:  
   - **PSC** may assess **civil penalty up to $1,000 per violation**; **each day** is a separate violation (Title 26).  
   - **Contracting municipalities** must adopt ordinances/regulations and may assess **civil penalty up to $1,000 per violation per day** (Title 22).

## Who Benefits
- **Residential occupants of dwelling units** receiving gas/electric (and municipal electric) service who face termination for nonpayment.  
- **Medically vulnerable occupants** needing continued service, supported by the **120-day** medical certification protection.  
- **Low-income households during heating season** where an occupant receives **LIHEAP, TANF, SSI, or POC**.  
- **Residents of master-metered complexes** who gain explicit requirements for notice to all occupants when termination is proposed.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Utilities and municipal electric providers**, due to increased compliance obligations (notice, contact documentation, dispute handling, deferral procedures) and restricted termination windows.  
- **Municipalities operating/contracting with municipal electric companies**, due to required adoption of enforcement ordinances/regulations and administration of enforcement.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** The Act takes effect **30 days after enactment into law**.  
- **Notice deadlines:**  
  - At least **72 hours’ notice** before termination for nonpayment (general rule).  
  - During heating/cooling season, written notice must be mailed at least **14 calendar days** before termination (and the earliest termination date must be **no less than 14 calendar days from mailing**).  
- **Medical certification duration:** Signed medical statements are effective for **120 days**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Penalty amounts:** Civil penalty **up to $1,000 for each violation**; **each day of continued violation is a separate violation** (PSC for Title 26 utilities; municipalities for municipal electric enforcement under Title 22).  
- **Compliance requirements include**:
  - Maintain **voluntary third-party notification** program (gas/electric and municipal electric contexts).  
  - Provide required **First Class mail** notices with mandated content.  
  - Make and **document** required pre-termination contact attempts (3 in heating season; 1 in cooling season).  
  - Observe **time-of-day/day-of-week** termination limits (8 A.M.–4 P.M., Monday–Thursday; non-emergency).  
  - Follow **extreme weather** termination prohibitions and provide **deferral notices** (including notice to all occupants in certain master-metered settings).  
  - Honor **medical certification** protections and provide **case manager/coordinator notice when possible**.  
  - Maintain service during specified disputes (PSC adjudication pending under Title 26; company dispute process pending under Title 22).
