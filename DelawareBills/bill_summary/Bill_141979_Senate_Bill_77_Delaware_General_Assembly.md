## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Establish a Delaware Department of Finance (Department) directory of electronic smoking device and nicotine vapor product manufacturers/products eligible for sale in Delaware, and prohibit sales of products not listed in the directory, with fees, compliance checks, and penalties to support enforcement.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **manufacturers** of electronic smoking devices and nicotine vapor products sold in Delaware to submit an **annual certification** to the Department **by August 1, 2025, and annually thereafter**, under **penalty of perjury**, showing federal FDA premarket authorization status.  
- Certification must show either: (1) an **FDA marketing granted order** (under **21 U.S.C. § 387j**), or (2) a **timely filed premarket tobacco product application (PMTA)** that is **under FDA review** or has a **stayed/rescinded/vacated** denial order.  
- Defines “**timely filed PMTA**” as a PMTA for products marketed in the U.S. as of **August 8, 2016**, submitted to FDA **on or before September 9, 2020**, and **accepted for filing**.  
- Imposes manufacturer fees: **$250 per product** the first time the product is submitted, then **$100 per product annually thereafter**.  
- Directs the Department to publish a **public online directory starting October 1, 2025** and **update it at least monthly**, with a notice process for retailers/distributors/wholesalers.  
- Starting **October 1, 2025** (or the date the directory is first posted, if later), **products not listed may not be sold** in Delaware, subject to specified **sell-through/removal windows** (generally **60 days** after directory publication).  
- Creates **civil penalties** for selling non-listed products (**$500 per product**, escalating for repeat violations) and much higher penalties for manufacturers (**$10,000 per product**), plus potential **Class B misdemeanor** for false certification statements.  
- Requires **at least 2 unannounced compliance checks annually** of wholesalers/distributors/retailers, **follow-up checks within 30 days** for noncompliance, and annual public reporting of results; also requires an annual legislative report starting **January 31, 2026**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Limits Delaware sales to federally reviewed/authorized products** by conditioning directory listing on an **FDA marketing granted order** or a qualifying **PMTA status** (under **21 U.S.C. § 387j**).  
- **Creates a clear, public compliance tool** (online directory, **monthly updates**) for retailers and distributors to verify which products may be sold.  
- **Establishes recurring enforcement activity** (minimum **2 unannounced compliance checks annually**, plus **30-day follow-ups**) and public transparency (annual publication of compliance check results; annual legislative report starting **01/31/2026**).  
- **Provides dedicated funding mechanism** by directing that **all fees and penalties** be used for **administration and enforcement** of the directory system.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Adds compliance costs** for manufacturers through per-product fees (**$250 first-time; $100 annually**) and documentation requirements (FDA orders/letters/court documents).  
- **Creates significant penalty exposure** for supply chain actors: retailers/distributors/wholesalers/importers face **$500 per product** penalties (with escalation to **$750–$1,000** plus **30-day license suspension**, and then **$1,000–$1,500** plus **license revocation** for a third violation within **12 months**).  
- **Products can be seized/forfeited/destroyed**, with **costs borne by the person from whom products are confiscated**, after applicable grace periods, creating potential inventory losses.  
- **Nonresident/foreign manufacturers** that are not registered to do business in Delaware must appoint an in-state **agent for service of process** and post a **$25,000 surety bond/cash security** to be listed/retained, adding barriers to market access.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/expanded definitions** in Title 30 include “FDA,” “nicotine vapor product,” “vapor product,” and “timely filed premarket tobacco product application,” including the key federal dates **August 8, 2016** and **September 9, 2020** for “timely filed PMTA.”  
- Creates/implements an **Electronic Smoking Device and Nicotine Vapor Product Directory** (§ 5372):  
  - **Manufacturer annual certification** due **by August 1, 2025**, and annually thereafter, under **penalty of perjury**.  
  - Certification must list **brand name, product name, category**, and **flavor** for each product sold in Delaware.  
  - Requires supporting documentation (FDA marketing granted order, FDA acceptance letter, or FDA/court documentation of stayed/rescinded/vacated denial).  
  - Imposes annual per-product fees: **$250** initially per product; **$100** annually thereafter.  
  - Treats certain FDA documentation as **confidential/proprietary** and **FOIA-exempt** (Title 29, Ch. 100), with permitted redactions.  
  - Requires notice of material changes **within 30 days** (e.g., FDA authorization/denial or court/FDA action affecting ability to market).  
  - Public directory required **starting October 1, 2025**; updated **at least monthly**.  
- **Sales prohibition**: beginning **10/01/2025** (or later directory posting date), products **not listed** may not be sold in Delaware, with **60-day** initial sell-through/removal windows after directory publication.  
- **Removal process**: Department must provide notice; cannot remove until **at least 30 days** after notice; manufacturer has **15 days** to cure; retailers get **30 days** after removal to sell inventory held as of removal date.  
- **Seizure/forfeiture/destruction**: after the applicable **30-day** (post-removal) or **60-day** (post-directory-publication) windows, noncompliant products may be seized/forfeited/destroyed at the confiscated-from person’s expense.  
- **Penalties**: escalating civil penalties for retailers/distributors/wholesalers/importers; **$10,000 per product** civil penalty for manufacturers selling non-listed products; **Class B misdemeanor** per false representation on certification.  
- **Nonresident manufacturer requirements**: in-state **agent for service of process** and **$25,000 bond** (if not registered as a foreign corporation/business entity), with detailed notice timing for agent changes and bond surety release rules (including a **60-day** surety discharge timeline).  
- Requires Department rulemaking authority and dedicates **all fees/penalties** to administration/enforcement.

## Who Benefits
- Consumers and the general public: benefit indirectly from a system limiting sales to products with documented **FDA marketing authorization or qualifying PMTA status** (as required for directory listing).  
- Compliant manufacturers and compliant retailers/distributors/wholesalers: benefit from a clearer market rule and a public directory intended to distinguish eligible from ineligible products.  
- Department of Finance: receives dedicated fee/penalty funding for administration and enforcement (fees/penalties must be used for that purpose).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Manufacturers whose products are not FDA-authorized or do not meet the “timely filed PMTA” criteria (including the **08/08/2016** and **09/09/2020** thresholds) may be excluded from Delaware sales via directory non-listing.  
- Retailers/distributors/wholesalers/importers holding inventory not listed in the directory may face **sell-through/removal deadlines**, **seizure/forfeiture/destruction**, and **per-product civil penalties**.  
- Nonresident/foreign manufacturers not registered to do business in Delaware: must appoint an in-state agent and post a **$25,000 bond/cash security** to be listed/retained.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **August 1, 2025**: Manufacturer certification due to the Department (and **annually thereafter**).  
- **Within 30 days**: Manufacturer must notify the Department of any **material change** to certification (e.g., FDA authorization/denial, court/FDA action affecting marketing).  
- **October 1, 2025**: Department must maintain and publicly post the directory starting this date; sales restrictions begin **October 1, 2025**, **or** the date the directory is first publicly available, **whichever is later**.  
- **60 days after** the directory is first made publicly available:  
  - Retailers must sell through/remove non-listed inventory.  
  - Distributors/wholesalers must remove non-listed products intended for Delaware sale from inventory.  
  - After the 60 days, non-listed products intended for sale become subject to **seizure/forfeiture/destruction** (with costs borne by the confiscated-from person).  
- Directory removal process timelines:  
  - Department must give notice and may not remove until **at least 30 days** after notice.  
  - Manufacturer has **15 days** from service of notice to cure/justify inclusion.  
  - Retailers have **30 days following removal** to sell inventory held as of removal date.  
  - After **30 days following removal**, products are subject to **seizure/forfeiture/destruction** (costs borne by confiscated-from person).  
- Nonresident agent changes:  
  - Manufacturer must notify the Department **30 calendar days prior** to terminating an agent.  
  - Must provide proof of new agent appointment **no less than 5 calendar days prior** to termination.  
  - If agent terminates, manufacturer must notify the Department **within 5 calendar days** and provide proof of new agent.  
- Bond surety release: surety released **after 60 days** from written request to the Department (does not relieve already-accrued liability); if no replacement bond filed by the end of that **60-day** period, the Department must remove the manufacturer/products from the directory.  
- **January 31, 2026**, and annually thereafter: Department report to the legislature on directory status, listed manufacturers/products, revenue/expenditures for administration, and enforcement activities.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements**:
  - Annual manufacturer certification under **penalty of perjury**; detailed product listing (brand/product/category/flavor) and supporting FDA/court documentation; fee payments (**$250 initial / $100 annual per product**).  
  - Nonresident/foreign unregistered manufacturers must appoint an in-state **agent for service of process** and post a **$25,000 bond/cash security** to be listed/retained.  
  - Covered wholesalers/distributors/retailers subject to at least **2 unannounced compliance checks annually**; **follow-up within 30 days** for noncompliance; annual publication of results.  
- **Penalties**:
  - Retailer/distributor/wholesaler/importer selling non-listed products: **$500 civil penalty per product** until removed or properly listed.  
    - 2nd violation within **12 months**: **$750–$1,000 per product** + **30-day license suspension**.  
    - 3rd violation within **12 months**: **$1,000–$1,500 per product** + **license revocation**.  
  - Manufacturer selling non-listed products: **$10,000 civil penalty per product** until removed or properly listed.  
  - Manufacturer false representations on certification: **Class B misdemeanor** for each false representation.  
  - Repeat violation within **12 months**: constitutes a **deceptive trade practice** under **Title 6, Chapter 25**.  
  - In enforcement actions, the Department may recover **costs**, including **investigation costs, expert witness fees, and reasonable attorney fees**.  
- **Product enforcement**:
  - Noncompliant products may be **seized, forfeited, and destroyed** after applicable grace periods; **costs** are borne by the person from whom products are confiscated.  
- **Rulemaking**:
  - Department may promulgate rules necessary to carry out § 5372.  
- **Use of funds**:
  - **All fees and penalties** collected must be used for **administration and enforcement** of the section.
