## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Establish a new Delaware licensing and regulatory framework for Earned Wage Access (EWA) services providers under the Office of the State Bank Commissioner by creating a new Chapter 26 in Title 5.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new **EWA provider license** administered by the **State Bank Commissioner** for any person transacting earned wage access services in Delaware (with specified exclusions and a carve-out for banking organizations and credit unions).
- Sets **application and licensing fees**: **$250 nonrefundable investigation fee** and **$1,500 annual license fee**; licenses **expire December 31** each year.
- Requires **background checks via fingerprints** for certain individuals associated with applicants.
- Establishes consumer protection operating rules, including **clear fee disclosures**, **free cancellation**, and a **required no-cost option** when a fee is offered or a tip is solicited.
- Prohibits specified collection practices (e.g., **no lawsuits**, **no outbound collection calls**, **no third-party debt collectors**, **no sale to debt buyers**) for repayment of outstanding proceeds/fees/tips, with an exception for **fraud or unlawful means**.
- Bars providers from charging **interest, late fees, deferral fees, or other penalties** for failure to repay.
- Requires **reimbursement of overdraft/NSF fees** caused by provider collection attempts that occur **before** or in an **incorrect amount** compared to what was disclosed (with a fraud exception).
- Clarifies EWA (when offered in accordance with this chapter) is **not a loan/credit**, **not money transmission**, and related fees/tips/donations are **not interest/finance charges**; the chapter **controls in conflicts** with other state law.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **New oversight and examination authority**: Providers become subject to the Commissioner’s supervision and examinations when deemed necessary (§ 2612), potentially improving compliance and consumer protection.
- **Upfront fee transparency**: Providers must “fully and clearly disclose” all provider-imposed fees or fee schedules before entering an agreement (§ 2609(b)(2)).
- **No-cost option requirement**: If a provider offers proceeds for a fee or solicits tips/donations, it must offer **at least 1 reasonable option** to obtain the same proceeds **at no cost** and explain how to select it (§ 2609(e)).
- **Reduced consumer harm from overdrafts**: Providers must reimburse consumers for **overdraft/NSF fees** caused by improper collection timing/amount compared to disclosure (§ 2609(h)(2)).
- **Limits on collections and penalties**: Prohibits lawsuits, outbound collection calls, third-party collectors, debt sales, and prohibits late fees/interest/penalties (§ 2610(a), § 2610(e)).
- **Regulatory clarity**: Expressly states compliant EWA is not a loan/credit/debt or money transmission and that fees/tips are not interest/finance charges (§ 2617).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **New compliance costs for providers**: Providers must obtain a license, pay fees (**$250** investigation; **$1,500** annual), and meet recordkeeping, examination, and bonding/LOC requirements (§ 2603; §§ 2612–2615; § 2608).
- **Bond/letter of credit requirement**: Commissioner-set surety bond or irrevocable letter of credit up to **$35,000** may increase costs and barriers to entry (§ 2608).
- **Restrictions on repayment/collections tools**: Prohibitions on lawsuits, outbound calls, third-party collectors, and debt sales restrict enforcement options for non-fraud nonpayment (§ 2610(a)).
- **Operational constraints around tips**: Providers must implement specific tip disclosures and cannot mislead about tip voluntariness or claim tips benefit a specific individual (§ 2609(i)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date**: The bill text provided does not state when Chapter 26 takes effect. (Not specified in source text.)
- **Bond/LOC amount setting**: The Commissioner determines the bond/LOC amount (cap **$35,000**) using factors including “dollar value” of Delaware business, but precise thresholds are not specified (§ 2608).
- **Definition of “1 reasonable option”**: The bill requires at least “1 reasonable option” for a no-cost proceeds delivery but does not define “reasonable” (§ 2609(e)).
- **Scope of “fraudulent or other unlawful means” exception**: The bill allows more collection avenues when amounts were incurred through fraud/unlawful means but does not define the standards or process for determining fraud (§ 2610(a); § 2609(h)(2)).
- **Commissioner exemption authority**: The Commissioner may exempt persons or classes from provisions of the chapter, with procedures potentially set by regulation (§ 2602(b)); criteria are general (“inappropriate to include” to effectuate purposes).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates new Title 5, Part IV, Chapter 26** regulating “Earned Wage Access Services Providers.”
- **Licensing requirement** for persons transacting EWA services in Delaware; excludes **banking organizations and credit unions** (§ 2602(a)).
- **Commissioner exemption authority** by regulation/procedure (§ 2602(b)).
- **Application requirements** include business location/online statement, principals/officers info, activity descriptions, and **fingerprints** for criminal background checks (§ 2603(a)).
- **Fees**:
  - **$250** nonrefundable investigation fee (§ 2603(a)).
  - **$1,500** annual license fee; **no abatement** if issued for less than a year or surrendered/revoked early; license expires **December 31** (§ 2603(b)).
- **Decision timeline**: Commissioner must approve/deny within **90 days** after determining the application is complete (§ 2604).
- **Change notices**: Licensee must notify the Commissioner “forthwith” of officer/partner/director changes (§ 2605).
- **Conditional approval after change of control**: Up to **60 days** to operate under existing license if a new application is filed (§ 2606(b)).
- **Renewal timing**: Renewal application must be filed at least **30 days before** expiration; may be treated as new if late; renewal may be refused for noncompliance with supervisory letters or nonpayment of supervisory assessment/exam fees (§ 2607).
- **Consumer operating requirements** (selected):
  - Consumer complaint/inquiry policies (§ 2609(a)(1)).
  - Clear rights and fee disclosures before agreement (§ 2609(b)).
  - Notice of material changes before implementation (§ 2609(c)).
  - Cancel anytime with **no cancellation fee** (§ 2609(d)).
  - No-cost option requirement when fees/tips are offered/solicited (§ 2609(e)).
  - Privacy and information security compliance (§ 2609(f)).
  - Overdraft/NSF reimbursement for certain provider-caused fees (§ 2609(h)(2)).
  - Tip voluntariness disclosures and restrictions on tip representations (§ 2609(i)).
- **Prohibitions** (selected):
  - No collections via lawsuit/outbound calls/third-party collectors/debt sale (fraud/unlawful exception) (§ 2610(a)).
  - Cannot share consumer-paid fees/tips/donations with employer (employer payments under employer-provider agreement not a violation) (§ 2610(b)).
  - Cannot require a credit score (§ 2610(c)).
  - Cannot accept repayment via credit card/other credit (§ 2610(d)).
  - Cannot impose interest, late fees, deferral fees, or other penalties for nonpayment (§ 2610(e)).
- **Surety bond or letter of credit** required, set by Commissioner, capped at **$35,000** (§ 2608).
- **Recordkeeping/examination** authority and requirements (§§ 2612–2615).
- **Multi-state automated licensing system participation** authorized; may add system-related fees/education/testing by regulation (§ 2616).
- **Legal characterization**: Compliant EWA is not a loan/credit/debt, not money transmission; fees/tips are not interest/finance charges; chapter controls if conflict with other state law (§ 2617).

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware consumers** using EWA services, through mandated disclosures, no-cost option availability, free cancellation, limits on penalties/collections, and overdraft/NSF reimbursement rules (§ 2609; § 2610).
- **Licensed EWA providers** by receiving a defined regulatory pathway and explicit statutory treatment that EWA is not a loan/credit or money transmission when compliant (§ 2617).
- **State of Delaware** through projected minimum fee revenue of **$35,000** (fee impact sheet) and regulatory oversight funding via revolving fund and general fund deposits.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **EWA providers** that must obtain licensure, pay fees, maintain bond/LOC (up to **$35,000**), undergo exams, maintain records, and comply with operational requirements and prohibitions (§§ 2603, 2608, 2609–2615).
- **Providers’ business models** relying on certain collection methods (lawsuits, outbound calls, third-party collectors, debt sales) or late fees/interest are restricted (§ 2610(a), § 2610(e)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Bill effective date**: Not specified in source text.
- **Application decision deadline**: Commissioner must approve/deny within **90 days** after determining the application is complete (§ 2604).
- **License expiration**: **December 31** each year (§ 2603(b)).
- **Renewal application deadline**: At least **30 days prior** to license expiration (§ 2607).
- **Conditional approval after change of control**: Up to **60 days** to operate under existing license if new application filed (§ 2606(b)).
- **Bond cancellation notice**: Surety cancellation not effective unless notice filed **at least 30 days before** cancellation date (§ 2608(a)(3)).
- **Surety claim payment timing**: Insurer pays claims to the Office of the State Bank Commissioner **no later than 90 days** after receipt; late payments accrue **daily interest at the legal rate** (§ 2608(a)(2)d).
- **Commissioner claim submission window**: **2 calendar years** after bond cancellation/termination effective date to submit claims (§ 2608(a)(6)); similarly may refuse release of letter of credit up to **2 years** after termination of licensure (§ 2608(b)(4)).
- **Exam third-party cost reimbursement**: Licensee must pay/reimburse within **10 days** after receipt of a statement (§ 2612(d)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Licensing required** to transact EWA services in Delaware (§ 2602(a)).
- **Regulatory enforcement** via supervision and examinations (§ 2612) and maintenance/retention of records as required by regulation (§§ 2613–2615).
- **License revocation/suspension** authority for violations, disqualifying facts, or unfair/deceptive practices; due process through notice and hearing under **Chapter 101 of Title 29** (with ability to temporarily suspend pending final order) (§ 2611).
- **Consumer protection compliance duties**: disclosures, no-cost option, free cancellation, privacy/security compliance, overdraft/NSF reimbursement in specified cases, and tip disclosure standards (§ 2609).
- **Prohibited conduct** includes certain collection methods, employer fee-sharing, credit score requirement, repayment via credit, and charging interest/late fees/penalties (§ 2610).
- **Financial assurance requirement**: surety bond or, at Commissioner discretion, irrevocable letter of credit, each capped at **$35,000** (§ 2608).
