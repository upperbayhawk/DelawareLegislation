## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Direct the State Registrar of Vital Statistics to amend the birth certificate of **Levi Ryan Murray** to list **“Ryan Murray”** as the father, and to issue that birth certificate **without marking it as “delayed” or “amended.”**

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the **State Registrar of Vital Statistics** to **amend Levi Ryan Murray’s birth certificate** to enter **“Ryan Murray”** as the father. (Section 1)
- Specifies the amendment must be done under **16 Del. C. § 3131(b)**. (Section 1)
- Overrides **16 Del. C. § 3131(c)** so the resulting birth certificate **may not be marked** as **“delayed”** or **“amended.”** (Section 2)
- The bill responds to a stated conflict between **16 Del. C. § 3121(h)(1)** (husband listed if married at conception or birth unless determined otherwise by Family Court) and **13 Del. C. § 8-707** (UPA assisted reproduction provision, as adopted in 2003). (Whereas clauses)
- The conflict is described as affecting **one family**: marriage in **January 2020**, husband’s death **one day before** scheduled embryo transfer in **February 2020**, embryo transfer in **March 2022**, and the child’s subsequent birth. (Whereas clauses)
- The bill is framed as an **immediate, individual remedy** “while the General Assembly considers how to resolve the conflict” more broadly. (Synopsis/Whereas clauses)
- The bill does **not** amend the general parentage statutes; it gives a **case-specific directive** for this single birth certificate. (Sections 1–2)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Immediate completion/correction of a vital record:** Explicitly orders the Registrar to list **Ryan Murray** as father on Levi’s birth certificate. (Section 1)
- **Avoids stigma/administrative complications tied to labeling:** Prohibits marking the issued certificate as **“delayed”** or **“amended.”** (Section 2)
- **Clarifies agency authority for this case:** Removes uncertainty the bill states exists because the Registrar otherwise “does not have clear authority” to add the deceased husband’s name. (Whereas clauses)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Narrow, individual-specific legislation:** The Act addresses **one named child** rather than resolving the broader statutory conflict identified between Title 16 and Title 13. (Synopsis/Whereas clauses; Sections 1–2)
- **Bypasses general adjudication pathway described in the findings:** The bill notes Family Court “does not have the authority” to determine paternity in this scenario, and the Act resolves the record via legislation rather than a generally applicable legal standard. (Whereas clauses)
