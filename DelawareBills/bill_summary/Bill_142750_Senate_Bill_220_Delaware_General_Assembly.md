## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends the Charter of the Village of Arden by deleting the existing charter in its entirety and replacing it with a new charter text that revises governance structure, procedures (meetings, ordinances, referenda), committee provisions, boundaries, enforcement language, and related administrative rules.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Replaces the entire Arden charter with a new charter, including a new preamble and updated, gender-neutral language.  
- Expands/clarifies the Village’s territorial limits to include two additions: **Sherwood Forest Addition (5.026 acres; purchased Feb. 7, 2004)** and **Mill Race Addition (1.6 acres; purchased June 30, 2017)**, in addition to the original **159.00 acres (more or less)** described by metes and bounds.  
- Defines key terms, including **“Resident”** as **18+** and living in the Village for **6 consecutive months** immediately before each referendum or Town Assembly meeting.  
- Sets regular Town Assembly meetings for the **fourth Monday of January, March, June, and September** each year; requires **at least 7 days’ notice** (or as state law requires).  
- Establishes special meeting procedures: special meetings can be requested by **25 residents**; must be held **no more than 45 days** after receipt of a valid request (or after approval vote), with **at least 7 days’ notice**.  
- Establishes a **35-resident quorum** and a default rule that **a majority of yeas** passes ordinances/resolutions/motions at meetings (subject to specified supermajority exceptions).  
- Revises Board of Assessors structure and appeal path: **7 residents + 1 non-voting alternate**, **1-year terms**, at least **2 open hearings**, and a leaseholder appeal requiring **2/3** approval at the **regular September meeting** to trigger a **general referendum**.  
- Updates enforcement provisions: Justice of the Peace Court jurisdiction is recognized; **fines/penalties and imprisonment may not exceed state-law limits**; appeals go to **Superior Court in and for New Castle County**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer meeting cadence and notice requirements:** Regular meetings are fixed (fourth Monday of Jan./Mar./Jun./Sept.) and notice is required **≥ 7 days** in advance, improving predictability and procedural clarity.  
- **Defined special-meeting access and timeline:** A special meeting may be demanded by **25 residents**, with a firm outer deadline (**≤ 45 days**) for holding it after a valid request/approval, supporting responsiveness.  
- **Governance clarity for ordinance adoption:** Ordinances generally require written submission and **readings at 2 consecutive meetings** before a vote, creating a structured process and record.  
- **Updated boundaries documented in the charter:** Explicitly includes **Sherwood Forest (5.026 acres)** and **Mill Race (1.6 acres)** additions, reducing ambiguity over Village limits.  
- **Assessment transparency measures:** Board of Assessors must conduct at least **2 open hearings** and maintain inspectable records, and leaseholders are given a defined appeal path culminating in a referendum if thresholds are met.  
- **Explicit limits on penalties:** Charter confirms that no Village ordinance may exceed **state-law limits** on fines/penalties/imprisonment.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Voting eligibility restricted by residency definition:** Only individuals **18+** who have lived in the Village for **6 consecutive months** immediately preceding a Town Assembly meeting or referendum qualify as “Resident” for participation/voting, potentially excluding newer residents.  
- **High threshold to challenge assessment rate:** A leaseholder appeal must secure **2/3** approval of residents voting at the **regular September meeting** to trigger a referendum on the assessment rate.  
- **Ordinance process may be slower:** The requirement for written ordinances and readings at **2 consecutive meetings** before a vote can delay enactment/amendment/repeal compared with single-meeting action.  
- **Charter relies on later ordinances for some governance details:** The synopsis states multiple election/process details are “transferred to an ordinance,” which may reduce charter-level specificity (see ambiguities).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Total charter replacement:** Deletes existing charter text and enacts a new charter framework.  
- **Territorial limits updated/clarified:**
  - Original boundaries include **159.00 acres (more or less)** (metes and bounds) and include Grubb/Harvey Road.  
  - Adds/recognizes:
    - **Sherwood Forest Addition** purchased **Feb. 7, 2004**: **5.026 acres (more or less)**.  
    - **Mill Race Addition** purchased **June 30, 2017**: **1.6 acres** (lots described).  
- **Resident eligibility definition:** “Resident” = **18+** and **6 consecutive months** residency immediately before each referendum or Town Assembly meeting.  
- **Town Assembly structure:** Town Assembly consists of **all residents** and holds the Village’s governing power.  
- **Meeting procedures:**
  - Regular meetings: **fourth Monday of Jan./Mar./Jun./Sept.**  
  - Notice: **≥ 7 days** for regular and special meetings (or as state law requires).  
  - Special meetings: requested by **25 residents** (written request stating purpose) or by majority vote of residents in attendance; business limited to the call; held **≤ 45 days** after request/approval.  
  - Quorum: **35 residents**.  
  - Voting: each resident has **1 vote**; **majority** passes ordinances/resolutions/motions unless otherwise provided.  
- **Ordinance process:** No vote on enact/amend/repeal unless submitted in writing and **read at 2 consecutive meetings** prior to the vote, with a postponement mechanism if third-reading changes are “significant” by majority vote.  
- **Referenda:** A referendum can be called by a **majority vote** at a Town Assembly meeting.  
- **Board of Assessors changes:**
  - Composition: **7 residents + 1 non-voting alternate**, **1-year term**.  
  - Duties include assessing **full rental value of leased lands** or the **community standard of living**.  
  - Must hold **minimum 2 hearings** open to residents, Trustees, and leaseholders.  
  - Reports assessment rate at **regular June meeting**.  
  - Appeal: leaseholder can propose a different rate at **regular September meeting**; if **2/3** approve, both rates go to referendum; **majority of valid votes cast** determines the rate.  
- **Budget rule:** If the budget referendum fails, the Village uses the **prior year’s budget** until a new one is approved.  
- **Enforcement and penalties:** Justice of the Peace Court nearest the Village has jurisdiction; penalties capped by **state law**; ordinance fines capped by **state law**; appeal from conviction goes to **Superior Court in and for New Castle County**.  
- **Contracts:** Formal contracts must be **signed by the Chair** and **witnessed by a second Village official**.

## Who Benefits
- **Village residents** who participate in Town Assembly governance, due to clarified meeting schedules, notice requirements, quorum rules, and procedures for ordinances and special meetings.  
- **Leaseholders and Trustees** who gain explicit rights to attend Board of Assessors hearings and inspect certain records, plus a defined assessment-rate appeal and referendum mechanism.  
- **Village administration/officials** due to clarified authority for contracts (signature + witness) and clarified enforcement/jurisdiction language.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Newer residents (less than 6 months)** and **individuals under 18**, because they are excluded from the defined “Resident” category that determines voting/participation rights under the charter.  
- **Parties seeking rapid ordinance changes**, due to the requirement for written submission and readings at **2 consecutive meetings** prior to a vote.  
- **Leaseholders seeking to overturn an assessment rate** may be impacted by the **2/3** vote threshold at the September meeting to trigger a referendum.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Jurisdiction:** Justice of the Peace Court nearest to the Village has jurisdiction over offenses against the Act or authorized Village ordinances committed within Village limits.  
- **Penalty caps:** No fine, penalty, or imprisonment may exceed **limits prescribed by state law**; no Village ordinance may provide for a fine exceeding **state-law limits**.  
- **Appeals:** A person convicted of a Village ordinance violation may appeal to the **Superior Court in and for New Castle County**.  
- **Procedural compliance requirements (examples):**

  - Special meeting requests require a written request from **25 residents** stating the purpose; special meeting business is limited to the stated purpose.  
  - Board of Assessors must hold **at least 2 hearings** and keep inspectable records.
