## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Adopt the Respiratory Care Interstate Compact in Delaware to allow a respiratory therapist licensed in a Compact “member state” to practice in other member states under a “compact privilege,” while preserving each state’s authority to regulate and discipline practice to protect public health and safety. (§ 1701C)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new Delaware Code chapter (Title 24, Chapter 17C) enacting the Respiratory Care Interstate Compact, enabling interstate practice of respiratory therapy through a “compact privilege.” (§§ 1701C, 1704C)
- Requires participating member states (including Delaware, once effective) to participate in a shared Compact data system and to report adverse actions and “significant investigative information.” (§§ 1703C(a)(3), (5)-(6); 1708C)
- Sets eligibility requirements for a therapist to use a compact privilege, including: active home-state license, active National Board for Respiratory Care credential, and no adverse action within the previous **2 years**. (§ 1704C(a)(1)-(3))
- Establishes reporting deadlines for licensees: report non-member-state adverse action within **30 days**; report domicile address changes within **30 days** of the effective date of the change. (§ 1704C(a)(7)-(8))
- Creates a multistate Compact Commission with rulemaking authority whose rules have “the force of law” in member states; includes public-meeting notice requirements (generally **30 days**, or **24 hours** for emergency meetings). (§§ 1707C; 1709C; 1707C(f)(1)-(2))
- Provides military mobility provisions: active military members and their spouses cannot be charged Compact Commission fees for a compact privilege; remote states may charge reduced or no fee. (§ 1705C(b))
- Makes Delaware’s Compact participation and this Act’s operation contingent on the Compact being enacted by the **7th state**; requires the Director of the Division of Professional Regulation to publish notice in the Register of Regulations once the 7-state threshold is met. (Section 2; § 1711C(a))
- Allows member states to withdraw by repealing statute, with withdrawal effective **180 days** after enactment; requires notice and continued recognition of Compact-granted privileges for at least **180 days** after notice. (§ 1711C(b)(1), (3))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved cross-border access to care:** The Compact’s stated goal is to “improv[e] public access to respiratory therapy services” by allowing practice across member states via a streamlined pathway. (§ 1701C(a)-(b))
- **Workforce mobility / shortage response:** The Compact explicitly aims to “promote mobility and address workforce shortages.” (§ 1701C(b)(6))
- **Enhanced public protection through information-sharing:** Member states must share adverse actions and significant investigative information through a coordinated data system. (§§ 1703C(a)(5)-(6); 1708C)
- **Military family support:** Prohibits Compact Commission fees for compact privileges for active military members and spouses (and allows states to reduce/waive state fees). (§ 1705C(b))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **New ongoing reporting and participation obligations for Delaware’s licensing authority:** Member states must participate in the Compact data system, receive/investigate complaints, and provide required notices to the Commission. (§ 1703C(a)(3)-(7); § 1708C)
- **Potential new fees/assessments tied to Compact operations:** The Commission may levy annual assessments on member states and impose fees on licensees for compact privileges; states may charge fees for granting/renewing compact privileges. (§§ 1707C(g)(3); 1703C(c); 1704C(a)(5))
- **Binding multistate rules:** Commission rules “have the force of law” in each member state, which could reduce Delaware’s flexibility within Compact-covered areas (while not limiting non-conflicting state laws). (§ 1709C(b); § 1709C(o))

## Health Impact
- The Compact’s stated health-related aims include improved public access to respiratory therapy services and improved patient outcomes through a “streamlined pathway” for multi-state practice. (§ 1701C(b)(1))
- Public safety is supported via interstate sharing of adverse action and investigation information. (§§ 1701C(b)(2); 1708C)

## Key Policy Changes
- Enacts Delaware participation in the Respiratory Care Interstate Compact by adding Title 24, Chapter 17C. (Section 1)
- Creates a “compact privilege” framework allowing a therapist licensed in a home member state to practice in a remote member state under remote-state scope-of-practice rules. (§ 1704C(c))
- Establishes eligibility conditions for compact privilege, including:
  - Active home-state license and active NBRC credential. (§ 1704C(a)(1)-(2))
  - **No adverse action** against a license in the prior **2 years**. (§ 1704C(a)(3))
  - Meet any remote-state jurisprudence requirement. (§ 1704C(a)(6))
  - Reporting obligations with **30-day** deadlines for certain events. (§ 1704C(a)(7)-(8))
- Requires criminal background checks for each new licensee at initial licensure in member states. (§ 1703C(a)(9))
- Creates the Respiratory Care Interstate Compact Commission with powers to adopt binding rules, collect fees, and operate a shared data system. (§§ 1707C; 1708C; 1709C)
- Sets default/termination processes for noncompliant member states and withdrawal rules with **180-day** timelines. (§§ 1710C; 1711C(b))

## Who Benefits
- Respiratory therapists licensed in Compact member states seeking to practice in multiple states without obtaining separate licenses for each state (through compact privilege). (§ 1701C(a); § 1704C)
- Patients and health systems in member states due to the Compact’s stated goal of increased access and improved outcomes. (§ 1701C(b)(1))
- Active military members and spouses who receive a prohibition on Compact Commission fees for compact privileges and may receive reduced/waived state fees. (§ 1705C(b))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Respiratory therapists with recent discipline: those who have had an adverse action against a license within the previous **2 years** are ineligible for compact privilege. (§ 1704C(a)(3))
- Respiratory therapists whose home-state license becomes encumbered: they lose compact privileges in all remote states until the license is no longer encumbered and **2 years** have elapsed afterward. (§ 1704C(e)(1)-(2))
- Delaware’s respiratory therapy licensing authority (administratively) due to required data submission, monitoring, and reporting duties. (§§ 1703C; 1708C)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Delaware Act effective date:** This Act “takes effect upon the enactment of the Respiratory Care Interstate Compact by the seventh state.” (Section 2)
- **Compact effective date (overall):** The Compact “takes effect on the date on which [it] is enacted into law in the seventh member state.” (§ 1711C(a))
- **Notice requirement upon 7th state enactment:** On enactment by the 7th state, Delaware’s Director of the Division of Professional Regulation must publish notice in the Register of Regulations that 7 states have adopted the Compact. (Section 2)
- **Licensee reporting deadlines:**
  - Report adverse action taken by a non-member state within **30 days** of the adverse action. (§ 1704C(a)(7))
  - Report domicile address changes within **30 days** of the effective date of the change. (§ 1704C(a)(8))
- **Commission meeting notice:** Public meeting notice at least **30 days** prior; emergency public meeting notice at least **24 hours** prior. (§ 1707C(f)(1)-(2))
- **Member state withdrawal:** Withdrawal effective **180 days** after enactment of repealing statute; withdrawing state must continue recognizing Compact-granted licenses/privileges for at least **180 days** after notice of withdrawal. (§ 1711C(b)(1), (3))
- **Commission rule rejection window:** A rule can be rejected if a majority of member-state legislatures reject it within **4 years** of the rule’s adoption. (§ 1709C(d))
- **Charter member state commissioner vacancy:** Must be filled within **60 days** of the vacancy. (§ 1707C(b)(5))
- **Emergency rulemaking:** Usual procedures must be applied retroactively “no event later than **90 days**” after the emergency rule’s effective date. (§ 1709C(l))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Member state compliance:** Member states must comply with Commission rules and data/reporting requirements; default can lead to termination from the Compact by majority vote of Commissioners. (§§ 1703C(a)(7); 1710C(b)-(c))
- **Investigations and subpoenas:** Remote states may issue subpoenas for hearings/investigations; subpoenas must be enforced by courts in the state where witnesses/evidence are located; issuing authority pays required witness/travel fees. (§ 1706C(d)(2))
- **Cost recovery from licensee:** Unless prohibited by state law, a remote state may recover from the licensee costs of investigations and case disposition resulting from adverse action. (§ 1706C(d)(3))
- **Compact privilege limitations:** Compact privilege can be removed by a remote state; an encumbered home-state license triggers loss of compact privilege in all remote states, with a **2-year** waiting period after the encumbrance is cleared. (§ 1704C(d)-(f))
- **Commission enforcement against states:** The Commission may initiate legal action against a defaulting member state in federal court to enforce compliance; prevailing party awarded litigation costs and reasonable attorney’s fees. (§ 1710C(j)(1))
