## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Renewable Portfolio Standard (RPS) in Title 26, §354 to reduce and temporarily hold the required percentage of electricity that must be sourced from “eligible energy resources,” as described in the bill’s synopsis, and to revise the compliance schedule.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes the RPS compliance schedule in 26 Del. C. §354(a) (Schedule I) for utilities and municipal electric companies serving Delaware end-use customers.
- Inserts a **10%** figure into the “Eligible Energy Resources” schedule beginning in **Compliance Year 2025** and continuing through **2035** in the table as amended.
- The synopsis states the intent is to **“roll[] back the Renewable Portfolio Standard to 10% and maintain[] the RPS requirement for the next 10 years”**, then resume annual increases after that period.
- Leaves a separate “Solar Photovoltaics” schedule in the table, showing **3.50%** for **2025–2035**, then increasing to **5.00% by 2040**.
- The synopsis cites claimed recent compliance costs: **Delmarva Power ~ $13 million annually** in state-mandated compliance fees in “each of the last two years.”
- The RPS schedule in the table continues out to **2040**, with solar percentages rising from **3.75% (2036)** to **5.00% (2040)**.
- The bill text provided does not include a standalone effective-date section.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Potential reduction in compliance fees passed to customers:** The synopsis states utilities pay state penalties when renewable energy is not obtainable, and that Delmarva Power spent **about $13 million annually** in such fees in each of the last two years; reducing/holding requirements could reduce this type of cost (as framed in the synopsis).
- **Ratepayer cost relief (as stated rationale):** The synopsis describes “cost multipliers” (transmission/distribution charges and penalties) being passed to consumers; the bill is presented as providing “relief to Delawareans” by reducing the mandate to **10%** for a period.
- **More time for supply to develop:** The synopsis states the policy provides “sufficient time for renewable generation capacity to meet demand” by holding the requirement for **10 years**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Lower renewable sourcing requirements than previously scheduled:** The synopsis states the RPS was set to rise to **40% in 2035**; the bill’s stated intent is to roll back to **10%** for a decade, which would reduce renewable procurement obligations relative to that prior trajectory.
- **Potential inconsistency/complexity in the amended schedule text:** The amended table shows both (a) rising “Eligible Energy Resources” percentages (e.g., **25.00% in 2025** up to **40.00% in 2035**) and (b) an inserted **“10%”** column/figure for **2025–2035**, creating unclear requirements from the text as shown.
