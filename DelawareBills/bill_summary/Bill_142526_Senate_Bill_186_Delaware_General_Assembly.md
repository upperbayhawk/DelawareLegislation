## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Landlord-Tenant Code (Title 25, § 5514(h)) to clarify acceptable evidence that required security-deposit-related communications were sent and to allow required notices to be sent electronically under defined conditions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates **25 Del. C. § 5514(h)** (security deposit communications/notices) to specify what counts as **presumptive evidence** that a communication was sent.
- Lists three forms of presumptive evidence: **certificate of mailing**, **registered or certified mailing showing proof of delivery**, and **delivery in person**.
- Allows required notices to be delivered via **email, text, or other written electronic means** if the parties **regularly communicated** that way.
- Requires the party relying on electronic notice to show the message was **received**, not merely sent.
- Examples of electronic receipt evidence include a **response**, a **read receipt**, or **other confirmation of receipt**.
- Retains existing rule: if a tenant does not provide a forwarding address, the landlord is relieved of certain notice duties and **double-security-deposit liability**, but still owes any **unused portion** of the deposit, subject to a **1-year written claim** deadline.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer proof standards**: Enumerating presumptive evidence (mailing certificate/certified mail/in-person delivery) may reduce disputes over whether security-deposit notices were sent. (New § 5514(h)(1).)
- **Modernizes communication options**: Permits electronic notice when electronic communication is an established, regular method between the parties. (New § 5514(h)(2).)
- **Receipt-focused protection**: Electronic notice is only valid if the sender can show the notice was **received**, potentially protecting recipients from missed emails/texts. (New § 5514(h)(2).)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential evidentiary disputes for electronic notice**: The bill requires proof of **receipt** for electronic communications, which could be contested (e.g., what qualifies as “some other indication of confirmation”). (New § 5514(h)(2).)
- **Uneven access to electronic communication**: Tenants or landlords who do not regularly use electronic methods may not benefit from the electronic-notice option. (Condition in new § 5514(h)(2).)

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What precisely qualifies as the parties “**regularly communicated**” via a particular electronic method (frequency, duration, or context not defined). (New § 5514(h)(2).)
- What counts as “**some other indication of confirmation of receipt**” beyond responses/read receipts. (New § 5514(h)(2).)
- Whether “presumptive evidence” in § 5514(h)(1) is intended to be an exclusive list or illustrative (text does not state “including” or “only”). (New § 5514(h)(1).)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds presumptive evidence categories** that a security-deposit-related communication was sent:
  - **Certificate of mailing**
  - **Registered or certified mailing showing proof of delivery**
  - **Delivery in person**  
  (New § 5514(h)(1)(a)-(c).)
- **Authorizes electronic notice** (email, text, or other written electronic means) when:
  - the parties **regularly communicated** using that method, and
  - the sender shows the communication was **received**.  
  (New § 5514(h)(2).)
- **Identifies examples of receipt evidence** for electronic notice: **response**, **read receipt**, or **other confirmation**. (New § 5514(h)(2).)
- **Maintains existing forwarding-address/claim rules**:
  - Tenant must provide forwarding address in writing at or before termination to preserve certain notice and remedies.
  - If tenant fails to do so, landlord is relieved of responsibility to give notice and liability for **double the security deposit**, but landlord remains liable for any **unused portion**, provided the tenant makes a **written claim within 1 year** from termination/expiration. (Existing § 5514(h) language retained.)

## Who Benefits
- **Landlords and tenants** who need clearer standards for proving required security-deposit notices were sent. (§ 5514(h)(1).)
- Parties who **regularly use electronic communications** and can document receipt, by gaining a compliant electronic option for notices. (§ 5514(h)(2).)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Parties who rely on electronic communication but **cannot prove receipt** (e.g., no response/read receipt/confirmation), potentially making notices harder to validate. (§ 5514(h)(2).)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **1 year deadline** (existing rule retained): If the tenant did not provide a forwarding address, the tenant must make a **claim in writing within 1 year** from the termination or expiration of the rental agreement to recover any unused portion of the security deposit. (25 Del. C. § 5514(h).)
