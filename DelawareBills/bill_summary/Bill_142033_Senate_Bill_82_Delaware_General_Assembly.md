## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To extend the maximum duration of a nonemergency lethal violence protective order (LVPO) issued under 10 Del. C. § 7704 from **1 year** to **5 years**, and to adjust the related termination-request process in 10 Del. C. § 7705.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- In nonemergency LVPO cases, the bill increases the maximum length of relief from **“not to exceed 1 year”** to **“not to exceed 5 years”**. (10 Del. C. § 7704(j))
- The bill preserves the existing **clear and convincing evidence** standard for issuing an LVPO in a nonemergency hearing. (10 Del. C. § 7704(b)(4), (d))
- The respondent’s procedural rights remain, including the right to be heard, notice, present evidence, and cross-examine witnesses. (10 Del. C. § 7704(b))
- Nonemergency hearings must be held within **15 days** of filing, unless extended for good cause. (10 Del. C. § 7704(b)(2))
- If the order is issued, it can require relinquishment of firearms/projectile weapons/ammunition to law enforcement, and can include search-and-seizure directions and certain residential prohibitions. (10 Del. C. § 7704(d))
- The act modifies termination procedure language so a respondent may submit **1 written request “at any time per year”** during the effective period for a hearing to terminate the order. (10 Del. C. § 7705(a))
- The enacted law is shown as **Chapter 148, Volume 85**, and was **Approved August 20, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Longer protective period:** Extending LVPO duration to up to **5 years** may reduce how often petitioners must return to court to maintain protections, because relief can last longer under § 7704(j).
- **Continued due process structure:** The statute continues to require a hearing process with notice and an evidentiary burden of **clear and convincing evidence**, which is explicitly maintained. (10 Del. C. § 7704(b)(3)–(4), (d))
- **Ongoing opportunity to seek termination:** The respondent is allowed to request termination **1 time per year** during the order’s effective period. (10 Del. C. § 7705(a))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Longer restrictions on respondent:** A respondent may be subject to firearm/projectile weapon/ammunition relinquishment and related restrictions for up to **5 years** rather than **1 year**. (10 Del. C. § 7704(j), (d))
- **Potentially extended housing impacts:** Because the order may prohibit the respondent from residing with another individual who owns/possesses/controls firearms/projectile weapons/ammunition, a longer maximum order term could extend these living restrictions. (10 Del. C. § 7704(d)(3))
- **Continued law enforcement activity over a longer period:** Orders may direct law enforcement to “immediately search for and seize” covered items; a longer order period may mean longer-term retention/administration of relinquished items. (10 Del. C. § 7704(d)(4))

## Key Policy Changes
- **Maximum LVPO duration increased:** Changes the cap on relief under **10 Del. C. § 7704(j)** from **1 year** to **5 years** (“not to exceed 1 year” → “not to exceed 5 years”).
- **Termination request timing clarified/expanded across longer orders:** Amends **10 Del. C. § 7705(a)** to allow a respondent to submit **1 written request at any time per year** during the order’s effective period for a hearing to terminate the order.

## Who Benefits
- **Petitioners seeking LVPOs:** Potentially benefit from the ability to obtain relief for up to **5 years** rather than needing a shorter maximum term. (10 Del. C. § 7704(j))
- **Individuals at risk of physical injury (self or others):** The LVPO framework is aimed at situations where the respondent poses a danger of causing physical injury by access to firearms or projectile weapons. (10 Del. C. § 7704(a)(1), (d))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Respondents subject to LVPOs:** May face longer periods (up to **5 years**) of firearm/projectile weapon/ammunition relinquishment and other restrictions authorized in the order. (10 Del. C. § 7704(d), (j))
- **Household members / cohabitants (indirectly):** If an order prohibits the respondent from residing with another individual who owns/possesses/controls firearms/projectile weapons/ammunition, shared living arrangements could be disrupted for a longer period. (10 Del. C. § 7704(d)(3))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approval date:** **Approved August 20, 2025** (Chapter 148, Volume 85).
- **Hearing deadline (nonemergency):** If a hearing is requested, it must be held within **15 days** of filing, unless extended for good cause. (10 Del. C. § 7704(b)(2))
- **Order duration cap:** Relief may not exceed **5 years**. (10 Del. C. § 7704(j))
- **Termination request frequency:** Respondent may submit **1 written request at any time per year** during the effective period of the order. (10 Del. C. § 7705(a))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Relinquishment requirement:** If the court finds by **clear and convincing evidence** an immediate and present danger, it “shall issue” an LVPO requiring the respondent to relinquish to a law-enforcement agency a firearm, projectile weapon, or ammunition owned/possessed/controlled by the respondent. (10 Del. C. § 7704(d))
- **Possible law enforcement search and seizure:** The order may direct a law-enforcement agency with jurisdiction to “immediately search for and seize” firearms/projectile weapons/ammunition owned/possessed/controlled by the respondent. (10 Del. C. § 7704(d)(4))
- **Service of order:** If respondent is not present, the court must direct that the LVPO be served immediately by personal service, mail, or other manner directed by the court (including publication if other methods fail or are inadequate). (10 Del. C. § 7704(g))
- **Appeal:** An aggrieved party in interest may appeal the Superior Court decision to the Delaware Supreme Court. (10 Del. C. § 7704(i))
