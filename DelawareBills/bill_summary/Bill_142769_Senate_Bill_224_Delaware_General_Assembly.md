## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 11 Del. C. § 4217(h) to make the Department of Correction’s (DOC) adoption of regulations for the sentence modification process **permissive (“may”) rather than mandatory (“shall”)**, while **prohibiting DOC from adding extra eligibility restrictions** beyond what the statute provides.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes 11 Del. C. § 4217(h) so DOC **may** (not must) adopt regulations to implement the sentence modification process.
- Adds/retains an express limitation: DOC **may not impose additional restrictions, exclusions, or eligibility requirements** on people seeking sentence modification under § 4217.
- Addresses implementation of the sentence modification process created/revised by the **Richard “Mouse” Smith Compassionate Release Act** (Senate Substitute No. 1 for Senate Bill No. 10, prior session).
- The stated rationale: DOC **does not believe regulations are necessary** to successfully implement the revised process, so the bill removes the requirement to promulgate them.
- No dollar amounts, appropriations, or fees are stated in the bill text.
- Enacted as **Chapter 235, Volume 85**, approved **January 30, 2026**.
- Applies specifically to DOC’s regulatory authority related to **sentence modification of incarceration** under § 4217.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Faster implementation / reduced delay risk**: By removing a requirement to adopt regulations, DOC can implement § 4217 without waiting for a formal rulemaking process (supported by synopsis stating DOC believes regulations are not necessary).
- **Prevents DOC from narrowing statutory access**: The text states DOC “**may not impose additional restrictions, exclusions, or eligibility requirements**” for people seeking sentence modification under § 4217.
- **Administrative flexibility**: Allows DOC to adopt regulations if needed (“may adopt regulations”) rather than mandating them in all cases.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Less formal procedural clarity**: If DOC chooses not to issue regulations, some operational details may be governed by internal practices rather than published rules (bill text makes rulemaking optional).
- **Reduced transparency compared to mandatory regulation**: Eliminating a requirement to promulgate regulations may reduce public visibility into implementation procedures (inferred from the shift from “shall” to “may,” but the bill does not detail transparency impacts).
