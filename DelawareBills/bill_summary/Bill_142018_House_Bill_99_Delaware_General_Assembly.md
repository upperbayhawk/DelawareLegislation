## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Increase Delaware’s refundable Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) to **20% of the corresponding federal EITC** for **tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025**, and clarify the effective date of a prior **4.5% refundable EITC** provision as beginning **January 1, 2021**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Sets Delaware’s EITC for **tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025** at **20% of the corresponding federal EITC** (Title 30, §1117).
- Removes the prior option (for 2021–2024 tax years) to claim **either**:
  - **20% nonrefundable** EITC (capped at tax due), **or**
  - **4.5% refundable** EITC (refund allowed above tax due).
- Clarifies that the earlier law establishing the refundable **4.5%** EITC took effect for **tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021** (amending Chapter 118, Volume 83).
- Bill is assumed (per fiscal note) to be **effective upon signature**, but the **credit increase applies to tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025**.
- Fiscal note estimates **General Fund revenue losses** of **$20.3 million (FY2026)**, **$20.9 million (FY2027)**, and **$21.5 million (FY2028)**.
- Fiscal note states EITC was claimed on **62,059** Delaware returns for **tax year 2023** (and **68,480** for **tax year 2022**) and assumes claims remain flat.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Increases tax relief for EITC-eligible residents** by raising the refundable credit from **4.5% to 20%** of the federal EITC for **tax years starting on/after January 1, 2025** (Synopsis; §1117(a)(3)).
- **Simplifies the EITC choice structure** after 2025 by replacing the prior two-option system (20% nonrefundable vs. 4.5% refundable) with a single **20%** credit framework (§1117(a)(2) vs. §1117(a)(3)).
- **Clarifies prior effective date** for the refundable EITC provision to **January 1, 2021**, potentially reducing confusion about eligibility timing (Section 2; Synopsis).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces General Fund revenues** by an estimated **$20.3M (FY2026)**, **$20.9M (FY2027)**, and **$21.5M (FY2028)** (Fiscal Note: “General Fund Revenue Loss”).
- **Administrative and fiscal exposure may increase** if EITC claim volume rises above the “flat claims” assumption used in the estimate (Fiscal Note assumption #4 states the estimate assumes claims remain flat).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- Whether the **20% credit for tax years beginning on/after January 1, 2025** is **fully refundable** is not stated explicitly in the amended §1117(a)(3); it states only that the taxpayer “may claim 20%” and does not repeat the prior “refundable” phrasing found in §1117(a)(2)(b). (Fiscal note and synopsis describe it as “refundable,” but the statutory language shown for (a)(3) does not specify refundability.)
- The bill text shows changes to the “before/after January 1, 2022 2021” dates; the precise intent of all date corrections beyond what is stated in the synopsis is not fully explained in the bill text excerpts provided.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 30, §1117 (Earned income tax credit):**
  - For **tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025**, a resident may claim **20% of the corresponding federal EITC** (§1117(a)(3)).
  - For **tax years beginning on/after January 1, 2021 and before January 1, 2025**, a resident could claim either:
    - **20%** of federal EITC **nonrefundable** (not exceeding tax due), or
    - **4.5%** of federal EITC **refundable** to the extent it exceeds tax due (§1117(a)(2)(a)-(b)).
- **Chapter 118, Volume 83, Section 2 (effective date contingency language):**
  - Changes prior effective-date language to clarify that the refundable EITC (4.5%) took effect for **tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2021** (Synopsis; Section 2 amendment).

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware resident taxpayers eligible for the federal EITC** under IRC **26 U.S.C. § 32**, for **tax years beginning on/after January 1, 2025**, due to the increase to **20%** of the federal credit (§1117(a)(3); Synopsis).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
