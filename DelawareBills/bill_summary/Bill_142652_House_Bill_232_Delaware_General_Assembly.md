## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Ensure impartiality and prevent undue influence in the assignment of cases in the Delaware Court of Chancery by implementing a randomized case assignment process. (Proposed 10 Del. C. § 330(a))

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **all civil actions, petitions, and matters** filed in the Court of Chancery to be assigned to the **Chancellor or a Vice Chancellor** via a **randomized assignment system** administered by the Chancellor. (§ 330(b)(1))
- Prohibits any **party, attorney, or judge** from selecting, directing, or influencing assignment to a specific judicial officer. (§ 330(b)(2))
- Allows reassignment only for specified reasons: **recusal/disqualification**, **related cases**, or **judicial workload/docket efficiency** (with limits). (§ 330(c))
- Requires the Court of Chancery to adopt **publicly available rules** governing the randomization process. (§ 330(d)(1))
- Requires the **Clerk of the Court** to **annually publish a statistical report** on assignments, including **reassignment rates and reasons**. (§ 330(d)(2))
- States there is **no right to choose a judge** (except under recusal/disqualification rules). (§ 330(e)(1))
- Subjects attempts to **manipulate or evade** the random assignment system to **sanctions**, including **potential dismissal or disciplinary action**. (§ 330(e)(2))
- Implementation deadline: Court must implement **no later than 90 days after enactment**; Act is effective **immediately upon enactment**. (Sections 2–3)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces forum-shopping / judge-shopping risk** by barring parties/attorneys/judges from influencing assignments and requiring random assignment. (§ 330(b)(2), § 330(e))
- **Improves transparency and accountability** via publicly available assignment rules and an annual statistical report with reassignment rates and reasons. (§ 330(d)(1)–(2))
- **Protects judicial impartiality** through stated purpose and structured reassignment limits (e.g., cannot be based on party/attorney identity). (§ 330(a), § 330(c)(iii))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative burden** on the Court and Clerk to create/maintain a randomized system, publish public rules, and produce annual statistical reports. (§ 330(d), Section 2)
- **Potential for sanctions including dismissal** could increase litigation risk for parties if conduct is deemed an attempt to manipulate or evade the system. (§ 330(e)(2))
- **Chancellor discretion in reassignments** for related cases or workload (“good cause” / “interest of docket efficiency”) could raise concerns about consistency, though the bill restricts party/attorney-identity-based reassignment. (§ 330(c)(ii)–(iii))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill requires a “randomized case assignment system” but does **not define the method** (e.g., algorithm, weighting, rotation, blind draw). (§ 330(b)(1))
- The bill does not specify what data will be included in the “statistical report” beyond reassignment rates and reasons (e.g., case types, time periods, anonymization). (§ 330(d)(2))
- “Substantially related” and the standards for “good cause” and “docket efficiency” are not defined in statute (left to discretion/rules). (§ 330(c)(ii)–(iii))
- The bill references sanctions but does not specify standards of proof, procedures, or a defined sanction schedule. (§ 330(e)(2))

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates a new requirement in Title 10 (Chapter 3) establishing **randomized assignment** of all Court of Chancery matters to the Chancellor or Vice Chancellor. (§ 330(b)(1))
- Prohibits **selection or influence** over judge assignment by parties, attorneys, or judges. (§ 330(b)(2))
- Limits reassignments to:
  - **Recusal/disqualification** under Delaware law or judicial ethics rules. (§ 330(c)(i))
  - **Substantially related cases** (Chancellor may consolidate or reassign). (§ 330(c)(ii))
  - **Workload/docket efficiency** for good cause, provided reassignment is **not based on identity of parties/attorneys**. (§ 330(c)(iii))
- Requires **public rules** governing the randomization process and an **annual statistical report** by the Clerk. (§ 330(d))
- Codifies **no right to choose a judge**, except via recusal/disqualification, and authorizes **sanctions** for manipulation/evasion attempts. (§ 330(e))
- Requires implementation within **90 days after enactment** and immediate effectiveness upon enactment. (Sections 2–3)

## Who Benefits
- **Litigants and the public**: increased confidence in impartial assignment through randomization and anti-influence provisions. (§ 330(a)–(b))
- **Court system**: tools to manage **related cases** and **workload** while maintaining stated neutrality constraints. (§ 330(c))
- **Observers/researchers/public**: access to publicly available rules and annual assignment statistics. (§ 330(d))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Parties or attorneys seeking a particular judge**: explicitly denied any right to demand assignment/removal (outside recusal/disqualification). (§ 330(e)(1))
- **Individuals/entities attempting to influence assignments**: subject to sanctions, including potential dismissal or disciplinary action. (§ 330(e)(2))
- **Court administrative staff/Clerk**: added ongoing reporting and rule-maintenance responsibilities. (§ 330(d), Section 2)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective immediately upon enactment.** (Section 3)
- Court of Chancery must implement the Act **no later than 90 days after enactment.** (Section 2(a))
- Clerk of the Court must **annually** publish a statistical report (no specific calendar date specified). (§ 330(d)(2))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirement:** all Chancery filings must be assigned through a **randomized system**; no selection or influence allowed. (§ 330(b))
- **Reassignment restrictions:** only allowed for recusal/disqualification, substantially related cases, or workload/docket efficiency with identity-based limits. (§ 330(c))
- **Transparency requirements:** publicly available rules; annual statistical report by Clerk. (§ 330(d))
- **Penalties/sanctions:** attempts to manipulate or evade the randomized assignment system are subject to **sanctions**, including **potential dismissal or disciplinary action**. (§ 330(e)(2))
