## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Equal Accommodations Law (Title 6, Chapter 45) to explicitly incorporate disability-discrimination protections aligned with **Section 504 of the federal Rehabilitation Act of 1973 (29 U.S.C. § 794)** and its implementing regulations **as in effect on January 1, 2025**, and to direct how Delaware statutes/regulations should interpret references to “Section 504.”

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Clarifies that § 4504 must not be read to reduce (abrogate) obligations under other applicable **federal or state laws or regulations**.
- Requires Delaware’s Equal Accommodations Law to be interpreted as providing **any additional rights/requirements** that federal or state law provides to protected classes or public accommodations.
- Codifies that disability discrimination prohibited under Delaware law **includes the acts prohibited under 29 U.S.C. § 794 (Section 504)** and its implementing regulations **as they existed on January 1, 2025**.
- Directs that the provisions of § 4504 be interpreted **broadly and to the fullest extent possible** under state and federal law to continue protections for persons with disabilities.
- Establishes an interpretation rule: references to **“section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act” / “section 504”** elsewhere in Delaware statutes or regulations (when used in the context of the Rehabilitation Act) must be read to mean **the Delaware Equal Accommodations Law (6 Del. C. § 4500 et seq.)**.
- Sets a **fixed regulatory reference date (January 1, 2025)** for the incorporated federal implementing regulations.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger clarity and continuity of disability protections:** The bill explicitly includes the acts prohibited by **Section 504 (29 U.S.C. § 794)** within Delaware’s discrimination prohibitions, anchored to regulations **in effect as of January 1, 2025**.
- **Broad construction directive:** Requires courts/agencies to interpret the law “**broadly and to the fullest extent possible**” to continue protections for persons with disabilities.
- **Prevents narrowing via interpretation:** States the section cannot be interpreted to **abrogate** requirements imposed by other applicable federal/state laws or regulations.
- **Reduces confusion in cross-references:** Reinterprets statutory/regulatory references to “Section 504” (in the Rehabilitation Act context) as references to **Delaware’s Equal Accommodations Law**, potentially simplifying state-law interpretation.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential compliance uncertainty:** By incorporating Section 504 acts and freezing implementing regulations to those “**in effect as January 1, 2025**,” entities may need to determine which federal regulatory provisions were in effect on that specific date.
- **Interpretive broadness may increase dispute risk:** The directive to interpret provisions “**broadly**” could lead to more contested interpretations of obligations under Delaware law (the bill mandates broad interpretation but does not enumerate specific new requirements).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Non-abrogation clause retained/clarified:** § 4504(e) states nothing in the section may be interpreted as an abrogation of requirements otherwise imposed by applicable federal/state laws or regulations.
- **Additional-rights rule:** If federal or state law provides additional rights to protected classes or requirements for places of public accommodation, § 4504 must be interpreted as providing that right/requirement.
- **Section 504 incorporation for disability discrimination:** Acts prohibited on the basis of disability include those prohibited by **29 U.S.C. § 794**, and implementing regulations **as in effect on January 1, 2025**.
- **Broad interpretation mandate:** The section must be interpreted broadly to continue disability protections across Delaware law.
- **Statutory/regulatory cross-reference rule:** References to “Section 504” in other Delaware statutes/regulations (in the Rehabilitation Act context) are to be interpreted as referring to **6 Del. C. § 4500 et seq. (Delaware Equal Accommodations Law)**.
