## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s County and Municipal Police/Firefighter Pension Plan (Title 11, Ch. 88) and the County and Municipal Employees’ Retirement Plan (Title 29, Ch. 55) to (1) allow paid emergency medical technicians (EMTs) to participate in the Police/Firefighter Pension Plan when working for a participating employer, (2) prevent certain newly hired firefighters/EMTs from participating in the County and Municipal Employees’ Fund, and (3) make technical and conforming code corrections (including deleting a duplicative section).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands eligibility in Title 11 Police/Firefighter Pension Plan to include **paid emergency medical technicians (EMTs)** (and recognizes **paramedics** in the disability section).  
- Changes the definition of covered “employee” in Title 11 to distinguish **individuals hired before vs. on/after the Act’s effective date**, specifically for paid firefighter/EMT roles in affiliated entities.  
- Makes participation provisions explicit: **counties, municipalities, volunteer ambulance companies, and volunteer fire companies** may elect to participate in the Police/Firefighter Pension Plan **beginning July 1 of any year** (on/after **July 1, 1984**), subject to an application deadline.  
- Updates Title 29 definitions so that people hired **on/after the Act’s effective date** who have duties as **paid firefighters and/or EMTs** are **excluded** from the County and Municipal Employees’ Fund (with a stated goal of federal tax/pension compliance).  
- Expands/clarifies which entities may participate in the Title 29 plan: includes **state governmental subdivisions**, specifically including the **Delaware State Housing Authority**, plus **volunteer ambulance and volunteer fire companies**.  
- Maintains application timing mechanics for participation in both plans: resolution by the joining entity, submitted **not later than 90 days** before participation begins (unless the Board reduces the time limit); once approved, participation is **irrevocable**.  
- Deletes **11 Del. C. § 8806** (described as duplicative of **29 Del. C. § 8308**).  
- Clarifies duty-connected disability eligibility language in Title 11 to include disability arising while employed as **police officer, firefighter, paramedic, or emergency medical technician**, including certain traumatic-event cardiovascular conditions.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader pension eligibility for paid EMTs:** The synopsis states the Act “allows a paid emergency medical technician working for a participating employer to be eligible for the County Municipal Police/Firefighter Pension Plan.”  
- **Plan alignment / compliance objective:** The Act explicitly aims to ensure Delaware’s pension plans “comply with federal tax and pension law” by limiting where newly hired firefighters/EMTs can participate (Police/Firefighter plan only).  
- **Administrative clarity on participating entities:** Explicitly lists eligible participating entities (including volunteer ambulance/fire companies and certain state subdivisions), reducing ambiguity about who may join each plan.  
- **Removes duplicative statutory language:** Eliminating § 8806 (identified as duplicative) may reduce confusion and streamline the code.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced retirement-plan choice for newly hired firefighters/EMTs:** The synopsis states firefighters and EMTs hired **after the effective date** are excluded from the County and Municipal Employees’ Fund and “eligible only for the Police/Firefighter Pension Fund.”  
- **Potential increased employer pension cost exposure is possible but not quantified:** The bill continues to require employer contributions as a **percentage of covered payroll approved by the Board** based on actuarial valuation (no new percentage stated), and expanding eligibility could affect covered payroll—however, **no fiscal numbers are provided** in the bill text.  
- **One-time lump-sum cost to purchase prior service credit remains significant:** Members seeking credit for prior non-covered police service must pay a **single lump sum equal to actuarial value** by a specified deadline (the bill text reiterates/retains this structure).

## Health Impact
- Expands/clarifies duty-connected disability pension eligibility for a member disabled by an act normally occurring while employed as a **police officer, firefighter, paramedic, or emergency medical technician**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 11 (Police/Firefighter Pension Plan):**
  - Updates the definition of “Employee” to address paid firefighter/EMT roles and distinguishes employees hired **prior to** vs. **on/after** the Act’s effective date.  
  - Expands optional participation language so a **county, municipality, volunteer ambulance company, or volunteer fire company** may elect to participate beginning **July 1** of any year on/after **July 1, 1984**.  
  - Repeals **§ 8806** (Board powers/duties section), per synopsis, as duplicative of **29 Del. C. § 8308**.  
  - Updates disability pension provision to include **paramedic** and **emergency medical technician** in the covered occupations.  
  - Conforms terminology from “county or municipality” to “entity affiliated with the Fund” for employer contributions language.
- **Title 29 (County and Municipal Employees’ Retirement Plan):**
  - Revises “Employee” definition to **exclude** people (hired **on/after the Act’s effective date**) whose duties are **EMT, paid firefighter, or both** from participating in the County and Municipal Employees’ Fund.  
  - Revises “Employer” definition to include: **counties, municipalities, state governmental subdivisions (including Delaware State Housing Authority), volunteer ambulance companies, and volunteer fire companies** that affiliate with the Fund.  
  - Updates optional participation section consistent with the expanded list of eligible employers and keeps the **90-day** application submission timing and **irrevocability** after approval.

## Who Benefits
- **Paid EMTs** working for a participating employer, who become eligible for the **County and Municipal Police/Firefighter Pension Plan** (per synopsis).  
- **Participating employers/entities** (counties, municipalities, volunteer ambulance/fire companies, and certain state subdivisions including DSHA) that gain clearer statutory authority on plan participation.  
- **Members seeking duty-connected disability benefits** whose covered job categories are clarified to include EMTs/paramedics.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Firefighters and EMTs hired on/after the Act’s effective date** who would otherwise have participated in the **County and Municipal Employees’ Fund**; the Act excludes them and makes them eligible only for the Police/Firefighter Pension Fund (per synopsis).  
- **Individuals purchasing prior service credit** remain subject to a potentially significant **single lump-sum actuarial payment** requirement by a defined deadline (though this structure appears to be existing law as reflected in the amended text).
