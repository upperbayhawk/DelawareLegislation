## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Encourage the Delaware General Assembly to invest in out-of-school-time and summer programs for middle school–age youth, particularly youth who have “aged out” of traditional childcare but are not yet able to enter the workforce.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- This is a **House Concurrent Resolution No. 92 (153rd General Assembly)** urging increased investment in **middle school youth programs** (out-of-school and summer services).
- Cites Delaware child wellbeing indicators from **Kids Count 2016**: **21%** of children living in poverty; **38%** proficient in math by **grade 5**; **49%** proficient in reading by **grade 8**.
- References prior funding: **Section 1 of HB 200 (147th General Assembly)** appropriated funds to DSCYF Prevention/Early Intervention for statewide after-school programs focused on **youth violence and child suicide prevention**.
- Notes that the DSCYF Secretary, supported by the **Criminal Justice Council**, was authorized to work with the **Department of Education** to determine allocation of that funding.
- States that in **2021**, funding for **middle school afterschool programs** was **reallocated** to prioritize another age group initiative.
- Cites unmet afterschool need: **11.3 million** students nationwide unsupervised after school; **28,000** in Delaware (described as “almost **20%**” of Delaware students); **48,000** Delaware students would likely participate if available.
- Asserts outcomes and cost claims: students not in programs are **3 times** more likely to be truant/use drugs/use alcohol and **37%** more likely to become teen parents; “every **$1** invested” saves “at least **$9**.”
- Highlights a childcare eligibility gap: childcare subsidies under the **Child Care Development Block Grant / Purchase of Care** expire upon a child’s **13th birthday**, and youth **age 13+** are described as “most at risk” due to lack of afterschool access.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Policy focus on a service gap at age 13:** The resolution targets youth “as they age out of traditional childcare” when subsidies expire at the child’s **13th birthday**.
- **Supports access to structured afterschool time:** Responds to the cited number of Delaware students unsupervised after school (**28,000**) and potential participants (**48,000**).
- **Aligns with prevention goals referenced in prior appropriations:** Builds on the concept of after-school programming for **youth violence and child suicide prevention** referenced from HB 200 (147th GA).
- **Potential long-run savings claim:** The text asserts **$1 → at least $9** in savings (crime/welfare costs, school performance, learning potential), indicating a stated rationale for investment.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Nonbinding action:** As a concurrent resolution, it **commits** the General Assembly to investing but does **not** itself appropriate funding, create a program, or require agencies to act (no implementable mandate is specified).
- **No defined funding level or eligibility criteria:** The resolution does not specify **how much** to invest, **who qualifies**, or **what program standards** apply.
- **Relies on generalized outcome assertions:** Claims (e.g., **3 times** more likely for certain behaviors; **$1 saves $9**) are included without specifying the underlying studies or Delaware-specific evaluation methods within the resolution text.
