## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16, Chapter 20 (Uniform Health Data) to update and clarify Delaware’s uniform health data laws, including correcting the uniform billing form reference, explicitly including emergency department data in advisory committee scope, and making technical drafting corrections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates statutory references from the **UB-82** uniform billing form to **UB-04 (or successor form)** for the Delaware uniform claims and billing data set.  
- Confirms the **Division of Public Health (DHSS)** is the “state agency” responsible for compiling, analyzing, and reporting collected data.  
- Provides that collected data is limited to what is contained in the **Delaware uniform claims and billing data set (UB-04 or successor form)**.  
- Requires **hospitals** to submit data **electronically** via the state agency’s **Secure File Transfer Protocol (SFTP)** in a format agreed upon with the **Delaware Healthcare Association**.  
- Continues/clarifies **nursing home** electronic submission via the agency’s **SFTP** in a format agreed upon with the **Delaware Health Care Facilities Association**.  
- Expands/clarifies the **Hospital Discharge Technical Advisory Committee** scope to include **hospital discharge and emergency department data reporting** issues (including confidentiality).  
- Requires creation of county cancer-incidence maps by census tract **within 90 days of the agency receiving cancer incidence data**, and posting those maps on the agency website.  
- Retains enforcement structure: DHSS may act for willful violations, with **fair hearing** required first and **30-day** appeal right to Superior Court after notice of action.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Data standardization/clarity:** Correcting to **UB-04 (or successor form)** aligns law with the commonly used billing form, reducing confusion and improving consistency in submitted data.  
- **Improved emergency department data oversight:** Explicitly including **emergency department data reporting** in the advisory committee’s scope supports more comprehensive review of collection, dissemination, and confidentiality practices.  
- **Public transparency on cancer incidence:** Requires public availability of **cancer incidence by census tract and cancer type**, plus public-facing county maps posted online.  
- **Timeliness requirement for mapping:** The **90-day** deadline to create maps after receiving cancer incidence data promotes timely public reporting.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Operational compliance burden (electronic submission):** Hospitals must submit data electronically via **SFTP** in an agreed format; nursing homes must submit electronically via SFTP as agreed—this may require ongoing IT and workflow coordination (requirement stated in §2004).  
- **Potential confidentiality concerns at small-area geography:** Although the bill allows combining contiguous census tracts when needed to prevent identification, releasing cancer incidence by **census tract** may still raise privacy sensitivity (mitigated by the combining provision in §2005(a)).  
- **Limited specificity on submission schedules:** Submission schedules are set by the state agency, which could create uncertainty for providers until schedules are issued (§2004(e)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Submission schedule details:** The bill authorizes the agency to establish “schedules for the timely submission” and grant waivers for good cause, but does not specify deadlines/frequency in statute (§2004(e)).  
- **Cancer map format and incidence scaling:** The law requires color-coding to designate “degree of cancer incidence” but does not define the metric (e.g., rates vs. counts) or standardization approach (§2005(b)).  
- **Which website/which “agency”:** §2005 uses “the agency” regarding maps and posting; the definition section defines “state agency” as Division of Public Health, but the text does not restate it in §2005.

## Health Impact
- **Cancer surveillance transparency:** Requires public reporting of **cancer incidence by census tract and cancer type**, plus public county maps of overall incidence by tract, potentially improving community awareness and public health communication (§2005).  
- **Systemwide health data quality/availability:** Updates and clarifies collection/reporting for hospital inpatient discharges, emergency department visits, and nursing home inpatient discharges (§2003–§2004).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Corrects uniform billing form reference:** Replaces **UB-82** with **UB-04 (or successor form)** in multiple sections (§2003(a), §2004).  
- **Hospital data submission method clarified:** Hospitals must submit data **electronically** via the agency’s **SFTP** in a format agreed upon by the agency and the **Delaware Healthcare Association** (§2004(b)).  
- **Nursing home submission clarified:** Nursing homes submit electronically via SFTP in a medium/format agreed with the agency and **Delaware Health Care Facilities Association** (§2004(c)).  
- **Advisory committee scope clarified:** Hospital Discharge Technical Advisory Committee studies issues related to **hospital discharge and emergency department data reporting**, including confidentiality (§2003(d)).  
- **Cancer incidence mapping deadline and posting:** County maps must be created **within 90 days** of receiving cancer incidence data and must be posted to the agency website (§2005(b)-(c)).  
- **Technical drafting corrections:** Conforms language to legislative drafting standards (per Synopsis; reflected in “shall mean means,” “willfully,” etc.).

## Who Benefits
- **General public and communities:** Access to public records on cancer incidence by census tract/type and publicly posted maps (§2005).  
- **Health-care purchasers/insurers/providers:** Regular reports compiled and disseminated by the state agency (§2003(a)).  
- **Researchers/policymakers:** More clearly defined, standardized datasets (UB-04 or successor form; inclusion of ED data in committee scope).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Hospitals and nursing homes:** Ongoing compliance responsibilities for electronic data submission and review/correction process tied to state reporting (§2003(b), §2004).  
- **Entities concerned about privacy in small-area data:** Even with tract-combination allowed to avoid identification, publication by tract may be viewed as sensitive (§2005(a)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Cancer maps deadline:** Maps must be created **within 90 days of the agency receiving the cancer incidence data** (§2005(b)).  
- **Appeal deadline:** A hospital or nursing home may appeal DHSS enforcement action to Superior Court **within 30 days of being notified** (§2007(b)).  
- **Nursing home reporting start date (existing statutory language retained):** Nursing home inpatient discharge reporting begins **not sooner than June 30, 1995** (§2004(c)).  
- **General effective date of the Act:** Not specified in source text.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements:**
  - Hospitals: submit UB-04 (or successor) discharge and emergency department visit data electronically via **SFTP** (§2004(a)-(b)).  
  - Nursing homes: submit UB-04 (or successor) inpatient discharge data electronically via **SFTP** (§2004(c)).  
  - Providers may review submitted data and submit corrections during a specified pre-release period (§2003(b)).  
- **Sanctions/enforcement process (§2007):**
  - A hospital or nursing home that **willfully violates** the chapter shall be reported to DHSS, which **may take action to enforce compliance**.  
  - **No action** may be taken without providing an opportunity for a **fair hearing**.  
  - An aggrieved hospital or nursing home may appeal to **Superior Court within 30 days** of notice of the action.  
- **Immunity (§2008):**
  - Broad good-faith immunity from civil/criminal claims for actions taken under the chapter, with good faith presumed unless proven otherwise and malice to be shown by the complainant.
