## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 29 to create the “Innovating Delaware Act” tax credit program (“Innovate Delaware”) to support (1) technology-company accelerators and (2) certain small or ownership-diverse innovative companies in Delaware through a capped, annually authorized tax credit.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes the **Innovate Delaware** program and an **Innovating Delaware Tax Credit (IDTC)**.  
- Allows IDTC awards for two project types: **technology accelerators** (with an **economic impact report**) and **programs** that provide **funding/workforce development/resources** to qualifying Delaware innovative companies.  
- Defines qualifying company thresholds, including **<15 employees** and **< $1,000,000 average gross revenues** for **at least 3 years** (for one eligibility path), or **<75 employees** with **≥51% ownership/control** by certain groups (second path).  
- Limits the **total annual authorized funding** under the program to **$10,000,000 per calendar year**, for **calendar years beginning January 1, 2026**.  
- Limits the IDTC’s use so it **cannot reduce a taxpayer’s tax liability by more than 50%**; **unused credits carry forward up to 5 years**.  
- IDTC may be applied against **income taxes**, **state franchise tax**, or **other taxes** as allowed by the **Division of Revenue or Secretary of State**.  
- Requires applicants to have a **conflict-of-interest policy** consistent with **IRS Form 1023 instructions**, plus a **notarized affirmation** of compliance.  
- Administration: **Secretary of State** and **Division of Revenue** provide administrative assistance; **Department of Finance** must adopt implementing **rules**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Supports early-stage tech ecosystem development** by funding the “creation, operation, or support” of accelerators for technology companies (requires an economic impact report).  
- **Targets small firms** by including an eligibility path limited to companies with **fewer than 15 employees** and **average gross revenues under $1,000,000** for **at least 3 years** prior to the funding agreement.  
- **Incentivizes investment and participation** by allowing credits to offset multiple tax types (income taxes, franchise tax, and other allowed taxes), with a **5-year carryforward** for unused credits.  
- **Includes an ownership-diversity pathway** for companies **≥51% owned and controlled** by a **woman, minority, veteran, service-disabled veteran, or person with a disability** (and **<75 employees**), potentially broadening access within the “innovative industry” scope.  
- **Adds reporting and guardrails** via funding agreements requiring **annual reports** including an **economic impact report**, and requiring funds be used only for approved purposes.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces state revenues** through a capped but material credit authorization of up to **$10,000,000 per calendar year** (calendar years beginning **January 1, 2026**).  
- **Taxpayer benefit is limited but still substantial**: IDTC cannot reduce liability by more than **50%**, but can still significantly reduce tax obligations for participating taxpayers.  
- **Administrative complexity**: requires application review, funding agreements, annual reporting with economic impact reports, and rulemaking by the Department of Finance.  
- **Broad discretion in “innovative industry” definition** because it includes listed industries “**or any other industry as determined by Innovate Delaware**,” potentially making eligibility dependent on program determinations.

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates **Subchapter VIII-A, “Innovating Delaware Act,”** establishing the **Innovate Delaware** program and the **Innovating Delaware Tax Credit (IDTC)**.  
- Defines key terms, including:
  - **“Accelerator”** (fixed-term support for early-stage technology companies recruited to Delaware).  
  - **“Technology company”** (expects **≥75% of revenue** from specified fields such as education, healthcare, energy, agriculture, infrastructure, software, robotics, etc.).  
  - **“Innovative industry”** (listed industries plus others as determined by Innovate Delaware).  
- Establishes eligible projects for IDTC applications:
  1) Accelerator creation/operation/support (must include an **economic impact report**).  
  2) Programs providing funding/workforce development/resources for qualifying innovative companies meeting either:
     - **<15 employees** and **< $1,000,000 average gross revenues** for **at least 3 years**, or  
     - **<75 employees** and **≥51% owned/controlled** by a woman, minority, veteran, service-disabled veteran, or person with a disability.  
- Sets application requirements:
  - Conflict-of-interest policy consistent with **IRS Form 1023** instructions.  
  - **Notarized affirmation** that application submission did not violate that policy.  
  - Detailed plan showing the project meets statutory criteria.  
- Requires a **funding agreement** for approved applications, including:
  - Funds used only for approved purposes.  
  - **Annual reports** including an **economic impact report**.  
  - Notice upon project completion.  
- Creates tax credit mechanics and limits:
  - Credit can offset **income tax**, **state franchise tax**, or **other allowed taxes**.  
  - **50% maximum** reduction of taxpayer liability.  
  - **Carryforward up to 5 years**.  
  - **$10,000,000 per calendar year** cap beginning **January 1, 2026**.

## Who Benefits
- **Accelerators** that fund/mentor/train early-stage technology companies in Delaware and can support applications with an **economic impact report**.  
- **Delaware-headquartered for-profit innovative companies** that meet the eligibility thresholds (employee counts, revenue limit, and/or ownership/control criteria).  
- **Taxpayers** who can apply IDTC against eligible Delaware taxes (subject to the **50% liability reduction cap** and carryforward rules).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State General Fund** due to revenue losses from credits (up to **$10,000,000 annually**, plus **$50,000 one-time** programming cost per fiscal note).  
- **Applicants** facing compliance and administrative burdens (conflict-of-interest policy, notarized affirmation, annual reporting including economic impact reports, and use restrictions in funding agreements).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon signature by the Governor** (fiscal note assumption).  
- **Tax credits effective for calendar years beginning January 1, 2026** (fiscal note; also consistent with statutory cap language applying to calendar years beginning **January 1, 2026**).  
- **Annual cap applies per calendar year**: cumulative funding approved may not exceed **$10,000,000** in a calendar year for calendar years beginning **January 1, 2026**.  
- **Carryforward deadline**: unused credit may be carried forward **no more than 5 years**.
