## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Titles 10, 11, and 19 of the Delaware Code to expand and standardize rights and protections for crime victims and certain witnesses (including witnesses/victims with cognitive disabilities), strengthen notification and confidentiality rules, establish complaint/enforcement and reporting mechanisms, revise certain parole/pardon/expungement procedures affecting victim participation, update employment protections for victims and their representatives, and revise aspects of the Victims’ Compensation Fund and compensation caps.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands the scope of covered “crime” for Crime Victims’ Bill of Rights protections (11 Del. C. ch. 94) to include enumerated offenses and **any offense** that is against a **protected class** (10 Del. C. § 1041(2)), constitutes **abuse** (10 Del. C. § 1041(1)), or is the basis for a **sexual violence protective order** (10 Del. C. ch. 72), including offenses underlying abuse/domestic violence/sexual violence protective orders.
- Strengthens and clarifies victim and witness rights across proceedings, including notice and attendance rights, court-authorized “support person” rules (subject to defendant fair-trial protections), and requirements that explanations be understandable to victims/witnesses with **cognitive disabilities**.
- Expands confidentiality/FOIA protections for victim and witness identifying information (e.g., addresses, phone numbers, schools, workplaces, identities), with specified exceptions including a limited school-alert exception after traumatic events.
- Adds custody/post-sentencing notification duties, including: Attorney General notice to victims about **appeals/post-conviction** hearings/decisions; and DOC/DSCYF notices about projected release, community-based program releases, parole/pardon hearing dates, and **immediate escape notification by telephone or in person**.
- Creates/standardizes notice delivery preferences: notices may be **verbal, electronic, or printed** (unless otherwise required); victims may set/change a **preferred contact method** (including opt-out) and must update contact info; agencies must use the preferred method **when possible**; and (subject to a funding trigger) DELJIS/DOC must add **email notification** functionality.
- Establishes compliance accountability: DOJ enforcement role; each law-enforcement agency must designate a **supervisor** to receive/investigate noncompliance complaints and provide a **written investigation summary**; plus annual compliance reporting by agencies and statewide public reporting by the Criminal Justice Council (CJC).
- Updates employment protections (Title 19 § 711(i)), including prohibiting discrimination based on being a victim of **domestic violence, a sexual offense, or stalking**, requiring reasonable accommodations absent **undue hardship**, and prohibiting discharge/discipline of a **crime victim or victim’s representative** for participating in/attending proceedings under **Title 11 § 9409(a)**.
- Raises the Victims’ Compensation Assistance Program funeral/burial cap within “pecuniary loss” from **$5,000 to $7,500**, and revises Victims’ Compensation Fund administration including a **$6,000,000** unencumbered-balance threshold for transfers to the General Fund.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Broader coverage: more victims receive statutory rights/services due to the expanded definition of covered “crime” (including protected-class, abuse, and sexual-violence protective-order related offenses).
- Improved safety and awareness: adds required notifications for custody changes and mandates **immediate** escape notifications **by telephone or in person**.
- Greater accessibility: requires understandable explanations and specified accommodations for victims/witnesses with **cognitive disabilities**; provides for support persons (subject to fair-trial limits).
- Increased privacy: strengthens confidentiality and makes specified victim/witness identifying information non-public under FOIA, with limited exceptions.
- Clearer accountability: creates complaint pathways (agency supervisor + DOJ) and requires annual reporting and public statewide reporting (CJC).
- Improved workplace stability for victims: bars discharge/discipline tied to proceeding-related participation and requires reasonable accommodations absent undue hardship (Title 19 § 711(i)).
- Increased compensation support: raises funeral/burial expense cap to **$7,500**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Increased state and local administrative workload: expanded notice duties (DOJ, DOC/DSCYF, Board of Parole), complaint investigations, training, public-facing materials/website maintenance, and annual reporting (agencies and CJC).
- Added technology and staffing costs for DOJ/DELJIS/DOC to implement and maintain expanded notification systems and compliance supports (fiscal note lists one-time and recurring costs).
- Some service access is conditional: victim advocate presence is described as “**if available**,” and agencies are not required to make an advocate available.
- Limited remedies for noncompliance: no damages claims; specified sole civil/criminal remedy identified is standing to seek a **writ of mandamus** (10 Del. C. § 564); noncompliance does not invalidate convictions/sentences/dispositions; defendants lack standing to object.
- Email notice requirement may be delayed because § 9414(a)(4) is contingent on publication of an appropriation notice.

- **DOJ:** statewide training and compliance duties; enforcement on behalf of victims/witnesses; creates bilingual (English/Spanish) and disability-accessible rights summaries and maintains an accessible rights/resources website; staffing reflected in fiscal note as **4.0 FTE** total (3.25 GF + 0.75 ASF) Victim Service Specialists; provides “reasonable and necessary” Council support staff/materials.
- **Law-enforcement agencies:** provide expanded rights information at initial contact; must designate a **supervisor** to receive/investigate complaints and issue written findings; must provide a DOJ-created witness rights summary to witnesses; must submit annual compliance reports (starting **February 1, 2026**).
- **DELJIS/DOC:** must implement notification system changes, including an email-notice mechanism (DELJIS CJIS interface; DOC DACS Victim Notification module) subject to the bill’s effective/implementation triggers.
- **DOC/DSCYF:** expanded written custody/release notifications; respond to victim requests for supervision conditions and compliance/noncompliance; **immediate** escape notification by telephone or in person; DOC authorized to issue implementing rules/regulations.
- **Board of Parole:** must provide parole hearing notice at least **30 days** before the hearing; postponement for failure to provide required notice capped at **45 days** from the originally scheduled hearing date.
- **Courts/CJC:** courts may need accommodations for certain witnesses; CJC must compile/post/distribute statewide annual compliance reports (starting **April 30, 2026**).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Covered “crime” expanded** (11 Del. C. ch. 94) to include enumerated offenses and any offense tied to: protected class (10 Del. C. § 1041(2)); abuse (10 Del. C. § 1041(1)); or sexual violence protective order grounds (10 Del. C. ch. 72).
- **Definitions updated**, including “victim” (including certain family of a deceased victim; and, under § 9419, certain incorporated/locally recognized neighborhood or homeowners’ associations in specified illegal drug activity contexts) and “witness” (excluding law-enforcement/probation officers acting in official duties, per notes).
- **Notice and participation rights expanded/standardized**, including presence/attendance, support person rules, cognitive-disability accessibility, and standardized notice methods and preferences (verbal/electronic/printed; preferred contact method; opt-out; victim duty to update contact info).
- **Custody and post-conviction notifications expanded**, including appeals/post-conviction notice by the Attorney General and DOC/DSCYF custody/release/program/parole/pardon and escape notifications, plus requested supervision condition/compliance information.
- **Confidentiality and FOIA changes**: specified victim/witness identifying information protected and treated as non-public under FOIA, with enumerated exceptions including limited school notification.
- **Complaint/enforcement framework**: DOJ enforcement role; required agency supervisor complaint review and written findings; annual law-enforcement compliance reporting and statewide CJC reporting with public posting.
- **Immigration-related protections**: law enforcement may not detain or turn over (1) a qualifying victim (per § 9403A, as noted) or (2) an uncharged/unconvicted witness exclusively for actual/suspected immigration violations **absent a judicial warrant**.
- **Victims’ compensation**: funeral/burial cap raised from **$5,000 to $7,500**; penalty assessment deposits routed to a separate Victims’ Compensation Fund account; unencumbered balances **over $6,000,000** on **June 30** each fiscal year transferred to the General Fund (rule referenced as beginning fiscal year ending **June 30, 2002**).
- **Employment (Title 19 § 711(i))**: anti-discrimination for victims of domestic violence/sexual offense/stalking; reasonable accommodations absent undue hardship; prohibition on discharge/discipline for victims/victim representatives participating in/attending proceedings under Title 11 § 9409(a).
- **Parole/pardon/expungement-related procedures**: parole notice **≥ 30 days**; notice failure postponement cap **45 days**; certain expungement petitions require victim contact/position reporting; in § 4374 expungements AG response due within **120 days** after service; petitions rejected without SBI criminal history attached; AG must promptly orally inform victims of decisions and may not provide a copy of the expungement order.

## Who Benefits
- Victims covered by the expanded “crime” definition, including victims tied to protected-class offenses, abuse, and sexual violence protective-order related offenses.
- Victims and witnesses needing stronger confidentiality and FOIA privacy protections.
- Victims/witnesses with **cognitive disabilities** (and their families) receiving accessibility requirements, accommodations, and support-person provisions.
- Child witnesses (expedited trial right, as noted) and victims receiving prioritized advocate attendance (child/cognitive disability prioritization noted).
- Victims seeking more complete custody, appeal/post-conviction, parole, pardon, and expungement-related notifications and participation opportunities.
- Victims concerned about immigration consequences while cooperating, and uncharged/unconvicted witnesses protected from immigration-based detention/turnover absent a **judicial warrant**.
- Victims and representatives receiving workplace protections and accommodation rights under Title 19 § 711(i).
- Victims’ compensation claimants for funeral/burial expenses (cap increased to **$7,500**).
- Qualifying neighborhood/homeowners associations electing “victim” status for certain illegal drug activity offenses in their area (§ 9419).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State agencies and local law-enforcement** (DOJ, DELJIS, DOC, DSCYF, Board of Parole, courts, CJC) due to expanded duties, compliance processes, reporting, staffing, and IT changes.
- **Employers** due to added anti-discrimination, accommodation, and limits on discharge/discipline obligations under amended Title 19 § 711(i).
- Members of the public requesting access to victim/witness identifying information, due to expanded confidentiality/FOIA limits.
- Defendants/petitioners to the extent expanded victim participation and notification requirements add procedural steps (e.g., parole/pardon/expungement processes), and to the extent confidentiality rules limit access to certain witness identifying information except under enumerated exceptions.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Generally:** Effective upon signature by the Governor (noted in analysis), but several provisions have specific implementation triggers/deadlines below.
- **Within 90 days after enactment:** Title 11 **§ 9414(c), § 9414(d), and § 9426** are effective immediately and must be implemented **no later than 90 days after enactment**.
- **Funding-triggered effective date for email mechanism:** Title 11 **§ 9414(a)(4)** takes effect **10 days after** the Secretary of the Department of Safety and Homeland Security publishes a notice in the **Register of Regulations** that **funds have been appropriated**; must be implemented **no later than 18 months** after that notice publication date.
- **Annual reporting:**
  - **Beginning February 1, 2026:** law-enforcement agencies must submit annual compliance reports to CJC (§ 9417(b)).
  - **Beginning April 30, 2026:** CJC must compile a statewide annual report, post it online, and distribute it (§ 9417(c)-(d)).
- **Parole notice deadline:** parole hearing notice must be sent at least **30 days** before the scheduled hearing; failure-to-notify postponement may not exceed **45 days** from the original hearing date.
- **Expungement (§ 4374):** Attorney General objection/answer due within **120 days** after service.
- **Advisory Council vacancy:** Governor must appoint a successor **within 30 days** of a vacancy (per notes).
- **Victims’ Compensation Fund transfer threshold date:** on **June 30** each fiscal year, unencumbered balances **over $6,000,000** must be deposited into the General Fund (rule referenced as beginning fiscal year ending **June 30, 2002**).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Complaint and enforcement structure:** DOJ must enforce compliance on behalf of victims/witnesses; each law-enforcement agency must designate a **supervisor** to receive/investigate complaints, address them promptly, and provide a **written investigation summary** (including DOJ escalation instructions if the agency finds it complied).
- **Annual compliance reporting:** law-enforcement agencies must report complaint/investigation metrics and explanations of unmet requirements (starting **Feb. 1, 2026**); CJC must publicly post a statewide report (starting **Apr. 30, 2026**).
- **Confidentiality/FOIA compliance:** specified victim/witness identifying information is protected and not a public record under FOIA, subject to enumerated exceptions; agencies and courts must follow disclosure limits.
- **Immigration-related compliance:** law enforcement may not detain or turn over a qualifying victim (per § 9403A as noted) or an uncharged/unconvicted witness exclusively for actual/suspected immigration violations **absent a judicial warrant**.
- **Employment compliance (Title 19 § 711(i)):** unlawful employment practices include discrimination based on victim status (domestic violence/sexual offense/stalking), failure to provide reasonable accommodation absent undue hardship, and discharge/discipline tied to participation in/attendance at Title 11 § 9409(a) proceedings. **Specific penalties/remedies are not specified in the provided notes.**
- **Limits on remedies for victims (as noted):** no damages claims; identified sole remedy is standing to seek a **writ of mandamus** under **10 Del. C. § 564**; noncompliance does not invalidate convictions/sentences/dispositions; defendants lack standing to object.
