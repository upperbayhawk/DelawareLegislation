## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware personal income tax law to temporarily exempt certain overtime compensation from Delaware state income tax and require employer reporting of overtime compensation data to the Division of Revenue.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Exempts from Delaware personal income tax amounts received by a **full-time hourly wage-paid employee** for **work performed in excess of 40 hours in a week**.  
- The exemption applies for **taxable years beginning January 1, 2026, and ending before January 1, 2028** (i.e., tax years **2026 and 2027**).  
- Requires employers to report to the Division of Revenue: (1) **total overtime compensation** paid to full-time hourly wage-paid employees and (2) **total number of employees** receiving such overtime.  
- Employers must also report the same overtime data for the **tax year beginning January 1, 2025**, due **no later than January 31, 2026**.  
- For tax years **2026–2027**, employer overtime data is due **at the same time as the employer’s withholding return under 30 Del. C. § 1154**.  
- The Division of Revenue must transmit compiled employer data to specified state officials **within 30 days after** the data is due.  
- Fiscal note estimates General Fund revenue loss of **$31.4 million (FY2027)** and **$32.6 million (FY2028)**; **$0 (FY2026)**.  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Tax relief for eligible workers:** Full-time hourly wage-paid employees may owe **less Delaware income tax** on overtime compensation for tax years **2026 and 2027** (overtime defined as **over 40 hours/week**).  
- **Improved data for fiscal oversight:** Required employer reporting and Division reporting deadlines provide the Controller General, OMB, and others with data about overtime compensation and affected employee counts.  
- **Time-limited policy:** The exemption ends for taxable years **beginning on/after January 1, 2028**, limiting long-term revenue exposure per the bill text.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **General Fund revenue loss:** Fiscal note estimates losses of **$31.4 million in FY2027** and **$32.6 million in FY2028** (with **$0 in FY2026**).  
- **Employer administrative burden:** Employers paying wages subject to withholding must submit new overtime totals and employee counts (including a **one-time 2025** data submission due **January 31, 2026**).  
- **State administrative workload:** Division of Revenue must collect, compile, and report employer data and may need regulations to administer/enforce the reporting requirement.

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new subtraction modification to Delaware taxable income: for tax years **beginning January 1, 2026 and ending before January 1, 2028**, subtract **amounts received** by a **full-time hourly wage-paid employee** as compensation for **work performed in excess of 40 hours in a week**.  
- Creates new employer reporting requirements to the Division of Revenue for overtime compensation totals and employee counts, including a **retroactive data report for tax year 2025**.  
- Requires the Division to provide compiled overtime data to specified state offices **within 30 days** after employer data is due.  
- Authorizes the Division to adopt regulations to administer/enforce the provision.
