## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Clarify Delaware’s statutory requirements for what qualifies as a “funeral establishment” and when a **funeral establishment permit** is required under **Title 24**, and make technical drafting corrections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates the definition of **“funeral establishment”** to expressly include a **place or office where funeral business matters are conducted**, not only locations where remains are prepared or services occur.  
- Confirms funeral establishments may include services related to **natural organic reduction**, alongside burial, entombment, and cremation.  
- Retains the grandfather rule that **satellite funeral establishments existing as of May 12, 1988** are **not required** to include an embalming area.  
- Clarifies that a person **may not** conduct, maintain, manage, or operate a funeral establishment **without a permit for each establishment** issued by the Board.  
- States that violating the permit requirement **is grounds for discipline** (professional/board discipline).  
- Keeps the exception that **no permit is required** when a funeral service is held in a **private residence, church, or lodge hall**, so long as the funeral director of record operates from a firm that has a funeral establishment permit.  
- Approved **August 25, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer licensing scope:** By explicitly including a “place or office” where funeral business is conducted in the definition of funeral establishment, the bill clarifies which locations may fall under permit requirements.  
- **Regulatory consistency:** Reaffirms that a permit is needed for **each** funeral establishment and that violations are grounds for discipline, supporting consistent Board oversight.  
- **Preserves limited venue flexibility:** Continues allowing services in a **private residence, church, or lodge hall** without requiring a permit for that venue, if tied to a permitted firm.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potentially broader coverage of “funeral establishment”:** Including an “office” where business matters are conducted could subject additional locations to the funeral establishment framework (definition change in §3101(8)); the bill text does not describe any exemptions beyond existing ones.  
- **Discipline-based enforcement:** The bill ties noncompliance to “grounds for discipline” without specifying penalty amounts or a graduated enforcement approach in this text.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What specific conduct constitutes “business matters associated with funeral services” for purposes of treating an office as part of a funeral establishment.  
- What specific disciplinary actions the Board may impose for a violation (the bill states “grounds for discipline” but does not list sanctions).  
- Whether and how the Board will interpret “permit for each establishment” when operations involve multiple offices, remote/virtual administrative locations, or shared facilities (not addressed in text).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Definition updated (§3101(8))**: A “funeral establishment” includes:
  - Any place used in the care and preparation of human remains for funeral service, burial, entombment, cremation, or **natural organic reduction**; and
  - Areas for embalming, viewing convenience, and other services associated with human remains; and
  - A **place or office** where “business matters associated with funeral services are conducted.”
- **Grandfathering preserved:** “Satellite funeral establishments existing as of **May 12, 1988**” are not required to include an embalming area.
- **Permit requirement clarified (§3117(b))**:
  - No person may conduct/maintain/manage/operate a funeral establishment unless a permit for **each** establishment has been issued by the Board.
  - Violation is **grounds for discipline**.
  - Exception retained: No permit required for services held in a **private residence, church, or lodge hall**, if the funeral director of record operates from a firm with a funeral establishment permit.

## Who Benefits
- **State licensing/disciplinary authorities (the Board):** Clearer statutory language supporting oversight of funeral establishment locations, including business offices.  
- **Consumers/families:** Clarified regulatory framework for what constitutes a funeral establishment and confirmation that unpermitted operation is disciplinable.  
- **Existing satellite establishments (pre–May 12, 1988):** Continued exemption from the requirement to include an embalming area.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Funeral service businesses operating separate offices:** Locations used for funeral business matters may more clearly fall under the “funeral establishment” definition, potentially increasing compliance obligations (definition change).  
- **Individuals/entities operating without permits:** Explicit confirmation that operating without a permit is grounds for discipline.
