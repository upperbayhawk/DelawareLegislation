## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
House Bill 10 concurs in “technical corrections” constitutional amendments previously adopted by a **two-thirds vote of each House** in the **152nd General Assembly** (Chapter 281, Volume 84, Laws of Delaware) so that, upon concurrence by the **153rd General Assembly**, the amendments become part of the Delaware Constitution. The amendments revise and clarify provisions across **Articles I–IV** (and related constitutional fiscal, transportation, education, election/convention, court/probate, and ethics provisions), including redistricting procedures and enforcement, court terminology, election administration and offenses, fiscal guardrails, Transportation Trust Fund protections, school finance/tax rules, and certain appointment/residency requirements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Adoption mechanism:** Makes prior “technical corrections” amendments part of the Delaware Constitution upon concurrence by the **153rd General Assembly** (following prior **two-thirds** passage in the **152nd General Assembly** and required publication).
- **Fiscal rules:** Limits aggregate appropriations to **98%** of estimated revenues; allows **98%–100%** appropriations only for emergencies (including “health, safety, or welfare”) by **three-fifths** vote; requires Budget Reserve deposits **within 45 days** after fiscal year end, capped at **5%** (with certain withdrawals requiring **three-fifths** vote).
- **Tax/fee voting threshold:** Requires a **three-fifths** vote to impose/levy any State tax or license fee or increase an effective rate; makes **retroactive personal income tax rate increases void**.
- **Transportation Trust Fund (TTF) protections:** TTF proceeds are continuously appropriated; appropriations for non-listed purposes require **three-fourths** concurrence of each House and must be enacted in a **separate act** (not the annual budget, bond/capital, or grants-in-aid acts) (reference noted: **May 18, 2017**).
- **Redistricting timelines and judicial enforcement:** Governor must file a reapportionment plan within **120 calendar days** after official decennial census reporting to the President of the U.S. (or **120 days after the amendment takes effect**); Secretary of State must publish a proclamation in **2 newspapers** for **2 consecutive weeks** within **20 days**; proclamation effective within **30 days**; **Superior Court** actions generally have **30-day** filing windows, with **Supreme Court** appellate review.
- **Elections and election offenses:** General election on the **Tuesday next after the first Monday in November**; canvass at **10:00 a.m. the second day after the election**; election offenses are **misdemeanors** with fines **$100–$5,000** and/or **1 month–3 years** imprisonment, and many include **10-year** voting incapacity after sentence; prosecution by Attorney General information **without grand or petit jury**; appeal **within 3 calendar months**.
- **Courts, probate, and appointments:** Updates legacy court references to read/print as **“Superior Court”** and certain “Chief Justice” references as **“President Judge of the Superior Court”** (tied to **May 14, 1951**); judicial terms **12 years**; nomination timing **30 days before to 90 days after** a vacancy; Governor public notice letter **≥ 10 days** before Senate submission; oath required **within 30 days** or appointment/confirmation forfeited; holdover limited to **≤ 90 days**; executor/administrator notice due **within 3 months after settlement**.
- **Education and ethics/residency:** Requires annual payment of **not less than $100,000** for free public schools (teacher salaries and free textbooks); restricts educational funds aiding sectarian schools; school-district dedicated property-tax receipts cannot be repurposed without approval by a **majority of eligible district voters voting on the question**; legislators with a personal/private interest must disclose and **shall not vote**; Secretary of State must establish Delaware residency within **6 months** (possible **one** additional **6-month** extension) and maintain it or face **automatic resignation**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer fiscal discipline rules:** Concrete constitutional limits and voting thresholds (e.g., **98%** appropriations cap; **three-fifths** emergency/tax votes; **5%** reserve cap; **45-day** deposit deadline) provide explicit guardrails.
- **Limits retroactive tax changes:** Declares **retroactive personal income tax rate increases void**, providing clearer taxpayer protection.
- **Stronger protection of transportation revenues:** Restricts non-listed-purpose use of the **Transportation Trust Fund** via a **three-fourths** vote and **separate-act** requirement.
- **Enforceable redistricting process:** Adds specific timelines (**120/20/30 days**) and court enforcement in **Superior Court** with **Supreme Court** review.
- **Modernized court terminology and clarified judicial transitions:** Standardizes legacy references (e.g., “Superior Court”), sets quorum rules (Supreme Court quorum **≥3**; full Court for death-sentence cases), and clarifies vacancy/oath/holdover timing (**30–90 day** nomination window; oath **within 30 days**; holdover **≤90 days**).
- **More explicit probate notice requirement:** Executor/administrator must provide written notice **within 3 months after settlement** to covered in-state persons/guardians (scope not fully defined in notes).
- **Ethics transparency:** Requires disclosure and mandatory **no-vote** rule when a legislator has a personal/private interest.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher legislative thresholds reduce flexibility:** **Three-fifths** votes for taxes/fees and certain fiscal actions, and **three-fourths** votes plus a **separate act** for certain TTF appropriations, may make policy changes harder to enact.
- **Budget constraints may limit responsiveness absent emergency votes:** The **98%** cap and reserve rules constrain appropriations unless an emergency is declared and approved by **three-fifths** vote.
- **Election-offense process and penalties are significant:** Misdemeanor penalties of **$100–$5,000** and/or **1 month–3 years** imprisonment, plus frequent **10-year** voting incapacity; prosecution by information **without grand or petit jury**.
- **Administrative complexity in redistricting descriptions and implementation:** Reliance on detailed/legacy geographic boundary references (e.g., wards/Hundreds/roads/streams) may increase implementation burden.
- **Automatic resignation consequence:** Secretary of State residency noncompliance results in **automatic resignation** (residency required within **6 months**, with possible single **6-month** extension for “good cause”).
- **Agricultural conversion liability:** Preferential valuation requires **two (2)** successive years of active agricultural use; conversion triggers “rollback” additional taxes for the current year plus prior years (**not less than two (2)** years).
