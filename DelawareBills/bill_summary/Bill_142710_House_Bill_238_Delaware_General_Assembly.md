## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To restrict disclosure by Delaware Department of Finance (DOF) and Department of Labor (DOL) employees of any information relating to a person’s citizenship or immigration status that appears in tax-related or agency records, unless specific legal conditions are met.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits DOF employees from disclosing citizenship or immigration status information contained in or attached to **federal or state tax returns** or other **state tax/DOF documents**, except under limited circumstances.  
- Prohibits DOL employees from disclosing citizenship or immigration status information contained in or attached to any **DOL document or database**, except under limited circumstances.  
- Allows disclosure only **(1)** with a **court order associated with a felony criminal investigation**, **(2)** with **Attorney General approval**, or **(3)** “**as otherwise provided by law**.”  
- Adds/establishes that violations (explicitly stated for DOL, and stated in the bill synopsis for DOF) are **misdemeanors**.  
- For DOL violations: penalty is **fine up to $1,000**, **imprisonment up to 6 months**, or **both**.  
- For DOL violations: **Superior Court** has **exclusive original jurisdiction** over the misdemeanor.  
- Amends **Title 30, §368** (DOF confidentiality) and adds a new confidentiality rule in **Title 19, §118** (DOL disclosure of information).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Enhanced privacy protections:** Limits release of citizenship/immigration status information held in DOF tax records and DOL records, which could reduce inappropriate dissemination of sensitive personal information.  
- **Clearer guardrails for agency staff:** Establishes explicit restrictions and permitted disclosure pathways (felony-investigation court order, Attorney General approval, or other legal authority).  
- **Deterrence via criminal penalties (at least for DOL in bill text):** The misdemeanor provision with defined penalties may discourage unauthorized disclosures.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **May constrain information-sharing:** DOF/DOL employees may be unable to share citizenship/immigration status information unless one of the specified exceptions applies (felony-investigation court order, Attorney General approval, or other law).  
- **Potential administrative burden:** Requiring **Attorney General approval** for certain disclosures may add process steps and slow response timelines where disclosures might otherwise occur (the bill text does not provide an expedited process).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 30 (DOF):** Adds a new restriction making it unlawful for DOF officers/employees to disclose citizenship/immigration status information contained in or attached to a **federal or state tax return** or other **state tax/DOF document**, except:  
  - with a **court order associated with a felony criminal investigation**, or  
  - with **Attorney General approval**, or  
  - **as otherwise provided by law**.  
- **Title 19 (DOL):** Creates/sets disclosure limits in **§118** making it unlawful for DOL officers/employees to disclose citizenship/immigration status information contained in or attached to any **DOL document or database**, except:  
  - with a **court order associated with a felony criminal investigation**, or  
  - with **Attorney General approval**, or  
  - **as otherwise provided by law**.  
- **Criminal enforcement (explicit in §118):** Any violation of Title 19 §118 is a **misdemeanor** punishable by **up to $1,000 fine**, **up to 6 months imprisonment**, or **both**; **Superior Court** has **exclusive original jurisdiction**.
