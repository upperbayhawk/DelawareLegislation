## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Adopts the **Dentist and Dental Hygienist Compact** in Delaware (Title 24, Chapter 11, Subchapter III-A) to facilitate **interstate practice** of dentistry and dental hygiene through a **“compact privilege”** for qualified licensees from other participating states, while preserving each state’s authority to regulate practice within its borders. (24 Del. C. § 1151)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes an **interstate compact** allowing a dentist or dental hygienist licensed in a **participating state** to practice in other participating states via a **compact privilege**, without obtaining a full additional state license. (§§ 1151, 1152, 1154)
- Requires participating states (including Delaware, if admitted/maintained) to **share licensure and discipline data** through a **Commission data system** and report adverse actions and significant investigative information. (§§ 1153, 1158)
- Creates the **Dentist and Dental Hygienist Compact Commission**, a joint governmental agency with **1 Commissioner per participating state** and rulemaking authority. (§§ 1157, 1159)
- Sets baseline eligibility for compact privileges, including a **qualifying (unencumbered) license**, graduation from **CODA-accredited** programs (or other recognized accreditor), passage of required exams, and **clinical assessment** completion. (§§ 1153, 1154)
- Allows remote states to enforce their laws: a **remote state** may **revoke/remove** a compact privilege and **impose fines** or other actions to protect health and safety; certain adverse actions apply across **all remote states**. (§ 1154(d))
- Establishes fee authority: licensees may owe **Commission fees** and **remote state fees**; participating states must pay a **participation fee** and may be assessed an annual assessment by formula. (§§ 1153(a)(12), 1154(a)(4), 1157(c)(12), 1157(e)(3))
- Provides **military-related fee relief**: the Commission fee for a compact privilege **must be waived** for an **active military member or their spouse**; a remote state may reduce/waive its own fee. (§ 1155)
- Compact becomes operational only after a multi-state trigger: it **takes effect when enacted in the 7th participating state**. (§ 1161(a))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved access and mobility**: Creates a “responsible, streamlined pathway” for licensed dentists and dental hygienists to practice across participating states, intended to increase access and address workforce shortages. (§ 1151(e)(2)-(3))
- **Reduced duplicative licensure burden**: Allows qualified practitioners to practice in other participating states “without satisfying burdensome and duplicative requirements” of obtaining another license. (§ 1151(e)(1))
- **Stronger cross-state disciplinary awareness**: Requires data sharing (adverse actions, significant investigative information, certain alternative program participation) through a coordinated database. (§ 1158)
- **State-level consumer protection preserved**: Remote states retain authority to regulate practice within their borders and to take adverse actions, including revoking/removing privileges and imposing fines. (§ 1154(c)-(d))
- **Military family support**: Waives Commission compact privilege fees for active military members and spouses, with optional additional remote-state fee reductions. (§ 1155)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **New administrative obligations**: Participating states must fully participate in the Commission’s **data system**, report uniform datasets, and notify the Commission of adverse actions and significant investigative information. (§§ 1153(a)(2), 1153(a)(4), 1158(b))
- **Costs/fees may increase for the State and licensees**: Participating states must pay a **participation fee** and may face **annual assessments**; licensees may face **Commission and remote-state fees** for privileges/renewals. (§§ 1153(a)(12), 1157(e)(3), 1157(c)(12), 1154(a)(4))
- **Commission rules have force of law** in participating states, and participating states’ own rulemaking procedures do not apply to Commission rules. (§ 1159(b), § 1159(n))
- **Complex multi-state governance and enforcement**: Compact includes default/termination processes and authorizes Commission litigation against a state in default (and vice versa), potentially increasing legal/administrative complexity. (§ 1160)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adopts the Compact** into Delaware law as **Title 24, Chapter 11, Subchapter III-A (§§ 1151–1163)**. (Section 1; Synopsis)
- Creates and recognizes a **“compact privilege”** pathway for interstate practice in participating states. (§§ 1151(c), 1152(7), 1154)
- Commits Delaware (as a participating state) to **data reporting** and operational standards for participation (including complaint investigations and criminal background checks). (§ 1153; § 1158)
- Establishes participation in a multi-state **Commission** with rulemaking authority whose rules have force of law. (§§ 1157, 1159)
- Sets an interstate framework for **adverse actions**, investigations, subpoenas, and cross-state effect of certain adverse actions. (§§ 1154(d), 1156)

## Who Benefits
- **Dentists and dental hygienists** holding an **unencumbered (qualifying) license** in a participating state seeking to practice in other participating states without obtaining separate licenses. (§§ 1151, 1154)
- **Patients/public** in participating states, through increased access to providers as described in the Compact’s purposes. (§ 1151(b), § 1151(e)(3))
- **Active military members and spouses** who receive a **Commission fee waiver** for compact privileges (and potentially reduced/waived remote-state fees if offered). (§ 1155)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State licensing authorities/administration** due to added obligations to submit data, monitor database, implement/report background checks, and participate in Commission governance/fees. (§§ 1153, 1157(e), 1158(e))
- **Licensees** may face new or additional **fees** for compact privilege issuance/renewal and must comply with reporting and service-of-process consent requirements. (§ 1154(a)(4), (10)-(12); § 1157(c)(12))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Compact effective date (multi-state trigger):** Takes effect **on the date it is enacted into law in the 7th participating state**. (§ 1161(a))
- **Adverse action reporting by licensee:** Must report to the Commission adverse action taken by any non-participating state **when applying** and otherwise **within 30 days** of the adverse action. (§ 1154(a)(10))
- **Commission meeting notice:** Public meeting notice must be posted **at least 30 days** before the meeting; emergency meetings require **at least 24 hours** notice. (§ 1157(d)(1)-(2))
- **Commissioner vacancy:** Must be filled **within 60 days** of vacancy. (§ 1157(b)(5))
- **Rulemaking timelines:**
  - Proposed rule notice: at least **30 days** before the public hearing. (§ 1159(f))
  - Non-emergency rule effective date: **not sooner than 30 days** after adoption notice. (§ 1159(k)(3))
  - Emergency rule: usual procedures applied retroactively **as soon as reasonably possible, but not later than 90 days** after the emergency rule’s effective date. (§ 1159(l))
  - Rule rejection window: If a majority of participating state legislatures reject within **4 years** of adoption, rule loses effect. (§ 1159(d))
  - Technical revisions challenge period: **30 days** after posting. (§ 1159(m))
- **Withdrawal from Compact:** Effective **180 days after** enactment of the repealing law. (§ 1161(b)(1))
- **Post-termination/withdrawal recognition of privileges:** A terminating or withdrawing state must continue recognizing existing compact privileges for **a minimum of 180 days after** the date of notice. (§ 1160(f); § 1161(b)(3))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Remote state enforcement powers:** May revoke/remove compact privileges for a specified period, impose **fines**, or take other necessary actions to protect health and safety; certain adverse actions limiting a privilege apply to **all compact privileges in all remote states**. (§ 1154(d))
- **Loss of privileges due to encumbrance:** If a participating-state license becomes **encumbered**, the licensee **loses compact privileges** and is ineligible until no longer encumbered. (§ 1154(e)-(f))
- **Alternative program restriction:** Licensees in an alternative program in lieu of discipline must agree **not to practice under a compact privilege in any other participating state during the program** without prior authorization from the other state. (§ 1156(c))
- **Subpoenas and investigations:** Remote states may issue subpoenas; other participating states’ courts must enforce them; issuing authority pays witness/travel costs per service statutes. (§ 1156(e)(2))
- **Commission enforcement against states:** Commission may issue default notices and offer technical assistance; may **terminate** a state for uncured default by majority vote; may initiate legal action in federal court to enforce compliance; prevailing party awarded costs and reasonable attorney fees in specified actions. (§ 1160(b)-(c), (j)(2), (h))
- **Service of process consent:** Licensees must consent to service of process and subpoenas by mail at primary residence on record with the Commission. (§ 1154(a)(12))
