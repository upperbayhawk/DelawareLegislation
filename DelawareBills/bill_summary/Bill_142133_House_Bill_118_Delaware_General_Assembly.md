## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend the definition of “nursing facility” in Title 30, Chapter 65 to ensure Exceptional Care for Children (ECC) can continue to be exempt from the nursing facility quality assessment when it opens a “Bridge Unit” that serves a small number of individuals who turn 21 while in ECC’s care.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Changes the “nursing facility” definition used for the nursing facility quality assessment in **30 Del. C. Ch. 65**.  
- Replaces an exclusion for facilities that **“exclusively serve children”** with an exclusion for facilities that **maintain an average daily census of 80% or more** serving children **under 21**.  
- The practical effect described in the synopsis is to allow **Exceptional Care for Children (ECC)** to keep its exemption even if it serves some patients after they turn **21** in a transitional “Bridge Unit.”  
- Continues existing exclusions for **Delaware Veterans Home** and **state/federal/other public government-owned facilities**.  
- Establishes a clear **percentage threshold (80%)** for the children-serving exemption.  
- The enrolled law indicates the Act was **Approved August 25, 2025**.  
- No dollar amounts, tax/assessment rates, or new penalties are stated in the provided text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Preserves ECC’s exemption** from the nursing facility quality assessment despite serving a small number of patients who turn **21** (explicitly stated in the synopsis as the intent).  
- **Creates a measurable standard** for the exemption by using an **“average daily census of 80% or more”** serving children **under 21**, rather than an “exclusively” test.  
- May support **continuity of care/transition planning** for individuals who age out (turn **21**) while in ECC’s care via the Bridge Unit (as described in synopsis).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Narrows the prior categorical exclusion for facilities that **“exclusively serve children”** by replacing it with a specific test (**80%+ average daily census under 21**); facilities that previously qualified under an “exclusive” standard may need to assess compliance under the new metric.  
- The new standard could create **measurement and documentation needs** (tracking “average daily census” and age composition) for facilities seeking the exemption (implied by the new threshold language).
