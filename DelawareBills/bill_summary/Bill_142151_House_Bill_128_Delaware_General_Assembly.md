## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML) Insurance Program (Title 19, Chapter 37) to (1) correct/clarify benefit-duration limits, (2) change the “once per period” eligibility limitation from 24 months to 12 months, (3) make PFML the primary payor and require coordination with other paid leave benefits (including allowing disability offsets), (4) adjust private plan documentation requirements, and (5) create a Paid Leave Advisory Committee.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Clarifies a 6-week cap**: The **maximum aggregate** weeks of **medical leave + family caregiving leave** payable under § 3702(a)(2)–(4) is **6 weeks in an application year** (correcting an error).  
- **Changes frequency limit**: For benefits **other than parental leave**, a covered individual may receive PFML benefits **not more than once in a 12-month period** (previously **24-month** period).  
- **PFML becomes “primary payor”**: The PFML program is the **primary payor**; **other available paid leave benefits must be coordinated** per the governing policy/procedure.  
- **Disability offset permitted**: **Disability insurance benefits may be offset** by PFML benefits **if allowed by the disability policy terms**.  
- **Private plan documentation reduced**: Employers meeting obligations via an approved **private plan** generally **do not have to provide claim documentation** to the Department of Labor **unless** there is an **appeal, complaint, audit, or specific inquiry**.  
- **Private plan applications rolling**: Department must accept private plan applications on a **rolling basis**, with effective dates of **January 1, April 1, July 1, or October 1**.  
- **Creates a 9-member advisory committee**: Establishes a **Paid Leave Advisory Committee**; Department staff must consult **monthly through December 31, 2028**, then **quarterly starting January 1, 2029**.  
- **Fiscal note—IT update**: Requires LaborFirst portal updates; **one-time cost ~$184,000 in FY 2026**; claim payout impact is **indeterminable** but may increase PFML fund liability.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clarifies statutory benefit limits**: Explicitly states the **6-week aggregate cap** for medical + family caregiving leave benefits **in an application year**, reducing confusion caused by the prior codification error.  
- **More frequent access (non-parental)**: Moving from **once per 24 months** to **once per 12 months** (except parental leave) can allow eligible individuals to access benefits **more often**, consistent with the amended eligibility rule.  
- **Clearer coordination rules**: Designating PFML as **primary payor** and requiring coordination with other paid leave may reduce duplicative payments and clarify how multiple benefits interact.  
- **Administrative relief for private plan employers**: Limits routine claim documentation submissions to the Department, except in specified oversight situations (appeal/complaint/audit/inquiry).  
- **Structured stakeholder input**: Advisory committee (employee and employer representation) provides a formal channel to review implementation/administration and proposed statutory/regulatory amendments.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential increase in PFML fund liability**: The Department believes changes **may increase claim payouts**; fiscal note states the payout impact is **indeterminable** but could increase PFML fund liability.  
- **Offsets may reduce disability payments**: Allowing disability insurance benefits to be **offset** by PFML (when permitted by policy terms) could reduce net disability benefits in situations where both would otherwise be payable.  
- **Reduced routine documentation visibility**: Less routine claim documentation from private plan employers could reduce day-to-day transparency to the Department (while still permitting access via appeal/complaint/audit/inquiry).  
- **Implementation cost**: Requires a **one-time IT update** estimated at **$184,000 (FY 2026)**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Benefit duration clarification**: Maximum aggregate weeks of **medical leave + family caregiving leave** benefits payable under § 3702(a)(2)–(4) is **6 weeks in an application year**.  
- **Eligibility frequency change**: Except for parental leave benefits (and as permitted under § 3706), covered individuals may receive benefits **not more than once in a 12-month period** (replacing **24-month** period).  
- **Coordination / primary payor**:
  - PFML program is the **primary payor**.
  - **Other available paid leave benefits must be coordinated** according to the governing policy/procedure.
  - Employers may require PFML payments to run concurrently or be coordinated with disability/family care leave under CBA or employer policy, with **written notice** to employees.
  - **Disability insurance benefits may be offset** by PFML benefits **pursuant to disability policy terms**.  
- **Private plans**:
  - Private plans must allow the maximum leave in the required **benefit year / 12-month period** (updated cross-reference consistent with the 12-month change).
  - Department must accept private plan applications on a **rolling basis**; approvals effective **Jan 1, Apr 1, Jul 1, Oct 1**.
  - Private plan employers generally **not required** to provide claim documentation unless **appeal, complaint, audit, or specific inquiry**.
  - Employers with **fewer than 25 employees** that voluntarily elect to provide otherwise-not-required coverage become subject to **all provisions** of Chapter 37 (including employee appeal rights under § 3711).  
- **Advisory committee created**:
  - **Paid Leave Advisory Committee** established by the Secretary.
  - **9 members**: 1 Paid Leave Division representative (chair), **4 employee reps**, **4 employer reps** (including at least one representing employers with **fewer than 25**).
  - **Two-year terms**, reappointment allowed; vacancies filled for remainder of term.
  - Consultation: **monthly through 12/31/2028**; **quarterly beginning 1/1/2029**.

## Who Benefits
- **Covered individuals** seeking PFML benefits, particularly those needing non-parental leave more than once within a 24-month span (due to the shift to a **12-month** limitation).  
- **Employers using approved private plans**, due to reduced routine claim documentation submissions to the Department (except in specified oversight circumstances).  
- **Employers and plan administrators** needing clearer statutory rules on coordination of PFML with other paid leave and disability benefits.  
- **Stakeholders (employees and employers)** participating in or represented by the new advisory committee.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **PFML fund / program finances** (and potentially contributors) if claim payouts increase; fiscal note states increased liability is possible but **indeterminable**.  
- **Employees with disability policies** that allow offsets, if disability benefits are reduced by PFML amounts under the policy terms.  
- **Small employers (<25 employees) with private plans** that voluntarily elect to offer otherwise-not-required coverage, because they become subject to **all** Chapter 37 provisions (including appeal rights and other requirements).
