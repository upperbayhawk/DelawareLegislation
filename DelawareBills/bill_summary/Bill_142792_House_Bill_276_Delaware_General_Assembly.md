## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s theft statute (11 Del. C. § 841) to allow aggregation of a “series of related thefts” into a single charge when committed as part of a continuing course of conduct, and to clarify how conduct across multiple Delaware jurisdictions may be used as evidence of that course of conduct.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Allows prosecutors to **aggregate multiple related thefts** into **one count/charge** when committed in a **continuing course of conduct**.  
- Defines a **“continuing course of conduct”** as a **pattern of actions composed of more than 1 act over a period of time, regardless of duration**.  
- Aggregation uses the **sum of the aggregate value** to determine theft grading (misdemeanor vs. felony class).  
- Allows theft acts occurring in **more than 1 Delaware jurisdiction** to be used by **another jurisdiction where an act occurred** as evidence of a continuing pattern/course of conduct.  
- Retains theft grading thresholds including **$1,500**, **more than $50,000 but less than $100,000**, and **$100,000 or more**.  
- Maintains enhanced grading rules where the victim is **62+**, an **“adult who is impaired”** (31 Del. C. § 3902), or a **“person with a disability”** (12 Del. C. § 3901(a)(2)).  
- Makes “technical corrections” to conform to the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (no specific technical changes detailed beyond the shown edits).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Addresses repeat theft patterns**: By allowing multiple thefts to be combined into a single case using an aggregate value, the bill can enable charging that reflects the **total harm** where thefts are “related” and part of a “continuing course of conduct.” (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **Improves cross-jurisdiction coordination within Delaware**: Permits acts occurring in one Delaware jurisdiction to be used as evidence of a continuing pattern in another jurisdiction **where an act occurred**. (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **Clarifies charging approach**: Provides statutory language explaining what qualifies as a “continuing course of conduct.” (New § 841(c)(4))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher exposure to felony charges through aggregation**: Combining multiple lower-value thefts can raise the “value considered” for grading, potentially moving conduct above thresholds like **$1,500**, **>$50,000**, or **$100,000**. (New § 841(c)(4); existing § 841(c)(1)-(3))  
- **Broader evidentiary reach across jurisdictions**: Allowing multi-jurisdiction acts to be used as evidence of a pattern may expand the amount of conduct introduced in a case. (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **Unclear limits on “related thefts”**: The statute defines “continuing course of conduct” generally, but does not define “related thefts,” which could increase disputes and litigation over what may be aggregated. (New § 841(c)(4))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **What qualifies as “related thefts”?** The bill authorizes aggregation of “a series of related thefts” but does not define “related.” (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **How broad is “over a period of time, regardless of duration”?** The text does not specify any minimum/maximum time window for aggregation. (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **How will aggregation interact with charging discretion and venue rules?** The bill states jurisdictions may use acts from other jurisdictions as evidence, but does not specify procedural requirements for doing so. (New § 841(c)(4))

## Key Policy Changes
- **Aggregation authority added**: A “series of related thefts” in a continuing course of conduct **may be aggregated into 1 count or charge**, and the **sum** determines the theft grade. (Adds/clarifies § 841(c)(4))  
- **Definition included**: “Continuing course of conduct” = **pattern of actions composed of more than 1 act over a period of time, regardless of duration**. (Adds/clarifies § 841(c)(4))  
- **Cross-jurisdiction evidence rule**: Acts indicating a course of conduct occurring in **more than 1 jurisdiction of Delaware** may be used by **any other jurisdiction in which an act occurred** as evidence of the pattern/course of conduct. (Adds/clarifies § 841(c)(4))  
- **Technical drafting correction**: In § 841(c)(3)(a), removes an apparent stray word so the clause reads **“theft is a class D felony”** for **more than $50,000 but less than $100,000**. (Edit to § 841(c)(3)(a))

## Who Benefits
- **Theft victims** (including victims across multiple Delaware jurisdictions) where repeated thefts can be treated as a single aggregated pattern reflecting total value. (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **Law enforcement and prosecutors** seeking to charge theft schemes spanning multiple acts and/or jurisdictions within Delaware. (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **Older adults (62+)**, **impaired adults**, and **persons with disabilities**, to the extent existing enhanced theft grading remains applicable and may combine with aggregation where permitted. (§ 841(c)(2); new § 841(c)(4))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Defendants accused of multiple theft incidents**: aggregation can increase the “value considered,” potentially elevating charges to a **class G felony (≥ $1,500)**, **class D felony (>$50,000 and <$100,000)**, or **class B felony (≥ $100,000)**, depending on the aggregate. (§ 841(c)(1), (3); new § 841(c)(4))  
- **Defendants in multi-jurisdiction cases**: more conduct from other Delaware jurisdictions may be introduced as evidence of a pattern. (New § 841(c)(4))

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Charging/grading framework (existing, retained thresholds):**
  - Generally, theft is a **class A misdemeanor** unless value is **$1,500 or more**, then **class G felony** (except where special-victim rule applies). (§ 841(c)(1))  
  - If victim is **62+**, an **adult who is impaired**, or a **person with a disability**: theft is **class G felony** unless value is **$1,500 or more**, then **class F felony**. (§ 841(c)(2))  
  - If value is **more than $50,000 but less than $100,000**: **class D felony**. (§ 841(c)(3)(a))  
  - If value is **$100,000 or more**: **class B felony**. (§ 841(c)(3)(b))  
- **New compliance/standard for aggregation**: Multiple thefts may be charged as **one count** when they constitute a **continuing course of conduct**, and **aggregate value** determines the theft degree. (New § 841(c)(4))  
- **Evidence across jurisdictions**: Acts in multiple Delaware jurisdictions may be used as evidence of a continuing pattern in another jurisdiction where an act occurred. (New § 841(c)(4))
