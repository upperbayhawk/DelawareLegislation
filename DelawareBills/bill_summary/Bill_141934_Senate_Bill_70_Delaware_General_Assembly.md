## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 24 (professional regulation) to clarify that Delaware **licensed genetic counselors (L.G.C.s)** may **order genetic tests** as part of the defined “practice of genetic counseling,” and to make related technical/licensure updates.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands the statutory definition of the **“practice of genetic counseling”** to expressly include **“ordering of genetic tests.”**
- Requires a **referral to a physician licensed to practice medicine** if, while providing genetic counseling, the genetic counselor finds an **indication of disease or condition requiring medical assessment.**
- Retains that **genetic tests** are defined by reference to **Title 16, § 1201.**
- Keeps the Board overseeing licensure as the **Board of Medical Licensure and Discipline.**
- Confirms the scope includes **telemedicine** (as defined in the chapter) and, as further described in regulation, participation in **telehealth.**
- Makes a technical redesignation/renumbering in the definitions section (e.g., “L.G.C.” definition numbering).
- Updates licensure provision to require evidence of certification as a **genetic counselor (ABGC or ABMGG)** or **medical geneticist (ABMGG).**
- **Approved August 21, 2025** (as shown in the enacted chapter document).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves access and timeliness**: By authorizing L.G.C.s to **order genetic tests** directly, patients may obtain needed testing without waiting for a separate ordering clinician (explicitly allowed in § 1799H(9)(10)d).
- **Clarifies scope of practice**: Statutory language explicitly includes “ordering” genetic tests, reducing uncertainty for providers and labs (definition of practice now includes “ordering of genetic tests”).
- **Built-in medical escalation**: Requires referral to a physician when a condition requires medical assessment, providing a safety pathway (referral proviso in § 1799H(9)(10)d).
- **Supports telehealth practice**: Confirms genetic counseling practice includes telemedicine/telehealth participation (item k).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential role overlap/coordination burden**: Allowing genetic counselors to order tests may require clearer coordination workflows between genetic counselors and physicians, especially when referral is triggered (ordering authority plus mandatory referral language).
- **Referral trigger is broad**: The bill requires referral when there is “any indication” of a disease/condition requiring medical assessment, which may lead to frequent referrals depending on interpretation (language in § 1799H(9)(10)d).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Which genetic tests can be ordered**: The bill broadly allows “ordering of genetic tests” but does not specify any limits by test type, setting, or patient age; it relies on the Title 16, § 1201 definition.
- **Operational standards for telehealth**: Telehealth is included “as further described in regulation,” but this act does not specify what those regulations require.
- **Referral standard details**: “Any indication of disease or condition that requires medical assessment” is not further defined (e.g., who determines, documentation requirements, timing).
