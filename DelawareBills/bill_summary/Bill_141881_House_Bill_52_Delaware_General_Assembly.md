## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29, Chapter 19 of the Delaware Code to extend the required time horizon for fiscal projections attached to certain bills and joint resolutions, generally changing projections from **3 years to 5 years**, and revenue-loss projections from **1 year to 5 years**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Extends required fiscal projections for covered bills/joint resolutions from **3 fiscal years to 5 fiscal years** following the effective date.  
- Requires a **5-year fiscal projection** (instead of 1-year) for bills/joint resolutions that **reduce state revenue**, covering the **first 5 full fiscal years** after the effective date.  
- Maintains the requirement that fiscal projections be **attached by the sponsor before initial committee consideration** in the House of origin for certain categories of legislation.  
- Keeps Controller General oversight: fiscal projections must be **prepared or reviewed by the Controller General** and reported to the sponsor **in writing**.  
- Continues special treatment for **public pension/retirement** measures: projection must include **estimated actuarial long-term cost**, obtained from the **Board of Pension Trustees or other qualified actuarial source**.  
- Requires updated fiscal projections when amendments/substitutes change fiscal effects; revised projections must be included **at the time of introduction** of the amendment/substitute.  
- Applies prospectively to **bills/joint resolutions filed after the Act is enacted into law** (effective date not specified in source text).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Longer budget visibility:** Moving from **3-year to 5-year** projections can provide additional out-year information about state obligations and consequences of legislation (§1902(a)).  
- **More complete view of revenue impacts:** Expands revenue-reduction projections from **1 year to 5 years**, requiring quantified revenue loss for the **first 5 full fiscal years** after a bill takes effect (§1903).  
- **Better tracking of multi-year federal programs:** Federal matching-funds measures must show state and federal shares for **5 fiscal years** and indicate when federal funding is likely to end (§1905).  
- **Consistency across bill types:** Aligns multiple provisions (appropriations/mandates, federal funds, local school district current expense impacts) around a **5-year** horizon (§1901, §1905, §1906).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased preparation burden:** Sponsors must attach **5-year** projections (instead of 3-year/1-year) before initial committee consideration for covered measures (§1901(a), §1906; §1903).  
- **More frequent/complex revisions:** The Controller General must revise projections at each legislative stage where an amendment/substitute changes fiscal effects, and those revisions must be included upon introduction (§1904). Extending the projection horizon may increase revision complexity.  
- **Potential for more assumptions:** Projections must state how estimates were calculated and the underlying facts/assumptions (§1902(b)); longer horizons may require more reliance on assumptions (the bill requires disclosure but does not change the uncertainty).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date of the Act itself:** The bill states it applies to bills/joint resolutions filed after enactment, but does not specify the calendar date when the Act takes effect.  
- **Scope trigger details unchanged/interpretive:** The bill retains phrases like “authorizes expenditures not previously authorized within the annual budget” and expenditures “not specifically provided for,” which may still require interpretation in application (§1901).  
- **No specified enforcement mechanism:** The bill requires projections be attached prior to committee consideration, but does not state what happens procedurally if missing.

## Key Policy Changes
- **§1901(a):** For bills/joint resolutions authorizing certain expenditures, required projection changes from **3-year to 5-year**, attached by sponsor **prior to initial committee consideration**.  
- **§1902(a):** Required projection content now covers impacts for **each of the 5 fiscal years** following the effective date (was 3).  
- **§1903:** Revenue-reduction bills now require a **5-year** projection (was **1-year**), describing revenue loss for the **first 5 full fiscal years** after the effective date and the rationale used.  
- **§1905:** To qualify for/receive federal funds involving state expenditures not previously authorized in the budget bill, projection changes from **3-year to 5-year**, including federal vs. state amounts for **5 fiscal years** and when federal funding is likely to end.  
- **§1906:** Local school district current expense fund measures change from **3-year to 5-year** projections, attached by sponsor before initial committee consideration.  
- **Technical corrections:** Conforms language to the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (per Synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- **General Assembly members and committees** receiving longer-horizon fiscal information (5 years) before acting on covered bills/joint resolutions.  
- **State fiscal oversight processes** (Controller General function) through expanded multi-year reporting requirements (§1902, §1903, §1904).  
- **Stakeholders impacted by revenue changes or long-term programs** (including federally funded programs) who may rely on longer-term fiscal projections (§1903, §1905).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Bill sponsors**, due to added time/effort to produce **5-year** projections and ensure attachment before initial committee consideration (§1901(a), §1906).  
- **Controller General and relevant actuarial sources**, due to increased review/revision workload across a longer projection horizon (§1902, §1904), including pension/retirement long-term cost estimates (§1902(c), §1901(b)).
