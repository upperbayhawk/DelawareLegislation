## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 4, Chapter 13 of the Delaware Code to (1) strengthen municipal control over where conversion and recreational marijuana facilities may be licensed, (2) pause licensing when a municipality is considering marijuana regulations/ordinances, (3) clarify a neighborhood petition process that triggers a public hearing on certain retail-related applications, and (4) allow suspension of certain already-issued licenses that conflict with municipal law so the licensee can relocate into compliance.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits the Commissioner from granting a **conversion license or recreational license** for a marijuana facility if the **facility location is contrary to municipal law adopted under § 1351**.  
- Prohibits issuing a conversion or recreational license when a **municipal ordinance or regulation concerning recreational marijuana is under consideration** until it is **finalized**.  
- Clarifies the Commissioner must **hear complaints** about **applications for a retail license or conversion license** to operate a marijuana retail facility in a particular location when a **petition is signed by at least 10 residents of the same neighborhood**.  
- Requires **10 days’ notice** of such hearings, sent by **registered mail** to the license holder/applicant and also **posted on the outside of an entrance door** of the establishment.  
- Makes clear that an applicant/licensee **may not begin operations** at a location subject to a petition-triggered hearing **until the hearing concludes and the Commissioner issues a decision**.  
- Grants hearing powers including **subpoenas** and ability to seek **contempt enforcement** through Superior Court for refusal to testify/produce records.  
- Applies **upon enactment**; authorizes the Commissioner to **suspend certain pre-enactment conversion/recreational licenses** that would violate the updated § 1354 while the holder secures a compliant location.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Strengthens local control over siting:** Licensing is barred when the location conflicts with municipal law under **§ 1351**, aligning state licensing with municipal zoning/ordinances as described in amended **§ 1354(a)**.  
- **Avoids “racing ahead” of local rulemaking:** If a municipality is actively considering a recreational marijuana ordinance/regulation, state licensing must wait until the local action is **finalized** (amended **§ 1354(a)**).  
- **Formal community input mechanism:** A **10-neighborhood-resident petition threshold** triggers a required **public hearing** for retail license/conversion license applications in a particular location (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Clear due process and transparency:** Mandates **public hearings** and **10 days’ notice** plus on-site posting (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential licensing delays:** Licenses cannot be issued while a municipality’s ordinance/regulation is “under consideration” and not yet “finalized,” which may slow application processing (amended **§ 1354(a)**).  
- **Operational delays from petitions:** An applicant/licensee cannot begin operations at a petitioned location until after the hearing and Commissioner decision, which can delay opening (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Potential instability for some existing licensees:** For certain licenses issued before enactment that would violate the updated rule, the Commissioner may **suspend** the license while the holder relocates (Section 2).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Under consideration” and “finalized” are undefined:** The bill does not specify what municipal actions count as “under consideration,” nor what constitutes “finalized” (amended **§ 1354(a)**).  
- **Scope of “same neighborhood” is undefined:** No definition or boundary standard for “same neighborhood” for the **10-signature** petition (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Timing for suspension/relocation is unspecified:** Section 2 allows suspension while a holder “secures a location” compliant with municipal law, but provides **no deadline** or process details.  
- **Which licenses are covered by Section 2 is limited but not fully detailed:** Section 2 references **conversion or recreational licenses** issued before enactment that would be “in violation of § 1354” as amended, but does not specify how violations will be determined procedurally.

## Key Policy Changes
1. **Municipal-law compliance requirement for conversion/recreational licenses:**  
   - The Commissioner **may not grant a conversion license or recreational license** to a marijuana facility if the facility location is **contrary to municipal law adopted under § 1351** (amended **§ 1354(a)**).
2. **Pause on issuance during municipal rulemaking:**  
   - If a municipal **regulation or ordinance concerning recreational marijuana is under consideration**, the Commissioner **may not issue** a conversion or recreational license until the municipal action is **finalized** (amended **§ 1354(a)**).
3. **Complaint/hearing trigger clarified for certain retail-related applications:**  
   - The Commissioner must hear complaints regarding applications for a **retail license or conversion license** to operate a marijuana retail facility in a particular location upon receipt of a **petition signed by at least 10 residents of the same neighborhood** (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).
4. **Mandatory notice and public hearing procedures:**  
   - **10 days’ notice** by **registered mail** to the holder/applicant and **posted notice** on an entrance door; hearing is **public**; Commissioner has subpoena and evidence-compulsion powers (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).
5. **Operations pause pending hearing decision:**  
   - A retail marijuana establishment or conversion licensee/applicant may not begin operations at a petitioned location until the **hearing concludes and the Commissioner issues a decision** (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).
6. **Pre-enactment license suspension authority to achieve compliance:**  
   - For a **conversion or recreational license issued prior to enactment** that would violate amended § 1354, the Commissioner **may suspend** the license while the holder secures a compliant location (Section 2).

## Who Benefits
- **Municipalities** seeking to enforce or finalize local marijuana ordinances/regulations before state licenses are issued (amended **§ 1354(a)**).  
- **Neighborhood residents** who want a formal mechanism to trigger a public hearing using a **10-resident petition** (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Regulators/the Commissioner** through clarified authority and procedures for hearings, evidence gathering, and enforcement (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Applicants for conversion or recreational licenses** in municipalities where local law prohibits the proposed location, or where ordinances/regulations are pending (amended **§ 1354(a)**).  
- **Retail marijuana establishment operators/applicants** whose opening could be delayed by a petition-triggered hearing and decision process (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Certain pre-enactment conversion/recreational license holders** whose licenses could be **suspended** if their location conflicts with municipal law as applied through amended § 1354 (Section 2).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon enactment** (Section 2).  
- **Hearing notice deadline:** **10 days’ notice** must be provided before petition-triggered hearings (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Operational deadline/condition:** Operations at a petitioned location may not begin **until the conclusion of the hearing and a decision by the Commissioner** (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance gate for licensing:** Commissioner must deny/withhold conversion or recreational licenses when the location conflicts with municipal law under **§ 1351**, and must pause issuance while municipal recreational marijuana ordinances/regulations are pending (amended **§ 1354(a)**).  
- **Mandatory public hearing process** triggered by petition of **≥ 10 neighborhood residents** with required **10-day** notice by **registered mail** and posted notice (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Subpoena and evidence authority:** Commissioner may issue subpoenas, compel witness attendance, administer oaths, take testimony, and compel production of records/documents (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Contempt enforcement:** If a person refuses to testify/produce evidence without reasonable cause, the Commissioner may certify the refusal to **Superior Court**, which may punish as **contempt of court** (amended **§ 1322(a)(6)**).  
- **Suspension authority for certain existing licenses:** Commissioner may **suspend** certain pre-enactment conversion/recreational licenses that would violate amended § 1354 while the licensee secures a compliant location (Section 2).
