## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To revise Delaware’s process for modifying sentences of incarceration (“compassionate release” and certain rehabilitation-based modifications) by routing applications directly to the sentencing court (rather than through the Board of Parole), defining eligibility pathways, ensuring victim and Department of Justice input, and establishing annual review and public reporting requirements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Creates the “Richard ‘Mouse’ Smith Compassionate Release Act.”**
- **Shifts sentence-modification process to the sentencing court** by removing the **Board of Parole** from the §4217 modification pathway; applications are made **directly to the court**.
- Allows people serving an **aggregate Level V sentence in excess of 1 year** to apply for modification **only** in specified circumstances: **serious medical illness/infirmity**, or **rehabilitation** after **(a) age 60+ and 15 years served**, or **(b) 25 years served** (application must be **based solely** on the stated ground).
- **DOC must conduct annual eligibility determinations** for each covered person and decide whether to recommend modification; if DOC does **not** recommend, the person may still apply to the court and must attach DOC’s written reasons.
- Court must make on-the-record findings, including whether release poses a **“substantial risk”** to the **community**, a **victim or witness**, or the **person**; court may **summarily dismiss** ineligible applications.
- Establishes **right to counsel** for applicants (with referral to **Office of Defense Services** for indigency determination/appointment).
- Creates **annual public reporting** by the **Statistical Analysis Center (SAC)** with a statutory deadline of **October 1** each year to publish and submit the report to specified officials.
- **Effective date:** **180 days after enactment** into law.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Direct court access**: Applications go straight to the sentencing court, rather than requiring Board of Parole involvement in the §4217 pathway (text: revisions removing Board role and allowing direct court filing).
- **Clear eligibility pathways with defined thresholds**: Eligibility tied to explicit thresholds—**Level V >1 year**, **60+ with ≥15 years served**, or **≥25 years served**, or **serious medical illness/infirmity** (11 Del. C. §4217(a)(1) & (a)(3)).
- **Victim/witness consideration is explicit**: Court findings must address risk to a **victim or witness**, and victims must be given time to be heard (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(1)c. and §4217(e)(3)).
- **Transparency via required annual reporting**: SAC must publish/submit a detailed annual report by **October 1** with demographic and outcome data (11 Del. C. §4217(i)(2)).
- **Counsel guaranteed**: Applicants have a **right to counsel**, with court referral to ODS for indigency determination/appointment (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(6)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher workload and costs for state agencies**: Fiscal note projects significant staffing, technology, and space needs for **DOC**, **Superior Court**, and **ODS** to handle increased reviews, filings, hearings, and representation (see Budget/Fiscal sections below).
- **Increased filings/hearings expected**: Superior Court anticipates “a significant increase in filings and hearings” due to eligible and potentially ineligible applicants filing as allowed (Fiscal Note, Assumption #5).
- **Limits on repeat applications**: If denied (other than medical/infirmity applications), person generally **may not file again for at least 1 year from denial** (11 Del. C. §4217(e)(4)), potentially delaying reconsideration.

## Educational Impact
- Bill changes how refusal to participate in compulsory **education programs** affects eligibility:
  - Refusal **does not prohibit** a sentence-modification application under **§4217** or eligibility for parole under **§4346(e)**, but **may be considered by the court** in evaluating the application. (Amendments to 11 Del. C. §6531A(e))
- No changes to education program requirements themselves are specified beyond eligibility consequences.

## Health Impact
- Creates/clarifies a medical-based pathway for sentence modification **based solely** on “serious medical illness or infirmity,” which is defined to include:
  - **Cognitively incapacitated** (e.g., dementia impacting activities of daily living),
  - **Permanently physically incapacitated** (including persistent vegetative state or progressive incurable condition),
  - **Severely ill** (extensive medical/behavioral health treatment with little/no recovery),
  - **Terminally ill** (likely death **within 18 months**, to a reasonable degree of medical certainty; excluding self-inflicted injury). (11 Del. C. §4217(c)(4))
- Court may require DOC to submit a **medical release plan**, including treatment course/site and insurance/financial resources (commercial insurance, **Medicare**, **Medicaid**, or other resources). (11 Del. C. §4217(e)(2))
- Reporting includes medical-utilization metrics (e.g., people receiving full-time 1:1 care; people spending **more than 1 month** in outside hospital care in the prior year). (11 Del. C. §4217(i)(1)a.5)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Renames/cites act**: “Richard ‘Mouse’ Smith Compassionate Release Act.” (Section 1)
- **Eligibility definition**: Applies to a “person” serving an aggregate **Level V sentence > 1 year**. (11 Del. C. §4217(a)(1))
- **New/clarified grounds and thresholds for applying** (must be **based solely** on the ground):
  - Serious medical illness or infirmity.
  - **Age ≥ 60** and served **≥ 15 years** of originally imposed Level V sentence, based solely on rehabilitation.
  - Served **≥ 25 years** of originally imposed Level V sentence, based solely on rehabilitation. (11 Del. C. §4217(a)(3))
- **Mandatory minimums do not bar applications** if eligible under §4217(a)(3) (11 Del. C. §4217(a)(4)).
- **Half-sentence restrictions remain for certain cases** outside the specific §4217(a)(3)/(a)(4) pathways:
  - Title 11 violent felony: not eligible until served **½** of Level V sentence.
  - Statutorily mandated Level V term: not eligible until served **½** (as written in §4217(a)(5)b. for mandated terms) (11 Del. C. §4217(a)(5)).
- **Court-centered decisionmaking and required findings**:
  - Court must find authorization, **good cause**, and no **substantial risk** to community/victim-witness/self; must state findings and rationale on the record. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(1), (b)(3), (b)(4))
- **Right to counsel** for applicants; possible ODS appointment process. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(6))
- **Information access**: DOC must provide reasonably necessary information for the application to the person/attorney. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(7))
- **Notice/copy requirement**: Applicant must provide a copy to the **Department of Justice**. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(9))
- **Court rules required**: Court must adopt implementing rules, including for self-representation with court permission. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(10))
- **Annual DOC determinations**: For each covered person, DOC must annually determine eligibility and whether DOC recommends modification; if DOC recommends, it files with the court with the person’s consent; if not, it must provide written reasons and the person may apply. (11 Del. C. §4217(d))
- **Victim opportunity to be heard**: Court may not act without providing DOJ and the victim a reasonable opportunity to be heard in writing or in person. (11 Del. C. §4217(e)(3))
- **Limits on “no early release” sentencing condition**: Amends 11 Del. C. §4204(k) to clarify that statutes authorizing reductions “do not include **§4217**,” meaning §4204(k)’s “without benefit of early release…” limitation is not meant to include the §4217 modification process.
- **Refusal to participate in programs** (drug/alcohol treatment, education, employment) no longer automatically bars §4217 applications or §4346(e) parole eligibility, though refusal may be considered by the court and may still trigger disciplinary measures. (Amendments to §§6531(e), 6531A(e), 6532(j))
- **SAC annual reporting due by October 1** and posted publicly. (11 Del. C. §4217(i)(2))
- **Amends 29 Del. C. §4604** to specify ODS shall counsel/defend at every stage following arrest and prosecute appeals/remedies “as authorized by statute.” (Section 8)

## Who Benefits
- **Incarcerated individuals** serving **aggregate Level V sentences > 1 year** who meet:
  - serious medical illness/infirmity criteria, or
  - rehabilitation criteria with thresholds (**60+ & ≥15 years served**, or **≥25 years served**).
- **Applicants without DOC recommendation** who are expressly allowed to apply directly to the court (with DOC written reasons attached).
- **Victims** (and witnesses) of underlying offenses, who are explicitly included in the substantial-risk analysis and are given an opportunity to be heard.
- **Public/oversight stakeholders** who receive standardized annual reporting by **October 1** each year.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **DOC, Superior Court, and ODS** due to increased administrative, staffing, technology, and space burdens and costs (as projected in the fiscal note).
- **Applicants denied on non-medical grounds** who face a **1-year** waiting period before a subsequent application (11 Del. C. §4217(e)(4)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** The Act takes effect **180 days after enactment into law**. (Section 9; also fiscal note assumption)
- **Annual reporting deadline:** SAC must publish and submit the annual report **not later than October 1 of each year**. (11 Del. C. §4217(i)(2))
- **Annual DOC determinations:** DOC must “annually determine” eligibility and recommendation status for each covered person. (11 Del. C. §4217(d)(1)) (No specific calendar date provided.)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Court rulemaking requirement:** Court “shall adopt rules to implement” §4217, including provisions for proceeding without an attorney with permission. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(10))
- **Mandatory notice/copy to DOJ:** Any party filing a §4217 application must provide a copy to the **Department of Justice**. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(9))
- **Victim/DOJ opportunity to be heard:** Court may not act without providing DOJ and the victim a reasonable opportunity to be heard. (11 Del. C. §4217(e)(3))
- **DOC information access requirement:** DOC must provide reasonably necessary information for the person’s application. (11 Del. C. §4217(b)(7))
- **Application resubmission restriction:** If denied on non-medical grounds for risk or lack of good cause, subsequent applications are barred for **at least 1 year from denial** (medical/infirmity exception). (11 Del. C. §4217(e)(4))
- **Program refusal disciplinary measures remain possible:** Refusal to participate in compulsory drug/alcohol, education, or employment programs may still be subject to disciplinary measures established by regulation, even though refusal no longer automatically bars §4217 applications. (§§6531(e), 6531A(e), 6532(j))
