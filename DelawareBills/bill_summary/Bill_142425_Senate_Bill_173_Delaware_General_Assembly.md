## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 to expand which school-based staff positions may be funded through Delaware’s “mental health services unit” funding in public schools, and require certain service-alignment standards and local policies for these funded roles.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds **registered nurses**, **board-certified assistant behavior analysts (BCaBAs)**, and **board-certified behavior analysts (BCBAs)** to the list of positions that may be funded under school “mental health services unit” allocations for **elementary (K-5), middle (6-8), and high school (9-12)**.  
- Sets/continues a funding unit ratio of **1 unit per 250 full-time equivalent (FTE) students** beginning in **Fiscal Year 2026**, with eligibility for **fractional funding** for partial increments of 250 FTE.  
- Defines “mental health services” as **prevention, response, and coordination services** delivered to students in the relevant school setting (elementary building, middle school, high school).  
- Requires school counselors, social workers, and psychologists funded under these provisions to align services with national models/standards: **ASCA**, **National Association of School Social Work standards**, and **NASP model**.  
- Requires districts and charter schools to **adopt policies** consistent with the statutory requirements to direct provision of services by **registered nurses, BCaBAs, and BCBAs** funded under the unit.  
- Allows districts/charters (with governing board approval) to **contract out** for school psychologists and other mental health providers **until** DOE no longer designates school psychologists as a **certification shortage area**.  
- Requires combining **fractional units** to fund additional **full-time** mental health staff “to the extent possible,” with a **cash option** for remaining fractional amounts (tied to **§ 1716(g)**), restricted to specified uses.  
- Directs DOE, **by December 31, 2025**, in coordination with the General Assembly, to develop a **strategic plan** to reasonably expand the unit into **district preschools, middle schools, and high schools**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More staffing flexibility:** Districts/charters may fund additional role types (RNs, BCaBAs, BCBAs) through the same unit structure (explicitly added to eligible lists in §§ 1716E, 1716F, 1716H).  
- **Supports prevention/response capacity:** Funds and cash-option spending must support “**preventative and responsive mental health services**” and related materials/services (explicit use restrictions).  
- **Standardized practice expectations:** Requires alignment with recognized national practice frameworks for counselors, social workers, and psychologists (ASCA/NASW/NASP references).  
- **Ability to address shortages:** Continues ability to **contract out** psychologists/other providers (with governing board approval) while DOE designates a **certification shortage area**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Policy and compliance burden:** Districts/charters must adopt policies to direct services by newly eligible roles (RNs/BCaBAs/BCBAs), which may require local administrative work (policy adoption requirement).  
- **Limits use of cash-option funds:** Remaining fractional funding used via the cash option must be spent only on materials/services supporting authorized prevention/response mental health services, “**and for no other purpose**,” reducing spending flexibility (explicit restriction).  
- **Potential staffing constraints:** Funding is tied to **1 unit per 250 FTE**; the bill does not alter the ratio and thus does not guarantee increased overall funding levels, only expands eligible position types (ratio specified; no new appropriation specified).
