## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 of the Delaware Code to require additional, standardized training for **school board members and charter school board members** at the **beginning of their term**, and to set **delivery, notice, and follow-up letter** requirements related to completion.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **all school board members** and **charter school board members** to complete new “beginning-of-term” training on: **(1) meeting requirements (including FOIA), (2) meeting conduct/rules of order, and (3) State Code of Conduct**.  
- Keeps the existing requirement for “beginning-of-term” training on **special education due process hearings**.  
- Directs the **Department of Education (DOE)** to **identify trainings** for the new topics; DOE also continues to set training criteria/material/method for the special education due process training **through regulations**.  
- Makes **each school district** (for district boards) and **each charter school** (for charter boards) responsible for **offering** the required new-topic trainings; delivery may be **delegated to or purchased from another entity**.  
- Requires training to be offered **virtually at least twice a year**.  
- Requires the **board president** to notify new members of the obligation and to send a **letter by January 15** to any member who has not completed required training **by January 1 following the member’s election or appointment**.  
- Allows combining these trainings/notices with existing **financial responsibility training** requirements under **14 Del. C. § 1803**.  
- **Effective upon Governor’s signature; approved September 2, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves compliance and governance consistency:** Mandatory training on **FOIA (Title 29, Ch. 100)**, meeting procedures, and the **State Employees’, Officers’ and Officials’ Code of Conduct (Title 29, Ch. 58, Subch. I)** may reduce procedural errors in board operations.  
- **Standardizes access and frequency:** Requiring trainings be offered **virtually at least twice a year** can make completion easier across districts and charter schools.  
- **Formal follow-up mechanism:** The **January 15 letter** requirement creates a clear administrative prompt for noncompliant members.  
- **Administrative efficiency option:** Expressly allows combining with **§ 1803 financial responsibility training and notices**, potentially reducing duplicative communications/training sessions.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential local cost increases:** Fiscal note indicates districts/charters may use attorneys charging **~$350–$400/hour**, estimating **$1,500–$2,000 per training (including travel)** if provided locally; statewide total is **indeterminable** due to unknown turnover and number of trainings.  
- **Administrative workload:** Districts/charters must schedule/offer trainings, track completion, and issue notices/letters (including by **January 15**).  
- **Unclear uniformity of content across providers:** Because delivery may be delegated/purchased from other entities, training content may vary unless DOE identification/criteria are sufficiently specific.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Completion standard not defined:** The bill requires members “complete training,” but does not specify minimum hours, assessment, or documentation standards in the statutory text.  
- **Scope of DOE “identify trainings”:** The bill states DOE “must identify trainings” for the (a)(2) topics but does not specify whether DOE must create trainings, approve vendor offerings, or maintain a statewide list.  
- **Consequences for non-completion:** The bill requires notice and a letter but does not state any penalty, removal process, or enforcement action if a member remains noncompliant.  
- **Interaction with existing annual trainings:** Fiscal note says training is already offered annually by the Delaware School Board Association; the statute requires “beginning-of-term” completion and “offered virtually at least twice a year,” but does not clarify whether annual trainings automatically satisfy all requirements.

## Budget Impact

## Key Policy Changes
- Expands “beginning-of-term” training for **school board** and **charter school board** members to include:
  - **Meeting requirements under Title 14 and FOIA (29 Del. C. Ch. 100)**  
  - **Best practices for conducting meetings, including rules of order**  
  - **State Employees’, Officers’ and Officials’ Code of Conduct (29 Del. C. Ch. 58, Subch. I)**  
- Requires DOE to **identify trainings** for these new topics, while continuing regulatory authority over special education due process training criteria/material/method.  
- Assigns responsibility to **each school district** / **each charter school** to **offer** the (a)(2) trainings; allows delegation/purchasing from another entity.  
- Requires trainings under subsection (a) be offered **virtually at least twice a year**.  
- Creates annual compliance follow-up: if training not completed by **January 1 following election/appointment**, board president must send a letter by **January 15**.  
- Allows combining required training/notices with those under **14 Del. C. § 1803** (financial responsibility training).

## Who Benefits
- **Newly elected/appointed school board and charter board members** (clearer expectations and training access).  
- **School districts and charter schools** (more standardized governance training; potential reduction in meeting/FOIA/code-of-conduct missteps).  
- **Public stakeholders** attending/engaging with boards (training emphasizes FOIA and meeting conduct requirements).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **School districts and charter schools** that choose to provide training independently (may incur costs such as **$350–$400/hour** attorney time and **$1,500–$2,000 per training** estimated total, plus administrative tracking).  
- **Board presidents/administration** responsible for compliance communications (required notices and **January 15** letters).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** “Effective upon signature by the Governor” (fiscal note). The enrolled act shows: **Approved September 2, 2025**.  
- **Training timing:** Must be completed **at the beginning of the member’s term**.  
- **Annual completion checkpoint:** If not completed by **January 1 following the member’s election or appointment**, the board president must send a letter by **January 15** each year.  
- **Training availability:** Must be offered **virtually at least twice a year**.

- **Notice requirements:**

  - Board president must send a **letter by January 15** to any member who has not completed training by **January 1** following election/appointment.
