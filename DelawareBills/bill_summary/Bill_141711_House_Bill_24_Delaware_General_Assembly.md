## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend the Town of Delmar charter to (1) change municipal election voter eligibility and voter list administration and (2) expand the Town Council’s authority to borrow money/issue bonds without using the full procedures in Charter §24(a).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Reduces the municipal election residency requirement to vote from **6 months** to **30 days** immediately preceding the election (Charter §7(c)).
- Maintains minimum voting age of **18** for municipal elections (Charter §7(c)).
- Establishes the **State of Delaware Voter Registration System (Title 15, Delaware Code)** as the source for Delmar’s list of registered voters (Charter §7(k)).
- Allows the Town of Delmar to **deny names from the State registration list** if the person **moved from Town**, **no longer owns property within incorporated Town limits**, or is **otherwise disqualified under the Charter** (Charter §7(l)).
- Increases the maximum aggregate amount of Town bonds/obligations that may be outstanding **at any time** under the expedited §24(b) process from **$1,000,000** to **$10,000,000** (Charter §24(b)).
- Keeps the §24(b) mechanism that permits issuance **without regard to** the requirements in §24(a)(1)-(7), if approved by **Town Council resolution** adopted by at least a **majority of all members** (Charter §24(b)).
- Does not change the municipal election day/time: **first Monday in October**, **7:00 a.m. to 7:00 p.m.** (Charter §7(a)).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expanded local voter access:** More residents can vote after **30 days** of residency rather than **6 months** (Charter §7(c)).
- **Administrative alignment with state system:** Using the **State’s Voter Registration System** may simplify maintaining voter rolls (Charter §7(k)).
- **Greater financing flexibility:** Raising the cap to **$10,000,000 outstanding at any time** increases capacity to finance “proper municipal purpose” projects via Council resolution (Charter §24(b)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential for voter roll disputes/administrative burden:** The Town “reserves the right to deny” names from the State list for specified reasons, which may require local review and could increase administrative workload (Charter §7(l)).
- **Higher potential municipal debt exposure:** Increasing the expedited bonding ceiling from **$1,000,000** to **$10,000,000** raises the maximum outstanding obligations permitted under §24(b) (Charter §24(b)).
- **Reduced procedural safeguards for larger issuances:** Bonds/obligations issued under §24(b) can be authorized “without regard to” §24(a)(1)-(7) requirements, now up to **$10,000,000** (Charter §24(b)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Process for denying/removing voters:** The bill states the Town may deny names from the State registration list, but does not specify procedures, notice, appeal rights, or documentation standards (Charter §7(l)).
- **Meaning and enforcement of “no longer own property” criterion:** The bill includes property ownership status as a basis to deny names, but does not explain how this interacts with the Charter’s voter qualifications or how ownership is verified (Charter §7(l)).
- **Definition of “proper municipal purpose” for §24(b):** The bill retains this standard but does not define it in the amended text (Charter §24(b)).
- **No stated requirements for public vote or referendum for §24(b) issuances:** The text describes Council resolution approval and bypass of §24(a)(1)-(7), but the bill does not clarify whether any separate approval is required elsewhere in the Charter (Charter §24(b)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Voter residency requirement:** Changes from **at least 6 months** to **30 days** residency in the Town immediately before the election (Charter §7(c)).
- **Voter list source:** Town “establishes the use and adoption” of the **State’s Voter Registration System** for its list of registered voters (Charter §7(k)).
- **Local authority to deny names from state list:** Town may deny names if voters have **moved**, **no longer own property within Town limits**, or are **otherwise disqualified** under the Charter (Charter §7(l)).
- **Bonding cap under §24(b):** Increases allowable aggregate outstanding bonds/obligations issued under §24(b) from **$1,000,000** to **$10,000,000** (Charter §24(b)).
- **Approval mechanism for §24(b) bonds:** Retains requirement that Town Council approve by **resolution** adopted by at least a **majority of all members**, stating amount, purpose, security, and confirming the aggregate cap is not exceeded (Charter §24(b)).

## Who Benefits
- **Newer residents of Delmar:** Individuals age **18+** who have lived in town for **30 days** (but less than 6 months) gain voting eligibility in municipal elections (Charter §7(c)).
- **Town of Delmar (municipal government):** Gains expanded capacity to issue debt up to **$10,000,000 outstanding** under §24(b) (Charter §24(b)).
