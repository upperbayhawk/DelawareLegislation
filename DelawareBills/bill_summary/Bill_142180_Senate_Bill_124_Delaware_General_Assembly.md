## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s adult and juvenile expungement statutes (Title 11 and Title 10) to clarify that **Title 21 motor vehicle offenses**, their **county/municipal equivalents**, and **civil violations** generally **do not bar** a person from obtaining an expungement for otherwise-eligible charges in the same incident/case—addressing the court interpretation in *Cornette v. State*, 2024 Del. Super. Lexis 455 (June 11, 2024).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Clarifies that for expungement purposes, a “**case**” **does not include** a **Title 21 offense (or equivalent)** or a **civil violation**, even if joined with other charges from the same incident. (11 Del. C. § 4372(c); 10 Del. C. § 1016(3))
- States that **pending criminal charges** generally prevent expungement, but **pending Title 21 (or equivalent) matters** and certain specified violations **do not count** as “pending criminal charges” for that bar. (11 Del. C. § 4372(f)(1))
- Provides that an **arrest/conviction** for a **Title 21 offense (or equivalent)** or **civil-violation responsibility finding/agreement** **does not bar eligibility** for mandatory or discretionary expungement of otherwise eligible matters. (11 Del. C. § 4372(h))
- Prohibits courts and the State Bureau of Identification (SBI) from **denying** expungement requests **because** the charge set includes a **Title 21/equivalent offense** or **civil violation**. (11 Del. C. § 4372(h)(2))
- Keeps in place that **Title 21 offenses are not eligible for expungement**, except as otherwise provided; and updates cross-references/wording to reflect the new “not a bar” rule. (11 Del. C. § 4372(f)(2); 10 Del. C. § 1015(b)(4))
- Clarifies in juvenile expungement definitions that “case” also **excludes** Title 21/equivalent offenses and civil violations. (10 Del. C. § 1016)
- Removes/changes the list of Title 21 offenses for discretionary expungement in Title 11 by repealing the prior enumerated list in § 4374(i)(2) (e.g., reckless driving, driving after judgment prohibited, operation causing death). (11 Del. C. § 4374(i))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Expands practical access to expungement** for eligible non-Title 21 charges when they were charged in the same incident as a Title 21 offense or civil violation, by preventing denials based solely on that bundling. (11 Del. C. § 4372(h)(2); Synopsis items (1)–(3))
- **Reduces denials based on *Cornette* “entire case must be eligible” reasoning** by redefining “case” to exclude Title 21/equivalent and civil violations for expungement purposes. (11 Del. C. § 4372(c); Synopsis)
- **Clarifies treatment of pending matters**, allowing expungement to proceed even if a person has pending Title 21/equivalent matters (and certain specified alleged violations) that otherwise would block eligibility. (11 Del. C. § 4372(f)(1))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Title 21 offenses remain generally ineligible for expungement**, meaning motor-vehicle offenses themselves typically cannot be expunged even while they no longer block expungement of other charges. (11 Del. C. § 4372(f)(2); 11 Del. C. § 4374(i))
- By changing/removing the prior enumerated Title 21 discretionary-expungement list in 11 Del. C. § 4374(i)(2), the bill may **reduce clarity** about whether any specific Title 21 offenses can ever be expunged under that subsection (the text now emphasizes non-expungability unless specifically enumerated, while the enumeration appears repealed/removed). (11 Del. C. § 4374(i))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **How the repeal/removal of the specific Title 21 list in 11 Del. C. § 4374(i)(2) operates in practice**: § 4374(i)(1) says Title 21 charges may not be expunged “unless…specifically enumerated,” but the bill text shows the formerly enumerated items and two repealed items in (i)(2) being removed/repealed. The bill synopsis states certain offenses are not eligible, but the statutory mechanics could require clarification in implementation. (11 Del. C. § 4374(i); Synopsis item (4))
- **Scope of “civil violation”**: the bill refers to “civil violation” and “finding of, or agreement to, responsibility,” but does not define “civil violation” in the provided sections. (11 Del. C. § 4372(h); 10 Del. C. § 1015(b)(4))
- **Interaction with other expungement pathways**: the bill references exceptions “as otherwise provided” under § 4375 and § 4374(i), and in Title 10 exceptions under § 1017(a)(1)–(a)(2), but those provisions are not included in the provided text. (11 Del. C. § 4372(f); 10 Del. C. § 1015(b))

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adult expungement—definition of “case” revised** so that “case” **does not include** Title 21/equivalent offenses or civil violations that could be joined with other charges from the same incident. (11 Del. C. § 4372(c)(1)b)
- **Adult expungement—pending charges exclusion narrowed**: “pending criminal charges” (which bar expungement) **do not include** alleged violations of:
  - **4 Del. C. § 904(e) or (f)**
  - **16 Del. C. § 4764(c)**
  - **A Title 21 offense (or equivalent offense)** (11 Del. C. § 4372(f)(1))
- **Adult expungement—Title 21 offenses remain generally not expungable**, but are also **not a bar** to expungement eligibility for other charges. (11 Del. C. § 4372(f)(2); § 4372(h))
- **Adult expungement—non-bar rule expanded**: an **arrest or conviction** of Title 21/equivalent, or civil-violation responsibility finding/agreement, **does not bar** discretionary or mandatory expungement. (11 Del. C. § 4372(h)(1))
- **Adult expungement—anti-denial rule**: court/SBI **may not deny** expungement because the requested “set of charges” includes Title 21/equivalent or civil violation matters. (11 Del. C. § 4372(h)(2))
- **Adult discretionary expungement—technical eligibility language updated** in § 4374(a)(4) to specify that, for eligibility under that paragraph, “the entire case” must be eligible—while “case” is now defined to exclude Title 21/equivalent and civil violations. (11 Del. C. § 4374(a)(4); § 4372(c))
- **Adult discretionary expungement—Title 21 subsection revised**: retains the general rule that Title 21/equivalent charges may not be expunged unless specifically enumerated, while the bill text shows changes/repeals to the enumerated list. (11 Del. C. § 4374(i))
- **Juvenile expungement—Title 21/civil violation not a bar**: in Title 10, clarifies that a Title 21/equivalent offense or civil violation **does not operate as a bar** to expungement and cannot be the basis to deny discretionary/mandatory expungement under §§ 1014–1019, but a **felony DUI conviction is treated as a conviction** for those purposes. (10 Del. C. § 1015(b)(4))
- **Juvenile expungement—definition of “case” revised** similarly to adult expungement to exclude Title 21/equivalent and civil violations. (10 Del. C. § 1016(3))

## Who Benefits
- Individuals seeking **mandatory or discretionary expungement** whose incident included **Title 21 (motor vehicle) offenses**, **equivalent local ordinance offenses**, and/or **civil violations**, because those matters generally **cannot be used to deny** expungement of otherwise eligible charges. (11 Del. C. § 4372(h); 10 Del. C. § 1015(b)(4))
- Applicants who have **pending Title 21/equivalent matters** (and the specified Title 4/Title 16 alleged violations), because those pending matters are not treated as “pending criminal charges” for the general expungement bar. (11 Del. C. § 4372(f)(1))
