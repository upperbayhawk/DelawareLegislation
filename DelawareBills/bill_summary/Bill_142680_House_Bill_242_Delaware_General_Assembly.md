## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Allow school districts located entirely in New Castle County to reset (reissue) 2025–2026 local school property tax rates using separate residential and non-residential rates, with limits, and require New Castle County to rebill/adjust tax bills and extend payment deadlines for any adjusted bills.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes any school district located entirely in **New Castle County** to reset its **2025–2026** local school tax rate using **two rates**: **residential** and **non-residential**.
- Sets a cap on the relationship between rates: the **non-residential rate must be at least equal to** the residential rate and **may not exceed 2×** the residential rate.
- Requires **revenue neutrality** relative to the district’s original warrant: projected revenue under the new split rates **may not exceed** what the district was projected to collect under its **original 2025–2026 tax warrant**.
- Imposes an administrative deadline: new rates must be reported to New Castle County and delivered with a **new warrant within 10 business days** of the Act’s effective date.
- Directs New Castle County to **supplement/adjust any already-issued tax bills** using the new rates once a new warrant is received.
- Extends the payment deadline for **supplemented/adjusted 2025–2026 property tax bills** to **November 30, 2025**.
- Requires overpayment handling when bills decrease after payment: overpayments must be **credited to future school tax liability**, unless the taxpayer submits a **written refund request**, in which case the County must issue a **refund**.
- Allows a district facing a cash-flow shortfall due to the extended payment deadline to request an advance from **State Division I funds** (the **State may advance** those monies).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Flexibility to shift tax burden between property types:** Districts may use separate residential and non-residential rates, with the non-residential rate permitted up to **2×** the residential rate (could reduce residential rates if increased non-residential rates offset within the revenue cap).
- **Revenue cap limits overall increase:** Projected revenue under the reset rates **may not exceed** the amount projected under the district’s original **2025–2026 tax warrant**, limiting total levy growth relative to the original warrant.
- **Taxpayer protections for downward adjustments:** If a taxpayer paid before a downward adjustment, the County must provide an automatic **credit** or a **refund upon written request**.
- **Cash-flow backstop for districts:** Districts may request, and the State may advance, **State Division I funds** if a cash-flow shortfall occurs due to the deadline extension.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential higher non-residential tax rate burden:** Non-residential rates must be **≥ residential** and may be as high as **2×**, which could increase taxes for non-residential properties depending on the district’s reset.
- **Administrative complexity and re-billing:** New Castle County must supplement/adjust already-issued bills and change payment timelines, increasing billing and customer service workload.
- **Delayed local revenue collection:** Extending payment deadlines to **November 30, 2025** may delay receipt of local school funds, potentially requiring reliance on State advances.
- **Uneven applicability:** The authority applies only to districts located entirely in **New Castle County**, not to districts in other counties.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Authority granted (New Castle County districts only):** A school board may reset the **2025–2026** local school tax rate using **two rates** (residential and non-residential).
- **Rate constraint:** Non-residential rate must be **≥** residential rate and **≤ 2×** the residential rate.
- **Revenue constraint:** Projected revenue under the reset rates **may not exceed** projected revenue under the district’s **original 2025–2026 tax warrant**.
- **Reporting/warrant deadline:** New rates must be reported to the County and delivered with a **new warrant within 10 business days** of the Act’s effective date.
- **County billing duties:** New Castle County must **supplement/adjust** already-issued bills and extend the payment deadline for affected bills to **November 30, 2025**.
- **Overpayment handling:** Downward adjustments after payment require a **credit** unless a taxpayer submits a **written refund request**, triggering a required **refund**.
- **Cash-flow provision:** If a cash-flow shortfall occurs due to the extended deadline, a district may request and the State may **advance** funds from **State Division I**.

## Who Benefits
- **School districts located entirely in New Castle County** that want to restructure the residential vs. non-residential tax rate mix for **2025–2026** within the revenue cap.
- **Taxpayers** whose bills are adjusted downward after payment (eligible for credit or refund upon written request).
- Potentially **residential taxpayers** if a district chooses a lower residential rate offset by a higher non-residential rate within the revenue neutrality limit (the bill authorizes this structure; specific outcomes depend on district action).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Non-residential property taxpayers** in affected districts if districts set a higher non-residential rate (up to **2×** the residential rate).
- **New Castle County finance/billing operations** due to required rebilling, deadline extensions, and refund/credit processing.
- **State Division I fund administration/cash management** if advances are requested (the Act allows advances but does not specify limits or terms).
