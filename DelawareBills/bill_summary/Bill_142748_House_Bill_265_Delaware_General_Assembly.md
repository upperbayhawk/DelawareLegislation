## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 11 to provide **limited immunity** from certain **low-level drug and alcohol offenses** for individuals who, **in good faith**, report a **sexual offense** or seek assistance related to a sexual offense, and to prevent related **pretrial release, probation, or parole** consequences based on immune conduct.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates **limited immunity** from being **issued a citation, charged, arrested, or prosecuted** for specified **alcohol offenses** and **drug offenses** when tied to reporting/seeking help for a **sexual offense**.  
- Applies when the person **in good faith** reports a sexual offense, requests assistance for a victim, or is a **victim or witness**.  
- Immunity applies only if evidence of the drug/alcohol offense was obtained **as a result of** the sexual-offense report or request for assistance.  
- Requires the person to provide **full name, contact information**, and **sufficient information** to determine a sexual offense **may have occurred**.  
- Does **not** apply if the person is **alleged to have committed the sexual offense**.  
- Bars **revocation or modification** of **pretrial release, probation, or parole** based on an incident for which the person would be immune under the new provision.  
- Preserves law enforcement ability to prosecute using **independent-source evidence** and to investigate/prosecute **other crimes or violations**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- May reduce reluctance to report sexual offenses by limiting consequences for **low-level drug/alcohol possession or consumption** discovered through the act of reporting (immunity from citations/arrest/prosecution for defined offenses).  
- Provides added protection for individuals under **pretrial release, probation, or parole** by preventing revocation/modification based on incidents covered by the immunity.  
- Maintains accountability avenues by allowing prosecution based on an **independent source** and not preventing investigation/prosecution of **other crimes**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Limits enforcement of specified **drug/alcohol civil penalties, violations, and misdemeanors** when the evidence is derived from a sexual-offense report/request for assistance (i.e., restricts charging/citation/arrest/prosecution in these circumstances).  
- Requires law enforcement and supervision agencies to assess “**good faith**,” causation (“**obtained as a result of**”), and whether information provided is “**sufficient**,” which may complicate on-scene and charging decisions.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- What qualifies as “**sufficient information** to determine that a sexual offense may have occurred” is not defined.

- The bill references drug offenses involving “possession or consumption,” but does not specify how to treat related conduct (e.g., paraphernalia) beyond the cited sections.  
- Implementation procedures for documenting eligibility (name/contact info and sufficiency of report) are not specified.

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds/updates **11 Del. C. § 762A (Immunity relating to sexual offense reporting)** to bar citations/charges/arrest/prosecution for certain offenses when conditions are met.  
- Defines:
  - **“Alcohol offense”**: any civil penalty/violation/misdemeanor involving possession or consumption of alcohol, including **11 Del. C. § 1315 (public intoxication)** and **Title 4 § 904(b), (c), (e), (f)** (underage drinking-related offenses).  
  - **“Drug offense”**: any civil penalty/violation/misdemeanor involving possession or consumption of drugs, including **Title 16 §§ 4761, 4763, 4764, 4771(a)**.  
  - **“Sexual offense”**: any offense in **Part D, Subchapter II, Chapter 5** of Title 11.  
- Creates immunity if all conditions are met:
  1) Person **in good faith** reports a sexual offense, requests assistance for a victim, or is a **victim/witness**.  
  2) Evidence of drug/alcohol offense obtained **as a result** of that report/request.  
  3) Person provides **full name, contact info**, and **sufficient information** suggesting a sexual offense may have occurred.  
  4) Person is **not alleged** to have committed the sexual offense.  
- Supervision protections: **pretrial release, probation, or parole** may not be revoked/modified; and the incident may not be used as the basis for a **probation violation**, or a **warrant or notice to appear** for violation of probation/suspended sentence conditions, if the incident is covered by immunity.  
- Savings clauses:
  - Does not preclude prosecution based on an **independent source** of evidence.  
  - Does not preclude investigation/prosecution of **other crimes or violations** by a qualifying person.

## Who Benefits
- **Victims and witnesses** of sexual offenses, and people seeking law enforcement assistance for a victim, who might otherwise face **civil citations, arrest, or prosecution** for qualifying low-level drug/alcohol offenses discovered through reporting.  
- Individuals on **pretrial release, probation, or parole** who would otherwise face revocation/modification based on immune incidents.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Prosecutors and law enforcement agencies may have reduced ability to pursue certain **drug/alcohol civil penalties, violations, or misdemeanors** when evidence arises from a protected report/request for assistance.  
- Supervision agencies (probation/parole) may be limited in using certain incidents as the basis for **violations or revocations** when immunity applies.
