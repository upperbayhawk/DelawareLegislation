## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware insurance law (Title 18) to (1) shorten the time window for health insurers/health plans to initiate overpayment recovery, (2) tighten the fraud/misconduct exception to that deadline, and (3) strengthen pharmacy audit/recoupment notice rules and narrow when Pharmacy Audit Integrity Program protections can be bypassed.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Reduces the deadline for a health insurer/health plan to **initiate overpayment recovery** from **24 months to 12 months** after the original claim payment.  
- Defines when recovery is “initiated”: when the insurer/plan first identifies an error via an **initial audit report or similar communication delivered to the provider**.  
- Narrows the exception allowing recovery beyond the deadline for fraud/abuse/misconduct: requires misconduct be **indicated by physical review or review of claims data or statements**, rather than merely a “reasonable belief.”  
- Keeps exceptions where the time limit does **not** apply when recovery is **required by or requested by a self-insured plan**, or **required by a state or federal government plan**.  
- Requires overpayment findings to be based on **actual overpayment**, not **projections** (e.g., based on similar diagnoses/orders/refills).  
- Prohibits use of **extrapolation** to calculate recoupment/penalties in audits **unless required by state or federal law/regulations**.  
- Requires PBMs to provide **written notice** to a pharmacy identifying the processing/payment error and justifying the recoupment/chargeback.  
- Limits when pharmacy audit rules don’t apply to “investigative audits”: requires more concrete indicators of misconduct (e.g., physical review/claims data/other investigative methods), including when the audit is conducted by the State.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Faster finality for providers:** Shortening the initiation window from **24 months to 12 months** limits how long providers face potential recoupment for a paid claim.  
- **More evidence-based exception for misconduct:** Replacing “reasonable belief” with misconduct **indicated by physical review or claims data/statements** may reduce use of broad suspicion to bypass the deadline.  
- **Audit fairness/accuracy protections:** Requiring **actual overpayment** (not projection) and limiting **extrapolation** unless legally required may reduce disputed or inflated recoupments.  
- **Improved transparency for pharmacies:** PBMs must give **written notice** identifying the error and justification for any recoupment/chargeback.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced recovery window for insurers/plans:** Cutting the period from **24 months to 12 months** may limit insurers’ ability to recoup overpayments discovered later.  
- **Higher evidentiary threshold to bypass protections:** Requiring misconduct to be **indicated by physical review/claims data/statements** (rather than reasonable belief) could constrain investigative flexibility for insurers/PBMs.  
- **Administrative burden:** Requirements for written notice and stricter audit standards may increase compliance steps for PBMs and entities conducting audits.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 18 § 2730(c):** Changes overpayment recovery initiation deadline from **24 months** to **12 months** after original payment.  
- **Definition of “initiated”:** Occurs when insurer/plan first identifies an error through an **initial audit report or similar communication delivered** to the provider.  
- **Fraud/abuse/misconduct exception narrowed:** Applies when misconduct is **indicated by physical review or claims data/statements** (not merely “reasonable belief”).  
- **Exceptions retained:** No time limit where recovery is (1) **required by/initiated at the request of a self-insured plan**, or (2) **required by a state or federal government plan**.  
- **§ 2730(h):** Overpayment finding must be based on **actual overpayment**, not a **projection** based on similar patients/diagnoses/orders/refills.  
- **§ 2730(i):** Audit entity may not use **extrapolation** to calculate recoupment/penalties unless required by **state/federal law or regulations**.  
- **§ 3305A(9):** PBM must provide **written notice** to the pharmacy identifying the error and justifying recoupment/chargeback efforts.  
- **§ 3310A:** Pharmacy audit rules exclusions clarified/narrowed for investigative audits—requires indicators of misconduct based on **physical review/claims data/statements** or other investigative methods; also applies when audit is conducted by the **State**.
