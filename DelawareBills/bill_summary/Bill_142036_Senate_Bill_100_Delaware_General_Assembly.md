## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Propose the first-leg constitutional amendment to add a new Article I, § 23 to the Delaware Constitution establishing (1) marriage as a fundamental right, (2) statewide recognition and licensing of marriages regardless of gender, (3) equal treatment of legally valid marriages, and (4) a religious-freedom clarification allowing clergy and religious organizations to refuse to solemnize marriages.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds a new constitutional provision: “The right to marry is a fundamental right.” (Article I, § 23).
- Requires Delaware and its political subdivisions to **recognize marriages** “regardless of the gender of the married individuals.”
- Requires Delaware and its political subdivisions to **issue marriage licenses** to couples “regardless of the gender of the 2 individuals.”
- Requires **equal legal treatment**: “All legally valid marriages must be treated equally under the law.”
- States that marriage rights “regardless of gender” **do not modify or infringe** religious freedom under Article I, § 1.
- Explicitly protects religious autonomy: **religious organizations and clergy may refuse to solemnize** a marriage under this section.
- This bill is the **first leg** of a constitutional amendment; the **same changes must pass again after the next general election** by the next General Assembly.
- Passage requires an **affirmative vote of two-thirds of the members elected to each house** (Article XVI, § 1).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Constitutional protection of marriage equality:** Embeds recognition, licensing, and equal treatment of marriages regardless of gender in the state constitution (Article I, § 23(1)–(2)).
- **Uniform statewide rule:** Applies to “this State and its political subdivisions,” reducing variability across local governments for recognition and licensing (Article I, § 23(1)).
- **Religious liberty clarification:** Expressly states the marriage provision does not infringe religious freedom and affirms clergy/religious organizations may refuse to solemnize marriages (Article I, § 23(3)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Extended timeline to take full effect as a constitutional amendment:** The synopsis states the amendment must pass again “after the next general election” by the next General Assembly, delaying final constitutional adoption.
- **Higher voting threshold to enact:** Requires a **two-thirds** vote in each chamber (synopsis referencing Article XVI, § 1), making enactment procedurally harder than ordinary legislation.
