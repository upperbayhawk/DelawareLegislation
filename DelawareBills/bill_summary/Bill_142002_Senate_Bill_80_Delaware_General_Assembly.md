## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Adopt the Uniform Public Expression Protection Act (UPEPA) in Delaware (Title 10, new Chapter 60) to provide expedited procedures and remedies to dismiss certain civil claims based on protected speech, petition, assembly, press, or association activity (i.e., to address “SLAPP” lawsuits), and to clarify how the new law interacts with Delaware’s existing anti-SLAPP provisions (10 Del. C. §§ 8136–8138).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new **Chapter 60 (Uniform Public Expression Protection Act)** in Title 10 establishing a **special motion for expedited relief** to dismiss covered claims.  
- Sets a default deadline to file the special motion: **within 60 days** after being served, unless the court finds **good cause** for a later filing.  
- Imposes an automatic **stay of proceedings and discovery** upon filing the motion (with limited exceptions), lasting through the court’s ruling and the appeal window; broader stay applies during an appeal.  
- Requires the court to **hear the motion within 60 days** of filing (subject to limited extensions) and **rule within 60 days** after the hearing.  
- Creates an **appeal as of right** from denial (in whole or part) of the motion; notice of appeal must be filed within **10 days** after entry of the order.  
- Establishes **mandatory fee-shifting**: prevailing movant gets **court costs, reasonable attorney’s fees, and reasonable litigation expenses** related to the motion; prevailing respondent gets the same only if the motion was **frivolous** or filed solely to **delay**.  
- Authorizes **punitive damages** for a prevailing movant if the court finds the claim was brought/continued to **harass, intimidate, punish, or maliciously inhibit** protected rights.  
- Provides **transitional rules**: Chapter 60 applies to civil actions filed/on causes of action asserted **on or after the Act’s effective date**; prior-filed matters continue under **§§ 8136–8138**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Faster resolution of covered speech-related suits**: mandated timelines (hearing within **60 days**; ruling within **60 days** after hearing) can reduce time a defendant remains in litigation.  
- **Reduced litigation costs during motion phase**: filing triggers a **stay of discovery and other proceedings** between the moving and responding parties (with limited discovery only if necessary and otherwise unavailable).  
- **Deterrence of abusive suits**: mandatory awards of **costs and attorney’s fees** to a prevailing movant, and potential **punitive damages** when suits are found maliciously inhibiting protected rights.  
- **Broader protection framework**: Chapter 60 is to be **broadly construed** to protect constitutional speech/press/petition/association activity and to promote **uniformity** with other states adopting the uniform act.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Delays for plaintiffs in stayed cases**: once the motion is filed, proceedings (including discovery) are stayed until the motion is decided and the **10-day** appeal window expires; if appealed, the stay generally extends until the appeal concludes.  
- **Increased motion practice**: the Act creates a new, specialized dismissal motion with required hearings and rulings, which may add procedural steps in covered cases.  
- **Fee and punitive exposure for responding parties**: if a defendant prevails on the special motion, the court **must** award costs/fees/expenses related to the motion, and may award **punitive damages** upon specified findings.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Title 10, Chapter 60 (UPEPA)** including:
  - **Coverage**: applies to civil causes of action based on a person’s:
    - communication in governmental proceedings;  
    - communication on issues under review in governmental proceedings; or  
    - exercise of constitutional speech/press/assembly/petition/association rights on a **matter of public concern**.  
  - **Exclusions / carve-outs (as enacted)**: Chapter 60 generally does **not** apply to causes of action:
    - against governmental units or their officials acting in an official capacity;  
    - **by** a governmental unit (or official) enforcing a law, regulation, or ordinance;  
    - against persons primarily selling/leasing goods or services where the claim arises from communications related to sales/leases;  
    - based on **common law fraud**; or  
    - alleging violations of **Subchapter II or III of Chapter 25 of Title 6**.  
  - **Consumer review exception to exclusions**: despite exclusions for (c)(3), (c)(4), or (c)(5), Chapter 60 **does apply** when the cause of action is a legal action related to **consumer opinions/commentary, evaluations of consumer complaints, or reviews/ratings of businesses** (i.e., communications “gathering, receiving, posting, or processing” such content).
- **Creates special expedited dismissal process**:
  - File motion within **60 days** of service (or later for **good cause**).  
  - **Stay** of proceedings and discovery upon filing (limited discovery allowed only if necessary and not otherwise available).  
  - Court must **hear** within **60 days** of filing (with limited extension rules) and **rule** within **60 days** after hearing.  
  - Denial is **appealable as of right**; appeal must be filed within **10 days**.
- **Remedies and deterrents**:
  - Mandatory award of **court costs, reasonable attorney’s fees, and reasonable litigation expenses related to the motion** to the prevailing movant; to the prevailing respondent only if motion was **frivolous** or solely for **delay**.  
  - **Punitive damages** to prevailing movant if suit was brought/continued to **harass, intimidate, punish, or otherwise maliciously inhibit** protected rights.
- **Interplay with existing Delaware anti-SLAPP statute (§§ 8136–8138)**:
  - The text indicates §§ 8136–8138 are effectively superseded for new cases (“[Repealed, but see Chapter 60 of this title.]” appears in those sections).  
  - **Section 5**: §§ 8136–8138 **continue to apply** to causes of action asserted **before the effective date** of the Act.

## Who Benefits
- Individuals and entities sued based on protected communications or participation related to governmental proceedings or matters of public concern (including speech/press/petition/association activity).  
- Defendants seeking early dismissal and recovery of **costs/fees/expenses** related to the special motion.  
- Persons and entities involved in handling or posting **consumer reviews/ratings** and related commentary, where Chapter 60 applies notwithstanding certain exclusions.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Plaintiffs bringing civil claims that fall within Chapter 60’s scope, due to:
  - potential early dismissal **with prejudice**;  
  - mandatory liability for the other side’s **costs/attorney’s fees/expenses** related to the motion if the movant prevails; and  
  - possible **punitive damages** findings in specified circumstances.  
- Parties seeking discovery and litigation progress during the pendency of a special motion (because of the **stay**).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approved:** **September 15, 2025** (enrolled law).  
- **Effective date:** Not specified in source text (Chapter 60 transitional provisions refer to “[the effective date of this Act]”).  
- **Deadlines created by the Act:**
  - Special motion to dismiss: file **within 60 days** after service (or later upon **good cause**).  
  - Hearing: court shall hear motion **within 60 days** after filing (extensions allowed for limited discovery or other good cause; if discovery allowed, hearing within **60 days** after the discovery order unless further good cause).  
  - Ruling: court shall rule **within 60 days** after the hearing.  
  - Appeal: moving party must appeal denial **within 10 days** after entry of the order.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Procedural compliance**: parties must meet the **60-day filing deadline** for the special motion unless good cause is shown.  
- **Automatic stay**: filing the motion triggers a **stay** of proceedings/discovery (subject to enumerated exceptions and limited discovery upon showing necessity).  
- **Fee-shifting enforcement**: court **shall award** court costs, reasonable attorney’s fees, and reasonable litigation expenses related to the motion to the prevailing party under the conditions stated in § 6010.  
- **Punitive damages**: court **shall award** punitive damages to a prevailing movant upon finding the claim was commenced/continued to **harass, intimidate, punish, or otherwise maliciously inhibit** protected rights.
