## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends **Title 24, § 1922 (Board of Nursing disciplinary proceedings)** to authorize the Delaware Board of Nursing to issue **non-disciplinary, confidential “letters of concern”** after an investigation finds **no violation warranting formal discipline**, and to allow a **competency hearing after a licensee receives 3 letters of concern**, at which point the letters may lose confidentiality and be used as evidence.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes the Board of Nursing to issue a **non-disciplinary, confidential letter of concern** when a nurse’s conduct is concerning but **does not amount to a violation warranting formal disciplinary action**.  
- Establishes a **threshold of 3 letters of concern**: upon receipt of **a total of 3**, the Board **may** hold a hearing to assess professional competency.  
- The purpose of the letter is explicitly to **encourage improvement in practice** by informing the licensee of the concern.  
- At a competency hearing triggered by **3 letters**, the letters **may be deemed no longer confidential**.  
- At that hearing, the letters **may be admitted into evidence**.  
- The bill uses discretionary authority (“**may**”) for both issuing letters and holding hearings.  
- The act was **approved August 25, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Earlier intervention without formal discipline:** Allows the Board to document and communicate practice concerns even when conduct does not meet the level for “formal disciplinary action.” (Creates a “letter of concern” mechanism.)
- **Structured escalation point:** Creates a clear escalation threshold (**3 letters of concern**) enabling the Board to assess competency to protect “health, safety, and welfare of the public.”
- **Confidentiality for minor concerns:** Provides for **confidential** letters initially, which may encourage improvement without immediate reputational harm.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential loss of confidentiality after 3 letters:** If a hearing occurs, letters “**may be deemed no longer confidential**” and used as evidence, which could expose prior concerns that were initially confidential.
- **Unclear standards for “matter of concern”:** The Board may issue letters when conduct is a “matter of concern” and practice “may be improved,” which may be viewed as a broad standard (the bill does not further define these terms).
- **Added compliance/appearance burden:** A licensee with **3 letters** may be required (through the Board’s discretionary decision to hold a hearing) to appear for a competency assessment.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Definition and criteria:** Not specified how the Board will define or apply “**matter of concern**,” “**practice may be improved**,” or what specific conduct qualifies.
- **Process details:** Not specified what procedures apply to issuing letters (notice, opportunity to respond, timelines) beyond being issued “after an investigation.”
- **Hearing mechanics and consequences:** Not specified what outcomes may follow a competency hearing (e.g., remedial education, monitoring, discipline), only that the hearing may assess competency and letters may be admitted.
- **Confidentiality standard:** The bill states letters “may be deemed no longer confidential” at the hearing, but does not specify who decides, under what standard, or the scope of disclosure.

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds/clarifies Board authority in **24 Del. C. § 1922(e)** to:
  1) Issue **non-disciplinary confidential letters of concern** after an investigation finds no violation warranting formal discipline, but identifies a practice concern.  
  2) If a licensee receives **a total of 3 letters of concern**, the Board **may hold a hearing** to assess professional competency.  
  3) At that hearing, all letters of concern **may lose confidentiality** and **may be admitted into evidence**.
