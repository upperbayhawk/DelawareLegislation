## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 11 Del. C. § 907B to expand Delaware’s criminal impersonation offense to cover impersonation of a **federal officer**, add definitions (including “law-enforcement official”), and make technical drafting corrections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands the existing felony offense covering impersonation of **police officer, firefighter, EMT, paramedic, or fire police** to also prohibit impersonation of a **federal officer**.  
- Creates a definition of **“Federal officer”** as an officer/agent of the federal government with **power to make arrests under federal law**.  
- Creates/uses a defined umbrella term **“law-enforcement official”** meaning **police officer, firefighter, EMT, paramedic, fire police, or federal officer**.  
- Keeps the offense tied to circumstances where the person acts **“intending to facilitate the commission of a crime or while in immediate flight therefrom.”**  
- Provides two ways to commit the offense: (1) impersonate/pretend to be a covered official, or (2) do an act intended to create/reinforce a false impression of being a covered official.  
- Retains felony grading structure: base offense is a **Class E felony**; elevated to **Class C felony** if certain aggravators occur (physical injury to a non-participant, or commission of certain serious offenses).  
- The enacted chapter heading reflects **Class E felony, Class D felony, Class C felony** (as amended by House Amendment No. 1), but the provided text does not clearly specify the operative **Class D** triggering criteria beyond listing it in subsection (b)’s penalty line.  
- Approved **July 14, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Closes a coverage gap** by explicitly including **federal officers** with arrest authority, allowing state prosecution under § 907B when someone impersonates federal law enforcement “intending to facilitate the commission of a crime” or during “immediate flight.”  
- **Improves clarity and consistency** by defining “law-enforcement official” to include all covered roles in one term, which may simplify charging and interpretation.  
- **Maintains enhanced penalties** (Class C felony) when impersonation is tied to **physical injury** or to commission of **Class A/Class B felonies or sexual offenses** (as defined in § 761(i)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Broader criminal scope**: Adding “federal officer” expands who is protected by the impersonation statute, increasing the set of conduct eligible for felony prosecution under § 907B.  
- **Potential penalty ambiguity**: The enacted heading and penalty clause reference **Class D felony**, but the provided text excerpt does not clearly describe when the offense becomes a Class D felony (creating potential interpretive/implementation challenges).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds federal officers to covered officials**: Criminal impersonation now includes impersonation of a **“federal officer”** (defined as having arrest power under federal law).  
- **Defines “law-enforcement official”** to include: police officer, firefighter, EMT, paramedic, fire police, or federal officer.  
- **Rephrases statutory language** for technical drafting conformity (e.g., punctuation/structure corrections) without changing the core requirement that the impersonation be tied to facilitating a crime or immediate flight.  
- **Penalty framework (as stated in text)**:
  - Base offense: **Class E felony** (and text also references **Class D felony**, but criteria are not clearly specified in the provided excerpt).  
  - Elevated: **Class C felony** if, during the crime or immediate flight, the person or another participant:
    - **Causes physical injury** to a non-participant; or  
    - **Commits a Class A or Class B felony** or **any sexual offense** as defined by **11 Del. C. § 761(i)**.
