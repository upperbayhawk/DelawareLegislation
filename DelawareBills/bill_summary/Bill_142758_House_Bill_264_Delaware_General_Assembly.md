## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16, §2605 of the Delaware Code to add a **school nurse** as a member of the **Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Advisory Committee (CLPPAC)**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds **one new committee seat**: a **school nurse in Delaware** becomes a CLPPAC member.  
- The **Speaker of the House of Representatives** appoints the school nurse member.  
- The **Delaware School Nurse Association (DSNA)** *may* provide a list of potential nominees for the Speaker to consider.  
- The bill updates **16 Del. C. §2605(c)** (committee membership list) to include the new member.  
- No changes in the bill text to lead screening requirements, testing thresholds, or reporting requirements—this is a **governance/representation change**.  
- The synopsis states the intent is to strengthen implementation of the Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Act by including school nurse perspectives in policy and practice.  
- **No funding, appropriations, fees, or penalties** are specified in the bill text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Adds frontline implementation expertise**: The synopsis states school nurses have been “on the front lines” ensuring lead screening is completed prior to starting pre-K and kindergarten, suggesting membership could align committee work with school-based realities.
- **Improves coordination around elevated blood lead levels**: The synopsis states school nurses connect students/families to academic and health resources when a child tests positive for an elevated blood lead level.
- **May support equity-focused work**: The synopsis notes DSNA advocacy participation addressing “racial, ethnic, and socioeconomic disparities” in lead poisoning.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expanded committee size/complexity**: Adding a member may modestly increase coordination needs (meeting scheduling, quorum dynamics), though the bill text does not quantify impacts.
- **Appointment discretion remains broad**: DSNA only “may” provide nominees and the Speaker “may consider” them, so representation goals are not guaranteed by a required nomination process.
