## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 11 of the Delaware Code to (1) remove statutory authorization for **private persons** to make **warrantless arrests** based on information that a person is **charged with a felony in another state**, and (2) remove a related provision about an arresting officer’s authority to **command assistance** in such arrests.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Eliminates authorization for a **private person** to make a **warrantless arrest** when the person is believed to be **charged in another state** with a crime punishable by **death** or **imprisonment exceeding 1 year** (i.e., felony-level threshold).  
- Retains that a **peace officer** may make such a warrantless arrest under the same described conditions (the text continues to reference “any peace officer”).  
- Strikes language in 11 Del. C. § 2509 concerning an arresting officer’s authority to **command assistance** in making arrests under that subchapter, including “like penalties against those who refuse their assistance.”  
- Leaves in place the requirement that, after such an arrest, the accused must be taken before a judge or justice of the peace **“with all practicable speed”** and a sworn complaint must be made as described in § 2513.  
- Applies to arrests “under this subchapter” concerning persons charged in the courts of another state (as reflected in the synopsis and the amended sections).  
- Enacted as **Volume 85, Chapter 72** (formerly House Bill 142).  
- **Approved July 14, 2025.**

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces private-citizen arrest authority** for out-of-state felony charges by removing explicit statutory authorization for private persons to arrest without a warrant in these circumstances (amended § 2514; synopsis).  
- **Clarifies/limits compelled assistance expectations** by striking § 2509 language about commanding assistance and penalties for refusing assistance (amended § 2509).  
- Keeps post-arrest procedural safeguards in place by continuing to require presentation to a judge/justice of the peace “with all practicable speed” and a **sworn complaint** (amended § 2514).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Narrows tools available to private individuals** to detain/arrest someone believed to be charged with a serious out-of-state crime, because the statute no longer authorizes private-person warrantless arrests in that specific scenario (synopsis; amended § 2514).  
- Removes statutory text referencing **penalties** for refusing assistance when commanded in these arrests (stricken § 2509 language), potentially reducing a mechanism described in the prior statute for compelling assistance (as previously stated in § 2509).

## Key Policy Changes
- **11 Del. C. § 2514 (Arrest without warrant):** Removes authorization for a **private person** to make a warrantless arrest based on “reasonable information” that the accused is charged “in the courts of a state” with a crime punishable by **death** or **imprisonment for a term exceeding 1 year.”  
- **11 Del. C. § 2509:** Strikes the provision describing authority of an arresting officer (or other empowered person) to **command assistance** in making arrests under the subchapter, including referenced **penalties** for refusal of assistance.  
- Keeps existing process language requiring prompt presentation before a judge/justice of the peace and a **sworn complaint** following the arrest (§ 2514 referencing § 2513).

## Who Benefits
- Individuals in Delaware who might otherwise be subject to a **warrantless arrest by a private person** based on an alleged out-of-state felony charge (change described in synopsis and § 2514 amendment).  
- Peace officers and courts may benefit from clearer delineation that **peace officers** (not private persons) are the actors authorized in this specific warrantless arrest scenario (as reflected by removal described in synopsis).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Private persons who previously could rely on § 2514 as explicit authority to make a **warrantless arrest** in Delaware based on knowledge of an out-of-state felony-level charge (synopsis; § 2514 change).  
- Persons/entities that could be **commanded to assist** in such arrests under prior § 2509 language—because that statutory command-assistance provision is struck (amended § 2509).
