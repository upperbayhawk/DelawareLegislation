## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 25 Del. C. § 5314 to expand a tenant’s statutory right to terminate a rental agreement early by adding a new qualifying reason: entering a contract to purchase a primary residence. The bill also makes technical and conforming drafting updates.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds a new ground for early lease termination: a tenant may terminate when the tenant **enters into a contract to purchase real property** the tenant **intends to occupy as a primary residence**.  
- Requires **30 days’ written notice** for early termination; the **30-day period begins on the first day of the month following the day of actual notice**.  
- Clarifies wording in existing law (e.g., “in excess of 30 miles” revised to **“more than 30 miles”** for job-relocation terminations).  
- Updates the senior-facility ground by replacing “group or cooperative living facility or retirement home” with **“long-term care facility as defined in § 1102 of Title 16.”**  
- Removes duplicative phrasing in the senior-housing ground by deleting references to “subsidized public or private housing” because similar language appears in the separate subsidized-unit ground.  
- Keeps the existing early-termination grounds (job relocation, serious illness/death, senior/long-term care admission, subsidized unit admission, military active duty, domestic abuse/sexual offenses/stalking, death of tenant).  
- Does not state any new fees, damages, or penalties associated with these changes in the provided text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Facilitates homeownership transitions:** Tenants under a lease gain an explicit statutory right to terminate early upon **signing a home purchase contract** for a **primary residence**, using the same **30-day written notice** framework as other protected situations.  
- **Administrative clarity:** Conforming edits (including the notice-period clarification and drafting-manual updates) may reduce disputes about when the **30-day clock** starts (first day of the following month after actual notice).  
- **Updated terminology:** Aligns certain housing/facility terms with an existing statutory definition (**Title 16, § 1102 “long-term care facility”**), which may improve consistency across Delaware law.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced lease certainty for landlords:** Landlords may face additional early terminations triggered by **home purchase contracts**, potentially increasing vacancy/turnover risk (the bill adds a new termination category).  
- **Potential timing impacts:** Because notice runs from **the first day of the month after actual notice**, landlords may have less immediate lead time depending on when notice is delivered (e.g., notice late in a month can push the 30-day period start to the next month).
