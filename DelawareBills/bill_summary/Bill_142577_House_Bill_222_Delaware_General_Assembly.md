## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 3 to modernize and increase civil fines the Delaware Nutrient Management Commission may impose for violations of the State Nutrient Management Program (SNMP).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Increases the minimum fine per violation from **$25** to **$125** under **3 Del. C. § 2244(b)**.
- Increases the maximum fine per violation from **$1,000** to **$5,000** under **§ 2244(b)**.
- Keeps the rule that **each day of a continued violation may be treated as a separate violation**, with a cumulative **limit of $10,000** (unchanged).
- Applies to enforcement actions where the Commission, **after notice and opportunity for a hearing**, finds a certified person “has been found guilty” of a violation of the chapter or Commission regulations (**§ 2244(a)**).
- Does not change the Commission’s existing authorities to **suspend, revoke, or modify** a certificate, or to impose a fine, or both (**§ 2244(a)**).
- Directs that **fines collected by the Department of Agriculture** remain **appropriated to the Department** for compliance assistance or research demonstrations benefiting compliance (**§ 2244(c)**).
- Leaves the appeal process for certificate actions (suspension/modification/revocation) tied to **§ 2263** unchanged (**§ 2244(d)**).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger deterrence for violations:** Raising fines to **$125–$5,000 per violation** increases financial consequences for SNMP noncompliance (text: amended **§ 2244(b)**; bill “modernize penalties”).
- **Supports compliance assistance/research:** Continued earmarking of collected fines for **assisting persons in achieving compliance** or demonstrating beneficial research may improve program outcomes (**§ 2244(c)**).
- **Targets chronic/noncompliant behavior:** Legislative findings emphasize holding the regulated community accountable for “**chronic violations**” (WHEREAS clauses).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher penalty exposure for regulated parties:** Minimum and maximum fines increase from **$25–$1,000** to **$125–$5,000** per violation, raising potential costs for violators (**§ 2244(b)**).
- **Potential for rapid accumulation via daily violations:** Because **each day** may be treated as a separate violation (up to **$10,000**), higher per-violation amounts could increase total liability more quickly in ongoing cases (**§ 2244(b)**).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date not stated:** The bill text provided does not specify when the new fine amounts take effect.
- **Scope of “found guilty” standard:** The text says the Commission may act if a person “**has been found guilty of any violation**,” but does not clarify whether this refers to an administrative finding, judicial determination, or other process (**§ 2244(a)**).
- **How the $10,000 limit applies:** The provision states daily violations may be separate “**up to a limit of $10,000**,” but does not specify whether this cap is per case, per continuing violation period, or per enforcement action (**§ 2244(b)**).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Fine range increased:** From **$25–$1,000** per violation to **$125–$5,000** per violation (**3 Del. C. § 2244(b)**).
- **Daily-violation provision retained:** Each day of continued violation may be treated as a separate violation, **capped at $10,000** (no change stated) (**§ 2244(b)**).
- **No change to certification enforcement tools:** Commission authority to suspend/revoke/modify certificates and/or fine after notice and hearing remains (**§ 2244(a)**).
- **No change to fines allocation:** Funds remain directed to Department of Agriculture compliance assistance or research demonstrations (**§ 2244(c)**).
