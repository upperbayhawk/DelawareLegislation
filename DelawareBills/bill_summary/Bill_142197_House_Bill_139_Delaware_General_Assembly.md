## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 18, § 6605 of the Delaware Code to change when the Insurance Commissioner must hold hearings on Line-of-Duty Death claims, and make technical drafting corrections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Eliminates the requirement that the Insurance Commissioner hold a hearing for **every** Line-of-Duty Death claim.  
- Requires a hearing **only for contested claims**.  
- Allows the Commissioner to decide **uncontested claims “on the papers.”**  
- Requires that **for all claims** (contested or uncontested), the Commissioner must **receive evidence, make findings, and render a decision**.  
- Maintains the Commissioner’s authority to **subpoena witnesses and records** and **administer oaths**.  
- Maintains the requirement to keep a **written or taped record of testimony** when testimony is given and to issue **written findings and reasons** for decisions.  
- Continues to allow the Commissioner to seek guidance from the **Attorney General**.  
- Continues to authorize the Commissioner to issue **regulations** governing submission, processing, and adjudication of claims, including hearings.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduced burden on families/covered persons:** The bill synopsis states hearings “can be emotional burdens for families and covered persons,” and the bill reduces hearings to only contested claims.
- **Potentially faster adjudication for uncontested claims:** The synopsis notes hearings “can delay the adjudication of the claim,” and the bill expressly permits uncontested claims to be determined “on the papers.”
- **Lower administrative cost for the Department of Insurance:** The synopsis states hearings “are costly for the Department of Insurance to conduct,” and the bill reduces the number of required hearings.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Fewer formal hearing opportunities in uncontested cases:** By removing the requirement for a hearing in every claim, claimants in uncontested matters will generally not have an automatic hearing forum unless the matter becomes contested (text: hearing required only on “every contested claim”; uncontested may be decided “on the papers”).
- **Less opportunity for live testimony in uncontested claims:** The bill allows paper adjudication for uncontested claims, which may reduce in-person presentation unless otherwise required by the Commissioner (text: “may determine uncontested claims on the papers”).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Hearing requirement narrowed:**  
  - Prior approach (as described by the bill’s changes): hearing required for **every** claim.  
  - New rule: the Commissioner **must hold a hearing on every contested claim**; **uncontested claims may be determined on the papers**.
- **Decision-making requirements apply to all claims:** For all claims, the Commissioner must **receive evidence, make findings, and render a decision**.
- **Powers and documentation preserved:**  
  - Commissioner retains power to **subpoena** witnesses/records and **administer oaths**.  
  - Must keep a **written or taped record of testimony** when testimony is given.  
  - Must provide **written findings and reasons** for the decision.
- **Regulatory authority continues:** Commissioner may issue regulations governing claim submission, processing, adjudication, and hearings.

## Who Benefits
- **Families and covered persons** associated with Line-of-Duty Death claims, by avoiding mandatory hearings in uncontested cases (synopsis: hearings can be an emotional burden and cause delays).
- **Delaware Department of Insurance**, due to reduced need to conduct hearings in uncontested matters (synopsis: hearings are costly).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Claimants who prefer an automatic hearing even when a claim is uncontested**, because the bill removes the across-the-board hearing requirement and permits paper adjudication for uncontested claims.
