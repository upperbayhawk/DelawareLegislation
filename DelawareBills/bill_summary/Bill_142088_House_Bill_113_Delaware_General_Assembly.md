## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29, Chapter 55 to deny Education Employees’ Retirement and disability benefits to covered education employees (and their survivors/beneficiaries) following certain child sex-crime convictions, and to deny benefits in a specific indictment/flight circumstance.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Denies retirement or disability benefits under Chapter 55 to covered “employees” after conviction of:
  - sexual abuse of a child,
  - sexual solicitation of a child, or
  - promoting sexual solicitation of a child.
- Applies the denial not only to the employee, but also to any survivor or beneficiary of the employee who would otherwise qualify for those benefits.
- Clarifies the definition of “employees” for this section to include individuals employed by:
  - the State Department of Education,
  - a school district in the state school system,
  - the University of Delaware,
  - Delaware State University, or
  - Delaware Technical and Community College.
- Adds benefit denial for an individual who is **under indictment** for the listed offenses and **willfully remains outside the United States for more than 1 month** to avoid prosecution.
- Establishes a specific time threshold: **more than 1 month outside the United States** (when willfully done to avoid prosecution) triggers denial while under indictment.
- No dollar amounts, appropriations, or explicit implementation deadlines are stated in the bill text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Strengthens consequences tied to serious child sex offenses by removing eligibility for retirement/disability benefits after conviction (bill text §5535(b)).
- Extends the benefit-denial rule to include flight to avoid prosecution, if the person is under indictment and remains outside the U.S. **more than 1 month** (bill text §5535(c)).
- Clarifies which education-related employers’ workers are covered by this section, potentially improving administrative certainty (bill text §5535(a)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Denial extends to an employee’s **survivor or beneficiary** who “may otherwise be qualified” to receive benefits (bill text §5535(b)), potentially reducing benefits to individuals other than the convicted employee.
- Creates a benefit denial trigger tied to being **under indictment** (not conviction) in the circumstance of remaining outside the U.S. for **more than 1 month** to avoid prosecution (bill text §5535(c)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Benefit forfeiture after conviction:** An employee (and the employee’s survivor/beneficiary) otherwise qualified for retirement or disability benefits under Chapter 55 **“shall not be permitted to receive these benefits”** following conviction of:
  - sexual abuse of a child,
  - sexual solicitation of a child, or
  - promoting sexual solicitation of a child. (§5535(b))
- **Indictment + flight rule:** Benefits are also denied to an individual **under indictment** for one of the listed offenses who **willfully remains outside the United States for more than 1 month to avoid prosecution**. (§5535(c))
- **Covered employers clarified for this section:** “Employees” for this section includes individuals employed by DOE, school districts in the state school system, UD, DSU, and Delaware Tech. (§5535(a))

## Who Benefits
- The State retirement/disability system administrators and the public interest in restricting benefits for covered employees convicted of the specified offenses (as reflected by the benefit-denial provisions in §5535(b)).
- Potentially, employing education institutions listed in §5535(a), through clearer applicability of the rule.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Covered education employees convicted of the specified offenses, who would otherwise qualify for retirement or disability benefits (denied under §5535(b)).
- Survivors and beneficiaries of such employees, who may otherwise qualify for benefits (also denied under §5535(b)).
- Individuals under indictment for the specified offenses who willfully remain outside the U.S. for **more than 1 month** to avoid prosecution (denied under §5535(c)).
