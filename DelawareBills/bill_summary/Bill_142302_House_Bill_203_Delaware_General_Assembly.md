## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Require Delaware public high schools (school districts and charter schools) to offer at least a **½-credit financial literacy course** and, starting with the **2026–2027** entering 9th-grade class, require students to **successfully complete** that **½-credit** as part of the **Social Studies credits** needed to earn a high school diploma.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires each **school district or charter school serving high school students** to provide, at minimum, a **½-credit** course in **financial literacy**.  
- Starting with **students entering grade 9 in the 2026–2027 school year**, students must **successfully complete** a **½-credit** in Financial Literacy to receive a high school diploma (as part of required **Social Studies** credits).  
- The required course must use a **standards-aligned curriculum** with **high rigorous instructional materials** and **assessments**.  
- Local education agencies must make available **high-quality initial and ongoing professional learning** for teachers.  
- The **Department of Education**, with **State Board of Education approval**, must adopt/update regulations to: (1) include **Financial Literacy Standards** as part of **Social Studies Standards**, and (2) treat **Financial Literacy** as a **content area within Social Studies**.  
- The **Department of Education and the Professional Standards Board** must determine what **educator certification** is needed to teach the required credit.  
- Fiscal note: costs are assumed to be **local funds**; fiscal impact is **indeterminable** for **FY2026–FY2028**, and could be **low to no cost** depending on implementation and use of no-cost providers.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Universal access requirement:** Ensures all high schools serving grades **9–12** offer at least a **½-credit** course in financial literacy.  
- **Graduation-linked completion:** By tying the **½-credit** to diploma requirements beginning with the **2026–2027** 9th-grade cohort, the bill increases the likelihood students complete financial literacy instruction.  
- **Standards and assessments required:** Mandates a **standards-aligned curriculum** with **assessments**, supporting consistency of instruction statewide.  
- **Professional learning requirement:** Requires that teachers have access to **initial and ongoing** professional learning, which may improve instructional quality.  
- **Potential for no-cost supports:** Fiscal note states “**several organizations**” provide professional development and curriculum **at no cost**, which could reduce local expenses.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Unfunded local mandate (per fiscal note assumptions):** All costs are assumed to be paid through **local funds**, with fiscal impact listed as **indeterminable** for **FY2026, FY2027, FY2028**.  
- **Staffing/certification uncertainty:** The number of educators needed is “**unknown**,” and certification requirements are to be determined by DOE and the Professional Standards Board, which could complicate staffing and scheduling.  
- **Implementation variability:** Local education agencies may choose providers and materials; this autonomy could lead to uneven implementation across districts/charters (the bill specifies requirements but leaves provider selection local).  

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Certification details and timing:** The bill directs DOE and the Professional Standards Board to determine certification needed, but does not specify **what certification** or **by when** it must be defined.  
- **Regulatory timeline:** DOE must adopt/update regulations with State Board approval, but the bill does not provide a **deadline** for issuing regulations.  
- **Definition of “successfully complete”:** The bill requires students to “successfully complete” the **½-credit**, but does not define a passing grade/standard in statute.  
- **How it fits into existing Social Studies credit totals:** The bill says the **½-credit** counts as part of Social Studies credits required for a diploma, but does not restate the required number of Social Studies credits or how schedules must change.
