## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Clarify in Delaware law that when a **law-enforcement agency** purchases firearms **for use by its law-enforcement officers in the performance of official duties**, those procurement transactions are **exempt** from certain firearm background-check and permit-to-purchase requirements in Title 11.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Amends **11 Del. C. § 1448A** (both the **currently effective** version and the version that becomes effective upon implementation of **84 Del. Laws, c. 259, § 1**) to clarify that the existing exemption for “transactions involving a law-enforcement officer” **includes agency purchases** for officers’ official-duty use.
- Amends **11 Del. C. § 1448B** (both the **currently effective** version and the future version tied to implementation of **84 Del. Laws, c. 259, § 1**) to explicitly exempt transactions where the buyer is a **law-enforcement agency** obtaining firearms for officers’ official-duty use.
- Amends **11 Del. C. § 1448D** to clarify that an application for a handgun qualified purchaser permit submitted **as a law-enforcement agency** for an official-duty handgun is not subject to specified requirements: **paragraphs (f)(1), (f)(4), or subsection (h)**.
- The bill is framed as a **clarification** of existing practice: agency procurement for duty weapons is treated as exempt under the permit-to-purchase framework.
- Applies to procurement by a **“law-enforcement agency” as defined under § 8584** and officers defined under **§ 222**.
- No dollar amounts, fees, or appropriations are stated in the bill text.
- The enrolled act states it was **Approved January 30, 2026** (Chapter 234, Volume 85).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Operational clarity for police procurement:** The Act expressly states that agency purchases for duty use are exempt under **§§ 1448A, 1448B, and 1448D**, reducing ambiguity about whether agency purchasing must follow retail-style background check/permit steps.
- **Administrative streamlining:** By clarifying exemptions and removing specified permit-application requirements in **§ 1448D(c)(1)** for agency-related duty handguns, agencies may face fewer procedural steps for duty-weapon acquisitions (as described in the text).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced application of purchase safeguards to certain transactions:** The Act confirms that certain agency procurement transactions are **exempt** from requirements under **§§ 1448A and 1448B**, and that certain **§ 1448D** requirements (paragraphs **(f)(1)**, **(f)(4)**, and subsection **(h)**) do not apply in specified law-enforcement contexts. (The bill text does not describe what those requirements are, but it expressly exempts these transactions/conditions.)

## Key Policy Changes
- **11 Del. C. § 1448A (current and future versions):** Clarifies that the exemption for transactions involving a “law-enforcement officer” **includes transactions by a law-enforcement agency (§ 8584 definition)** purchasing firearms for officers’ **official duties**.
- **11 Del. C. § 1448B (current and future versions):** Adds/clarifies an explicit exemption for transactions where the buyer is a **law-enforcement agency** obtaining firearms for duty use by its officers.
- **11 Del. C. § 1448D(c)(1):** Clarifies that a person applying for a handgun qualified purchaser permit **as a law-enforcement agency** for a duty handgun (or as a qualified law-enforcement officer or qualified retired law-enforcement officer) is **not subject to** **(f)(1)**, **(f)(4)**, or **(h)** of § 1448D.

## Who Benefits

- **Law-enforcement officers** (as defined by **11 Del. C. § 222**) who use agency-procured firearms for official duties.
