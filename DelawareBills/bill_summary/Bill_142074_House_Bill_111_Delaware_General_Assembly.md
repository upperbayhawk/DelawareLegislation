## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Reduce the automatic provision of single-use food service items (including utensils, condiment packets, napkins, straws, stirrers, and similar items) by food establishments and third-party delivery services, to decrease litter and solid-waste impacts. (Chapter 30Q, Title 16; new § 3007Q)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits food establishments and third-party food delivery services from providing “single-use food service items” unless the customer **explicitly requests** them. (§ 3007Q(a))
- Requires ordering channels (phone, internet, mobile app) to present a **prominent, conspicuous** option for requesting these items, with the **default set to “no”** single-use items. (§ 3007Q(a)(1))
- Bars employees/services from asking **why** a customer requests the items. (§ 3007Q(a)(2))
- Allows restaurants to keep **self-service stations** where single-use items are available without violating the restriction. (§ 3007Q(a)(3))
- Creates an enforcement approach requiring an **initial written warning** and a **30-day correction period** before certain penalties apply. (§ 3005Q(c))
- Beginning **July 1, 2026**, uncorrected violations (after the warning + 30 days) become a **core item violation** of the Delaware Food Code and can trigger **administrative penalties** under the Food Code and **16 Del. C. § 107**. (§ 3005Q(a))
- Limits licensing consequences: a food establishment license **may not be suspended or revoked solely** for any single or recurring core item violation of these sections. (§ 3005Q(b))
- Establishes protections for restaurants using third-party platforms: reliance on platform-provided request information is a **complete defense**, and platforms must provide supporting records within **72 hours** of a restaurant request. (§ 3007Q(b))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduced waste/litter inputs**: The bill’s central mechanism (items only upon explicit request) directly limits automatic distribution of disposable items. (§ 3007Q(a))
- **Systemwide “default no”** on all ordering methods may reduce unnecessary items in takeout/delivery orders. (§ 3007Q(a)(1))
- **Clear compliance off-ramp before penalties**: written warning plus **30 days** to correct before violations (after July 1, 2026) escalate to core item violations. (§ 3005Q(c), § 3005Q(a))
- **Operational clarity for restaurants using delivery apps**: complete defense when relying on third-party information, plus evidentiary standards (prima facie evidence via screenshot/communication). (§ 3007Q(b)(2)-(3))
- **Limits severe sanctions**: prohibits suspension/revocation solely for these violations. (§ 3005Q(b))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Implementation burden on ordering systems**: food establishments and third-party platforms must add and display a “request single-use items” option prominently across phone, internet, and mobile app ordering, with a “no items” default. (§ 3007Q(a)(1))
- **Compliance exposure after July 1, 2026**: uncorrected violations become “core item violations” subject to administrative penalties under the Delaware Food Code and 16 Del. C. § 107. (§ 3005Q(a))
- **Record-production obligation for delivery platforms**: must provide a copy/screenshot of relevant customer-choice communications **within 72 hours** of restaurant request. (§ 3007Q(b)(3))

## Key Policy Changes
- **Opt-in requirement**: single-use food service items may be provided **only if explicitly requested** by the customer. (§ 3007Q(a))
- **Ordering interface requirements**: must provide a **prominent and conspicuous** option to request items across ordering methods; **default** must be “no single-use items requested.” (§ 3007Q(a)(1))
- **No questioning requirement**: staff/services may not ask the reason for a request. (§ 3007Q(a)(2))
- **Self-service carve-out**: self-service stations with single-use items are permitted. (§ 3007Q(a)(3))
- **Third-party reliance safe harbor**: restaurants may rely on third-party platform info; reliance is a **complete defense**; communications/screenshots are prima facie evidence; platform must provide them within **72 hours** of request. (§ 3007Q(b)(1)-(3))
- **Enforcement structure**:
  - Mandatory **written warning** and **30-day cure period**. (§ 3005Q(c))
  - Starting **July 1, 2026**, uncorrected violations become **core item violations** subject to administrative penalties (Delaware Food Code; **16 Del. C. § 107**). (§ 3005Q(a))
  - No license suspension/revocation **solely** for any single or recurring core item violation of these provisions. (§ 3005Q(b))

## Who Benefits
- **Consumers who do not want disposable items**: default “no” option reduces receipt of unwanted single-use items. (§ 3007Q(a)(1))
- **Food establishments**: may avoid penalties via the **30-day cure period** and may assert a **complete defense** when relying on third-party platform information. (§ 3005Q(c); § 3007Q(b)(2))
- **Communities/public spaces**: bill findings cite reducing litter and solid-waste burdens by limiting single-use items. (Whereas clauses, Page 1)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
