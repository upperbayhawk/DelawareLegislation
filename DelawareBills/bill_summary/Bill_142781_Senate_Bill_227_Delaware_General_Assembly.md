## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To repeal (“sunset”) the committee in 14 Del. C. § 1707(i) that annually reviews and makes recommendations on the Division III equalization funding formula for school districts, because the Public Education Funding Commission (PEFC) is currently performing those functions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Repeals 14 Del. C. § 1707(i), eliminating the statutory committee that annually reviews and recommends changes to the equalization formula for Division III equalization funding. (“[Repealed.]”)
- The repealed committee previously had **10–15 members** appointed by the **Secretary of the Department of Education** and included specified state, legislative, and stakeholder representatives.
- The bill’s stated reason is that the **Public Education Funding Commission (PEFC)** (created by **House Concurrent Resolution No. 2, 153rd General Assembly**) is currently handling the same functions.
- The synopsis states PEFC will recommend **how to equalize school funding** and **how to address equalization in the future**, including how to **periodically review** the formula.
- The synopsis notes that **almost all** members of the repealed committee are members of PEFC, and PEFC includes additional stakeholders not on the repealed committee.
- The synopsis states PEFC is holding **public meetings** to discuss and make recommendations on the formula and periodic review.
- The bill text itself does not specify new replacement procedures in statute for annual review once § 1707(i) is repealed.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces duplicative structures:** The synopsis states PEFC is “currently handling the functions under § 1707(i),” so repealing § 1707(i) may avoid running two overlapping bodies.
- **Potentially broader stakeholder input:** The synopsis states PEFC includes “stakeholders who are not on the § 1707(i) committee,” which may expand participation compared with the repealed committee’s membership list.
- **Aligns review work with an active commission process:** The synopsis states PEFC is already holding **public meetings** and developing recommendations on equalization and future review.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Removes a statutory annual review requirement:** § 1707(i) required a committee “to annually review and make recommendations on the equalization formula.” Repealing it eliminates that statutory annual review mechanism.
- **Less certainty in law about future periodic review:** The synopsis says PEFC will recommend how to “periodically review the formula,” but the bill text does not codify a replacement process or schedule in Title 14.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **What statutory process replaces annual review?** The bill repeals § 1707(i) but does not add new statutory language establishing a successor body or required review cadence.
- **Duration and authority of PEFC:** The synopsis references PEFC established by HCR No. 2 (153rd GA), but the bill text does not specify PEFC’s legal status, timeline, or whether it continues long-term.
- **Implementation of PEFC recommendations:** The bill does not specify how or when PEFC recommendations will be adopted or translated into law or regulation.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Repeals 14 Del. C. § 1707(i)** in full (the committee provision is marked “[Repealed.]”).
- Removes from statute the requirement that the **Secretary of the Department of Education** appoint a **10–15 member** committee to:
  - **Annually** review and recommend on the equalization formula; and
  - Analyze related equalization issues affecting the state’s ability to achieve equalization’s basic purpose.
- Eliminates statutory membership requirements that included (among others): representatives from the State Board of Education, Governor’s Office, House and Senate (designated by leadership), Secretary of Finance (or designee), OMB Director (or designee), Controller General (or designee), State Education Association, and **at least 3** district representatives (**1 from each county**).
