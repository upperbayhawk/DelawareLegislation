## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 11 of the Delaware Code to prohibit Delaware government entities and courts from requesting, issuing, enforcing, or using **reverse-location** and **reverse-keyword** court orders and related voluntary requests, and to create remedies (suppression of evidence and a private right of action) for violations.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Bans** Delaware government entities from seeking **reverse-location court orders** and **reverse-keyword court orders** (including warrants or subpoenas) that identify unknown people via location or search/query activity.  
- **Bans** Delaware government entities from making **reverse-location requests** or **reverse-keyword requests** (voluntary requests, including offers to purchase data) without a court order.  
- **Bars use of the resulting data**: a government entity may not “seek, secure, obtain, borrow, purchase, or review” data obtained through prohibited court orders or requests.  
- **Prohibits Delaware courts** from issuing these reverse-location or reverse-keyword court orders.  
- **Non-cooperation / non-enforcement rule**: Delaware courts and government entities may not “support, assist, or enforce” such orders issued by Delaware or **any other state** (including “domestication”).  
- **Anti-circumvention rule**: Delaware government entities may not ask federal agencies or other states to obtain this data if Delaware would be barred from obtaining it directly.  
- **Suppression remedy**: unlawfully obtained information—and **any evidence derived from it**—is inadmissible in **criminal, civil, administrative, or other** proceedings (except to prove a violation of the section).  
- **Civil liability**: creates a private right of action with **$1,000 per violation or actual damages (whichever is greater)**, possible **punitive damages**, injunctive/declaratory relief, and **mandatory reasonable attorneys’ fees** for a prevailing plaintiff; also requires **written notice** to the affected person.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Strengthens privacy protections** by prohibiting broad collection of location and search data about “an unknown number” of people not individually identified by probable cause (see definitions and prohibitions in § 2312).  
- **Reduces risk of broad “general search” practices** by prohibiting both court-ordered and voluntary/request-based reverse searches, including **offers to purchase** such records (§ 2312(c)).  
- **Creates enforceable remedies**: automatic suppression of unlawfully obtained and derived evidence across multiple proceeding types (§ 2312(e)), and civil damages plus attorneys’ fees (§ 2313).  
- **Limits workarounds** by preventing Delaware entities from using federal or out-of-state agencies to obtain prohibited data (§ 2312(d)).  
- **Improves accountability and transparency** through a required **written notification** to persons whose identifying info was obtained unlawfully (§ 2313(a)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Restricts investigative tools**: government entities may not seek or use reverse-location or reverse-keyword methods even via warrants/subpoenas (§ 2312(b)), potentially limiting certain investigative approaches described in the synopsis.  
- **Evidence exclusion consequences**: evidence obtained in violation—and evidence “derived from” it, even if claimed “attenuated,” “inevitably” discoverable, or “subsequently reobtained”—is inadmissible (§ 2312(e)(2)), which could affect prosecutions or enforcement actions when violations occur.  
- **Litigation exposure for government entities**: civil actions can yield **$1,000 per violation** (or actual damages), **punitive damages**, and **mandatory attorneys’ fees** (§ 2313(c), (e)), increasing financial risk tied to noncompliance.  
- **Operational compliance burden**: agencies must ensure they do not “seek, secure, obtain, borrow, purchase, or review” prohibited data and must provide written notices upon violations (§ 2312(b)-(c); § 2313(a)).

- **Scope of “per violation”**: § 2313(c)(1) provides **$1,000 per violation**, but the bill text does not define how violations are counted (e.g., per person affected, per request/order, per dataset, per day).  
- **Notification timing and content**: § 2313(a) requires written notice and notice of legal recourse, but does not specify **how soon** notice must be provided or the required level of detail.

- **Definition boundaries**: “Reverse-keyword court order” includes identifying unnamed persons who searched a word/phrase/website or visited a website through a link generated by such a search “regardless” of geographic/time limitation (§ 2312(a)(2)); the bill does not clarify edge cases (e.g., orders targeting a known account plus additional unknowns).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates/updates Title 11, Chapter 23** provisions (§§ 2312–2313) to:
  - Define “government entity,” “reverse-location court order/request,” and “reverse-keyword court order/request,” including that requests include **offers to purchase** records (§ 2312(a)(1)–(5)).  
  - **Prohibit** government entities from seeking reverse-location/reverse-keyword **court orders** and prohibit Delaware courts from issuing them (§ 2312(b)(1), (3)).  
  - **Prohibit possession/use**: government entities may not “seek, secure, obtain, borrow, purchase, or review” information obtained through such court orders (§ 2312(b)(2)).  
  - **Prohibit enforcement/assistance**, including for orders from **other states** and “domestication” of such orders (§ 2312(b)(4)).  
  - **Prohibit voluntary reverse requests** (without court order) and prohibit obtaining/using data from such requests (§ 2312(c)).  
  - **Ban circumvention** by requesting federal or out-of-state assistance to obtain prohibited data (§ 2312(d)).  
  - **Mandate suppression**: unlawfully obtained records/information and **derived evidence** are inadmissible in any proceeding type, except to prove a violation (§ 2312(e)).  
  - Establish **private right of action** with required **written notice**, damages, punitive damages factors, injunctive/declaratory relief, and mandatory attorneys’ fees (§ 2313).

## Who Benefits
- **Individuals whose location or search/query activity data could be swept in** by reverse-location or reverse-keyword techniques, including people not suspected of a crime (as described in § 2312 and the synopsis).  
- **People whose constitutional activities** (speech, association, religion, assembly, movement, press) could be affected by mass surveillance practices described in the bill’s findings and synopsis.  
- **Civil plaintiffs** whose identifying information was obtained in violation, due to statutory damages (**$1,000 per violation or actual damages**) and fee-shifting (§ 2313(c)(1), (e)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State and local law enforcement / government entities** that would otherwise use reverse-location or reverse-keyword techniques, due to prohibitions on seeking/using such data and potential civil liability (§ 2312; § 2313).  
- **Prosecuting entities** when violations occur, due to broad suppression of unlawfully obtained and derived evidence in criminal (and other) proceedings (§ 2312(e)).  
- **Courts** to the extent they must decline to issue or domesticate such orders and cannot support/assist/enforce such orders (§ 2312(b)(3)–(4)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance prohibitions**:
  - Government entities may not seek reverse-location/reverse-keyword court orders (§ 2312(b)(1)).  
  - Government entities may not obtain/use data from prohibited court orders or requests, including by borrowing/purchasing/reviewing (§ 2312(b)(2); § 2312(c)(2)).  
  - Delaware courts may not issue such orders (§ 2312(b)(3)).  
  - Delaware courts/government entities may not support/assist/enforce such orders from Delaware or other states (including domestication) (§ 2312(b)(4)).  
  - Government entities may not use federal/out-of-state agencies to obtain prohibited data indirectly (§ 2312(d)).
- **Suppression (exclusionary rule)**: unlawfully obtained information and any derived evidence is **inadmissible** in criminal, civil, administrative, or other proceedings, except as proof of a violation (§ 2312(e)).  
- **Mandatory written notice**: if a person’s identifying information was obtained in violation, the violating government entity **must notify** the person **in writing** and inform them of legal recourse (§ 2313(a)).  
- **Civil enforcement (private right of action)**: affected persons may sue the violating governmental entity (§ 2313(b)); remedies may include:
  - **$1,000 per violation or actual damages (whichever is greater)** (§ 2313(c)(1));  
  - **Punitive damages** (§ 2313(c)(2)), with statutory considerations including number of people affected, whether targeting constitutional activity occurred, and persistence of violations (§ 2313(d));  
  - **Injunctive/declaratory relief** and other relief (§ 2313(c)(3)–(4));  
  - **Reasonable attorneys’ fees** must be awarded to a prevailing plaintiff (§ 2313(e)).
