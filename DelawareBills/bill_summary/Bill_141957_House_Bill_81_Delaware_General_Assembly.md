## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Update Delaware’s professional engineering law (Title 24, Ch. 28) by modernizing examination-discipline provisions for computerized testing, expanding comity recognition to certain Council-approved foreign jurisdictions (including International Professional Engineer registrants), clarifying internal disciplinary/complaint processes, and strengthening cease-and-desist enforcement tools.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands comity licensure to include “other Council-approved foreign jurisdiction[s]” and recognizes International Professional Engineer (IntPE) registrants under the International Engineering Alliance (IEA) International Professional Engineers Agreement (IPEA).  
- Requires an IntPE comity applicant to have **at least 5 years of continuous and verifiable post-licensure experience** and to meet Delaware’s reference requirement.  
- Maintains/clarifies the licensure reference requirement: **not less than 5 references**, with **at least 3** being licensed/registered professional engineers (including certain foreign jurisdictions or IntPE registrants).  
- Updates examination misconduct rules (e.g., removing/transmitting exam content, unlawful possession of exam info, impersonation, violating exam security agreements) and authorizes on-site proctor actions, including collecting materials/property and seeking law enforcement assistance.  
- Authorizes Council penalties for exam impropriety including voiding exam results, barring re-examination for **up to 2 years**, requiring an ethics course, revoking a license/EI certification obtained from the compromised exam, and reporting to other jurisdictions.  
- Clarifies investigating committee → Council reporting for dismissal recommendations, including required written rationale and standards for Council rejection (contrary to law, not supported by substantial evidence, arbitrary/capricious).  
- Sets disciplinary fine authority for certain violations at **up to $5,000**, with **payment due within 90 days** of mailing of the order; fine proceeds go to the General Fund after allowed deductions for direct administrative/prosecution expenses.  
- Clarifies cease-and-desist order process must include a **date-certain compliance deadline** and that Council may seek **injunctive relief** to enforce such orders.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves licensure mobility and workforce access** by allowing comity recognition for applicants from Council-approved foreign jurisdictions and for **IntPE (IEA/IPEA) registrants**, reducing redundant exams for qualified engineers (Section 5, §2817(6)).  
- **Strengthens exam integrity in a computerized environment** with more explicit prohibitions and clearer immediate proctor powers (Section 7, §2823(b)).  
- **More transparent/structured discipline processing** by requiring detailed written dismissal recommendations and limiting when Council can reject them (Section 8, §2824(b)(1)a.).  
- **Clearer enforcement tools** through explicit authorization for injunctive relief to enforce cease-and-desist orders (Section 9, §2825(n)(4)).  
- **Potentially supports reporting consistency across states/jurisdictions** by allowing Council to report exam-related discipline to other jurisdictions (Section 7, §2823(b)(3)e.).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expanded investigatory authority and proctor discretion**: the chief proctor may (at sole discretion) collect an examinee’s personal property believed to contain exam content and seek law-enforcement assistance (Section 7, §2823(b)(2)).  
- **License pathway still requires multiple references** (minimum **5**, with **3** professional engineers), which may be burdensome for some applicants, including internationally trained engineers (Section 5, §2817(7)a.).  
- **Sanctions can be significant for exam issues**, including a **2-year** prohibition on retaking an exam and possible revocation of a license/EI certification tied to the exam (Section 7, §2823(b)(3)).  
- **Fines and deadlines are strict**: up to **$5,000**, due within **90 days** of mailing of an order, which may be difficult for some disciplined parties (Section 8, §2824(c)(1)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill authorizes licensure by comity for “**other Council-approved foreign jurisdiction**” but does not specify in statute **which jurisdictions** qualify or the approval criteria (Section 5, §2817(6)).  
- The cease-and-desist provision requires a “**reasonable date certain deadline**” but does not define what timeframes are presumed reasonable (Section 9, §2825(n)(1)).  
- The text indicates it “removes outdated references to permits” in the synopsis, but “permit” and “permittee” remain used in multiple amended provisions; the scope of “removal” versus modernization is not fully clear from the provided excerpts.

- Investigating committee must produce more formal written dismissal recommendations with verbatim allegations and reasoning; Council review is constrained to the written recommendation record (Section 8, §2824(b)(1)a.).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Comity / international recognition:** Adds/clarifies that comity licensure may be based on licensure from “**other Council-approved foreign jurisdiction**” and creates a path for **IntPE (IEA/IPEA) registrants** (Section 5, §2817(6)).  
- **IntPE experience threshold:** Requires **minimum 5 years of continuous and verifiable experience** as a professional engineer **after receipt of the initial license** for IntPE-based applicants (Section 5, §2817(6)b.).  
- **Reference requirements updated for international context:** Still requires **≥ 5 references**, with **≥ 3** from licensed/registered professional engineers, which may include Council-approved foreign jurisdictions or IntPE registrants (Section 5, §2817(7)a.).  
- **Modernized exam misconduct framework:** Enumerates prohibited exam behaviors and authorizes chief proctor interventions (collect materials/property; dismiss; seek law enforcement) (Section 7, §2823(b)(1)–(2)).  
- **Exam impropriety penalties:** Includes voiding results; retake ban up to **2 years**; ethics course requirement; revocation of license/EI certification tied to the exam; reporting to other jurisdictions (Section 7, §2823(b)(3)).  
- **Complaint dismissal recommendation process formalized:** Requires verbatim listing of allegations recommended for dismissal and written reasoning; Council may reject only on specified grounds (Section 8, §2824(b)(1)a.).  
- **Disciplinary fine cap and payment timing:** Up to **$5,000**; payment due within **90 days**; fine revenue disposition rules stated (Section 8, §2824(c)(1)).  
- **Cease-and-desist enforcement clarified:** Proposed orders must include a **date-certain deadline** and notice of right to request hearing before deadline; Council may seek **injunctive relief** to enforce (Section 9, §2825(n)(1), (4)).  
- **Whistleblower protection cross-reference:** Complainants/witnesses in matters under §2823 or §2825 must be afforded protections of the Delaware Whistleblowers’ Protection Act (Title 19, Ch. 17) (Section 8, §2824(d)).

## Who Benefits
- Qualified engineers licensed outside Delaware, including those from **Council-approved foreign jurisdictions** and **IntPE (IEA/IPEA) registrants**, who may obtain Delaware licensure without further examination if requirements are met (Section 5, §2817(6)).  
- The Delaware Association of Professional Engineers/Council, through clearer authority and procedures for exam security, discipline, cease-and-desist enforcement, and publication of penalty guidelines (Sections 7–9).  
- Public agencies and the public, through reinforced requirements that public works engineering be performed under authorized, responsible professional oversight and through stronger exam integrity protections (Sections 6–7).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Exam candidates found to have engaged in exam impropriety, who may face exam invalidation, a retake prohibition of **2 years**, required ethics coursework, and potential revocation of credentials tied to the exam (Section 7, §2823(b)(3)).  
- Individuals/entities subject to cease-and-desist orders, who face enforceable deadlines and potential injunctive enforcement (Section 9, §2825(n)).  
- Disciplined parties subject to fines up to **$5,000** with **90-day** payment requirement (Section 8, §2824(c)(1)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approved:** **August 21, 2025** (enacted act text).  
- **Fine payment deadline:** All fines must be paid within **90 days** of the date of mailing of an order (Section 8, §2824(c)(1)).  
- **Exam retake prohibition:** Council may bar an examinee from taking the examination for **2 years** (or other time Council determines to avoid repeated questions) (Section 7, §2823(b)(3)b.).  
- **Cease-and-desist compliance deadline:** Must include a “**reasonable date certain**” compliance deadline; order becomes final/enforceable after the compliance deadline passes if no timely hearing request (Section 9, §2825(n)(1)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Exam misconduct enforcement:** Chief proctor may collect exam materials and certain personal property, dismiss examinee, and seek law enforcement assistance (Section 7, §2823(b)(2)).  
- **Exam impropriety penalties (after hearing or waiver):** void results; bar retesting for up to **2 years**; require ethics course completion; revoke license/EI certification obtained via the compromised exam; report discipline to other jurisdictions (Section 7, §2823(b)(3)).  
- **Disciplinary penalties for §2823(a) violations:** fines up to **$5,000**, additional training/education, warnings/reprimand/censure, refusal/revocation of licensure, probation, suspension up to **2 years**, refusal to renew authorization to use “engineer” or practice engineering in Delaware (Section 8, §2824(c)(1)).  
- **Unlicensed practice/false representation violations:** Prohibits use of another’s license/seal, false evidence, impersonation, use of expired/revoked credentials, and misrepresentation/improper means regarding applications/exams (Section 9, §2825(d)–(h)).  
- **Public works compliance:** Contracts for public works involving engineering are **null and void** if statutory authorization/supervision conditions are not met (Section 6, §2822(b)).  
- **Cease-and-desist orders:** Must include allegations, findings summary, and a date-certain compliance deadline; respondents may request a hearing in writing before the deadline; Council may seek **injunctive relief** to enforce (Section 9, §2825(n)(1), (4)).
