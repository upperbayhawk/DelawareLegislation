## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Require Delaware-regulated health coverage (individual, group/blanket, State employee health plan, and State Medicaid) to cover medically necessary HIV prevention medications—pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP)—and related administration services, with major limits on patient cost-sharing and utilization management.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Mandates coverage of **medically necessary PrEP and PEP** for people **at increased risk of HIV acquisition** in **individual** and **group/blanket** health insurance policies delivered/issued/renewed/extended/modified in Delaware.  
- Requires the **State employee health plan** to cover medically necessary PrEP and PEP.  
- Requires **State Medicaid** carriers to cover medically necessary PrEP and PEP **to the extent permitted by federal law**.  
- Mandated coverage must include **services related to administering** PrEP/PEP.  
- Coverage must be provided **without**: **(1) cost-sharing** (deductibles, coinsurance, copays, out-of-pocket expense), **(2) prior authorization or step therapy**, or **(3) unreasonable delay in coverage determination** (subject to specified exceptions).  
- **Exceptions** limit the $0 cost-sharing requirement for certain **catastrophic plans** (ACA § 1302(e)) and **high-deductible health plans (HDHPs)/HSA compatibility** (IRC § 223(c)(2)).  
- Does **not** apply to certain **limited-benefit policies** (e.g., accident-only, specified disease, hospital indemnity, Medicare supplement, long-term care, disability income).  
- Applicability date: applies to policies/contracts/certificates **renewed/modified/altered/amended/reissued after December 31, 2026**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces patient financial barriers** by prohibiting **cost-sharing** (deductibles, coinsurance, copays, out-of-pocket expenses) for covered PrEP/PEP in most plans (Titles **18**, **29**, and **31** provisions; with stated exceptions).  
- **Improves timeliness of access** by barring **prior authorization**, **step therapy**, and **unreasonable delay in coverage determinations** for PrEP/PEP.  
- **Expands uniformity across major coverage sources** by applying requirements to **individual**, **group/blanket**, **State employee**, and **Medicaid** coverage (as specified in the bill and synopsis).  
- **Includes administration-related services**, not just the medication, which may support access to the full covered treatment regimen.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits plan cost-control tools** by prohibiting **prior authorization** and **step therapy** for PrEP/PEP, and requiring no “unreasonable delay” in determinations, which may reduce utilization management options explicitly referenced in the bill.  
- **May shift costs from patients to payers** because cost-sharing is generally prohibited for required coverage (except as stated for certain plan types), which could increase insurer and plan expenditures (no dollar estimate provided in text).  
- **Creates different cost-sharing outcomes for HDHP/HSA enrollees** because, if needed to preserve HSA eligibility, the $0 cost-sharing requirement applies only **after the deductible is met** for a qualified HDHP (Title 18 provisions).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill prohibits “**unreasonable delay in coverage determination**” but does **not define** what timeframes or standards constitute “unreasonable.”  
- “**Medically necessary**” is defined to include prescribing PrEP/PEP to individuals “**at increased risk of HIV acquisition**,” but the bill does **not specify clinical criteria** for “increased risk.”  
- For the State employee plan and Medicaid provisions, the bill does not detail **implementation guidance** (e.g., formularies, provider billing processes), beyond the required coverage and prohibitions.  
- Medicaid coverage is required only “**to the extent permitted by federal law**,” but the bill does not specify which federal constraints are anticipated.

## Health Impact
- Requires broad insurance coverage of **HIV prevention treatments (PrEP and PEP)** and related administration services, and removes common access barriers (cost-sharing and utilization management restrictions) for most covered plan types.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 18 (Individual market):** Requires all **individual** health insurance policies/contracts/certificates delivered/issued for delivery/renewed/extended/modified in Delaware to cover **medically necessary PrEP and PEP**, including administration-related services, **without** cost-sharing, prior authorization/step therapy, or unreasonable delays—subject to exceptions for certain **catastrophic plans** and **HDHP/HSA** rules; excludes specified limited-benefit policies.  
- **Title 18 (Group/blanket):** Requires all **group and blanket** health insurance policies/contracts/certificates delivered/issued for delivery/renewed/extended/modified in Delaware to provide the same PrEP/PEP coverage with the same prohibitions; includes HDHP/HSA-related exception language; excludes specified limited-benefit policies.  
- **Title 29 (State employee health plan):** Requires the plan to cover medically necessary PrEP/PEP and administration-related services **without** cost-sharing, prior authorization/step therapy, or unreasonable delays.  
- **Title 31 (Medicaid):** Requires Medicaid carriers to cover medically necessary PrEP/PEP and administration-related services and, **to the extent permitted by federal law**, provide it **without** cost-sharing, prior authorization/step therapy, or unreasonable delays.

## Who Benefits
- Individuals **at increased risk of HIV acquisition** who seek **PrEP** (before exposure) or **PEP** (after possible exposure).  
- Enrollees in **individual**, **group/blanket**, **State employee**, and **Medicaid** coverage in Delaware who need PrEP/PEP and related administration services (subject to stated exceptions/limitations).  
- Providers and clinics administering PrEP/PEP, to the extent coverage includes “**services related to administering**” the treatment.
