## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 26 of the Delaware Code to require a **Public Service Commission (PSC) “Certificate to Operate” (COP)** before any person or entity begins (or expands into) a business that uses **30 megawatts (MW) or more of electricity**, and to set review timelines, required application contents, and oversight tools (including conditions, transfer limits, and potential suspension/revocation).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new requirement: **no person or entity may begin the business of using ≥ 30 MW of electricity without first obtaining a PSC COP**.  
- Applies not only to new facilities, but also to **changes to existing facilities** that increase usage to **≥ 30 MW**.  
- **Exempts public utilities** when engaged in such business **within their service territories**.  
- Sets a PSC decision deadline: **within 90 days** of a **completed** application; PSC may extend **up to an additional 90 days** for good cause and public interest.  
- Requires extensive application information, including **transmission capacity impacts, needed upgrades, generation sourcing (new vs. existing), curtailment plans, backup power, facility lifespan, cost impacts, and grid reliability impacts**.  
- Requires PSC to consider PJM readiness; PSC **may only grant COPs for facilities interconnecting into the PJM grid** and must consider whether PJM has completed studies and assured supply.  
- Allows intervention by the **State Energy Office** and **local governments with land use jurisdiction** in PSC proceedings.  
- Makes COPs **non-transferable without PSC written approval** and allows PSC to **suspend or revoke** COPs for “good cause” including material noncompliance or unsafe/unreliable operation.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved grid planning and reliability review**: Applications must include studies of **Delaware electricity cost impacts** and **grid reliability impacts**, plus transmission capacity and upgrade needs.  
- **Ratepayer and economic considerations required**: PSC must consider **impacts to the State’s ratepayers** and the **State’s economy** when deciding whether to grant a COP.  
- **Alignment with state climate/renewable standards**: PSC must consider consistency with **greenhouse gas reduction targets (Title 7 § 10003)** and **renewable portfolio standards (Title 26 § 354)**.  
- **Tools to manage transmission investment risk**: PSC may require **financial commitments** to protect against **stranded transmission investments** and may require contributions to **grid modernization** for necessary transmission investment.  
- **More coordination with PJM interconnection process**: PSC must consider whether **PJM has completed relevant studies** and assured adequate energy/capacity supply; COPs are limited to **PJM-interconnecting facilities**.  
- **Public-sector input is built in**: The bill requires PSC to allow intervention by the **State Energy Office** and **local land-use jurisdictions**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added permitting layer for high-load businesses**: Entities using **≥ 30 MW** must obtain a COP, adding a PSC approval step and a potentially longer timeline (up to **180 days** if extended).  
- **Broad information and study requirements may increase applicant burden**: The application must include multiple studies and detailed technical and sourcing information (e.g., cost impacts, grid reliability, transmission/distribution upgrades, generation type and sourcing).  
- **Potential uncertainty due to discretionary conditions**: PSC may require financial commitments, grid modernization contributions, and additional generation commitments “by a set timeline,” which may affect project planning.  
- **Transfer restrictions may affect transactions**: A COP **cannot be transferred** without PSC written approval, potentially complicating sales, reorganizations, or assignments.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill does not define what it means to “**begin the business of using 30 MW or greater**” (e.g., measurement methodology, averaging interval, peak vs. continuous load).  
- “**Completed application**” is not defined; the completeness determination could affect the start of the **90-day** decision clock.  
- The threshold trigger for expansions states “**increases the usage to 30MW or greater**,” but does not specify how incremental increases are measured or verified.  
- The bill requires applicants to report “**electricity utilized by generation type**” and whether sourced from “**new or existing generation**,” but does not specify how this is determined or enforced.  
- “**Good cause**” for extending the decision deadline is not defined beyond requiring a public-interest finding; “good cause” for suspend/revoke is defined, but terms like “material noncompliance” are not further specified in this text.  
- The scope of “**plans for development**” and what constitutes “**final plan approval**” by a local jurisdiction is not defined in this text.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New COP requirement** for any person/entity beginning the business of using **≥ 30 MW** of electricity.  
- **Expansion trigger**: changes to an existing facility that increase usage to **≥ 30 MW** must obtain a COP.  
- **Exemption**: does not apply to **public utilities** engaged in such business within their **service territories**.  
- **Decision timeline**: PSC must act within **90 days** of a completed application; may extend **up to 90 additional days** for good cause and public interest.  
- **Mandatory application contents** including transmission impacts, infrastructure upgrades, generation sourcing, curtailment, backup power, lifespan, supplemental transmission projects, Delaware electricity cost impacts, and grid reliability impacts.  
- **PSC authority to impose conditions**, including:  
  - Financial commitments to protect against **stranded transmission investments**  
  - Contributions toward **grid modernization** for necessary transmission investment  
  - Requirements to secure **additional generation commitments by a set timeline**  
- **Decision factors** include PJM study completion/adequacy, grid safety and reliability, impacts in rights-of-way, economy, ratepayers, GHG targets (Title 7 § 10003), RPS (Title 26 § 354), impacts on renewable project access, design facilitating future renewable interconnections, public health/safety/welfare, applicant experience/viability, transmission engineering design, and impacts on local transmission facilities.  
- **Suspension/revocation authority** for good cause (material noncompliance or material failure to operate safely/adequately/reliably).  
- **Intervention rights** for the State Energy Office and local governments with land-use jurisdiction.  
- **Transfer restriction**: COP not transferable without PSC written approval (with transferee qualification review).  
- **Rulemaking authority**: PSC may promulgate regulations to implement the section.  
- **Applicability limitation**: applies to development plans **that have not obtained final plan approval from the local jurisdiction by the date of enactment**.
