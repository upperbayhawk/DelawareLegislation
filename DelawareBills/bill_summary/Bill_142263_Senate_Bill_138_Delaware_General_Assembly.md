## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Chapter 103, Title 16 of the Delaware Code to update statutory provisions governing the Delaware Health Information Network (DHIN), including DHIN’s purpose language, Board composition and terms, regulatory references, claims database findings language, and antitrust-guidance references for claims data reporting.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Clarifies DHIN’s statutory purpose and confirms DHIN is the State’s sanctioned provider of health information exchange services.  
- Changes DHIN Board structure by reducing the Board from **19 to 13 members** and revising the Governor-appointed membership from **15 to 9** members.  
- Replaces specific “industry seat” concepts with a requirement that Governor appointees be **broadly representative** and include representatives of **medical consumers, hospitals/health systems, physicians, nurses, businesses/employers, and payers**.  
- Confirms Board members serve **3-year terms**, which may end earlier if a member **resigns or is removed**, and members may **hold over** until a successor is appointed.  
- Cleans up references to Delaware Health Care Commission rules, while keeping prior Commission rules in effect **until amended/repealed by DHIN**.  
- Updates statutory terminology related to DHIN service users and confirms DHIN’s authority to set **reasonable fees or charges** for DHIN services to **network participants and users**.  
- Updates claims database findings to remove reference to the **Delaware Center for Health Innovation** (no longer operating) and defines “**Triple Aim**” as improving patient care, improving population health, and reducing per-capita health-care costs.  
- Updates claims data external/public reporting language by removing reliance on **DOJ/FTC “Statement 6”** (withdrawn **February 2023**) and requiring compliance with **any applicable federal antitrust guidance**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Governance efficiency:** Board size reduction (**19 → 13**) and Governor-appointed reduction (**15 → 9**) may streamline DHIN governance (described in the bill synopsis as aligning with historical size and best practices).  
- **Broader stakeholder representation:** Explicit requirement to include representatives of **medical consumers, hospitals/health systems, physicians, nurses, businesses/employers, and payers** in Board membership.  
- **Updated legal compliance for data release:** Claims data releases must follow DHIN confidentiality/security protocols and comply with state/federal privacy laws and **current federal antitrust guidance**, replacing a withdrawn federal policy reference.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Less prescriptive appointment structure:** By removing named “positions held for certain groups or industries” and relying on “broadly representative” criteria, the statute provides fewer fixed guarantees of specific seat allocations than the prior model described in the synopsis.  
- **Regulatory transition ambiguity:** Prior Delaware Health Care Commission rules remain effective until DHIN replaces/updates them, which could create reliance on older frameworks until DHIN acts.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Board composition mechanics:** The statute states the Board is reduced to **13 members** and changes Governor-appointed members to **9**, but does not, in the provided text, fully enumerate how the remaining Board seats are selected or allocated (beyond the subsection shown).  
- **Definition of “broadly representative”:** The bill lists required stakeholder categories but does not define criteria or minimum numbers for each category (other than inclusion).  
- **Antitrust “guidance” scope:** “Any applicable federal antitrust guidance” is referenced, but the bill does not specify which guidance documents control or how DHIN will determine applicability.

## Health Impact
- Reinforces DHIN’s role as the State-sanctioned health information exchange provider.  
- Updates and supports the health-care claims database purpose tied to the “**Triple Aim**”: **improving patient care, improving population health, and reducing per-capita health-care costs**.  
- Requires claims data sharing to follow DHIN confidentiality/security protocols and applicable privacy laws for protected health information, and to comply (to the fullest extent practicable) with relevant antitrust guidance regarding exchange of price/cost information.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 16 §10301 (Purpose):** Technical correction; continues defining DHIN as a not-for-profit public instrumentality and the State’s sanctioned HIE provider.  
- **Title 16 §10302 (Board):**
  - Board reduced from **19 to 13** members (per synopsis).  
  - Governor-appointed members reduced from **15 to 9**.  
  - Recommendation sources expanded to include **Delaware Nurses Association** (in the enacted text) among groups that may make **nonbinding** recommendations to the Governor.  
  - Appointment standard shifts to “**broadly representative**” with required inclusion of representatives of **medical consumers, hospitals/health systems, physicians, nurses, businesses/employers, and payers**.  
  - Chair elected by Board; chair serves a **3-year term**.  
  - Members serve **3-year terms**, may resign/be removed earlier, and may continue beyond term until a successor is appointed.
- **Title 16 §10303 (Powers/Duties):**
  - Prior Health Care Commission DHIN rules remain effective until DHIN replaces them with bylaws/policies.  
  - Updates fee language to allow DHIN to establish reasonable fees/charges for DHIN services to **network participants and users of DHIN services** (while stating no change in ability to charge fees).
- **Title 16 §10306 (Regulations):** DHIN may promulgate rules under **Subchapter II of Chapter 101 of Title 29**; Commission rules promulgated prior to **July 12, 2010** remain effective until amended or repealed by DHIN.  
- **Title 16 §10311 (Claims DB findings):** Removes reference to Delaware Center for Health Innovation; references “This State’s ongoing effort” and defines “Triple Aim.”  
- **Title 16 §10314 (Claims data reporting):** Removes reference to DOJ/FTC **Statement 6** and substitutes compliance with **any applicable federal antitrust guidance**.
