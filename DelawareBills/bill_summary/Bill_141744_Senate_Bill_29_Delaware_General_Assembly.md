## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware insurance law (Title 18, § 6750) to (1) raise the maximum funeral expense benefit for certain deceased volunteer fire/ambulance members and (2) clarify eligibility rules, including how the benefit interacts with certain state employee burial benefits.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Increases the maximum payable funeral expense benefit from **$7,000 to $10,000** for eligible deceased members of volunteer fire companies, volunteer fire company ladies auxiliaries, and volunteer ambulance and rescue companies.  
- Removes the statutory requirement that only **“reasonable”** funeral expenses are reimbursed (replacing “reasonable funeral expenses” with a benefit amount “not to exceed **$10,000**”).  
- Clarifies that eligibility requires **at least 10 years** of active service in Delaware, including combined service across **1 or more** qualifying volunteer organizations.  
- Clarifies the interaction with state employee burial benefits: if an eligible individual was a state employee entitled to certain burial benefits, the individual **is also eligible** for this Chapter 67 benefit (in specified circumstances).  
- Retains the rule that if the deceased member was a state employee entitled to a funeral benefit, **the chapter does not apply** (while also adding clarifying language and specific benefit cross-references).  
- Effective date is **October 1, 2025** (fiscal note notes implementation timing after Governor signature, but bill text states Oct. 1, 2025).  
- Fiscal note estimates an annual cost increase of **$535,877**, with FY2026 prorated due to the October 1, 2025 start.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Higher benefit cap:** Raises the maximum payable amount from **$7,000 to $10,000**, increasing support for families/estates of eligible volunteers.  
- **Clearer eligibility rules:** Codifies **10-year** active service requirement and confirms that years across multiple eligible organizations count, reducing uncertainty.  
- **Clarifies dual-coverage situations:** Explicitly identifies certain state employee burial benefit statutes (Title 11 and Title 29 provisions) and states the person can be **also eligible** for this Chapter 67 benefit under § 6750(c).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased state cost:** Fiscal note projects added costs (estimated annual increase **$535,877**; FY2027 and FY2028 costs **$537,877** each).  
- **Potentially broader/less constrained payouts:** Removing the “reasonable funeral expenses” limitation may reduce an explicit statutory constraint on what expenses qualify, while still capped at **$10,000**.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective-date phrasing conflict:** Fiscal note assumption says “effective upon signature of the Governor” and “become effective October 1, 2025,” while the bill text states: **“This Act takes effect on October 1, 2025.”**  
- **Interaction between § 6750(b)(1) and § 6750(c):**  
  - § 6750(b)(1) states if the deceased was a state employee entitled to a funeral benefit, “this chapter shall not apply.”  
  - § 6750(c) states if an eligible individual was a state employee entitled to certain listed benefits, the individual is **also eligible** under this chapter.  
  The precise boundary between these provisions (which state-employee “funeral benefit” triggers exclusion vs. which listed benefits allow dual eligibility) may require administrative/legal interpretation.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Benefit cap increase:** From **$7,000** to **$10,000** (Title 18, § 6750(a)).  
- **Removes “reasonable” qualifier:** Deletes “reasonable funeral expenses” language, leaving an amount paid “not to exceed **$10,000**.”  
- **Eligibility clarification:** Defines eligible individual as having served as an active member in Delaware for **at least 10 years**; allows combining years across **1 or more** qualifying volunteer organizations (§ 6750(b)(1)-(2)).  
- **State employee benefit interaction clarified:** Specifies listed Title 11 and Title 29 burial benefit sections and provides that an eligible individual who was a state employee entitled to those benefits is **also eligible** under this chapter (§ 6750(c)).
