## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Require Delaware-regulated health insurance coverage of medically supported biomarker testing across individual, group, State employee, and certain public assistance health plans, with applicability tied to policy issuance/renewal/activity after a specified date.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Mandates coverage for **biomarker testing** for **diagnosis, treatment, appropriate management, or ongoing monitoring** of a covered person’s disease/condition when supported by **medical and scientific evidence**.  
- Applies to **individual** and **group/blanket** health insurance policies in **Title 18**, the **State Employee Group Health Insurance Plan (GHIP)** in **Title 29**, and certain **public assistance/Medicaid-related health benefit plans** in **Title 31**.  
- Defines “biomarker,” “biomarker testing” (including **single-analyte**, **multi-plex panel**, and **whole exome/genome/transcriptome sequencing**), plus “consensus statement” and “nationally recognized clinical practice guideline.”  
- Treats as “supported by medical and scientific evidence” when backed by sources including **FDA-approved/cleared test indications**, **FDA drug label warnings/precautions**, **CMS NCDs/MAC LCDs**, and **national guidelines/consensus statements**.  
- Requires coverage to be provided in a way that **limits disruptions in care**, including reducing the need for **multiple biopsies/biospecimen samples**.  
- Preserves patient cost-sharing/plan features: deductibles, coinsurance, allowable charge limits, and coordination of benefits may still apply.  
- Excludes certain limited-benefit policies (e.g., **accident-only, specified disease, hospital indemnity, Medicare supplement, long-term care, disability income**).  
- **Effective upon Governor’s signature**, but applies to policies **issued/renewed/modified/altered/amended/reissued after December 31, 2026**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves access to testing**: Requires coverage for biomarker testing when supported by recognized evidence sources (FDA, CMS determinations, national guidelines/consensus statements).  
- **Supports continuity of care**: Plans must provide coverage in a manner that limits disruptions, including avoiding **multiple biopsies** when possible.  
- **Applies broadly across markets**: Extends requirements to **individual, group, GHIP, and certain Title 31 health benefit plans**, increasing consistency in covered benefits.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **State fiscal cost**: Fiscal note estimates new costs for **Medicaid** and **GHIP** (see Budget Impact).  
- **Potential additional State obligation for ACA plans**: Fiscal note states that if interpreted as exceeding **Essential Health Benefits (EHB)**, the State may have to **defray costs** for ACA plans under **45 CFR 155.170**.  
- **Cost-sharing still permitted**: The bill explicitly allows deductibles and coinsurance to apply, so coverage may still involve out-of-pocket costs.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- Whether and to what extent the mandate is deemed **beyond EHB** for ACA plans (fiscal note flags possible State defrayal under **45 CFR 155.170**, but does not quantify).  
- “Supported by medical and scientific evidence” is defined by examples (FDA/CMS/guidelines/consensus statements), but the bill does not specify a single controlling hierarchy if sources conflict.  
- The bill requires limiting disruptions in care (e.g., avoiding multiple biopsies) but does not specify operational standards or metrics for compliance.

## Budget Impact

- **Fiscal Year 2027:** Total **$1,850,000 – $2,025,000** (GHIP **$150,000 – $275,000**; Medicaid **$1,700,000 – $1,750,000**).  
- **Fiscal Year 2028:** Total **$1,850,000 – $2,025,000** (GHIP **$150,000 – $275,000**; Medicaid **$1,700,000 – $1,750,000**).  
- Fiscal note assumption for Medicaid: **1% utilization** of **253,000** enrollees; **1 biomarker test per affected enrollee per year**.  
- Fiscal note uses **FFY 2025 FMAP 60.15% federal / 39.85% state** for Medicaid cost sharing.

## Fiscal or Administrative Impact

- **State Employee GHIP (Title 29):** Fiscal note states GHIP already covers biomarker testing “as clinically indicated,” but estimates additional costs for “expanded coverage as directed by the Act” (**$150,000–$275,000** annually starting FY 2027).  
- **Potential ACA defrayal requirement:** Fiscal note highlights possible administrative/fiscal responsibility under **45 CFR 155.170** if the mandate is beyond EHB.

## Health Impact
- Expands required insurance coverage for biomarker testing used in **diagnosis, treatment, management, or ongoing monitoring** when supported by specified evidence sources (FDA/CMS/guidelines/consensus statements).  
- Seeks to reduce care disruptions by requiring coverage administration that may reduce need for **repeat biopsies/biospecimen sampling**.

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates/updates Delaware Code sections to require biomarker testing coverage in:
  - **Title 18, Ch. 33** (individual policies): **§ 3370M**.  
  - **Title 18, Ch. 35** (group and blanket policies): **§ 3571EE**.  
  - **Title 29, Ch. 52** (State employee plan): **§ 5223**.  
  - **Title 31, Ch. 5** (certain health benefit plans under § 503(3)): **§ 538**.
- Defines key terms: **biomarker**, **biomarker testing**, **consensus statement**, **nationally recognized clinical practice guideline**.
- Sets evidence bases that qualify (non-exclusive list): **FDA test indications**, **FDA drug indications**, **FDA label warnings/precautions**, **CMS NCDs/MAC LCDs**, and **national guidelines/consensus statements**.
- Requires coverage administration to **limit disruptions in care**, including minimizing **multiple biopsies/biospecimen samples**.
- Clarifies that mandated coverage may still be subject to **deductibles, coinsurance, allowable charge limits, and coordination of benefits**.
- Exempts limited-benefit policies (accident-only, specified disease, hospital indemnity, Medicare supplement, long-term care, disability income, other limited benefit policies).

## Who Benefits
- **Covered persons/patients** in Delaware-regulated **individual and group** plans who need biomarker testing supported by specified evidence sources.  
- **State employees and covered dependents** enrolled in **GHIP**.  
- **Enrollees in applicable Title 31 health benefit plans** (fiscal note indicates applicability to **Medicaid**).
