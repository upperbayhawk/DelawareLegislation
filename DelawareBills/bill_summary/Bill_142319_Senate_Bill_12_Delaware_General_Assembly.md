## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Titles 18 and 29 of the Delaware Code to reform health insurance utilization review and prior/pre-authorization processes to reduce delays and negative impacts on patient care by standardizing decision timelines, notice requirements, reviewer/appeal standards, electronic submission and provider-portal requirements, and de-identified reporting; and extend compliance expectations to state employee health plan carriers and certain DHSS (Medicaid-related) carrier contracts “to the extent feasible.” The act is referred to in the notes as the “Delaware Pre-Authorization Reform Act of 2025.”

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **When it applies:** The act is effective **upon signature**, but core requirements apply to policies/contracts/certificates **issued, renewed, modified, altered, amended, or reissued on or after December 31, 2026**.
- **Advance notice of changes:** Requires **≥ 6 months** advance notice for changes to utilization review terms/clinical criteria, with exceptions (e.g., guideline status changes, recalls, market withdrawals, FDA-published safety information); and **≥ 60 days** written notice before implementing **new or amended** pre-authorization requirements/restrictions.
- **Operational access:** Requires utilization review on **weekends** and access to a medical director/clinical decision-maker **Monday–Friday 7:00 AM–7:00 PM** and during **“reasonable business hours”** on weekends.
- **Decision timelines (non-emergency; after a “clean” pre-authorization):** Includes **2 business days** for pharmaceuticals; urgent services and patient transfers **within 24 hours** (certain electronic submissions) or **48 hours** (other pathway). Some non-urgent service timeframes appear to be **3 vs. 5 business days** depending on electronic submission, but the notes flag drafting/citation ambiguity.
- **Appeals:** Appeals may be submitted **in writing, electronically, or by telephone**; plans must allow **at least 30 days** from an adverse determination to appeal; and must issue an appeal decision/notice generally **within 15 days**, with a written information request option within that period and a decision **within 15 days after** receiving requested materials.
- **Clinical integrity standards:** Requires qualified and independent review (e.g., specialty alignment/consultation; appeal reviewer not involved in the initial denial; reviewer compensation **not contingent** on outcomes).
- **Electronic/portal + reporting:** Requires electronic pre-authorization capability and a provider portal by **January 1, 2027**, including responding via the **same digital method** used for submission; requires **de-identified** reporting to the Delaware Health Information Network (DHIN) **at least twice annually** (and upon Department request).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Faster access to care** supported by explicit decision timelines (e.g., **24/48 hours** urgent services/transfers; **2 business days** for pharmaceutical requests).
- **Greater continuity and predictability** from change-notice rules: **≥ 60 days** for new/amended requirements and **≥ 6 months** for utilization review term/criteria changes, plus protection for people with **existing authorizations** until re-authorization (subject to stated exceptions).
- **Reduced repeat administrative burden** via limits of **no more than 1 pre-authorization per episode of care** (with exceptions) and “deemed approval” of other **covered in-network** bundled services when one bundled service is authorized.
- **Stronger clinical appropriateness and fairness** from reviewer qualification and independence rules (including prohibition on outcome-contingent compensation; independent appeal review).
- **More transparency and oversight** through required appeal notice content (findings summary, reviewer qualifications, and clinical criteria linkage) and **twice-yearly minimum** de-identified reporting to DHIN.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative and staffing burden** for insurers/plans/utilization review entities due to weekend utilization review, extended access-hour requirements, compressed decision/appeal timelines, multi-channel appeal intake, and expanded notice obligations.
- **Technology and workflow burden** from building/maintaining electronic pre-authorization processes and a provider portal by **January 1, 2027**, and potential later **portal-only** submission requirements (subject to exceptions).
- **Reduced notice in certain situations** because utilization review term/criteria changes may occur without **6 months** advance notice under specified exceptions (e.g., recalls, market withdrawals, FDA safety information).
- **State fiscal uncertainty**: notes indicate fiscal impact for state programs (state employee health plan administration and Medicaid contracting) is **indeterminable/undeterminable**.

## Fiscal or Administrative Impact
- **Regulated insurers/health plans/health service corporations** must implement or ensure their utilization review entities implement: weekend operations; clinical decision-maker availability (**Mon–Fri 7:00 AM–7:00 PM** and “reasonable business hours” weekends); multiple appeal submission channels; statutory decision/notice timelines; and advance notice requirements (**≥ 60 days** and **≥ 6 months**, with exceptions).

  - The **State Employee Benefits Committee** must ensure carriers administering state employee health insurance contracts comply with applicable Title 18 requirements.
  - **DHSS** must ensure, **to the extent feasible**, that certain Medicaid-related carrier contracts include specified requirements (notes reference **Title 31 § 505(3)** and contracts awarded after the act’s effective date).

## Health Impact
- The notes cite legislative findings (referencing an **AMA 2023 survey**) that prior authorization delays care and can negatively affect outcomes; the act’s timelines, continuity protections, and reviewer standards are designed to improve timeliness and appropriateness of care.
- Requires expedited handling for **urgent services** and **patient transfers** (**24/48 hours** depending on submission method) and bars pre-authorization for **medically necessary interfacility transport** in the specified circumstance (lower level of care determined clinically appropriate).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Applicability trigger:** Applies to policies/contracts/certificates issued/renewed/modified/altered/amended/reissued **on or after December 31, 2026** (act effective upon signature).
- **Advance notice requirements:**
  - **≥ 60 days** written notice before implementing a **new or amended** pre-authorization requirement or restriction (to currently authorized covered persons and contracted providers; electronic delivery permitted).
  - **≥ 6 months** notice before changes to utilization review terms/clinical criteria; changes generally do not apply to persons with an existing authorization until **re-authorization**, with specified exceptions (guideline status changes, recalls, market withdrawals, FDA safety information).
- **Operational access:** Weekend utilization review; access to a medical director/clinical decision-maker **Mon–Fri 7:00 AM–7:00 PM** and **reasonable business hours** on weekends.
- **Decision timelines (non-emergency; after “clean” request):**
  - **Pharmaceuticals:** decision/adverse determination and provider notification **within 2 business days**.
  - **Health-care services (non-urgent):** notes indicate **3 business days** for certain electronic submissions vs **5 business days** otherwise, but the notes also flag drafting ambiguity.
  - **Urgent services and patient transfers:** **24 hours** (certain electronic submissions) or **48 hours** (other pathway).
- **Interfacility transport:** May not require pre-authorization for **medically necessary interfacility transport** when a **lower level of care** at a facility is determined clinically appropriate.
- **Authorization duration and frequency limits:**
  - Minimum pre-authorization validity increased to **no less than 90 days** from the date the provider receives the authorization (subject to continued coverage/eligibility and valid policy changes as referenced in the notes).
  - Generally **no more than 1 pre-authorization per episode of care**, with allowance for separate pre-authorization for new/additional testing/procedures.
  - **Bundled services deemed approved:** if one service in a bundled-payment group is authorized, other **covered in-network** services in that group are deemed approved.
- **Appeals and notice content:**
  - Appeal submission allowed **in writing, electronically, or by telephone**.
  - **At least 30 days** from adverse determination to submit an appeal.
  - Appeal decision/notice **within 15 days** of appeal receipt; if more information is needed, written request **within 15 days**, then decision/notice **within 15 days** after receiving materials.
  - Appeal/adverse-determination notices must include findings summary, reviewer qualifications, and diagnosis-to-criteria relationship including specific basis.
- **Reviewer independence and qualifications:** Specialty/profession alignment (or consultation) requirements; appeal reviewer not involved in original decision; reviewer compensation not contingent on outcomes.
- **Electronic pre-authorization + portal + reporting:**
  - By **January 1, 2027**, must accept/respond electronically via the same digital method used for submission and establish a provider portal with required features.
  - Within **12 months** after establishing the portal, entity may require portal submission (subject to exceptions).
  - De-identified reporting to **DHIN at least twice annually** and upon Department request.
  - Notes also reference an existing pharmacy ePA standard using **NCPDP SCRIPT** and a date of **January 1, 2018**; how SB 12 modifies versus restates this requirement is **unclear from the notes**.

## Who Benefits
- **Patients/covered persons** subject to prior authorization, especially those needing urgent services, transfers, or ongoing care requiring continuity of an existing authorization.
- **Health-care providers** (including physician practices) through longer minimum authorization validity (**≥ 90 days**), fewer repeat authorizations per episode of care, clearer timelines, expanded appeal submission channels, and standardized electronic/portal processes.
- **Regulators and DHIN** through access to de-identified prior authorization/appeal outcome data for oversight.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Insurers, health plans, health service corporations, and utilization review entities** due to staffing, operational-hour coverage, reporting, notice, tighter timelines, and IT/portal implementation requirements.
- **Providers** who may later be required to use a payer portal for submissions **within 12 months** after the portal is established (unless an exception applies).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon signature** (overall act).
- **Applicability:** requirements apply to policies/contracts/certificates issued/renewed/modified/altered/amended/reissued **on or after December 31, 2026**.
- **By January 1, 2027:** electronic pre-authorization acceptance/response (same digital method as submission) and provider portal must be in place.
- **Within 12 months after** establishing the provider portal: the entity may require portal submission (subject to exceptions).
- **Notice lead times:**
  - **≥ 60 days** before implementing a new/amended pre-authorization requirement/restriction.
  - **≥ 6 months** before changing utilization review terms/clinical criteria (exceptions apply).
- **Appeal deadlines:** at least **30 days** to file; decision/notice **within 15 days** of appeal receipt (and **within 15 days after** receipt of additional materials if requested).
- **Reporting:** **no less than twice annually** to DHIN; Department may request data **at any time**.
- Notes mention **August 25, 2025** as an “approved” date, but this is **not corroborated within the notes as an operative effective date**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements:** operational access (including weekends and specified hours), decision timelines, notice requirements, appeal intake methods and timelines, reviewer qualification/independence standards (including non-contingent compensation), electronic pre-authorization and portal requirements by **January 1, 2027**, and DHIN reporting **at least twice annually** plus Department-requested data.
- **Oversight roles:** State Employee Benefits Committee must ensure carriers for state employee health plans comply; DHSS must ensure specified contract terms are included **to the extent feasible** for certain carrier contracts awarded after the act’s effective date.
