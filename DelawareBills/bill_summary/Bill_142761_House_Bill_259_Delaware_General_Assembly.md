## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16, §2603 of the Delaware Code to strengthen documentation, data access, and public reporting requirements related to lead-poisoning screening for children in child care and early school enrollment, particularly kindergarten.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires child care facilities and public/private nursery schools, preschools, and kindergartens to require **proof of lead-poisoning screening** for admission or continued enrollment for children **age 12 months and older**. (§2603(a))
- Allows lead screening (except for **kindergarten enrollment**) to be completed **within 60 calendar days after enrollment**. (§2603(b))
- Maintains two ways for parents/guardians to meet documentation requirements: **provider statement of screening** or **religious exemption certificate** signed by parent/guardian. (§2603(c))
- Directs the **Division of Public Health (DPH)** to ensure **all school nurses have access** to data confirming whether each enrolled child was screened and the **results of all lead screenings/tests**. (§2603(d))
- Requires lead screening results be recorded in **micrograms per deciliter** and “**provided by Jan 1, 2025**.” (§2603(d)) (date appears in bill text)
- Requires annual reporting: by **November 1** each year, districts/charters report, by school, counts of **kindergarten students not meeting §2603(c)** and total kindergarten enrollment. (§2603(e)(1))
- Requires DPH, by **December 1** each year, to compile and submit a single **school-disaggregated** report to specified officials and **post it publicly** on the DPH website. (§2603(e)(2))
- Requires any **contracts or computer upgrades** to “continue to include lead results.” (§2603(f))

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved oversight and transparency:** Mandates **public posting** of school-level kindergarten compliance data on the DPH website and distribution to state officials. (§2603(e)(2); Synopsis)
- **Better care coordination:** Ensures **school nurses** can access screening status and **test results**, potentially improving follow-up for children with elevated lead levels. (§2603(d))
- **Standardized reporting cadence:** Establishes recurring annual deadlines (**Nov 1** and **Dec 1**) to track compliance by school. (§2603(e))
- **Data standardization:** Requires results be in **micrograms per deciliter**, supporting consistent interpretation and comparisons across records. (§2603(d))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative burden on schools and districts:** Requires annual, school-by-school reporting of kindergarten compliance and enrollment totals by **November 1**. (§2603(e)(1))
- **Administrative burden on DPH:** Requires compilation, submission to multiple recipients, and **public web posting** by **December 1** annually. (§2603(e)(2))
- **Potential implementation complexity:** Requirement that data access and records include screening confirmations and results for “**each enrolled child**” may require system/process updates. (§2603(d), (f))

## Key Policy Changes
- **DPH reporting and public posting mandate:** DPH must compile school-reported data and submit a school-disaggregated report to specified officials and post on its website by **December 1** annually. (§2603(e)(2); Synopsis)
- **Annual school reporting mandate:** Districts and charter schools must report by **November 1** annually, by school, the number of kindergartners not meeting §2603(c) and total kindergarten enrollment. (§2603(e)(1))
- **Nurse data access requirement:** DPH must ensure **all school nurses** can access screening confirmation and all lead screening/test results for each enrolled child. (§2603(d))
- **Units and date requirement:** Results must be in **micrograms per deciliter** and “provided by **Jan 1, 2025**.” (§2603(d))
- **Systems/contracts continuity:** Contracts or computer upgrades must continue to include lead results. (§2603(f))
