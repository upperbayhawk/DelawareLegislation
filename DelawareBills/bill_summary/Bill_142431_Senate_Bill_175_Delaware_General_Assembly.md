## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s net energy metering statute (Title 26, §1014) to ensure customers who generate excess electricity receive the benefit of “Excess kWh Credits” by requiring those credits to be **credited or carried over**, rather than being forfeited at the end of an annualized billing period.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires net energy metering rules to provide that **Excess kWh Credits at the end of an annualized billing period carry over to subsequent annualized billing periods**, instead of reverting to the electric distribution company.
- Removes statutory language stating utilities and co-ops **shall not** reimburse/credit/remunerate customers for Excess kWh Credits at the end of the annualized billing period.
- Applies to **commission-regulated electric utilities, municipal electric companies, and electric cooperatives** (including those in any exemption period under **§223**).
- Keeps the existing system sizing cap: facilities must be designed to produce **no more than 110%** of expected aggregate consumption, based on the **average of the 2 previous 12-month periods** (or estimated for new construction).
- Allows, for **nonresidential customers that are not qualifying farm customers**, a potential **limit** on how much Excess kWh Credits may be carried forward (limit may be imposed by the Commission/municipals/co-ops).
- Permits **commission-regulated electric utilities** to **recover implementation costs**, including **billing system IT costs**, tied to carrying credits forward.
- Effective upon enactment; utilities/co-ops/municipals have until **December 1, 2026** to update billing systems to comply.
- Approved **July 16, 2025** (enactment date in the chapter law).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Prevents forfeiture of customer-earned credits:** Excess kWh Credits no longer “revert to the electric distribution company” at annual true-up; instead they **carry over** (26 Del. C. §1014(e)(1)a as amended).
- **Improves customer value of net metering:** Customers can apply accumulated credits to offset future usage beyond the annualized billing period (carryover to subsequent annualized periods).
- **Maintains protections for grid reliability/safety/capacity:** Net metering rules must still consider “reliability, safety, and capacity of the electric distribution system” (§1014(e)).
- **Provides implementation cost recovery for regulated utilities:** Commission-regulated utilities may recover “implementation costs including billing system information technology costs” tied to the carryover change (§1014(e)(1)a).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential rate/charge impacts from cost recovery:** Because commission-regulated utilities are permitted to recover implementation and billing IT costs, customers may ultimately bear some costs through rates or charges (authorization is explicit; exact mechanism/amount not specified in text).
- **Reduced value capture for utilities at annual true-up:** Utilities/electric distribution companies no longer receive end-of-year unused credits that previously “revert[ed]” to them (revenue/energy accounting impacts not quantified in text).
- **Nonresidential carryover may be limited:** For nonresidential customers (excluding qualifying farm customers), the Commission/municipals/co-ops “may impose a limit” on carryforward credits, potentially reducing benefits for some larger nonresidential generators (§1014(e)(1)a).

## Key Policy Changes
- **End-of-year credit treatment:** Changes end-of-annualized-period treatment of Excess kWh Credits from **reversion to the electric distribution company** to **carryover to subsequent annualized billing periods** (§1014(e)(1)a).
- **Removes “no remuneration” prohibition:** Strikes/overrides language that utilities/co-ops/municipals “shall not…reimburse, credit, or otherwise remunerate” net metering customers for Excess kWh Credits at annual true-up (§1014(d)(5) as amended).
- **Nonresidential cap authority:** Allows the Commission/municipals/co-ops to **limit carryforward amounts** for **nonresidential customers who are not qualifying farm customers** (§1014(e)(1)a).
- **Implementation cost recovery:** Allows commission-regulated utilities to **recover implementation and billing IT costs** incurred because of the new carryover requirement (§1014(e)(1)a).

## Who Benefits
- **Net energy metering customers** who generate excess electricity and would otherwise lose unused Excess kWh Credits at the end of the annualized billing period.
- Potentially **residential and qualifying farm customers** (farm customer reference: §902(3) of Title 3) because the statute specifically allows limits on carryover only for certain **nonresidential non-farm** customers.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Electric distribution companies/utilities/municipals/co-ops** that previously received end-of-year reverted Excess kWh Credits.
- **Ratepayers** of commission-regulated utilities to the extent cost recovery for implementation/billing IT costs is passed through in rates (the bill authorizes recovery but does not quantify impacts).
- **Nonresidential non-farm net metering customers** if the Commission/municipals/co-ops impose a limit on end-of-year carryforward credits.
