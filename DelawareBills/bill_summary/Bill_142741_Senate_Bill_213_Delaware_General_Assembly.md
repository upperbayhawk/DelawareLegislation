## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 16’s Hospital Budget Review Act framework to continue annual hospital financial reporting and benchmark accountability while modifying processes in response to constitutional concerns, including adding a “Meaningful Cost Containment Arrangement” (MCCA) pathway that can exempt a hospital from the benchmark compliance plan requirement for an applicable year. (Approved **January 30, 2026**.)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Establishes definitions and thresholds for a **Meaningful Cost Containment Arrangement (MCCA)**, including “Medicare or Medicaid Global Budget Arrangement” and “Substantial Financial Downside Risk Arrangement.”  
- Defines a global budget arrangement as covering at least **50%** of historical patient volumes and putting at least **3%** of the hospital’s **net patient revenue at risk** for the applicable year.  
- Defines a substantial downside risk arrangement to require: total annual costs ≥ **3%** of net patient revenue; at least **10%** of those costs “at risk” to the hospital; and for hospitals with annual operating budgets > **$1,000,000,000**, at least **5,000 covered lives**.  
- Requires the Diamond State Hospital Cost Review Board (“Board”) to adopt a **Uniform Reporting Manual** by regulation to standardize hospital budget submissions.  
- Updates hospital annual submission requirements (including a required narrative explaining year-over-year changes and actions to adhere to the benchmark) and maintains the requirement to submit **audited financial statements within 30 days** of finalization (beginning with **2023** audited statements).  
- Requires the Board to issue written findings and determinations on: (1) benchmark/spending directive compliance; (2) benchmark compliance plan compliance (if applicable); and (3) whether a hospital qualifies for MCCA status (upon application).  
- Beginning in **2027** (per the synopsis), if a hospital exceeds the spending benchmark, the Board may require a **benchmark compliance plan** within **45 days**, unless the hospital qualifies for an MCCA for that year (with specified CEO attestations).  
- Retains enforcement authority: civil penalties up to **$500,000** for knowingly failing to provide required information or adhere to standards/procedures/deadlines, with notice and opportunity to be heard.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More standardized reporting:** Requires a Board-adopted **Uniform Reporting Manual** to “ensure the consistency” of hospital submissions.  
- **Clearer cost-containment alternatives:** Creates a defined MCCA route with concrete thresholds (e.g., **3%** revenue at risk; **10%** costs at risk; **5,000 lives** for very large hospitals) that can substitute for a benchmark compliance plan for a given year.  
- **More formal accountability record:** Requires **written findings of fact and determinations** regarding benchmark compliance and related requirements.  
- **Ongoing transparency mechanisms:** Keeps annual hospital submissions and requires at least **1 public hearing per hospital per year**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added administrative steps for MCCA exemption:** Hospitals seeking MCCA status must apply and submit **CEO attestations** and provide mid-year notifications if key MCCA conditions change (breach, penalty waiver, renegotiated terms).  
- **Potential compliance burden remains significant:** The bill continues annual detailed reporting, audited statement submissions within **30 days**, Board meetings with each hospital, and public hearing requirements.  
- **Penalty exposure remains:** Knowingly failing to comply with reporting/standards/deadlines may trigger civil penalties up to **$500,000**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates/clarifies definitions and thresholds for MCCAs** (16 Del. C. § 9951), including:  
  - Global budget arrangement: ≥ **50%** historical patient volume; ≥ **3%** net patient revenue at risk.  
  - Substantial downside risk arrangement: total annual costs ≥ **3%** of net patient revenue; ≥ **10%** of total annual costs at risk; and if annual operating budget > **$1,000,000,000**, ≥ **5,000 covered lives**.  
  - Allows the Board by regulation to **increase** the amounts/percentages in the downside-risk thresholds.  
- **Uniform Reporting Manual requirement:** Board must adopt a Manual by regulation to standardize budget submissions (§ 9951(5), § 9952(g), § 9953(a)).  
- **Hospital reporting content updates:** Requires labor costs “by units of service and budget category,” and a narrative explaining year-over-year changes and actions to adhere to the benchmark (§ 9953(a)).  
- **Audited financial statement deadline maintained/clarified:** Audited financial statements due **within 30 days** after finalization; requirement begins with **2023** audited statements (§ 9953(b)).  
- **Written findings requirement expanded/explicit:** Board must issue written findings/determinations on benchmark compliance, benchmark compliance plan compliance (if applicable), and MCCA status upon hospital application (§ 9953(d)(7)).  
- **MCCA application conditions and ongoing notice duty (as enacted text shows):**  
  - CEO attestation: not in material breach; no penalty waivers; penalties/risk provisions not modified.  
  - Hospital must notify the Board during the year if it breaches, receives penalty waivers, or negotiates new terms (§ 9953(d)(7)c.).  
- **Benchmark compliance plan process:** If benchmark exceeded, Board may require submission within **45 days**, with timetable not exceeding **12 months**, and resubmission allowed up to **30 calendar days** if incomplete/unacceptable (§ 9954).  
- **Enforcement maintained:** Civil penalties up to **$500,000** for knowing failures; enforcement orders require notice and opportunity to be heard (§ 9957).

## Who Benefits

- **Hospitals using downside-risk or global budget models:** can qualify for an **MCCA** and be **not subject to the benchmark compliance plan process** for the applicable year (if they meet requirements).
