## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Create a new Delaware criminal offense and related civil remedies for using force, threats, or physical obstruction to interfere with (1) reproductive health services or (2) lawful exercise of religious freedom at a place of worship, and to protect related facilities/property.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new offense in Title 11, § 1310: **“Interference with reproductive health services or exercise of religion.”**
- Prohibits intentionally **injuring, intimidating, or interfering** with a person **by force/threat of force/physical obstruction** because they are obtaining/providing reproductive health services, or to discourage such services.
- Prohibits the same conduct targeting a person **lawfully exercising religious freedom at a place of worship**.
- Criminalizes **intentional damage or destruction** of property of a **facility providing reproductive health services** or of a **place of worship**.
- Sets escalating criminal grades: **Class A misdemeanor** (default), **Class G felony** (2nd/subsequent), **Class D felony** (bodily injury), **Class B felony** (death).
- Creates civil enforcement options for affected persons/entities and the **Attorney General**, including **injunctions**, damages, and specified **statutory damages and civil penalties**.
- Allows private plaintiffs to elect **$5,000 statutory damages per violation** instead of actual damages (if elected before final judgment).
- Vests **Superior Court** with **original jurisdiction** over criminal proceedings under this section.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Enhanced protections and deterrence:** Establishes specific criminal and civil consequences for force/threats/obstruction tied to access to reproductive health services and religious worship (subsection (a); grading in (b)).
- **Civil remedies with fee-shifting:** Courts may award **injunctive relief**, **compensatory and punitive damages**, and **attorneys’ and expert witness fees** (subsection (c)(1)b.), which can improve access to enforcement for plaintiffs.
- **Clear statutory-damages option:** Provides a predictable alternative remedy of **$5,000 per violation** (subsection (c)(1)b.).
- **Attorney General enforcement tool:** Authorizes AG civil actions when there is “reasonable cause” of injury/potential injury, including civil penalties up to specified dollar caps (subsection (c)(2)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expanded potential liability and litigation exposure:** Creates both criminal liability and multiple civil enforcement pathways, including **punitive damages**, **fee awards**, **statutory damages ($5,000 per violation)**, and AG civil penalties up to **$25,000** per violation depending on circumstances (subsection (c)).
- **Escalating criminal severity:** Conduct can be charged as a felony (Class G/D/B) based on prior convictions or harm outcomes (subsection (b)(2)-(4)), increasing criminal justice system impacts for covered conduct.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “physical obstruction”:** Defined as making passage “unreasonably difficult or hazardous” (subsection (e)(4)); the bill does not specify concrete thresholds for what counts as “unreasonably” difficult/hazardous.
- **Meaning of “nonviolent physical obstruction”:** Civil penalty caps differ for “nonviolent physical obstruction” vs. “any other violation” (subsection (c)(2)b.), but “nonviolent” is not defined in the text.
- **Interaction with existing Delaware offenses:** The bill creates a new crime but does not specify how charging decisions should be made when other assault, trespass, vandalism, or disorderly conduct statutes may also apply.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates new Title 11 offense (§ 1310)** covering:
  - Force/threat/obstruction-based interference with people obtaining/providing reproductive health services (subsection (a)(1)-(2)).
  - Force/threat/obstruction-based interference with lawful religious exercise at a place of worship (subsection (a)(3)).
  - Intentional property damage/destruction of reproductive health facilities or places of worship (subsection (a)(4)).
- **Criminal grading:**
  - Default: **Class A misdemeanor** (subsection (b)(1)).
  - **Second or subsequent offense after a prior conviction**: **Class G felony** (subsection (b)(2)).
  - If **bodily injury** results: **Class D felony** (subsection (b)(3)).
  - If **death** results: **Class B felony** (subsection (b)(4)).
- **Civil remedies (private right of action):**
  - Eligible plaintiffs are limited depending on the violated paragraph:
    - For (a)(1)-(2): only persons involved in obtaining/providing services in a reproductive health facility (subsection (c)(1)a.).
    - For (a)(3)-(4): persons lawfully exercising religious freedom at a place of worship or the entity that owns/operates the place of worship (subsection (c)(1)a.).
  - Courts may award **injunctions**, **compensatory and punitive damages**, **costs**, and **reasonable attorneys’ and expert witness fees** (subsection (c)(1)b.).
  - Plaintiff may elect **$5,000 statutory damages per violation** before final judgment (subsection (c)(1)b.).
- **Attorney General civil enforcement:**
  - AG may sue with “reasonable cause” of injury/potential injury (subsection (c)(2)a.).
  - Civil penalties (per respondent), caps:
    - **First offense:** up to **$10,000** for **nonviolent physical obstruction**; up to **$15,000** for any other violation (subsection (c)(2)b.1).
    - **Second/subsequent:** up to **$15,000** for **nonviolent physical obstruction**; up to **$25,000** for any other violation (subsection (c)(2)b.2).
- **Parent/guardian carve-out:** A parent or legal guardian of a minor is not subject to criminal penalties or civil remedies for activities described in (a) **directed exclusively at that minor** (subsection (d)).
- **Definitions added:** “facility,” “interfere with,” “intimidate,” “physical obstruction,” and cross-reference definition of “reproductive health services” (subsection (e)).
- **Jurisdiction:** **Superior Court** has **original jurisdiction** over criminal proceedings under § 1310 (subsection (f)).

## Who Benefits
- Individuals **obtaining or providing reproductive health services** in covered facilities (subsection (a)(1)-(2); civil standing in (c)(1)a.).
- Individuals **lawfully exercising religious freedom at a place of worship** and **entities owning/operating places of worship** (subsection (a)(3)-(4); civil standing in (c)(1)a.).
- The State (via the **Attorney General**) gains an additional civil enforcement mechanism with penalty authority (subsection (c)(2)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Individuals or groups engaging in conduct that meets the statute’s elements (force, threats of force, or physical obstruction; or property damage/destruction) may face:
  - Criminal penalties up to felony levels (subsection (b)), and/or
  - Civil liability including **injunctions**, **punitive damages**, **attorneys’ fees**, **$5,000 statutory damages per violation**, and **AG civil penalties up to $25,000** (subsection (c)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Criminal enforcement:** Violations of subsection (a) are punishable as:
  - **Class A misdemeanor** (default) (b)(1)
  - **Class G felony** for second/subsequent after prior conviction (b)(2)
  - **Class D felony** if bodily injury results (b)(3)
  - **Class B felony** if death results (b)(4)
- **Private civil enforcement:** Eligible plaintiffs may seek **injunctive relief**, **compensatory and punitive damages**, **costs**, and **reasonable attorneys’ and expert witness fees**; may elect **$5,000 statutory damages per violation** before final judgment (c)(1).
- **Attorney General civil enforcement:** AG may seek injunctive relief, compensatory damages, and civil penalties capped at:
  - **$10,000** (first offense nonviolent physical obstruction) / **$15,000** (first offense other)
  - **$15,000** (second/subsequent nonviolent physical obstruction) / **$25,000** (second/subsequent other) (c)(2)
- **Jurisdiction:** Criminal proceedings under this section are in **Superior Court** (f).
- **Exemption:** Parent/legal guardian conduct directed exclusively at their minor is exempt from criminal penalties and civil remedies under this section (d).
