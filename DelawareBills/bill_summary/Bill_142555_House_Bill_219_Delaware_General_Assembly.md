## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 1 of the Delaware Code to designate “Diwali Day” as a ceremonial day recognized annually in Delaware.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new statutory ceremonial day: **“Diwali Day.”**
- Defines Diwali Day as **the 15th day of the Hindu lunisolar month of Kartik**.
- Ties Delaware’s recognition to the day recognized by the **Indian government** (“in accordance with the official day recognized by the Indian government”).
- Places the designation in **Chapter 6, Title 1** as new **§ 610. Diwali Day**.
- The bill’s synopsis states the day is to be **“commemorated appropriately annually in Delaware.”**
- The bill does **not** establish a State holiday, mandatory closure, leave entitlement, or required programming.
- No funding, enforcement mechanism, or administrative process is specified in the bill text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Official recognition of a widely observed cultural/religious celebration:** The bill notes Diwali is celebrated by “**millions of Americans**” and “many Delawareans,” and provides official recognition via statute (§ 610).
- **Clear annual reference point for commemoration:** Establishes a consistent annual designation tied to a specific lunar-calendar day (**15th of Kartik**), rather than an ad hoc proclamation.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Date determination may be unclear for practical scheduling:** The bill defines the day by a **Hindu lunisolar calendar** reference (15th of Kartik) and references the Indian government’s official day, but does not specify how Delaware will determine the corresponding Gregorian date each year.
- **No implementation guidance:** The synopsis references commemoration “appropriately,” but the bill text does not specify what commemoration entails or who is responsible.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- How will Delaware determine the **Gregorian calendar date** each year for “the 15th day of the Hindu lunisolar month of Kartik,” and what source will be used to align “with the official day recognized by the Indian government”?
- Does “commemorated appropriately” imply any expectations for state agencies, schools, or local governments, or is it purely symbolic?
- Is there any intended interaction with existing ceremonial days in Title 1, Chapter 6 (e.g., whether proclamations are expected)?
