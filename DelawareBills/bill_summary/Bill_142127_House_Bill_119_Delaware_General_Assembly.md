## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Establish statewide “Freedom to Read Act” standards for how **public libraries** and **school libraries** develop collections and handle **objections/removal requests**, including required written policies, limits on who may object, non-retaliation protections for staff, appeals processes, and public-records transparency.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires every **public library** receiving state/county/municipal funding to adopt and follow a **written collection development policy** consistent with state principles.  
- Requires each **local education agency (LEA)** (including school districts, charter schools, vo-tech districts, juvenile correctional facilities) to develop and implement **school library objection review policies** aligned to state principles.  
- Prohibits removing/excluding library materials **because of** an author’s origin/background/views or **because of partisan, ideological, or religious disapproval** (public libraries and school libraries).  
- Requires **materials under review remain available** to patrons/students/staff **until the review process concludes**.  
- Limits who may file objections: public libraries—only **residents, business owners, property owners** in the service area, or **taxpayers of a library district financing a library**; school libraries—only **students, parents/guardians of enrolled students, or school personnel** in the school with the material.  
- Caps public-library objections to **1 at a time per individual** (a second may be filed only after completion of review and any appeals).  
- Creates/uses appeal structures: public library removals—**State Librarian makes the final decision**; school library removals—appeal to LEA board (decision due **within 30 days**), then final appeal to a newly established **School Library Review Committee**.  
- Makes records about objections/removals/reviews/appeals **public records** notwithstanding **29 Del. C. § 10002**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardizes statewide procedures**: Requires written, principle-aligned collection policies for public libraries and LEAs (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c); 14 Del. C. § 7201(c)).  
- **Reduces viewpoint-based removals**: Bars exclusion/removal based on creator’s origin/background/views or partisan/ideological/religious disapproval (29 Del. C. § 8731A(b)(2)-(3); 14 Del. C. § 7201(b)(2)-(3)).  
- **Maintains access during disputes**: Materials must remain available while under objection review (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c)(1); 14 Del. C. § 7201(c)(2)).  
- **Protects staff from retaliation**: Public libraries and LEAs may not retaliate against staff acting consistent with the principles/policies (29 Del. C. § 8731A(d); 9 Del. C. § 803(c); 14 Del. C. § 7201(e)).  
- **Creates clear appeals and final decision points**: Public library appeals end with the **State Librarian**; school appeals end with the **School Library Review Committee** (29 Del. C. § 8731A(f); 14 Del. C. § 7201(g)-(h)).  
- **Increases transparency**: Objection/removal/appeal records are expressly public records (29 Del. C. § 8731A(g); 14 Del. C. § 7201(j)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative workload**: Public libraries must adopt written policies with specified procedures; LEAs must develop and implement objection policies and run review/appeal processes, including a board decision deadline of **30 days** (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c); 14 Del. C. § 7201(c), (g)).  
- **Limits number of objections per person (public libraries)**: Individuals may submit only **1 objection at a time**, potentially delaying additional concerns (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c)(3)).  
- **Expanded public disclosure**: All records relating to objections/removals/reviews/appeals are public records notwithstanding otherwise-applicable exemptions under **§ 10002** (29 Del. C. § 8731A(g); 14 Del. C. § 7201(j)).  
- **Centralizes final authority for public library removals**: The **State Librarian** must make the final decision on removal requests (29 Del. C. § 8731A(f)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- “**Reasonable timeline**” for school-library review processes is required but not defined (14 Del. C. § 7201(c)(3)).  
- The bill authorizes rules for public-library appeals and states the process “**may include**” review by specified bodies, but does not require any particular intermediate step before the State Librarian’s final decision (29 Del. C. § 8731A(f)).  
- Scope of the public-records requirement versus privacy protections is not detailed beyond the statement that all related records are public (29 Del. C. § 8731A(g); 14 Del. C. § 7201(j)).  
- The cited criminal-law carve-out references **Title 11, Chapter 5, Subchapter 7, Subpart C** but the bill text does not describe what materials that covers (29 Del. C. § 8731A(b)(4); 14 Del. C. § 7201(b)(4)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates new public library collection development statute** (29 Del. C. § 8731A) defining “public library” (funded by state/county/municipality; includes privately incorporated public libraries and county libraries; excludes school libraries, higher ed libraries, and Department of Correction libraries).  
- Sets **public library collection development principles** and adds a criminal-law carve-out: nothing overrides state law on material violating **Title 11, Chapter 5, Subchapter 7, Subpart C** (29 Del. C. § 8731A(b)(1)-(4)).  
- Requires public libraries to adopt written policies including:  
  - material stays available during review;  
  - only certain local stakeholders (plus **library-district taxpayers**) may object;  
  - **1 objection at a time** per individual (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c)(1)-(3)).  
- Adds **non-retaliation protections** for public library employees/contractors and for school librarians/media specialists/employees (29 Del. C. § 8731A(d); 14 Del. C. § 7201(e); also mirrored for county libraries in 9 Del. C. § 803(c)).  
- Makes objection/removal/review/appeal records **public records** notwithstanding **29 Del. C. § 10002** (29 Del. C. § 8731A(g); 14 Del. C. § 7201(j)).  
- Requires LEAs to establish objection policies with a uniform submission process and a “reasonable timeline” (14 Del. C. § 7201(c)).  
- Establishes the **School Library Review Committee** (7 specified members or designees), chaired by the State Librarian/designee; DOE provides staff support; quorum and voting rules set by statute (14 Del. C. § 7201 [committee section]).  
- Makes adoption/compliance with the public library policy a condition for **public libraries contracting with the Division** (29 Del. C. § 6604(b)).

## Who Benefits
- **Public library patrons** and **students/school personnel** who retain access to materials during review processes.  
- **Library and school library staff** protected from retaliation when acting consistent with the principles/policies (29 Del. C. § 8731A(d); 14 Del. C. § 7201(e)).  
- **Local residents and stakeholders** seeking predictable, standardized objection procedures.  
- **General public** through expanded public access to records about objections/removals and decisions (29 Del. C. § 8731A(g); 14 Del. C. § 7201(j)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Public libraries and LEAs** due to added policy, recordkeeping, and process requirements (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c), (g); 14 Del. C. § 7201(c), (g), (j)).  
- **Individuals outside a library’s service area or library-district taxpayers** (public libraries) and individuals not within the school community (school libraries) who are barred from filing objections (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c)(2); 14 Del. C. § 7201(d)).  
- **Individuals wanting to raise multiple public-library objections at once** due to the **1-at-a-time** limitation (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c)(3)).

- **School library appeal deadline:** LEA board must issue a decision **within 30 days** after an appeal is made to the board at the conclusion of the LEA’s review process (14 Del. C. § 7201(g)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (public libraries):**
  - Must adopt and follow a written policy consistent with state principles, including a removal-request procedure and keeping challenged material available during review (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c)).  
  - Objection eligibility and **1-at-a-time** objection limit (29 Del. C. § 8731A(c)(2)-(3)).  
  - Contracting condition: public libraries contracting with the Division must adopt/comply with the policy (29 Del. C. § 6604(b)).  
- **Compliance requirements (school libraries/LEAs):**
  - LEAs must implement objection review policies meeting statutory elements and aligned with principles; material remains available during review; board appeal decision within **30 days**; final appeal to School Library Review Committee (14 Del. C. § 7201(c), (g)-(h)).  
- **Non-retaliation mandates:** public library governing bodies and LEAs are prohibited from retaliating against covered staff for compliant actions (29 Del. C. § 8731A(d); 9 Del. C. § 803(c); 14 Del. C. § 7201(e)).
