## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14 to create a broad, statewide nondiscrimination rule for Delaware public schools (including school districts and charter schools), align charter school admissions restrictions and bullying-related provisions with that rule, and require the Delaware Department of Education (DOE) to update related nondiscrimination regulations.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new nondiscrimination provision in Title 14 (§ 136) applying to **all public schools**, including **school districts and charter schools**.
- Prohibits schools from discriminating against students based on an expanded list of protected characteristics, including **protective hairstyle, body size, pregnancy/childbirth or related conditions, genetic information, socioeconomic status, familial status, immigration status, and housing status** (among others).
- Incorporates by reference certain definitions from **Chapter 45 of Title 6** (e.g., **disability, gender identity, protective hairstyle, race, religion, sexual orientation**).
- Maintains an express exception allowing certain charter schools to **restrict student admission by gender** if authorized under **§ 506(a)(3)c.**
- Updates charter school restriction language in **§ 506** to prohibit admissions discrimination on **any basis protected under new § 136** (while preserving the gender exception for certain schools).
- Expands/aligns bullying law: modifies the bullying definition in **§ 4161** and bullying investigation requirements in **§ 4164(b)(2)f.** to include targeting based on **any § 136 protected class** (and age).
- Requires DOE to **update all DOE regulations related to non-discrimination** to reflect the additional protected classes in **§ 136**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader student protections:** The bill explicitly adds protections against discrimination based on **body size, pregnancy/childbirth or related conditions, genetic information, socioeconomic status, familial status, immigration status, and housing status** (§ 136(b)).
- **Uniformity across schools:** Applies the same nondiscrimination baseline to **public schools, districts, and charter schools** (§ 136(a)(1), (b)), and aligns charter admissions restrictions accordingly (§ 506(a)(4)).
- **Stronger linkage to bullying prevention:** Bullying definition and investigative procedures are aligned to ensure bullying tied to **any protected class under § 136** is recognized and assessed (§ 4161; § 4164(b)(2)f.).
- **Regulatory alignment mandate:** DOE must update nondiscrimination regulations to match the new protected-class list (§ 5), which may reduce inconsistency between statute and regulations.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Implementation workload:** DOE must update “all of the Department’s regulations related to non-discrimination” (§ 5), and districts/charters may need policy/procedure updates to reflect the expanded protected classes (implied by new § 136 and the bullying provisions).
- **Gender-based admissions exception remains:** The bill expressly permits certain charter schools to **restrict admission by gender** (§ 136(c); § 506(a)(4) reference), which may be viewed as limiting the scope of nondiscrimination in admissions for those schools.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date and implementation timeline:** The bill text provided does not state when changes take effect or by when DOE regulations must be updated.  
- **Enforcement mechanism:** § 136 creates a prohibition but the bill text provided does not specify a complaint process, remedies, or penalties for violations.
- **Scope of “school” beyond students:** § 136 focuses on discrimination “against a student,” while bullying provisions address acts against a student, volunteer, or employee (§ 4161). The bill does not clarify whether § 136 protections extend to volunteers/employees.
- **Definitions for newly added categories:** Some terms are defined by reference to Title 6 (e.g., gender identity, protective hairstyle), but the bill text does not provide definitions for additional categories such as **body size, socioeconomic status, familial status, immigration status, or housing status**.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New § 136 (Title 14) — Prohibition on discrimination**
  - Defines “school” as a **public school, including school districts and charter schools** (§ 136(a)(1)).
  - Imports definitions from **Title 6, Chapter 45** for: **disability, gender identity, protective hairstyle, race, religion, sexual orientation** (§ 136(a)(2)).
  - Prohibits discrimination against a student based on: **race, ethnicity, creed, color, religion, national origin, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, protective hairstyle, body size, pregnancy or childbirth or related conditions, disability, genetic information, socioeconomic status, familial status, immigration status, or housing status** (§ 136(b)).
  - Allows certain charter schools to **restrict student admission by gender** if authorized under **§ 506(a)(3)c.** (§ 136(c)).
- **Amends § 506 (Charter school restrictions)**
  - Prohibits admissions discrimination on “**any other basis protected under § 136**” (while preserving the gender exception for certain authorized schools) (§ 506(a)(4) as amended).
- **Amends § 4161 (Bullying definition)**
  - Updates bullying-related language to include hostile environment created due to targeting based on the target being in any class protected under **§ 136** or based on **age** (§ 4161(2)b as amended).
- **Amends § 4164(b)(2)f. (Bullying policy investigation component)**
  - Requires bullying investigations to include determining whether the target was targeted due to listed categories and adds “**or any other basis protected under § 136**” (§ 4164(b)(2)f as amended).
- **DOE regulatory update requirement**
  - DOE must update all nondiscrimination-related regulations to reflect the additional protected classes listed in **§ 136** (§ 5).
