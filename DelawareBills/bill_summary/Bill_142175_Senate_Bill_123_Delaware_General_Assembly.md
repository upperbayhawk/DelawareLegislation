## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To clarify Delaware intestate succession and related inheritance rights by (1) aligning Title 12 definitions of “child” and “parent” with parentage law in Title 13, (2) updating rules for after-born and posthumous children (including assisted reproduction), and (3) repealing older Title 13 intestacy provisions for “illegitimate” persons that are superseded by Title 12’s framework.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates Title 12 §301 so an after-born child omitted from a will can take an intestate share **if the testator becomes a parent after executing the will** and made no provision for that child (unless the will says later-born children do not affect it).
- Establishes detailed intestacy rules for **posthumous children**, including children conceived through **assisted reproduction**, in Title 12 §505.
- Adds survivorship and timing thresholds for posthumous children: child must **live at least 120 hours after birth** to be treated as living at the decedent’s death.
- For assisted reproduction begun after death, requires the personal representative receive notice/actual knowledge of intent to use genetic material **within 6 months of death**, and requires pregnancy/birth within set timeframes (**36 months** to be “in utero” or **45 months** to be born after death).
- Clarifies that, for intestate succession, “child” and “parent” are based on parentage established under **Chapter 8 of Title 13** (Title 12 §508).
- Repeals Title 13 §§1302–1304 (older intestacy rules relating to “illegitimate” persons and inheritance from/to mother/father).
- Act is enacted as **Chapter 156, Volume 85**, approved **August 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer inheritance rules for posthumous children** (including assisted reproduction) by adding explicit thresholds: **120-hour survivorship**, **6-month notice/knowledge**, **36-month in utero**, **45-month birth** (Title 12 §505).
- **Greater consistency across statutes** by tying intestacy “child”/“parent” determinations to Title 13 parentage law (Title 12 §508; synopsis notes cross-referencing Titles 12 and 13).
- **Modernizes statutory structure** by repealing older Title 13 intestacy sections (§§1302–1304) that conflict with/are superseded by Title 12’s intestacy framework.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Time limits may exclude some posthumously conceived children** from intestate inheritance if notice is not received within **6 months**, or if the embryo is not in utero within **36 months** or the child is not born within **45 months** after death (Title 12 §505(b)(2)).
- **120-hour survivorship requirement** may prevent inheritance for a child born alive who dies within **120 hours** after birth (Title 12 §505(b)(1)–(2)).
- The Act states §505 **is not applied** if it would result in a **taking of intestate estate by the State** (Title 12 §505(c)), which may limit application in certain estates.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 12 §301 (After-born children and wills):** Reframes language to apply when “a testator becomes a parent” after executing a will and made no provision for the child; child (or descendant if the child predeceased the parent) takes the same share as if the parent died intestate, **unless** the will states later-born children do not affect the will.
- **Title 12 §505 (Posthumous children; time of determining relationship):**
  - Defines “assisted reproduction” and “gestational period.”
  - If decedent dies **during gestation**, a child who is born and **lives at least 120 hours** is deemed living at decedent’s death.
  - If decedent dies **before pregnancy begins** via assisted reproduction, child is deemed living at decedent’s death **only if**:
    - Personal representative receives notice/actual knowledge of intent to use genetic material **not later than 6 months after death**, and
    - Embryo is **in utero not later than 36 months** after death **or** child is **born not later than 45 months** after death, and
    - Child **lives at least 120 hours** after birth.
  - Limits application where it would cause a **taking of intestate estate by the State**.
- **Title 12 §508 (Definitions for intestate succession):** “Child” and “Parent” depend on parentage established under **Chapter 8 of Title 13**; retains rules on adoption and children born out of wedlock, including paternity proof **by preponderance of the evidence** after death, with conditions for father or kindred to inherit (must have openly treated child as his and not refused support).
- **Title 13 §§1302–1304:** Repealed (prior intestacy rules addressing inheritance involving “illegitimate” persons).
- **Title 12 §503 / §3301:** Technical/formatting and cross-reference adjustments as shown in amendments (including tenancy-in-common clause and defined terms organization).

## Who Benefits
- **Posthumous children**, including those conceived using **assisted reproduction**, who meet the Act’s timing and survivorship requirements (Title 12 §505).
- **Estates, heirs, and courts** seeking clearer statutory rules for determining parent-child relationships for intestate succession (Title 12 §508; synopsis).
- **After-born children omitted from wills** when the testator becomes a parent after executing the will and did not provide for the child (Title 12 §301).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Potential posthumously conceived heirs** who do not meet the **6-month notice**, **36-month in utero**, **45-month birth**, or **120-hour survivorship** thresholds (Title 12 §505).
- **Other intestate heirs** whose shares could be reduced when a qualifying posthumous child is treated as living at the decedent’s death (Title 12 §505; Title 12 §503 distribution rules).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approval date:** **August 21, 2025** (Chapter 156, Volume 85).
- **Deadlines created by the Act (Title 12 §505(b)(2)):**
  - Personal representative must receive notice or have actual knowledge **not later than 6 months after the decedent’s death**.
  - Embryo must be **in utero not later than 36 months after the decedent’s death** *or* the individual must be **born not later than 45 months after the decedent’s death**.
  - Individual must live **at least 120 hours after birth** to be deemed living at decedent’s death.
- General effective date (e.g., “upon enactment”) is **not specified in source text** beyond the approval date.
