## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To strengthen Delaware’s regulation and enforcement of accessible parking spaces by (1) defining and standardizing signage/markings and maintenance requirements, (2) requiring local permits and compliance verification for new or modified accessible parking spaces, and (3) increasing penalties for unlawful use of accessible parking spaces. (Titles 9, 21, and 22)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates Title 21 definitions and enforcement rules for “accessible parking spaces,” including what counts as a “vehicle being used by a person with a disability.”  
- Requires accessible parking spaces (where provided) to comply with ADA-related federal regulations, applicable local building codes, and new Delaware-specific design/signage/maintenance requirements.  
- Establishes detailed signage and marking standards, including: sign size **12" x 18"**, minimum height **60 inches**, and inclusion of the **current maximum fines** under §4183(f); and access aisle paint/striping requirements (blue border and **4-inch** diagonal hatching at **45 degrees**).  
- Adds a Delaware-specific requirement: for every **3** federally-required van accessible spaces, **1** must be “marked for use only by a wheelchair or scooter user,” unless it would violate zoning/building-code minimum parking requirements.  
- Requires a **county/municipal permit** before installing a new accessible space or altering an existing one (installing, restriping, repainting, resurfacing, or otherwise altering), with post-work compliance verification via inspection or sufficient documentation/photos.  
- Authorizes counties/municipalities to impose a civil penalty **up to $500** for permit-related violations, but must allow **90 days** to cure before imposing a fine.  
- Increases the mandatory fine for unlawfully using an accessible parking space from **$200 → $250** (first offense) and **$300 → $500** (subsequent offense); replaces the prior “imprisonment … 10 to 30 days” option with **community service** (as reflected in the amended text).  
- Requires counties and municipalities (via Titles 9 and 22) to adopt ordinances/regulations consistent with new Title 21 requirements, including enforcement, penalties, and a **30-day** warning-to-cure process for certain local violations.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More consistent accessibility features statewide:** Specific sign, access aisle marking, and placement/maintenance requirements (e.g., shortest accessible route; snow/ice/debris removal “as soon as is practicable”) can reduce inconsistent application described in the bill’s findings.

- **Local pre-permit + verification mechanism:** Requiring permits before alterations and requiring inspection or documentation/photos to verify compliance may reduce noncompliant accessible space installations.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Updated definitions (Title 21 §4183):**
  - Defines “accessible parking space” and clarifies criteria for a “vehicle being used by a person with a disability,” including valid Delaware plate/placard or similar out-of-state/country item and that the eligible person is operator/passenger (including organizational placards with an eligible passenger).  
- **Signage/marking standards (new Title 21 §4183A(c)):**
  - Standard sign: **12" x 18"**, clearly visible, minimum height **60 inches**, international symbol of access, reserved-for-disabled notice, and must display **current maximum fines** under §4183(f).  
  - **Van accessible** spaces must say “Van Accessible.”  
  - Access aisles: signage prohibiting parking/obstruction; and paint requirements (blue border; interior hatched lines **4 inches** wide at **45 degrees**, contrasting color).  
  - Exceptions: if a site has **4 or fewer total spaces**, accessible space identification is not required; and where spaces are assigned to specific dwelling/sleeping units, identification is not required (still subject to anti-discrimination provisions).  
- **Additional design/operation rules (Title 21 §4183A(d)):**
  - For every **3** federally-required van accessible spaces, **1** must be for wheelchair/scooter user only (unless it would violate minimum parking requirements in zoning/building code).  
  - Placement: closest spaces on the shortest accessible route to the accessible entrance (unless prevented by local fire codes).  
  - Maintenance: snow/ice/debris removal “as soon as practicable”; access aisles must remain clear (including from plowed snow, bicycle racks, shopping carts).  
  - Time limits: no time limit in accessible spaces unless metered.  
- **Compliance timing triggers (Title 21 §4183A(e)):**
  - Applies to all **new** spaces installed after the effective date, and to **existing** accessible spaces when altered (restriped/repainted/resurfaced/otherwise altered) after the effective date or **within 5 years** of the effective date, whichever is sooner.  
  - Signage compliant with §4183A(c) must be installed by **2 years after** the effective date.  
  - Exception timing: accessible spaces in lots with **25 or fewer** spaces or in a **national register district** or **national historic area** need not comply until altered after the effective date.  
- **Permit + verification (Title 21 §4183A(f)-(h)):**
  - Permit required before installing/altering accessible spaces.  
  - Limited allowance for subsequent restriping/repainting/resurfacing without a permit if previously permitted at least once after the effective date, with no change to the plan and continued compliance.  
  - Civil penalty up to **$500** for violating the permit requirement; must allow **90 days** to cure before imposing fine.  
  - Compliance verification required after permitted work via on-site inspection or sufficient documentation/photos; if insufficient, inspection required.  
- **Increased unlawful parking fines; penalty structure change (Title 21 §4183(f)):**
  - Mandatory fine **$250** first offense; **$500** subsequent offense; subsequent offense may include **community service** (replacing the prior imprisonment option in the amendatory text).  
  - Additional penalty for placard/plate holders who commit certain misconduct: up to **6-month suspension** or **permanent revocation** of plate/placard.  
- **Local ordinance alignment (Titles 9 §310 and 22 §110):**
  - Requires State/county/municipal regulations/ordinances consistent with §4183A, including permit requirement and compliance confirmation.  
  - Requires enforcement and penalty provisions and a **written warning first**, with **30 days** to cure before summons/warrant.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Unlawful parking and related misconduct (Title 21 §4183):**
  - Mandatory fine **$250** for first offense; **$500** for subsequent like offense; subsequent offense may include **community service** (§4183(f)(1)).  
  - Plate/placard misuse (counterfeit, altering, allowing another to use, etc.) is prohibited (§4183(d)); placard/plate holders violating certain provisions may face **up to 6-month suspension** or **permanent revocation** (§4183(f)(2)).  
  - Illegally parked vehicles in accessible spaces may be towed; removal/storage costs borne by vehicle owner/operator (§4183(c)).  
- **Permit compliance (Title 21 §4183A(f)):**
  - Permit required before installing/altering accessible spaces.  
  - Civil penalty up to **$500** for violating permit requirement, with mandatory **90-day** cure opportunity before fine.  
- **Post-permit verification (Title 21 §4183A(g)):**
  - Issuing agency must verify compliance via inspection or sufficient documentation/photos; if insufficient, inspection required.  
- **Local ordinance enforcement (Titles 9 and 22):**
  - Ordinances must include enforcement and penalty provisions and require a written warning first; if not cured within **30 days**, enforcement officer may issue summons or seek a warrant.
