## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To limit the amount a public school district may increase property-tax revenue through tax-rate adjustments following a countywide general reassessment, by reducing the allowable annual increase from **10% to 2% per year for 5 years**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Reduces the maximum annual increase in **actual revenue** from school real estate taxes after a general reassessment from **10% to 2% per year**, for **each of the next 5 years**. (Amends 14 Del. C. § 1916(b))
- Applies to **all public school tax rates after July 1, 2025**. (Section 2)
- Fiscal note assumes the Act is **effective upon Governor’s signature** and **retroactive to July 1, 2025**. (Fiscal Note Assumption #1)
- Keeps an exception allowing up to a **10%** rate increase to address a **projected revenue loss** caused by reassessment to attempt to reach an “overall revenue neutral position.” (14 Del. C. § 1916(b))
- Makes conforming changes to the cross-reference in § 1916(c)(2) to reflect the new reassessment-based calculation. (14 Del. C. § 1916(c)(2))
- Estimated local revenue losses (fiscal note) attributable to the new 2% cap: **FY 2026: $29,256,270; FY 2027: $21,357,369; FY 2028: $13,458,469**.
- Fiscal note total planned revenue increase across listed districts: **$37,778,881**, with a **2% limit** amount shown as **$8,522,612** (table), and multi-year differences shown for 2026–2028.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Constrains post-reassessment tax-driven revenue growth:** Lowers the statutory cap from **10%** to **2% per year** for **5 years**, limiting rapid increases in school property-tax revenue after reassessment. (14 Del. C. § 1916(b); Synopsis)
- **Predictability for taxpayers:** A defined **2% annual cap** provides a clear maximum pace of revenue increases after reassessment. (14 Del. C. § 1916(b))
- **Still allows limited correction for reassessment-driven revenue drops:** Districts may increase the rate by up to **10%** if reassessment would otherwise cause a projected revenue loss. (14 Del. C. § 1916(b))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced local school revenue relative to current law:** Fiscal note estimates local revenue losses of **$29,256,270 (FY26), $21,357,369 (FY27), $13,458,469 (FY28)** due to the lower cap. (Fiscal Note “Cost”)
- **Constrains districts’ ability to raise local revenue without referendum:** The mechanism that previously allowed up to **10%** annual revenue increase after reassessment is reduced to **2%** (for 5 years), potentially limiting revenue capacity absent voter approval under § 1903. (14 Del. C. § 1916(b))
- **Retroactive application may disrupt planned budgets/rates:** Fiscal note assumes retroactivity to **July 1, 2025**, and the bill states applicability to rates after **July 1, 2025**, potentially affecting already planned post-7/1/2025 rate changes. (Section 2; Fiscal Note Assumption #1)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Change in post-reassessment revenue-growth cap:** Amends 14 Del. C. § 1916(b) to reduce the maximum annual increase in actual revenue from real estate tax levies after reassessment from **10%** to **2% per year**, for **each of the next 5 years**. (14 Del. C. § 1916(b); Synopsis)
- **Retains reassessment-loss exception:** If reassessment would cause a projected revenue loss compared to the previous year, the district may increase the rate by **no more than 10%** to attempt to reach revenue neutrality. (14 Del. C. § 1916(b))
- **Applicability:** Applies to “all public school tax rates after **July 1, 2025**.” (Section 2)
- **Conforming update:** Updates § 1916(c)(2) language tied to reassessment-based calculations (removing prior “plus … the 10% increase as authorized by subsection (b)”). (14 Del. C. § 1916(c)(2))

## Who Benefits
- **Property taxpayers in school districts affected by post-reassessment rate calculations**, to the extent the lower cap limits the pace of property-tax revenue increases to **2% per year** for **5 years** after reassessment. (14 Del. C. § 1916(b); Synopsis)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **School districts/local boards of education** that planned to increase local revenue through post-reassessment rate changes above **2% per year** (up to the former **10%** cap), as reflected in the fiscal note’s projected revenue losses. (Fiscal Note; 14 Del. C. § 1916(b))
- **District programs/services dependent on local revenue growth**, to the extent reduced local revenue occurs (loss amounts shown in fiscal note). (Fiscal Note “Cost”)

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date / applicability (bill text):** “This Act takes effect on **July 1, 2025**, and applies to all public school tax rates after **July 1, 2025**.” (Section 2)
