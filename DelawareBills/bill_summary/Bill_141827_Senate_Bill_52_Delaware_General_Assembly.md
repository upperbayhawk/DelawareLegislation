## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29, § 5502 of the Delaware Code to change when state pensioners may work for the State (and as substitute teachers) without interrupting pension payments, including shortening the required separation-from-service period, changing the age threshold for that separation requirement, and removing the earnings cap beginning in calendar year 2025.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Allows state pensioners to be employed in specified roles while still receiving a service or disability pension, but makes eligibility contingent on meeting new separation-of-service requirements.  
- Reduces the required separation from service from **6 months to a bona fide 3 months** before reemployment (applies under revised rules tied to subsection (a) employment).  
- Changes the age threshold for when the separation requirement applies to individuals **less than 59½ years old** (replacing prior “under age 65” framing in the amended text).  
- Keeps an earnings limit of **$50,000** only for **calendar years 2023 and 2024** for employment as a **temporary/casual/seasonal/substitute employee** or **substitute teacher**.  
- Specifies a pension offset when the earnings limit applies: pension is reduced **$1 for every $2** earned over **$50,000**, with the deduction beginning in **July of the year following** the calendar year in which earnings were reported.  
- Clarifies substitute teacher employment includes service in **charter schools** (not just school districts).  
- Adds/retains that pensioners may be employed as **per diem employees of the General Assembly** and other listed categories (elected official, Governor-appointed official, temporary justice of the peace, etc.).  
- States the changes are intended (per synopsis) to help address staffing shortages in **schools** and **state agencies with 24-hour shifts** and to comply with **federal law** regarding a bona fide separation period.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Addresses staffing shortages (stated intent):** The synopsis cites alleviating shortages in **schools** and **state agencies with 24-hour shifts** by making it easier for pensioners to return to work (shorter separation period; earnings limit removed beginning **calendar year 2025**).  
- **Faster return-to-work pathway:** Reduces mandatory waiting period from **6 months to 3 months**, enabling agencies/schools to rehire experienced retirees sooner.  
- **Expanded substitute teacher pool:** Explicitly includes **charter schools** for substitute teacher employment under the same framework as school districts.  
- **More earning flexibility starting 2025:** By removing the earnings limit beginning **calendar year 2025** (per synopsis), pensioners can work more without triggering pension reductions (previously tied to the **$50,000** cap in 2023–2024).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential increase in pension-and-salary overlap:** By reducing the separation period to **3 months** and removing the earnings cap starting **2025** (per synopsis), more pensioners may collect pension benefits while also being paid as employees, depending on how agencies utilize the authority.  
- **Administrative complexity remains for 2023–2024 earnings limit:** For **calendar years 2023 and 2024**, exceeding **$50,000** triggers a pension reduction formula (**$1 per $2 over**) and requires the Board-determined deduction beginning **July of the following year**, which requires tracking and enforcement.  
- **Age-based eligibility rule change may limit some return-to-work scenarios:** The amended statutory text ties the separation requirement to being **less than 59½ years old**, which may change who is constrained by the separation rule compared to prior law (previously referenced “under age 65”).
