## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s human trafficking laws to (1) expand property forfeiture eligibility for additional trafficking-related offenses, (2) update juvenile delinquency provisions to reflect that prostitution is an adult-only offense, and (3) treat human trafficking conduct as “abuse” for purposes of Family Court protective orders. (Title 11 § 787; Title 10 § 1041)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands mandatory forfeiture eligibility in **11 Del. C. § 787(e)(1)** from convictions under **§ 787(b)(1)-(3)** to convictions under **§ 787(b)(1) through (b)(5)**.  
- Per the bill synopsis, the forfeiture expansion is intended to include **“patronizing a victim of sexual servitude”** and **“trafficking of persons for use of body parts”** as predicate offenses for forfeiture under the trafficking statute.  
- Retains the Family Court process allowing a minor charged with **loitering** (and historically referenced prostitution) to seek an **indefinite stay** of juvenile delinquency proceedings and receive specialized services.  
- Provides that if the minor **substantially complies** with services, the Attorney General must move to **nolle prosequi** the stayed charges **no earlier than 1 year after** the stay is imposed.  
- Allows the Attorney General to move to lift the stay for further proceedings if the minor **has not substantially complied** with required services; the Family Court **shall lift** the stay upon such a motion.  
- Adds **“human trafficking” (as defined in 11 Del. C. § 787)** to the definition of **“abuse”** in **10 Del. C. § 1041(1)**, enabling trafficking conduct to support a **protective order** in Family Court.  
- The enacted version is **Approved May 7, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader asset forfeiture tool for trafficking-related convictions:** By extending forfeiture to convictions under **§ 787(b)(1) through (b)(5)**, courts must order forfeiture (on motion) for a wider range of trafficking crimes covered by those subsections.  
- **Improved victim protection in Family Court:** Explicitly classifying human trafficking conduct as **“abuse”** (10 Del. C. § 1041(1)k) may make it easier to seek **protective orders** based on trafficking-related conduct.  
- **Service-oriented option for affected minors:** The statute allows stays of juvenile proceedings and connection to **specialized services**, and offers case dismissal (nolle prosequi) after **≥ 1 year** of substantial compliance.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased forfeiture exposure for defendants:** More trafficking-related convictions trigger mandatory forfeiture (on motion), increasing the property at risk of forfeiture as “used…to commit or facilitate” the offense or derived from proceeds (11 Del. C. § 787(e)(1)).  
- **Potentially longer pending juvenile cases:** The Family Court may stay juvenile delinquency proceedings **indefinitely**, which could prolong uncertainty for minors and the system (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).  
- **Compliance monitoring burden:** The “substantial compliance” standard may require ongoing monitoring and contested litigation about whether compliance occurred (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Which exact offenses are in § 787(b)(4) and (b)(5):** The bill text expands forfeiture to (b)(4)-(b)(5) but does not reproduce those subsections; the synopsis states they include “patronizing a victim of sexual servitude” and “trafficking of persons for use of body parts.”  
- **Definition of “substantially complies”:** The statute uses “substantially complies” but does not define the threshold or metrics for compliance (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).  
- **Scope of “available specialized services”:** The text allows DSCYF and/or Family Court to identify and order “available specialized services” but does not specify what services must exist or be offered.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Forfeiture expansion (11 Del. C. § 787(e)(1)):** Changes forfeiture eligibility from convictions under **§ 787(b)(1)-(3)** to **§ 787(b)(1) through (b)(5)**; forfeiture covers interests in real/personal property used or intended to be used to commit/facilitate the offense, or proceeds derived from the offense.  
- **Juvenile delinquency stay procedure retained/updated (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)):**
  - Eligible movants: a party to the juvenile delinquency proceeding, an attorney guardian ad litem, or a CASA appointed under **10 Del. C. § 901 et seq.**  
  - Motion seeks to **stay** delinquency proceedings; may be opposed by the Attorney General.  
  - Family Court may stay **indefinitely** and may identify/order specialized services via DSCYF and/or the Court.  
  - If substantial compliance: Attorney General **shall** (upon motion) nolle prosequi **no earlier than 1 year** after the stay.  
  - If noncompliance: upon AG motion, Family Court **shall lift** the stay for further proceedings.  
- **Protective orders—definition of abuse expanded (10 Del. C. § 1041(1)k):** Adds **human trafficking** (as defined in **11 Del. C. § 787**) as conduct constituting “abuse.”

## Who Benefits
- **Victims of human trafficking** seeking Family Court protective orders, due to trafficking being expressly included as “abuse” (10 Del. C. § 1041(1)k).  
- **The State (law enforcement/prosecutors)** through expanded ability to pursue forfeiture tied to a broader set of trafficking convictions under § 787(b)(1)-(b)(5).  
- **Minors in juvenile delinquency proceedings** eligible for an indefinite stay and specialized services, with potential dismissal after **at least 1 year** of substantial compliance (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Individuals convicted under § 787(b)(4) or (b)(5)** (in addition to (b)(1)-(b)(3)) who may now face forfeiture of real/personal property used to facilitate the offense or derived from proceeds (11 Del. C. § 787(e)(1)).  
- **Minors who do not substantially comply** with ordered/identified services may have stays lifted and proceedings resumed (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines

- **Deadline/threshold in juvenile stay process:** The Attorney General may nolle prosequi stayed charges **no earlier than 1 year after** the stay was imposed, conditioned on substantial compliance (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Mandatory forfeiture (on motion):** Courts “shall order” forfeiture for qualifying convictions under **§ 787(b)(1) through (b)(5)** (11 Del. C. § 787(e)(1)).  
- **Juvenile compliance requirement:** Minor must **substantially comply** with specialized services identified by DSCYF and/or ordered by Family Court to obtain dismissal via nolle prosequi after **≥ 1 year** (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).  
- **Stay lifted for noncompliance:** If the Attorney General moves alleging the minor has not substantially complied, the Family Court **shall lift** the stay and resume proceedings (11 Del. C. § 787(g)(2)).  
- **Protective order eligibility:** Human trafficking conduct can now qualify as “abuse” for protective order purposes in Family Court (10 Del. C. § 1041(1)k).
