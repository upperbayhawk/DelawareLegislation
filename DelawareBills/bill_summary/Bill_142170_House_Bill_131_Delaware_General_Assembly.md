## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To foster humane treatment of animals and prevent animal cruelty by prohibiting **retail pet stores and brokers** from selling or transferring **dogs and cats**, while allowing pet stores to partner with shelters/rescues to showcase animals for adoption; and to assign/enhance enforcement authority for the **Office of Animal Welfare (DHSS)**. (Synopsis; Title 6, Ch. 40A; Title 16 amendments)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits a **retail pet store or broker** from **selling, leasing, offering to sell/lease, bartering, auctioning, transferring ownership of, or disposing of any cats or dogs**. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(a))
- Allows retail pet stores to **collaborate with animal shelters or animal rescues** to provide space to **showcase cats or dogs for adoption**, subject to conditions. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(b))
- Requires, for such collaborations: **(1)** no showcased animal was **purchased** by the shelter/rescue; and **(2)** the pet store owner/operator has **no financial interest** in the adoption/sale of those animals. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(b)(1)-(2))
- Establishes a **civil penalty of $500 per violation**, with **each offer** of an animal for sale in violation treated as a **separate violation**. (6 Del. C. § 4003A(a))
- Designates the **Office of Animal Welfare (DHSS)** as the enforcing authority for the retail pet store prohibition, using existing enforcement processes in Title 16. (6 Del. C. § 4003A(b))
- Expands/clarifies Title 16 provisions so the Office of Animal Welfare enforces **retail pet store laws** along with animal cruelty, dog control, dog barking, animal fighting, and dangerous animal laws; and confirms certain **law-enforcement powers** for animal welfare officers within those subject areas. (16 Del. C. § 3031F)
- Bars a **retail pet store** (as defined in Title 6) from being granted a **dog or kennel license** and reiterates it may not sell/transfer/dispose of dogs. (16 Del. C. § 3042F(g))
- Effective date: **6 months after enactment into law**. (Section 8)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces retail-market transfer of dogs/cats from pet stores and brokers** by banning sales/leases/auctions/transfers, which the bill frames as supporting humane treatment and preventing cruelty. (Synopsis; 6 Del. C. § 4002A(a))
- **Creates a clear enforcement mechanism and penalty amount**: $500 per prohibited offer/sale, with each offer a separate violation. (6 Del. C. § 4003A(a))
- **Permits adoption-focused partnerships** by allowing pet stores to host shelter/rescue animals for adoption (with “no purchase” and “no financial interest” guardrails). (6 Del. C. § 4002A(b))
- **Clarifies enforcement responsibility** by assigning retail pet store law enforcement to the Office of Animal Welfare (DHSS). (6 Del. C. § 4003A(b); 16 Del. C. § 3031F(b))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Eliminates a line of business** for retail pet stores and brokers involving dogs and cats (sale/lease/auction/transfer/disposal prohibited). (6 Del. C. § 4002A(a))
- **Compliance risk from per-offer penalties**: each unlawful “offer” is a separate violation, multiplying exposure at **$500 per offer**. (6 Del. C. § 4003A(a))
- **Licensing restriction**: a retail pet store may not be granted a **dog or kennel license**, which may limit operational flexibility for businesses that previously sought such licenses. (16 Del. C. § 3042F(g))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill prohibits pet stores/brokers from “**dispose of**” dogs/cats; the scope of “dispose of” (e.g., surrender/transfer to shelter, euthanasia arrangements, etc.) is **not further defined** in the text. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(a))
- The collaboration allowance requires the store have **no financial interest in the sale of the animals**; whether stores may charge shelters/rescues **fees for space, marketing, or services** is **not specified**. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(b)(2))
- How the Office of Animal Welfare will **investigate, document, and count “each offer”** as a separate violation is **not specified** (though enforcement is tied to Title 16 processes). (6 Del. C. § 4003A(a)-(b))

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/updated definitions** for: animal rescue (must be 501(c)(3); cannot obtain animals from breeders/brokers for compensation), animal shelter, breeder (excludes individuals selling a single litter per calendar year; excludes shelters/rescues), broker, consumer, and retail pet store (includes flea markets/farmer’s markets/temporary structures selling or auctioning animals for profit; includes exclusions). (6 Del. C. § 4001A)
- **Prohibition**: Retail pet stores and brokers may not sell/lease/offer/barter/auction/transfer ownership/dispose of **cats or dogs**. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(a))
- **Permitted activity**: Pet stores may host shelter/rescue animals for adoption if (1) shelter/rescue did not purchase the animals and (2) store has no financial interest in the animals’ sale/adoption. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(b))
- **Penalty**: Civil penalty of **$500**; **each offer** is a separate violation. (6 Del. C. § 4003A(a))
- **Enforcement**: Office of Animal Welfare enforces retail pet store provisions and is expressly charged with enforcing retail pet store laws under Title 16. (6 Del. C. § 4003A(b); 16 Del. C. § 3031F(b))
- **Licensing linkage**: A “retail pet store” may not be granted a **dog or kennel license** and may not sell/transfer/dispose of dogs (cross-referencing Title 6 definition). (16 Del. C. § 3042F(g))
- Updates Title 16 to include “retail pet store” laws in: enforcement scope, remittance of collected penalties to enforcing agency, and service of process provisions. (16 Del. C. §§ 3031F, 3033F, 3034F)

## Who Benefits
- **Animal shelters and qualifying animal rescues (501(c)(3))** that can partner with retail pet stores to showcase adoptable dogs/cats, provided animals were not purchased and the store has no financial interest. (6 Del. C. § 4001A(1); § 4002A(b))
- **Consumers** seeking adoption opportunities through collaborations hosted at retail pet stores. (6 Del. C. § 4002A(b); § 4001A(5))
- **Office of Animal Welfare (DHSS)**, which receives enforcement authority and, under Title 16, the enforcing agency receives remitted fines/penalties/forfeitures for covered laws. (6 Del. C. § 4003A(b); 16 Del. C. § 3033F)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Retail pet stores** (as defined, including flea markets/farmers markets/temporary structures selling/auctioning animals for profit) that sell or formerly sold dogs/cats. (6 Del. C. § 4001A(6); § 4002A(a))
- **Brokers** transferring dogs/cats for resale as household pets. (6 Del. C. § 4001A(4); § 4002A(a))
- **Breeders** indirectly, to the extent their distribution relied on retail pet stores or brokers for resale (the bill restricts those resale channels, though it does not directly ban breeding). (6 Del. C. § 4001A(3)-(4); § 4002A(a))
