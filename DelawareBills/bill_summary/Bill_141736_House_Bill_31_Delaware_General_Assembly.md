## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 6 of the Delaware Code to regulate and restrict **non-U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA)-accredited** persons or businesses that **receive compensation** for advising or assisting individuals with **veterans’ benefits matters**, including requiring specified disclosures, limiting/conditioning fees, and creating remedies and enforcement pathways under Delaware consumer protection law.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits a **non-VA-accredited** person from receiving **compensation** for advising/assisting on a **“veterans’ benefits matter,”** except as permitted under federal law and as allowed by the bill’s conditions.  
- Prohibits non-VA-accredited persons from receiving compensation for **referrals** to others for veterans’ benefits matters.  
- Requires non-VA-accredited persons who seek compensation to use a **written, signed fee agreement** that adheres to the criteria applicable under **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** (VA fee rule).  
- Bars non-VA-accredited persons from receiving compensation **unless they would be eligible** to receive compensation under **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** if they were accredited.  
- Prohibits **“excessive or unreasonable fees,”** using the factors in **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** to judge reasonableness.  
- Mandates specific **oral and written disclosures** at the outset of the relationship (written disclosure must be in **at least 12-point font**) and requires certain **advertising disclosures**.  
- Creates a **private right of action** for victims and treats violations as **unlawful practices** under Delaware’s consumer protection statute (Title 6, Chapter 25).  
- Allows an **additional civil penalty up to $10,000 per violation** when the violation is committed against an **elder person (65+)** or a **person with a disability** (as defined by § 4602 of Title 6).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Consumer protection for veterans:** Requires clear disclosures that the business is **not affiliated with the VA** and that **free services may be available**, reducing risk of deception (required disclosure language in § 2599(b)).  
- **Fee constraint tied to federal standards:** Limits compensation and prohibits “excessive or unreasonable” fees using **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** factors, potentially reducing burdensome fees (§ 2599(a)(4)-(5)).  
- **Documentation and retention requirement:** Requires written agreements and retention of written disclosures **during services and for at least 1 year after termination**, improving auditability and dispute resolution (§ 2599(b)(1)).  
- **Enhanced remedies:** Provides a **private cause of action** and integrates enforcement into Delaware’s unlawful trade practices framework (§ 2599A(a)-(b)).  
- **Extra protection for vulnerable populations:** Permits an additional penalty **up to $10,000 per violation** when victims are **65+** or have a disability (§ 2599A(d)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Compliance burdens for non-accredited providers:** Requires specific oral/written disclosures, advertising disclaimers, contract formalities, and document retention for at least **1 year** after the relationship ends (§ 2599(b)).  
- **Potential reduction in paid assistance options:** By restricting compensation unless aligned with **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** and requiring eligibility consistent with accredited standards, some non-accredited businesses may be unable to charge for certain services (§ 2599(a)(4)).  
- **Increased litigation exposure:** Establishes a **private cause of action** and treats violations as unlawful trade practices, increasing legal risk for violators (§ 2599A(a)-(b)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- How Delaware courts will apply **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** standards (which are written for accredited representatives) to **non-accredited** persons for “eligibility” and “reasonableness” determinations (§ 2599(a)(3)-(5)).  
- What specific **civil penalties** apply under the incorporated unlawful practices provisions (the bill deems violations unlawful under § 2513 and Subchapter II, but does not restate penalty amounts here) (§ 2599A(b)).  
- Scope boundaries between regulated “veterans’ benefits matters” and unregulated services (the Synopsis states some advice like **investment/insurance/banking** may remain permissible if unrelated to VA claims/services, but the operative statutory text relies on the definition in § 2599B(5)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/updated prohibition on compensated assistance by non-VA-accredited persons:**  
  - No compensation for advising/assisting on veterans’ benefits matters, **except as permitted** under federal law and within the bill’s conditions (§ 2599(a)(1)).  
  - No compensation for **referrals** to others for veterans’ benefits matters (§ 2599(a)(2)).  
- **Mandatory written fee agreement** (signed by both parties) meeting criteria that would apply under **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** (§ 2599(a)(3)).  
- **Compensation allowed only if it would be permissible** under **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** if the provider were accredited (§ 2599(a)(4)).  
- **Ban on excessive/unreasonable fees** using **38 C.F.R. § 14.636** factors (§ 2599(a)(5)).  
- **Required disclosures at the outset (oral + written)** including: not affiliated with VA/Delaware agencies/federally chartered VSOs; other orgs may provide free services; no endorsement; may qualify for other benefits (§ 2599(b)(1)).  
  - Written disclosure must be **12-point font** and conspicuous in the agreement; the individual must **verbally acknowledge** and **sign** the document (§ 2599(b)(1)).  
  - Provider must **retain a copy** during the relationship and for **at least 1 year after** termination (§ 2599(b)(1)).  
- **Advertising disclosure requirement** for compensated veterans’ benefits services; printed disclosures must be readily visible (including online); verbal disclosures must be clear/intelligible (§ 2599(b)(2)).  
- **Remedies and enforcement:**  
  - **Private cause of action** for victims (§ 2599A(a)).  
  - Violations deemed **unlawful practice** under § 2513 and Subchapter II of Chapter 25 (§ 2599A(b)).  
  - Court may award **reasonable attorneys’ fees** to plaintiff if defendant **willfully violated** a cease-and-desist agreement or a court order (§ 2599A(c)).  
  - Additional civil penalty **up to $10,000 per violation** for violations against an **elder person (65+)** or **person with a disability** (§ 2599A(d)).  
- **Definitions added** for “compensation,” “elder person” (**65+**), “person,” “person with a disability,” and “veterans’ benefits matter” (§ 2599B).

## Who Benefits
- **Veterans, dependents, survivors, and other eligible individuals** seeking VA- or Delaware-administered veterans’ benefits, through mandated disclosures, fee constraints, and remedies (§ 2599(b); § 2599A; § 2599B(5)).  
- **Elder persons (65+)** and **persons with a disability**, who may trigger enhanced penalties for violations committed against them (§ 2599A(d); § 2599B(2), (4)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Non-VA-accredited individuals and businesses** offering compensated veterans’ benefits claim assistance, due to compensation limits, disclosure/advertising requirements, record retention, and increased liability exposure (§ 2599; § 2599A).  
- **Veterans seeking paid help from non-accredited providers** may face fewer options if providers exit the market rather than comply (§ 2599(a)(4)-(5); § 2599(b)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** The Act takes effect **18 months after its enactment into law** (Section 2).  
- **Record retention deadline:** Providers must retain the written disclosure **during services and for at least 1 year after** the service relationship terminates (§ 2599(b)(1)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (non-VA-accredited providers):**
  - No compensated advising/assistance or referrals in veterans’ benefits matters except as allowed (§ 2599(a)(1)-(2)).  
  - Written, signed fee agreement meeting **38 C.F.R. § 14.636**-like criteria (§ 2599(a)(3)).  
  - Compensation only if it would be allowed under **38 C.F.R. § 14.636**; no excessive/unreasonable fees (§ 2599(a)(4)-(5)).  
  - Mandatory oral + written disclosures at outset; written disclosure in **≥ 12-point font**; client verbal acknowledgment and signature; retain copy through relationship and **≥ 1 year** after termination (§ 2599(b)(1)).  
  - Advertising must include specified disclosure (printed: readily visible, including online; verbal: clear/intelligible) (§ 2599(b)(2)).  
- **Enforcement/remedies:**
  - **Private cause of action** available to victims without requiring prior Attorney General action (§ 2599A(a)).  
  - Violations deemed an **unlawful practice** under § 2513 and Subchapter II of Chapter 25 (§ 2599A(b)).  
  - **Attorneys’ fees** may be awarded to plaintiff if willful violation of a cease-and-desist agreement or court injunction is found (§ 2599A(c)).  
  - Additional civil penalty **not to exceed $10,000 per violation** when committed against an elder person (65+) or person with a disability (§ 2599A(d)).
