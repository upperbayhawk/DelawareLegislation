## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To protect Delaware healthcare providers and others involved in providing **healthcare services lawful in Delaware** from **out-of-state civil actions, investigations, professional discipline, insurance adverse actions, and certain information-sharing requests**, including matters involving **reproductive health services** and **telehealth prescribing for termination of pregnancy**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands and clarifies statutory definitions of **“healthcare services”** and **“reproductive health services,”** expressly including **abortion/termination of pregnancy, emergency contraception, counseling/referrals, and fertility treatment**.  
- Prohibits Delaware medical licensing authorities from treating the **lawful provision/recommendation of reproductive health healthcare services** as **unprofessional conduct**, even when the patient resides in a state where the service is illegal.  
- Limits disclosure in civil proceedings of communications/records relating to **reproductive health healthcare services** unless the patient (or guardian/legal representative) provides **written authorization**, with listed exceptions.  
- Bars disclosure of records to licensing boards or law enforcement **when sought to investigate a provider for care unlawful elsewhere but lawful in Delaware**.  
- Declares certain out-of-state laws authorizing civil actions (including for **aiding/abetting**, **attempts**, **fertility treatment**, and other healthcare services lawful in Delaware) **contrary to Delaware public policy**, and restricts Delaware process/enforcement assistance tied to those actions.  
- Creates a Delaware cause of action allowing a person hit with an **out-of-state judgment** based on lawful Delaware reproductive health healthcare services to recover **damages (including the judgment amount and attorneys’ fees/costs)** from parties who brought/enforced that action (with exceptions).  
- Prohibits **professional liability insurers** from increasing premiums or taking adverse action against providers/organizations for providing lawful **reproductive health healthcare services** to **out-of-state** individuals, including **telehealth prescribing** of medication for termination of pregnancy.  
- Prohibits Delaware state/local officers and employees (including law enforcement) from providing **information or assistance** to federal or other-state law enforcement (or private/quasi-law-enforcement entities) investigating the lawfulness of healthcare services that would be lawful if occurring entirely in Delaware (except as required by federal law).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Provider protection from discipline:** Physicians, physician assistants, and nurses are protected from Delaware professional sanctions for providing/recommending **lawful reproductive health healthcare services** to out-of-state residents.  
- **Stronger confidentiality protections:** Limits civil-action disclosure of reproductive health healthcare communications and exam information absent **written patient authorization**, with defined exceptions.  
- **Reduced exposure to out-of-state civil enforcement:** Declares certain out-of-state civil-action regimes contrary to Delaware public policy and restricts Delaware courts/process from supporting them.  
- **Financial backstop against out-of-state judgments:** Allows recovery of **the out-of-state judgment amount plus costs and reasonable attorneys’ fees** in specified circumstances.  
- **Insurance nondiscrimination:** Prohibits premium increases or other adverse insurer actions based on providing lawful reproductive health healthcare services to out-of-state individuals, including via **telehealth** for medication abortion.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits interjurisdictional cooperation:** State and local personnel (including law enforcement) are prohibited from providing information/assistance to certain external investigations regarding healthcare services lawful in Delaware (except where federal law requires otherwise).  
- **Potential litigation burdens:** Creating a new cause of action to recover damages for certain out-of-state judgments may increase Delaware court litigation related to out-of-state reproductive health disputes.  
- **Constraints on subpoenas/process:** Delaware courts are restricted from issuing/enforcing certain legal processes tied to out-of-state civil actions and some criminal investigations unless the conduct would be a crime in Delaware.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “healthcare services” protections beyond reproductive care:** The bill extends some protections to “any healthcare service… unlawful in another state but lawful in this State,” but the practical boundaries may depend on how “lawful” and “occurred entirely in the State” are applied in specific fact patterns.  
- **Interaction with federal requirements:** Multiple provisions include or imply federal-law carveouts (e.g., “except as required by federal law”), but the bill text does not specify which federal mandates would override Delaware restrictions.  
- **Application to mixed-location conduct:** The out-of-state judgment recovery section excludes cases where **“no part of the acts… occurred in this state,”** but does not further define what counts as acts occurring “in this state” for telehealth or multi-state care.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 24 (Definitions):** Defines **“healthcare services”** broadly and defines **“reproductive health services”** to include:
  - **Abortion** (as defined elsewhere in Title 24),
  - **Termination of pregnancy** (as authorized elsewhere in Title 24),
  - **Emergency contraception** approved by the FDA (OTC, prescription, or dispensed per Ch. 25),
  - Pregnancy/termination-related **medical, surgical, counseling, or referral** services,
  - **Fertility treatment**.  
- **Title 24 (Professional discipline protections):**
  - Physicians: Lawful reproductive health healthcare services are not “unprofessional conduct” even for out-of-state residents where illegal.
  - Physician assistants: Board/Council may not sanction for lawful reproductive health healthcare services under the same cross-border condition.
  - Nurses: Board may not sanction for lawful reproductive health/healthcare services under the same cross-border condition.  
- **Title 10 (§3926A) (Records disclosure limits):**
  - In civil actions/proceedings, prohibits healthcare providers from disclosing specified reproductive health healthcare communications/info unless **written patient authorization** (or guardian/legal representative).
  - Exceptions include negligence litigation by a patient-plaintiff, licensing-board investigations tied to a complaint, and law-enforcement/abuse investigations—**but** adds a limitation: no disclosure under the licensing-board or law-enforcement exceptions if sought to investigate care unlawful elsewhere but lawful in Delaware.  
- **Title 10 (§3928) (Out-of-state civil/criminal action protections):**
  - Declares certain other-state civil-action laws contrary to Delaware public policy (including laws targeting termination of pregnancy, aiding/abetting, attempts, fertility treatment, and other healthcare services lawful in Delaware).
  - Delaware will not apply such other-state laws in Delaware courts and will not issue/enforce specified legal process supporting such actions; criminal-process assistance is limited unless the underlying acts would be a crime in Delaware.  
- **Title 10 (§3929) (Recovery for out-of-state judgments):**
  - Allows a person with an out-of-state judgment based on lawful Delaware reproductive health healthcare services to recover damages from the party that brought/enforced the action, including:
    - the **amount of the out-of-state judgment**, and
    - **costs, expenses, and reasonable attorneys’ fees** defending the out-of-state action and bringing the Delaware action.
  - Excludes certain categories (e.g., certain patient-initiated claims that would similarly exist in Delaware; contract-based claims with a contractual relationship; and cases where no part of the acts occurred in Delaware).  
- **Title 18 (§2535) (Insurance protections):**
  - Prohibits professional liability insurers from increasing premiums or taking adverse action because a provider/organization performs or assists in lawful reproductive health healthcare services for an **out-of-state** individual.
  - Applies to policies covering telehealth prescribing of medication for termination of human pregnancy to an out-of-state patient.  
- **Title 29 (§611) (Non-cooperation provision):**
  - Except as required by federal law, bars state/local officers/employees (including law enforcement) from providing information/assistance to federal or other-state law enforcement, private citizens, or quasi-law-enforcement agencies investigating the lawfulness of healthcare services that would be lawful if occurring entirely in Delaware.

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware healthcare professionals and organizations** providing healthcare services (including reproductive health services) lawful in Delaware.  
- **Patients**, including **out-of-state residents**, receiving lawful Delaware reproductive health healthcare services (including via telehealth as described).  
- Individuals facing **out-of-state judgments** tied to lawful Delaware reproductive health healthcare services who may pursue damages and fee recovery in Delaware.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Out-of-state litigants** seeking to bring or enforce civil actions in Delaware based on another state’s laws targeting abortion/termination-related conduct, fertility treatment, or other healthcare services lawful in Delaware.  
- **External investigating entities** (federal/other-state law enforcement, private citizens, quasi-law-enforcement agencies) seeking information/assistance from Delaware state/local personnel regarding healthcare services lawful in Delaware (subject to federal-law requirements).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Professional licensing limits:** Medical boards/councils **may not impose sanctions** for specified lawful services under the amended provisions.  
- **Record confidentiality compliance:** Healthcare providers are prohibited from disclosing specified reproductive health healthcare communications/records in civil actions without **written authorization**, except under enumerated exceptions; additional prohibition applies to certain investigations targeting care lawful in Delaware but unlawful elsewhere.  
- **Court/process limitations:** Delaware courts/state entities are restricted from issuing/enforcing certain subpoenas, warrants, orders, or other process tied to out-of-state civil actions described, and from assisting certain criminal investigations unless the acts would be a Delaware crime.  
- **Insurance compliance:** Insurers may not raise premiums or take other adverse action on covered professional liability policies for covered lawful conduct.  
- **State/local non-cooperation requirement:** State/local officers and employees may not provide information/assistance in specified investigations/inquiries (except as required by federal law).
