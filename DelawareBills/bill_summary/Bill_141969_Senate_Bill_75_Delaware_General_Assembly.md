## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To limit and standardize the restrictions that Delaware counties may impose on licensed marijuana establishments—especially retail marijuana stores—by creating statewide preemptive rules for county regulation and zoning treatment, including minimum operating hours and specific distance-based siting limits.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Shifts primary “local control” for counties into new § 1351A and narrows § 1351 to apply to **municipalities**.  
- Provides that county rules under new § 1351A **preempt and supersede all existing and future county ordinances/regulations** about operation of licensed marijuana establishments (Title 4, Ch. 13).  
- Requires county ordinances on “time or manner” to allow retail store hours of at least: **Mon–Sat 9:00 a.m.–9:00 p.m.** and **Sun 12:00 p.m.–8:00 p.m.**  
- Requires a county to allow a **medical marijuana compassion center** with a **conversion license** under **§ 1335B** to operate the retail marijuana store as a **nonconforming use**, and restricts denial of related building permits if zoning physical requirements are met.  
- Limits county ability to prohibit **indoor, fully enclosed cultivation** in areas zoned **agricultural or industrial**.  
- Limits county ability to prohibit **retail marijuana stores** in areas zoned **commercial or industrial** except for proximity buffers: **within ½ mile of another retail store** and **within 500 feet** of specified sensitive uses (worship, schools, licensed child care, residential treatment, parks, libraries).  
- Protects currently lawful marijuana establishments by allowing continued operation as a **nonconforming use** if zoning changes or a sensitive use later locates nearby.  
- Allows counties (and municipalities, under their separate authority) to establish **civil penalties** for violations of local ordinances/regulations.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Statewide uniformity for counties:** County rules are expressly **preempted and superseded** by § 1351A, reducing patchwork county regulation for licensed marijuana establishments.  
- **Predictable retail access/hours:** Minimum required hours (**9 a.m.–9 p.m. Mon–Sat; 12 p.m.–8 p.m. Sun**) can reduce county-by-county restrictions on consumer access.  
- **Regulatory certainty for conversion licensees:** Compassion centers converting under **§ 1335B** must be allowed to operate as **nonconforming uses**, addressing the bill’s stated conflict where nonconforming-use rules can bar structural alterations while conversion licensees may need alterations to operate both medical and retail.  
- **Zoning stability for existing operators:** Lawfully operating establishments may continue as **nonconforming uses** after zoning changes or after a nearby sensitive use is established.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced county discretion:** Counties are limited in prohibiting or restricting establishments and are subject to broad **preemption** of county ordinances/regulations on marijuana establishment operations.  
- **Mandatory minimum operating hours:** Counties cannot set more restrictive “time or manner” limits that would reduce hours below the required minimums.  
- **Siting limits may constrain county planning choices:** In commercial/industrial zones, counties may prohibit retail only under the bill’s specified buffers (**½ mile** from another store; **500 feet** from listed sensitive uses), which may be narrower than some counties’ preferred approaches.

## Key Policy Changes
- **§ 1351 revised (municipalities):** Clarifies and conforms language so § 1351 applies to **municipalities** (including municipal authority to prohibit establishments by ordinance or ballot measure, and to regulate time/place/manner/number if not in conflict, and to set civil penalties).  
- **New/expanded § 1351A (counties):**
  - Counties may regulate only with ordinances/regulations **not in conflict** with Title 4, Ch. 13 and commissioner regulations.  
  - **Minimum retail hours requirement:** at least **9 a.m.–9 p.m. (Mon–Sat)** and **12 p.m.–8 p.m. (Sun)**.  
  - **Preemption:** § 1351A **preempts and supersedes** all existing/future county ordinances/regulations on operations of licensed marijuana establishments (notwithstanding specified Title 9 chapters).  
  - **Conversion license protections (§ 1335B):** Counties must allow operation as a **nonconforming use** and may not deny a **building permit** if improvements comply with the physical requirements for that zoning category.  
  - **Cultivation in agricultural/industrial zones:** Counties may not prohibit **indoor, fully enclosed** cultivation facilities in areas zoned agricultural or industrial.  
  - **Retail in commercial/industrial zones:** Counties may not prohibit retail except:
    - **Within ½ mile** of another retail marijuana store; and
    - **Within 500 feet** of: place of worship; school; institution of higher education; licensed child care facility (Title 14, Ch. 30A); residential treatment facility (as defined in Title 16, § 2203); **park**; or **library**.  
  - **Grandfathering/nonconforming continuation:** Lawfully operating establishments may continue as **nonconforming uses** after zoning changes or after a listed sensitive use is established.  
  - Counties may set **civil penalties** for violations of county ordinances/regulations enacted under § 1351A.
- **Definition change (§ 1302):** Adds/updates definition of **“Park”** as government-owned/developed/controlled property for outdoor recreation, excluding development open space recorded on a plan.

## Who Benefits
- **Licensed marijuana businesses** operating or seeking to operate in counties, especially:
  - **Retail marijuana stores** in commercial/industrial zones (subject to the specified buffers and minimum hours).  
  - **Medical compassion centers** receiving **conversion licenses** under **§ 1335B** (nonconforming use treatment and building permit protections).  
  - **Indoor, fully enclosed cultivation facilities** in agricultural/industrial zones.  
- Consumers may benefit indirectly from **minimum operating hours** (as established in the bill text).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **County governments** (and county planning/zoning authorities) may have reduced discretion due to **preemption** and detailed statutory limits on prohibitions and operating-hour restrictions.  
- Nearby property owners or institutions in commercial/industrial areas may see more limited county ability to prevent retail stores beyond the bill’s **½ mile / 500 feet** buffers (beyond those buffers, prohibition is restricted).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Counties:** May establish **civil penalties** for violating county ordinances/regulations enacted under **§ 1351A**.  
- **Municipalities:** May establish **civil penalties** for violating municipal ordinances/regulations governing time/place/manner of marijuana establishment operations under **§ 1351**.  
- **Compliance thresholds (siting/hours):**
  - Minimum retail hours: **Mon–Sat 9:00 a.m.–9:00 p.m.; Sun 12:00 p.m.–8:00 p.m.**

  - Cultivation protection: counties may not prohibit cultivation in agricultural/industrial zones if **indoors and fully enclosed**.
