## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware law to (1) forfeit the State’s contribution to certain public employee pensions when the employee is convicted of a child sexual offense connected to or aided by the employee’s position, and (2) allow courts to attach/assign a portion of the employee’s pension payments to the victim to satisfy restitution, subject to specified percentage caps.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a “Child Abuse Pension Attachment” process allowing a court to assign part of a qualifying offender’s pension to the victim for restitution when the offense was connected to or aided by the offender’s position.  
- Defines “qualifying offense” as a sexual offense (as defined in **11 Del. C. § 761(i)**) committed against a child.  
- Sets pension assignment caps: **up to 25%** of each pension distribution for a **misdemeanor** qualifying offense; **up to 50%** for a **felony** qualifying offense—each “up to the limit of the total restitution owed, until satisfied.”  
- Requires the **Board of Pension Trustees** to promulgate **regulations** to implement court orders issued under the new attachment authority.  
- Expands exceptions to anti-garnishment/anti-assignment pension protections in multiple pension statutes to allow assignments “under **11 Del. C. § 4108**.”  
- Denies an eligible employee the **State’s contribution** to the pension (but allows the employee’s own contributions) when the employee is convicted of a qualifying child sexual offense (or equivalent out-of-state offense) connected to or aided by the employee’s position.  
- Limits the pension-forfeiture provision to convictions occurring **after the effective date of the Act**.  
- Preserves **survivor benefits** for survivors of a person denied a pension under this provision, unless the person has already taken a specified “withdrawal benefit.”

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Supports restitution collection for victims:** Explicitly authorizes courts to divert pension payments to victims to pay restitution, with clear caps (**25% misdemeanor / 50% felony**) and an endpoint (until restitution is satisfied).  
- **Creates a pension-related consequence tied to abuse of public position:** Forfeits the **State’s contribution** when the offense was “in connection with or aided by” the offender’s position.  
- **Maintains some financial protections for survivors:** Survivor benefits remain available after the offender’s death if the offender has not received the withdrawal benefit referenced in **29 Del. C. § 5530**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced retirement benefits for affected employees:** The bill removes entitlement to the **State’s contribution** to the pension for covered convictions (employee keeps only their own contributions).  
- **Pension payments may be diverted for restitution:** Up to **50%** of each distribution (felony) may be assigned to the victim, reducing the offender’s available pension income during the restitution period.  
- **Implementation burden on pension administrators:** The Board of Pension Trustees “must make regulations” to implement court orders, requiring administrative work and ongoing compliance.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates/expands pension attachment for child sexual offenses (11 Del. C. § 4108):**
  - Court may, in its discretion, direct that a covered pension is assignable to the victim for restitution if the court finds by a **preponderance of the evidence** that the offense was committed “in connection with or was aided by” the offender’s pension-qualifying position.
  - Assignment limits:
    - **Misdemeanor:** up to **25%** of each pension distribution, up to the total restitution owed, until satisfied.
    - **Felony:** up to **50%** of each pension distribution, up to the total restitution owed, until satisfied.
  - Requires implementing regulations by the Board of Pension Trustees.
- **Adds pension anti-garnishment exceptions to allow § 4108 assignments:**
  - Amends anti-attachment/anti-assignment provisions in **Title 11, Chapter 88** and **Title 29, Chapters 53, 55, 55A, and 56** to permit assignment/attachment “under **§ 4108 of Title 11**” (in addition to existing Family Court periodic payment order exceptions).
- **Creates pension forfeiture of State contribution for certain convictions (29 Del. C. § 5127):**
  - A person otherwise eligible for a service or disability pension under specified chapters is **not entitled to the State’s contribution**, but may receive **their own contributions**, after the Executive Secretary receives employer certification that:
    1) the person was convicted of a child sexual offense (as defined in **11 Del. C. § 761(i)**) or equivalent out-of-state offense;  
    2) the conviction was based on acts connected to or aided by the offender’s position; and  
    3) the conviction occurred **after the effective date of the Act**.
  - **Survivor benefits** remain payable after death, provided the person has not received the withdrawal benefit under **29 Del. C. § 5530**.

## Who Benefits
- **Victims owed restitution** in qualifying cases, who may receive court-ordered assignment of a portion of the offender’s pension distributions.  
- **Survivors** of a person denied a pension under the forfeiture provision, who may still qualify for survivor benefits (subject to the withdrawal-benefit condition).  
- **Public employers/pension systems (indirectly)** via statutory clarity that § 4108 court orders are permissible exceptions to anti-attachment provisions.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State, judicial, county, or municipal employees** convicted of a qualifying child sexual offense connected to or aided by their position:
  - may **forfeit the State’s contribution** to their pension; and/or
  - may have **25% (misdemeanor) or 50% (felony)** of each pension distribution assigned to the victim until restitution is paid.
- **Pension administrators/Board of Pension Trustees** due to required regulation-making and administration of court-ordered assignments.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Court-ordered pension assignment for restitution (11 Del. C. § 4108):**
  - Requires a **court finding by a preponderance of the evidence** that the offense was connected to or aided by the offender’s position.
  - Assignment capped at **25%** (misdemeanor) or **50%** (felony) per distribution, limited to restitution owed, “until satisfied.”
- **Employer certification for pension forfeiture (29 Del. C. § 5127):**
  - The Executive Secretary must receive **written notification** from the employer (or former employer) certifying the required elements (conviction, nexus to position, and conviction date after the Act’s effective date) before the State contribution is denied.
- **Administrative compliance:** The **Board of Pension Trustees must issue regulations** to implement and process court orders under the new pension attachment authority.
