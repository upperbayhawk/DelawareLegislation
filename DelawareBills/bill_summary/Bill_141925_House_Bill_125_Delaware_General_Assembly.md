## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 14 § 4137A to expand the Free School Meals Program so that **all attending students** in participating public schools can receive **free breakfast and free lunch**, and require the **Delaware Department of Education (DOE)** to **reimburse schools** for meal costs not covered by federal reimbursements.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **public schools participating** in the **School Breakfast Program (SBP)** to offer **free breakfast to any attending student**, regardless of household income.  
- Requires **public schools participating** in the **National School Lunch Program (NSLP)** to offer **free lunch to any attending student**, regardless of household income.  
- Limits free meals to **“a maximum of 1 free meal for each meal service period.”**  
- DOE must reimburse participating public schools each budget year by a formula: **(federal free reimbursement rate × eligible meals served) − (total federal reimbursements received for those meals under SBP/NSLP).**  
- Adds/updates definitions, including references to federal definitions: **Breakfast (7 C.F.R. § 220.2)** and **Lunch (7 C.F.R. § 210.2)**, and defines **“eligible meal.”**  
- **Effective date is contingent on funding**: takes effect **30 days after** publication in the **Register of Regulations** of a **Controller General notice that funds have been appropriated**.  
- Fiscal note estimates State costs of **$45,538,434 (FY 2026)**, **$47,202,855 (FY 2027)**, and **$48,931,013 (FY 2028)**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Universal access:** The bill text and synopsis state it expands free meals to **all public school students** at participating schools, regardless of income.  
- **Predictable reimbursement mechanism:** The statute specifies a **DOE reimbursement formula** tied to federal rates and actual meals served (budget-year reconciliation approach).  
- **Participation guardrails:** The bill retains a clear limit of **1 free meal per meal service period**, which may help schools manage distribution and program integrity.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Significant State cost:** Fiscal note projects new State expenditures of **$45.54M in FY 2026**, increasing to **$48.93M by FY 2028**.  
- **Administrative complexity:** Reimbursement depends on calculating (1) total eligible meals served and (2) federal reimbursements received, which requires accurate tracking and reporting for each participating school.  
- **Delayed implementation tied to appropriations:** The program expansion does not start until after a specific funding-and-notice process occurs (see Effective Dates).
