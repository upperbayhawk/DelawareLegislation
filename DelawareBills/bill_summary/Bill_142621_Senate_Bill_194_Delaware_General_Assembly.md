## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Establish statewide protections for nonexempt employees working in covered warehouse distribution centers related to **warehouse production quotas**, including **quota disclosure**, **limits on quotas that interfere with meal/rest breaks, bathroom use, or safety laws**, and **protections against retaliation**, with enforcement authority granted to the Delaware Department of Labor and coordination with the Department of Health and Social Services.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Applies to **nonexempt employees** at covered “warehouse distribution centers” and to employers with **100+ employees at a single warehouse distribution center** or **1,000+ employees across one or more warehouse distribution centers in Delaware**.
- Requires employers to give employees a **written description of each quota** (including tasks/material amounts, time period, and potential adverse actions) **upon hire or within 30 days of the chapter’s effective date**.
- Prohibits requiring or enforcing quotas that prevent compliance with **meal/rest periods**, **bathroom use (including reasonable travel time)**, or **occupational health and safety laws/Department standards**.
- Creates employee rights to request quota information and **the most recent 90 days of the employee’s personal work speed data**; employer must respond **within 21 calendar days** (former employees limited to **one request**).
- Establishes a **rebuttable presumption of unlawful retaliation** if adverse action occurs **within 90 days** after an employee’s first quota/data request in a calendar year or after a related complaint.
- Expands Department of Labor enforcement tools (inspections, subpoenas, investigations) and authorizes civil actions in Superior Court; prevailing enforcement actions allow **injunctive relief** plus **costs and reasonable attorney’s fees**.
- Requires the Secretary of Labor to **report to the General Assembly by January 1, 2028** on claims, quota data tied to high-injury warehouses, and investigations/enforcement actions.
- Fiscal note estimates added staffing and costs: **2.0 FTEs** and new operational/marketing/start-up costs; **FY2027 one-time $54,600** and **FY2027 ongoing $187,069** (plus outyear estimates).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved break and safety compliance:** Employers cannot require quotas that prevent **meal/rest periods**, **bathroom access**, or compliance with **occupational health and safety laws/Department standards** (§3904).
- **Transparency on performance expectations:** Written quota disclosures are required **upon hire** (or **within 30 days** after effective date) (§3903).
- **Access to work speed data:** Employees can obtain their **own personal work speed data for the most recent 90 days** to evaluate quota impacts (§3905(a)-(b)).
- **Stronger anti-retaliation leverage:** A **90-day rebuttable presumption** supports employees who request data or complain (§3906).
- **Enhanced enforcement capacity:** Department may investigate, enter workplaces, inspect/copy records, and issue subpoenas (§3908(d)); Secretary can seek civil enforcement and penalties via Superior Court (§3908(d)(3)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Compliance and administrative burden on employers:** Employers must prepare written quota descriptions and timely respond to data requests **within 21 calendar days** (§3903; §3905(d)).
- **Increased exposure to enforcement and litigation:** Secretary may bring civil actions; employees may sue for injunctive relief; courts may award **costs and reasonable attorney’s fees** to prevailing parties in specified actions (§3908(e); §3909).
- **State administrative cost increase:** Fiscal note projects new staffing and program costs for the Department of Labor (see Budget Impact).

## Health Impact
- The bill links quota practices to increased risk of **injury and illness** in its findings (§3901) and:
  - Prohibits quotas that prevent compliance with **occupational health and safety laws/standards** (§3904).
  - Directs coordinated enforcement with the Department of Health and Social Services and allows Secretary access to **employer-reported injury data** (§3908(a)(1)).
  - Requires the Secretary to consider investigations where a worksite/employer has an annual injury rate **≥ 1.5 times** the warehousing industry’s average annual injury rate (§3908(b)).

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates a new **Warehouse Worker Protection Act** in Title 19, Chapter 39.
- Defines covered employers using size thresholds: **100+ employees at a single center** or **1,000+ employees statewide across centers** (§3902(5)).
- Requires **written quota disclosure** to employees **upon hire** or **within 30 days** of the chapter’s effective date (§3903).
- Prohibits quotas that interfere with **meal/rest periods**, **bathroom access (including travel time)**, or **occupational health and safety laws/Department standards**; bars adverse action tied to such quotas or undisclosed quotas (§3904).
- Provides a right to request quota descriptions and **90 days** of the employee’s work speed data; employer response deadline **21 calendar days**; former employees limited to **one request** (§3905).
- Establishes a **90-day rebuttable presumption of retaliation** following certain requests/complaints (§3906).
- Grants enforcement powers and rulemaking authority to the Secretary/Department, including inspections, subpoenas, and Superior Court civil actions (§3908).
- Allows employees to sue for **injunctive relief** and recover **costs and reasonable attorney’s fees** if they prevail (§3909).
- Requires a legislative report by **January 1, 2028** (§3908(a)(3)).
- States the Act **does not preempt** local ordinances providing equal or greater protections (§3911).

## Who Benefits
- **Nonexempt warehouse distribution center employees** covered by the Act, especially those subject to quotas, through disclosure rights, data access, break/safety protections, and anti-retaliation provisions.
- State enforcement agencies (Department of Labor) gain clearer authority and tools to investigate and enforce quota-related protections.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Covered warehouse employers** (meeting the 100/1,000 employee thresholds) may face increased compliance obligations, record production demands, investigations, and potential litigation exposure.
- State government budgets (Department of Labor) due to added staffing and operational costs.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Employer compliance requirements:**
  - Provide **written quota descriptions** with required elements (§3903).
  - Do not require/enforce quotas that impede breaks, bathroom use, or safety compliance; do not take adverse action for failing to meet such quotas or undisclosed quotas (§3904).
  - Provide requested quota descriptions and **90 days** of the employee’s work speed data (if monitored) within **21 calendar days**; former employees limited to **one request** (§3905).
- **Enforcement mechanisms:**
  - Department may **enter workplaces**, inspect/copy records, take depositions, and issue **subpoenas** (§3908(d)(1)).
  - Secretary may **adopt regulations** for complaint procedures (§3908(c)).
  - Secretary may **commence civil actions in Superior Court** for violations and to seek civil penalties (§3908(d)(3)).
  - Courts in successful Secretary actions must grant **injunctive relief** and award **costs and reasonable attorney’s fees** (§3908(e)).
  - Employees may sue for **injunctive relief** for compliance with §§3903–3906 and recover **costs and reasonable attorney’s fees** if they prevail; in certain cases injunctive relief is limited to **suspension of the quota and any adverse action resulting from its enforcement** (§3909).
  - Attorney General retains independent authority to prosecute civil or criminal actions for violations (§3910).
- **Penalties:** Civil penalties are referenced, but **specific penalty amounts are not specified in the source text** (§3908(d)(3)).
