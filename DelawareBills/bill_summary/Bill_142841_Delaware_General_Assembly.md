## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Designate **February 28, 2026** as **“Rare Disease Day”** in the State of Delaware to recognize and raise awareness of rare diseases and the people affected.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Recognizes **February 28, 2026** as **“Rare Disease Day”** in Delaware (House Concurrent Resolution No. 93).
- Cites that there are **nearly 7,000** rare diseases/conditions, each affecting **fewer than 200,000 Americans**.
- Notes rare diseases collectively affect **almost 30 million Americans** and that **nearly 1 in 10 Americans** have a rare disease.
- States only **about 500** rare diseases have **approved treatments** in the U.S. (per NIH), while the FDA has approved **more than 450** drugs/biologics for rare diseases.
- Highlights common challenges for affected individuals/families: **diagnosis delays**, difficulty finding specialists, and **lack of access** to treatments/services.
- References Delaware’s creation of a **Rare Disease Advisory Council** in **July 2023** via **SB 55** to raise awareness and make policy recommendations.
- Connects the resolution to the broader national observance of **Rare Disease Day** (noting a nationwide observance on **February 29, 2024**).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Public awareness and recognition:** Official designation may help elevate attention to rare diseases and the needs of patients and families (resolution’s stated recognition and “WHEREAS” findings).
- **Support for advocacy and education:** Aligns with the stated role of Delaware’s Rare Disease Advisory Council to **educate** the public, agencies, and the General Assembly about rare diseases.
- **Visibility for access challenges:** The resolution explicitly acknowledges barriers like **diagnosis delay** and **difficulty accessing experts and treatments**, potentially helping focus stakeholders on these issues.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Symbolic only:** The resolution designates a day of recognition but does **not** create new programs, benefits, or requirements in the text.
- **No direct service expansion:** Although it describes unmet treatment needs, it does not provide funding, coverage mandates, or access improvements.
