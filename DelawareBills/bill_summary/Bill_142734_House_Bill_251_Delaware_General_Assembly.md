## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 9 to restructure New Castle County Council governance by (1) increasing the Council from 12 to **13** district seats after the **2030** census, (2) ending the **countywide at-large election** of the Council President and instead having the Council **elect its President from among its members**, and (3) conforming vacancy and redistricting provisions to the new structure.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands New Castle County Council to **13 members**, all elected **from council districts**, starting with the **2032 general election**.  
- Requires the County Council to reapportion the County into **13 districts** using the **2030 U.S. Decennial Census**, completed **on or before January 1, 2032**.  
- Changes Council President selection: **after the 2032 general election**, the Council **elects the President by majority vote** from among its members (no more at-large President election).  
- Adjusts Council term structure after 2032: members from **districts 1–6** elected in **2032** serve until after the **2034** general election; members from **districts 7–13** serve until after the **2036** general election (then continuing **every 4 years** thereafter by cohort).  
- Extends terms for members in **districts 7–12** elected in **2028** (and certain special elections) so those terms end after the **2032** general election (first Wednesday in November following that election).  
- Updates redistricting commission rules for the post-2030 cycle: commission of **13 qualified voters**, with a **party-affiliation cap** (no more than **7** from the same political party) and **population deviation** limit (no more than **15%** from district average).  
- Removes provisions for **special elections to fill a vacancy in the Office of President** (consistent with moving to an internally elected President).  
- Staggered effective dates: some sections effective **immediately upon enactment**; others effective **November 9, 2032**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Aligns President selection with a district-based council structure:** After the **2032 general election**, the President is elected by **majority vote** of Council members, eliminating the separate **at-large** contest (Section 2).  
- **Adds representation by increasing districts from 12 to 13:** All Council members must be elected from districts beginning with **2032** (Section 1(f)).  
- **Creates a clear redistricting schedule and deadline:** Requires reapportionment using the **2030 Census** and completion **by January 1, 2032** (Section 1(g)).  
- **Codifies commission composition and guardrails:** Requires a **13-member** Redistricting Commission, with no more than **7** from the same political party and a **15%** maximum population deviation per district (Section 4).  
- **Streamlines handling of a President vacancy:** Removes the need for a separate President special election and instead allows Council to elect a President from among its members (Sections 2 and Synopsis).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Eliminates direct countywide voting for Council President:** The President is no longer elected “at large” by voters after the 2032 transition (Section 2; Section 6 removes countywide ballot language effective 2032).  
- **Adds complexity to election/term transitions:** The bill creates different term end dates for districts **1–6** vs. **7–13** following the **2032** general election (Section 1(g)(1)-(2)).  
- **Extends certain current terms:** Members from districts **7–12** elected in **2028** have terms extended to end after the **2032** general election (Section 1(e)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Date specificity for “first Wednesday in November following the [year] general election”:** The bill uses this phrase for multiple term end dates but does not list calendar dates for those Wednesdays (Section 1(e), (g), (h)).  
- **How the Council will select the President immediately after 2032:** The bill requires a majority vote from among members but does not specify timing (e.g., at organization meeting) beyond “Following the 2032 general election” (Section 2(a)(2)).  
- **Interaction with existing provisions on vacancies after removing President special election language:** The bill removes President-vacancy special election provisions but does not restate a full alternative process within §1147 beyond referencing a Council election “following any special election conducted under § 1147” (Section 2(a)(2)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Council size and districts**
  - Council becomes **13 members**, all from **council districts**, effective with the **2032 general election** and thereafter (Section 1(f)).  
  - Requires reapportionment into **13 districts** using the **2030 U.S. Decennial Census**, completed **on/before January 1, 2032** (Section 1(g)).  

- **Term adjustments / staggering**
  - Members in districts **7–12** elected in **2028** (and certain special elections through before 2032) have terms ending the **first Wednesday in November following the 2032 general election** (Section 1(e)).  
  - Members elected in **2032**:
    - Districts **1–6**: terms end the **first Wednesday in November following the 2034 general election**, and **every 4 years thereafter** (Section 1(g)(1)).  
    - Districts **7–13**: terms end the **first Wednesday in November following the 2036 general election**, and **every 4 years thereafter** (Section 1(g)(2)).  
  - Elected officials take office the **first Wednesday in November following the general election** (Section 1(h)).  

- **Council President selection**
  - Until the transition tied to post-2030 redistricting, the President is elected at-large as previously provided (Section 2(a)(1), as amended).  
  - **Following the 2032 general election**, the Council elects a President **by majority vote** from among its members (Section 2(a)(2)).  
  - Council continues to elect a **President pro tempore** by majority vote (Section 2(b)).  

- **Vacancies**
  - Removes provisions dealing with **special elections to fill a vacancy in the Office of President** of County Council (Section 3; Synopsis).  
  - Retains special-election timing rules for vacancies of **Council Members other than President** (generally **within 30 days** after proclamation posting; with specified exceptions) (Section 3(e)).  

- **Redistricting Commission changes**
  - After the **2030** census and each decennial census thereafter, Council must appoint a **13-member** Redistricting Commission **within 60 days** after official census reporting (Section 4(a)(3)).  
  - For the post-2030 cycle: appointments are **1 from each of the 12 council districts and 1 at-large**; thereafter **1 from each of 13 districts** (Section 4(a)(3)).  
  - Commission membership limits: members cannot be employed by the county in another capacity; no more than **7** members affiliated with the same political party (Section 4(a)(3)).  
  - Commission deadline: report due **within 90 days** after appointment (Section 4(a)(3)).  
  - District population deviation limit: no district may deviate more than **15%** from the average population (computed by dividing total county population by **13**) (Section 4(a)(3)b.).  
  - Council must adopt ordinance **within 60 days** after the report is filed; if not adopted/approved within **60 days**, the commission plan becomes effective automatically (Section 4(d)).  

- **Repeals / conforming changes**
  - Repeals §1166(b) regarding officials elected to “district 13” (Section 5).  
  - Amends §334 to remove language requiring the New Castle County President be elected “by a county wide ballot” (effective 2032) (Section 6).

## Who Benefits
- **Voters in New Castle County**: gain an additional district seat (moving from **12** to **13** districts) beginning with the **2032 general election** (Section 1(f)).  
- **County Council members**: gain authority to elect the **Council President** from among themselves after **2032** (Section 2(a)(2)).  
- **Redistricting stakeholders**: benefit from explicit commission structure, timelines (60/90/60 days), party-affiliation cap (**7**), and population deviation limit (**15%**) (Section 4).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Voters who prefer direct election of the Council President**: the at-large election for President is removed after the **2032** transition (Section 2(a)(2); Section 6).  
- **Candidates for a countywide Council President race**: that standalone elected office is effectively eliminated after the change to an internally elected President (Section 2(a)(2)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective immediately upon enactment:** **Sections 1, 2, and 4** (Section 7).  
- **Effective November 9, 2032:** **Sections 3, 5, and 6** (Section 7).  
- **Redistricting/reapportionment deadline:** reapportionment into **13 districts** must be completed **on or before January 1, 2032** (Section 1(g)).  
- **Redistricting Commission appointment deadline:** **within 60 days** after official census reporting by the President of the United States to Congress (Section 4(a)(3)).  
- **Commission report deadline:** **within 90 days** after appointment (Section 4(a)(3)).  
- **Council action deadline on commission plan:** ordinance adoption within **60 days** after the report is filed; otherwise the commission plan becomes effective (Section 4(d)).  
- **Vacancy special election timing (non-President council seats):** generally **no more than 30 days** after proclamation posting, with scheduling exceptions (Section 3(e)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Mandatory duty to redistrict after each decennial census** (Section 4(a)).  
- **Automatic effectiveness mechanism:** If Council and County Executive do not adopt/approve a redistricting ordinance within **60 days** after the commission report is filed, the commission plan **becomes effective as if adopted and approved** (Section 4(d)).  
- **No penalties specified** beyond the automatic effect of the commission plan and the statutory “mandatory duty” language.
