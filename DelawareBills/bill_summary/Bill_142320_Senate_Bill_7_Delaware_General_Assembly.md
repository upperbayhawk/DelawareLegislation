## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Modernize Delaware’s probation system by requiring courts, the Board of Parole, and probation/parole officers to use the least restrictive special conditions and minimally intrusive reporting needed to meet supervision goals; limit probation lengths; expand administrative requirements around programs and reporting; remove marriage-license restrictions tied to probation/parole status; and discharge certain house-arrest service-fee balances.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates statutory definitions for **“criminogenic needs”** and **“goals of supervision”** (deterrence, community protection, victim protection, rehabilitation).  
- Requires that, unless an offense mandates specific conditions, courts/Board of Parole/officers **presumptively impose the least number and least restrictive special conditions**, and document the **reasonable relation** to criminogenic needs/goals.  
- Bars imposing special conditions a person **cannot reasonably comply with**, and requires consideration of the person’s **resources and ability** to comply (unless mandated by offense).  
- Directs **minimally intrusive reporting**, including **remote reporting options** when appropriate; reporting times/locations must reasonably accommodate **work, education, childcare, healthcare, and transportation** constraints.  
- Requires facilitation of **referrals** when programming is a supervision requirement; limits ability to extend probation when referral facilitation was not reasonable; and sets court considerations if required programs are unavailable.  
- Limits probation/suspended sentence length to **2 years (violent felony)**, **18 months (Title 16 offenses)**, or **1 year (all others)**, with specified exceptions (sex offenses, some violent felonies, restitution collection, and up to **90-day** treatment-completion extensions with findings).  
- Updates earned compliance credit administration, including requiring DOC to give **written notice** of eligibility and eligibility date; retains exclusions (e.g., **violent felonies** and **sexual offenses**).  
- Repeals Title 13 provisions that restricted/prohibited marriage for individuals **on probation or parole** and required official consent to marry.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduced supervision burden where not necessary:** The “least restrictive” and “least number” special-condition requirements (unless offense-mandated) may reduce unnecessary conditions and align conditions to stated supervision goals and criminogenic needs.  
- **Improved ability to maintain employment/education/healthcare:** Reporting must be set to accommodate **verifiable work schedules** and considerations including **education, childcare, healthcare, and transportation**; remote reporting is explicitly allowed when appropriate.  
- **More structured response before sanctions:** Officers must make **reasonable efforts** to understand noncompliance and attempt to **re-engage** someone who fails to report before deeming a violation, and connect individuals to needed services/resources before pursuing sanctions (including revocation).  
- **Clearer limits on probation length:** Statutory caps (**2 years / 18 months / 1 year**) and concurrency rules can reduce prolonged supervision periods, subject to enumerated exceptions.  
- **Eliminates marriage-related collateral restriction:** Repeal of the probation/parole marriage-consent prohibition removes a legal barrier tied to supervision status.  
- **Discharges certain outstanding house-arrest service-fee balances:** As of the effective date, outstanding balances for the repealed **§ 4332(b) service fee** (including fees, costs, interest, or other charges assessed on it) are discharged.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Implementation cost to DOC systems:** DOC estimates a **one-time $245,000 cost in FY 2026** to modify the **Delaware Automated Correction System (DACS)** to support required enhancements/modifications for compliance-driven supervision management.  
- **Added documentation/decision steps:** For each special condition, the court/Board/officer must **state the reasonable relation** to criminogenic needs/goals and must consider whether conditions are more restrictive than necessary and whether fewer/less restrictive conditions would suffice—potentially increasing administrative workload.  
- **Limits on extending probation when programming referrals are not facilitated:** If the court finds on the record that the Board/officer did not reasonably facilitate a program referral, **a longer period of probation/suspension is prohibited**, which may constrain courts’ options in some cases.  
- **Discharge of service-fee balances reduces collections:** Discharging outstanding balances eliminates recovery of those amounts (no dollar estimate provided in the bill text).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- **Scope and amount of discharged house-arrest service-fee balances:** The bill discharges balances but does not quantify total balances or define the affected population size.  
- **Which “other categories of offenses” may be excluded from earned compliance credit by DOC rules:** Statute references exclusions “set forth in the rules and regulations adopted by DOC” without listing them in the bill text.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New definitions (Title 11 § 4302):**
  - **Criminogenic needs** (dynamic factors/conditions addressable with evidence-based interventions to reduce recidivism).  
  - **Goals of supervision** (deterrence, community protection, victim protection, rehabilitation).
- **Least restrictive conditions (Title 11 § 4321 & § 4332):**
  - Unless mandated by offense, probation/parole officers and courts/Board must use **least restrictive** special conditions necessary to achieve supervision goals and presumptively impose the **least number** of special conditions.  
  - Special conditions must be **reasonably related** to criminogenic needs/goals, not more restrictive than reasonably necessary, and decision-makers must **state the reasonable relation** for each special condition.  
  - Prohibits imposing special conditions the person **cannot reasonably comply with**; requires considering resources/ability to comply (unless mandated by offense).
- **Minimally intrusive reporting (Title 11 § 4332):**
  - Reporting requirements must be as **minimally-intrusive as possible**; may allow **remote reporting**; must accommodate work/education/childcare/healthcare/transportation considerations; may use audio/visual or audio-only communication; may excuse in-person visits as a graduated incentive.
- **Programming requirements (Title 11 § 4332):**
  - If programming is required, Board/officer must **facilitate a referral**.  
  - If a required program is not available and a VOP is filed, court must consider **totality of circumstances** in deciding whether to establish the violation.  
  - If the sentencing court finds on the record that referral was not reasonably facilitated, **a longer probation/suspended sentence is prohibited**.  
  - DOC is **not required** to contract for or create a program just because it is required as a supervision condition.
- **House arrest program standards (Title 11 § 4332):**
  - House arrest programs must include **clear and consistent incentives and sanctions** for non-compliance and require that DOC **owns or leases all equipment**.  
  - DOC may use electronic monitoring and emerging monitoring technology **as reasonably necessary**, and may supervise without specific equipment if sufficient reasonable compliance methods are used.
- **Probation length limits and exceptions (Title 11 § 4333):**
  - Caps: **2 years (violent felony)**; **18 months (Title 16 offense)**; **1 year (all others)**.  
  - Multiple cases: consecutive probation cannot exceed caps; excess is deemed **concurrent**, except for offenses committed while already on probation/suspended sentence.  
  - Exceptions allow longer periods with on-the-record findings for: **sexual offenses** (as defined in § 761), certain **violent felonies** (public safety enhanced), and **restitution collection** (must be **Accountability Level I — Restitution Only** for the excess).  
  - For **Accountability Level I – Restitution Only**, probation level may not be increased based on **reasonable inability to pay**.  
  - Up to **90-day** extensions for incomplete court-ordered substance abuse treatment, with hearing and specific findings.
- **Earned compliance credit updates (Title 11 § 4383):**
  - Up to **30 days credit for 30 days compliance**, not to exceed **1/2** of probation period.  
  - Forfeiture upon **new conviction**; may be forfeited upon **revocation**.  
  - Not available for **sexual offenses**, **violent felonies**, **restitution-only probation**, and other categories set by DOC rules.  
  - DOC must provide **written notice** of eligibility and eligibility date when calculated.
- **Marriage restrictions repealed (Title 13 §§ 101, 113):**
  - Repeals prohibition/voidability trigger and consent-to-marry process for individuals on probation/parole.  
- **Discharge of house-arrest service-fee balances (Section 11):**
  - As of effective date, outstanding balances owed for service fees under **repealed § 4332(b)** (including fees/costs/interest/other charges assessed on that service fee) are **discharged**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Mandatory decision-making standards:** Courts/Board/officers must apply “least restrictive/least number” special-condition standards (unless offense mandates specific conditions) and must make specific considerations (resources/ability to comply; relation to criminogenic needs/goals) and **state the reasonable relation** for each special condition.  
- **Reporting requirements compliance:** Officers must set reporting times/locations to accommodate listed considerations and may use remote/audio/video methods.  
- **Pre-sanction efforts:** Before pursuing sanctions up to and including revocation for an alleged VOP, officers must make **reasonable efforts** to inquire into reasons for noncompliance, attempt to re-engage non-reporters, and connect individuals with services/resources.  
- **Treatment-related extension requirements:** Any probation extension (up to **90 days**) for incomplete substance abuse treatment must be preceded by a **hearing** and **on-the-record findings** meeting statutory criteria.  
- **Record-access relief:** Individuals denied access to certain DOC documents under § 4322(c) or (d) may seek relief under **29 Del. C. § 10005**.
