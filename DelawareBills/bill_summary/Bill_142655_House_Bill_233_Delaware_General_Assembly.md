## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 26 of the Delaware Code to define “large energy use facility” and require Commission-regulated electric utilities to create a separate retail electricity service classification and rate (tariff) for those facilities to reduce the risk that infrastructure and reliability costs are shifted to other customers.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines a **“large energy use facility”** as a facility that **uses or is able to use 20 megawatts (MW) or more** and is primarily engaged in **NAICS 2022 code 518210** (data processing, hosting, and related services).  
- Requires **Commission-regulated utilities** to create a **separate and distinct service classification** and **separate tariff schedule** for large energy use facilities (separate from other commercial/industrial classes).  
- Directs that rates for this class must **allocate or directly assign costs** to the large energy use facility class/customer in a way **equal or proportional to the costs of serving** them.  
- Requires the rate design to **mitigate risk** that other customer classes pay **unwarranted costs** or experience **unwarranted cost shifting** tied to serving large energy use facilities (including costs to meet load requirements).  
- Requires the rate schedule to **mitigate risk of jeopardizing grid reliability** in Delaware and the **PJM** region.  
- In reviewing/approving a utility’s proposed rate schedule, the **Public Service Commission (PSC)** must examine specified factors including impacts on other customers, grid benefits, and consistency with **renewable energy targets (§ 354, Title 26)** and **greenhouse gas reduction policy (§ 10003, Title 7)**.  
- **Deadline:** Regulated electric utilities must file an application to establish the required rates **within 180 days of the effective date**.  
- **Effective date:** **Upon enactment**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Customer protection from cost shifting:** The bill expressly requires rates to **mitigate the risk** that residential, small business, and other customers pay **unwarranted costs** associated with serving large energy use facilities (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(2)-(3)).  
- **Cost causation alignment:** Requires costs of serving large energy use facilities to be **allocated proportionally** to that class or **directly assigned** to a large energy use customer (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(1)).  
- **Grid reliability focus:** Requires rate design to address risks of **jeopardizing grid reliability** in Delaware and PJM (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(4)).  
- **Policy alignment with clean energy targets:** PSC review includes whether rates **impede** or **support** meeting renewable energy targets under **§ 354** and greenhouse gas reduction policies including **7 Del. C. § 10003** (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(3)-(4)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential higher rates for covered facilities:** Because the bill requires costs to be allocated/assigned to large energy use facilities and to mitigate cost shifting, large energy use facilities may face **higher or more specifically targeted charges** than under general commercial/industrial rates (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(1)-(3)).  
- **Administrative complexity for utilities and PSC:** Creating a new class and tariff and conducting PSC review against multiple criteria adds regulatory workload (26 Del. C. § 316(a)-(b); application required within **180 days**).  
- **Scope limited to a specific industry code:** The definition applies only to facilities primarily engaged in **NAICS 518210**, even if other industries also create similar large-load impacts; this may limit the policy’s applicability (26 Del. C. § 102(7)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Meaning of “able to use 20 megawatts or more”:** The bill does not specify whether this is based on interconnection capacity, contracted demand, nameplate capacity, peak usage, or other metric (26 Del. C. § 102(7)).  
- **How “equal or proportional” cost allocation will be determined:** The bill sets a standard but does not define the cost methodology, cost categories, or evidentiary requirements (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(1)).  
- **What qualifies as “unwarranted” costs or “unwarranted” cost shifting:** The bill uses these terms but does not define them (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(2)-(3); § 316(b)(1)).  
- **What “equitable contributions” means in practice:** PSC must examine whether rates provide “equitable contributions to grid efficiency, reliability, and resiliency benefits,” but the bill does not specify metrics or minimum contributions (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(2)).  
- **Commission “public interest” conditions:** PSC may require “any other conditions … in the public interest,” without further limits in the bill text (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(5)).  
- **Whether and how legacy vs. new facilities are treated:** The bill requires a class and rates for large energy use facilities but does not specify transition rules for existing customers vs. new interconnections.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/updated definition:** “Large energy use facility” means a facility that **uses or is able to use 20 MW or more** and is primarily engaged in providing a service under **NAICS 2022 code 518210** (26 Del. C. § 102(7)).  
- **Mandated separate rate class and tariff:** Commission-regulated utilities must create a **separate classification** for retail electricity consumers that are large energy use facilities, with its **own tariff schedule** (26 Del. C. § 316(a)).  
- **Rate design requirements for that class:** Rates must:
  - Allocate/directly assign costs **equal or proportional** to service costs (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(1));  
  - Mitigate risk other customer classes pay **unwarranted costs** (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(2));  
  - Mitigate **unwarranted cost shifting**, including costs to meet load requirements due to serving the facility (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(3));  
  - Mitigate risk of jeopardizing **grid reliability** in Delaware and PJM (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(4)).  
- **PSC approval factors:** PSC must examine whether rates:
  - Could increase costs or unwarranted risk to other customers (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(1));  
  - Provide equitable contributions to grid efficiency/reliability/resiliency benefits (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(2));  
  - Impede meeting renewables and GHG reduction policies (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(3));  
  - Allow procurement/contracts supporting renewables and GHG reduction (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(4));  
  - Meet other PSC public-interest conditions (26 Del. C. § 316(b)(5)).

## Who Benefits
- **Residential and small business electric customers** and other non-covered customer classes, to the extent the new rate design mitigates “unwarranted” cost shifting from large energy use facilities (bill findings; 26 Del. C. § 316(a)(2)-(3), (b)(1)).  
- **Grid reliability interests statewide and in PJM**, due to the requirement to mitigate reliability risk (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(4)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Large energy use facilities (≥ 20 MW) in NAICS 518210** (e.g., data centers) that may be assigned a greater share of infrastructure and service costs through the separate class and tariff (26 Del. C. § 316(a)(1)-(3)).  
- **Commission-regulated utilities**, due to required tariff development and filing within a fixed timeframe (Section 3).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines

- **Utility filing deadline:** Regulated electric utilities must file an application to establish the required rates **within 180 days of the effective date** (Section 3).
