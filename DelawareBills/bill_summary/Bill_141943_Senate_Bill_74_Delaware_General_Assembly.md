## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Lease-Purchase Agreement Act (Chapter 76, Title 6) to (1) add a definition for “lease-purchase property” and (2) clarify how required item price disclosures must be provided for online lease-purchase offers and for items offered for lease-purchase that are not owned by the lessor (third-party inventory).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Defines **“lease-purchase property”** as personal property **owned by the lessor** when it is **physically displayed and offered** for lease-purchase and **before** any lease-purchase agreement is executed.  
- Requires item-level price disclosures (cash price; lease payment amount; total lease payments to acquire ownership) to be **clearly and conspicuously indicated in Arabic numerals** and readable by visual inspection for **every item of lease-purchase property** displayed or offered.  
- Allows lessors to meet the item-level disclosure requirement for **online** lease-purchase property (where the consumer can enter an agreement online/remotely) via **electronic disclosure**, if shown clearly/conspicuously in Arabic numerals.  
- Requires that when a lessor offers **personal property that is not “lease-purchase property”** (i.e., not owned by the lessor), the lessor **must** provide the same item-level information via **electronic disclosure**.  
- Sets a sequencing requirement: the electronic disclosures under these new provisions must be given **before** the disclosures required by **§ 7603**.  
- Maintains existing advertising rules, including prohibitions on misleading pricing/terms and required statements when ads mention payment amounts or ownership.  
- The Act also makes **technical drafting corrections** to conform with the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual.  
- Approved **May 22, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved price transparency at the item level:** Requires disclosure of **cash price**, **lease payment amount**, and **total lease payments necessary to acquire ownership** for each displayed/offered item (§ 7606(c)).  
- **Clarifies compliance for e-commerce:** Explicitly allows item-level disclosures for online lease-purchase property to be delivered **electronically** (§ 7606(e)), aligning requirements with remote transactions.  
- **Addresses third-party inventory offers:** Ensures consumers still receive item-level pricing information even when the lessor does **not** own the item (must be disclosed electronically) (§ 7606(f)).  
- **Earlier disclosure timing:** Requires these electronic item-level disclosures to occur **before** § 7603 disclosures, potentially ensuring consumers see core pricing info earlier in the process (§ 7606(e), (f)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added compliance steps for lessors offering non-owned inventory:** When property is **not** “lease-purchase property,” the lessor **must** provide item price disclosures electronically (§ 7606(f)), which may require system/process changes.  
- **Potential operational burden to ensure “clear and conspicuous” numeric disclosures:** The bill requires disclosures to be in **Arabic numerals**, readable and understandable by visual inspection (§ 7606(c), (e), (f)), which could require reformatting labels/web flows.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Enforcement mechanism not stated in the bill text:** The amendments do not specify which agency enforces § 7606 compliance or how violations are processed.  
- **Meaning of “electronic disclosure” delivery method details:** The bill requires electronic disclosure be “clearly and conspicuously indicated” and readable by visual inspection but does not specify acceptable formats (e.g., product page display vs. checkout pop-up) (§ 7606(e), (f)).  
- **Scope of “third-party inventory” situations:** The bill covers “personal property that is not lease-purchase property” offered for lease-purchase (§ 7606(f)) but does not further define the commercial arrangements included.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New definition (§ 7601(5)):**  
  - “**Lease-purchase property**” = personal property **owned by the lessor** at the time it is **physically displayed and offered** for lease-purchase, and **prior to execution** of any lease-purchase agreement.
- **Item price disclosure rule (§ 7606(c)):**  
  - Every item of lease-purchase property displayed/offered must show, in **Arabic numerals** affixed to the item and readable by visual inspection:  
    1) **Cash price**; and  
    2) **Lease payment amount** and **total lease payments necessary to acquire ownership**.
- **Online compliance option (§ 7606(e)):**  
  - For lease-purchase property displayed/offered online where the consumer can enter an agreement online/remotely, the lessor **may** satisfy § 7606(c) via **electronic disclosure**, if made **before** § 7603 disclosures.
- **Non-owned property rule (§ 7606(f)):**  
  - When property **not owned by the lessor** (i.e., not lease-purchase property) is offered for lease-purchase, the lessor **shall** provide the § 7606(c) information via **electronic disclosure**, **before** § 7603 disclosures.
- **Technical corrections:** Conforms existing law to Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (no specific substantive changes described beyond those above).

## Who Benefits
- **Consumers/lessees** entering lease-purchase agreements, who receive clearer item-level information (cash price; payment amount; total payments to ownership), including in **online/remote** contexts and when items are offered that are **not owned by the lessor**.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Lessors** offering lease-purchase agreements, particularly those offering **online transactions** or offering items they **do not own**, who must implement/ensure compliant electronic disclosures and required sequencing relative to § 7603.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (advertising and disclosures):**
  - Ads cannot claim specific items are available at specific amounts/terms unless the lessor usually/customarily offers them that way (§ 7606(a)).  
  - If an ad mentions payment amount or right to acquire ownership, it must clearly/conspicuously state:  
    1) It is a lease-purchase agreement;  
    2) The total lease payments necessary to acquire ownership; and  
    3) The lessee will not own the property until the total amount necessary is paid in full (or by lawful prepayment) (§ 7606(b)).  
  - Item-level disclosures for lease-purchase property must be clearly/conspicuously shown in **Arabic numerals** (cash price; lease payment; total lease payments to ownership) (§ 7606(c)).  
  - For qualifying online offers, item-level disclosures may be electronic; for non-owned items offered for lease-purchase, they must be electronic; and in both cases must be provided **before** § 7603 disclosures (§ 7606(e), (f)).
