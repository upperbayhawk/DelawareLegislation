## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16 of the Delaware Code to require hospitals and freestanding surgical centers to adopt and implement policies mandating use of surgical smoke evacuation systems during procedures likely to generate surgical smoke.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new Chapter 93B, “Surgical Smoke Evacuation,” in Part VIII, Title 16.
- Defines “health care employer” as a **hospital** (per **16 Del. C. § 1001**) or **freestanding surgical center** (per **16 Del. C. § 122**).
- Defines “smoke evacuation system” as equipment that captures and filters surgical smoke **at the site of origin** before it contacts occupants’ **eyes or respiratory tract**.
- Defines “surgical smoke” broadly to include gaseous by-products from energy-generating devices (e.g., plume, bio-aerosols, laser-generated contaminants, lung-damaging dust).
- Requires covered facilities to **adopt and implement policies** requiring use of a smoke evacuation system during **any surgical procedure likely to generate surgical smoke**.
- Sets a compliance deadline of **on or before April 1, 2026**.
- Does not specify penalties, an enforcement agency, or funding.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Establishes a statewide requirement for policies that use smoke evacuation systems to reduce exposure of room occupants’ **eyes and respiratory tract** to surgical smoke (as reflected in the definition of “smoke evacuation system”).
- Applies to both **hospitals** and **freestanding surgical centers**, creating consistent expectations across major surgical settings.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Requires covered facilities to adopt and implement new policies and operational practices by a fixed date (**April 1, 2026**), which may create administrative and implementation burdens (policy adoption and implementation are expressly required).
- No funding source is provided in the bill text for purchasing or maintaining smoke evacuation equipment.
