## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To expressly authorize all Delaware municipalities to set different municipal property tax rates for **residential** versus **nonresidential** real property, while requiring **uniformity within each class** as required by the Delaware Constitution.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Codifies municipal authority to create **two real property tax classifications**: **(1) residential** and **(2) nonresidential**.  
- Allows municipalities to set a **different tax rate for each classification** by **local law or ordinance**.  
- Requires tax rates to be **uniform for all property within the same classification**, consistent with **Article VIII, § 1** of the Delaware Constitution.  
- Intended to avoid the need for **municipal charter-by-charter amendments** to authorize split rates.  
- Described as codifying “**longstanding authority**” recognized under Delaware common law (cases cited in the synopsis).  
- Requires a **two-thirds vote** in each chamber for passage due to the constitutional rule for amending municipal charters (**Article IX, § 1**, as stated in the synopsis).  
- Act was **approved August 12, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clarifies statewide municipal authority**: The bill states it “clarifies that all municipalities have this authority,” reducing reliance on individual charter amendments.
- **Local flexibility in tax policy**: Municipalities may set different rates for residential vs. nonresidential property “by local law or ordinance,” enabling tailored local revenue strategies.
- **Uniformity requirement provides guardrails**: Explicitly requires uniform rates within each class, aligning with **Article VIII, § 1**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential for higher taxes on one class**: The bill authorizes municipalities to set different rates for residential vs. nonresidential property; this could enable shifting tax burden between classes (the bill does not limit the spread).
- **Administrative complexity for municipalities**: Implementing and maintaining two classifications (residential/nonresidential) may require updated ordinances and billing practices (the bill authorizes the structure but does not provide implementation resources).
- **Limited classification options**: Only two categories are specified—residential and nonresidential—potentially limiting finer-grained classifications.
