## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Propose the first-leg constitutional amendment to add a new § 22 to Article I of the Delaware Constitution establishing an individual right to hunt, fish, and trap wildlife in Delaware (including by traditional methods), while expressly preserving the General Assembly’s authority to regulate and clarifying limits on how the new right may be construed.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds a proposed new **Article I, § 22** creating a constitutional right for an individual to **hunt, fish, and trap wildlife** in Delaware, including by **traditional methods** (subject to stated limits).  
- Declares hunting, fishing, and trapping to be a **valued part of Delaware’s natural heritage** and “**forever preserved for the public good**.”  
- States that hunting, fishing, and trapping are the **preferred means of managing and controlling wildlife**.  
- Explicitly allows the **General Assembly to prescribe laws**, and—if authorized by statute—**state agencies to adopt regulations** and **local governments to enact ordinances** to promote conservation/management and preserve hunting/fishing/trapping.  
- Provides construction limits: the amendment may not be read to limit **trespass/property laws**, affect certain **water rights/minimum flows**, diminish other public/private rights, restrict state power over **commercial regulation**, prevent **license suspension/revocation**, or alter existing **burden of proof** rules for challenges.  
- Requires a **two-thirds vote of members elected to each house** to pass because it amends the Delaware Constitution (per Article XVI, § 1).  
- Notes it is the **first leg** of a constitutional amendment and must pass again in the **next General Assembly after the next general election** with the same changes to become effective.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Constitutional protection of hunting/fishing/trapping:** The bill would place an express right into the Delaware Constitution (Article I, proposed § 22(a)).  
- **Affirms wildlife management approach:** Declares these activities the “preferred means” for wildlife management and control (Section 1(2)), potentially strengthening policy support for such management tools.  
- **Preserves regulatory authority:** Explicitly confirms the General Assembly’s authority to regulate and authorize agency/local rules for conservation and management (proposed § 22(b)).  
- **Reduces interpretive conflict with existing laws:** The “may not be construed” clauses expressly protect trespass/property laws, water rights provisions, commercial regulatory power, license actions, and burden-of-proof rules (proposed § 22(c)(1)-(5)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Elevates policy preference to constitutional status:** Declaring hunting/fishing/trapping the “preferred means” of wildlife management (Section 1(2)) could constrain future policy debates by embedding a preference in constitutional text, even though regulation is still allowed.  
- **Potential for litigation over scope:** Creating a new constitutional right (proposed § 22(a)) may invite legal challenges testing the boundaries of what regulations remain permissible, notwithstanding the listed construction limits (proposed § 22(b)-(c)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Meaning of “traditional methods”:** The bill does not define “traditional methods” in proposed § 22(a).  
- **Scope of “preferred means” statement:** The practical legal effect of declaring hunting/fishing/trapping the “preferred means” of managing and controlling wildlife (Section 1(2)) is not further specified.  
- **Local government authority trigger:** Local ordinances are allowed only “**if authorized by the General Assembly by law**” (proposed § 22(b)); the bill does not specify what authorizing legislation exists or is contemplated.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates proposed constitutional right:** Adds **Article I, § 22(a)** establishing an individual right to **hunt, fish, and trap wildlife**, including by traditional methods, subject to subsections (b) and (c).  
- **Affirms state/local regulatory authority:** Authorizes the General Assembly to regulate; and, if authorized by statute, allows **state agencies** to adopt regulations and **local governments** to enact ordinances to:
  - Promote wildlife **protection, conservation, replenishment, and management** (proposed § 22(b)(1)); and
  - Preserve the future of hunting, fishing, and trapping (proposed § 22(b)(2)).  
- **Adds explicit construction limits:** The new section may not be construed to:
  1) Limit trespass or property-rights laws (§ 22(c)(1));  
  2) Affect certain water rights/minimum water provisions (§ 22(c)(2));  
  3) Diminish other rights or the state’s power to regulate commercial activities (§ 22(c)(3));  
  4) Prevent license suspension/revocation under state law (§ 22(c)(4));  
  5) Alter existing burdens of proof for challenges to hunting/fishing/trapping laws or regulations (§ 22(c)(5)).  
- **Findings/Declarations:** Declares the right is part of Delaware’s natural heritage and is “forever preserved for the public good” (Section 1(1)), and declares hunting/fishing/trapping the preferred wildlife management method (Section 1(2)).
