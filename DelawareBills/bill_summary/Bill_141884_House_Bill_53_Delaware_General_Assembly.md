## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 26, § 220 to broaden and clarify the scope of the Telecommunications Service and Devices Fund/program for Delaware residents with deafness, hearing loss, or speech disabilities (as related to deafness/hearing loss), including adding authority to provide additional assistive devices, hire staff for administration/outreach, impose new fund-spending limits, require a 3‑month reserve, and require annual reporting.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Expands the program’s covered support beyond “analog communication devices” to include **assistive devices** that facilitate communication or provide **emergency-related information**.  
- Directs the **Delaware Office of the Deaf and Hard of Hearing (Department of Labor)** to administer applications and provide eligible devices; the program remains capped at **no more than 10 new users approved per month** (first come, first served).  
- Directs the **Department of Technology and Information (DTI)** to provide a **statewide telecommunications relay service**, with authority to contract with third parties; costs paid from the Fund.  
- Authorizes the Office of the Deaf and Hard of Hearing to **publish an online list of eligible devices**, including examples like **alarm clocks, baby monitors, doorbells, smoke detectors**, and specialized telecom equipment (e.g., hearing-aid compatible cell phones, phones with amplifiers).  
- Authorizes the Office to **employ individuals** as needed to administer the program, educate the public, and manage resources—subject to spending caps.  
- Restricts Fund uses and sets spending limits: **≤ 15%** of Fund revenue received during the fiscal year for employing individuals to administer/manage the program, and **≤ 15%** of Fund money for administrative costs.  
- Requires the Fund to maintain a **3‑month reserve** (based on prior-year average actual expenses) for relay service, devices, and administration.  
- Requires an **annual report due by September 30** each year (covering the prior fiscal year), posted on the Office website and sent to specified state officials.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader eligible supports**: Explicitly allows funding for a wider array of assistive and emergency-related devices (e.g., **smoke detectors**, **emergency response kits**), which can improve access and safety for eligible residents.  
- **Greater program transparency and accountability**: Requires a detailed **annual report by September 30** with application counts, approvals/denials, spending, item distribution counts, and ending Fund balance.  
- **Improved financial stability safeguards**: Mandates a **3‑month reserve** and limits administrative/management spending (both capped at **15%**, as specified), which may help preserve funds for direct services.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Enrollment constrained**: Continues the limit of **no more than 10 new users approved per month**, which may restrict access regardless of need or demand (cap is explicit in the text).  
- **Administrative flexibility constrained**: Caps on staffing and administrative spending (each **≤ 15%**) may limit the Office’s ability to scale administration, outreach, or program management even if workload increases.  
- **Potentially slower expansion of direct benefits**: The **3‑month reserve requirement** may limit the amount immediately available for distribution of devices/services in a given period because funds must be held back.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Scope expansion/clarification**: Program covers assistive devices for individuals with disabilities “**related to or resulting from an individual being deaf or hard of hearing**.”  
- **Device support expanded**: Office may fund devices that directly/indirectly facilitate communication or provide emergency information; eligible examples include:  
  - Everyday items: **alarm clocks, baby monitors, doorbells, smoke detectors**.  
  - **Assistive communication devices**.  
  - **Hearing-related emergency response kits**.  
  - Specialized telecom equipment: **hearing-aid compatible cell phones**, computer conversion packages, large visual displays, pagers, phones with amplifiers, alerting devices.  
- **Hiring authority added**: Office may employ individuals to administer program, conduct public education, and manage resources.  
- **Fund use restrictions added**: Fund may be used only for specified services/costs and is subject to:  
  - **15% cap** on employing individuals for administration/management (as described).  
  - **15% cap** on administrative costs.  
  - **3‑month reserve** requirement.  
- **Non-appropriated special fund**: Fund balances **carry forward after June 30** and do **not revert to the General Fund**.  
- **Annual reporting requirement**: Report **due by September 30** each year; must be posted online and include six specified data elements (applications, approvals, denials, expenditures, itemized breakdown/counts, ending balance).  
- **Effective-date mechanism updated**: The Act takes effect **one year after enactment into law** (see “Approved July 21, 2025” in the enrolled act).
