## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 23, §131 of the Delaware Code to set pilotage rate schedules (per-unit charges and maximum billable units) for the Pilots’ Association for the Bay and River Delaware for calendar years **2026, 2027, and 2028**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Sets pilotage **per-unit charges** for **2026–2028** at **$17.78 (2026)**, **$18.31 (2027)**, and **$18.86 (2028)**.
- Retains a **minimum charge of 200 units** (no change stated for that minimum).
- Increases the **maximum unit charge** (cap) to **1,600 units** for **2026–2028** (from **1,550 units** in **2025**).
- Applies these changes in both **§131(a)** and **§131(b)** (two parallel rate provisions in the statute).
- Leaves the **2025 per-unit charge** at **$17.26** (as shown in the amended schedule) and updates future-year entries.
- Enacted as **Chapter 16, Volume 85**; **Approved May 7, 2025**.
- Synopsis states the bill “establishes the pilotage rates… for **2026, 2027, and 2028**.”

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Rate certainty/predictability:** Establishes explicit pilotage rates for **three future years (2026–2028)** in statute.
- **Potential revenue alignment with higher service demands/costs:** Per-unit charges rise from **$17.26 (2025)** to **$17.78 (2026)**, **$18.31 (2027)**, and **$18.86 (2028)**, which may support the pilotage system’s ongoing operations (the bill itself states only the new rates, not the rationale).
- **Higher cap may better match larger movements:** Raises the maximum billable units to **1,600** for **2026–2028**, allowing charges to scale for larger qualifying transits relative to a **1,550** cap in **2025**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher costs for users subject to pilotage charges:** The per-unit charge increases year-over-year to **$18.86 per unit by 2028**, and the cap increases to **1,600 units** (which can increase charges for higher-unit movements compared to a **1,550** cap).
- **No offsetting cost controls stated:** The bill text provides new rates and caps but does not include performance standards, cost justifications, or mitigation measures for affected payers.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
