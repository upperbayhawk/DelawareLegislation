## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 19, § 711 of the Delaware Code to make it an unlawful employment practice for employers to (1) request certain political information from employees or applicants and (2) take adverse employment actions based on political party/voting history or political contributions, with limited exceptions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits an employer from **requesting** political information from an employee or job applicant, including **political party**, **voting history**, and **political contributions** to candidates or political committees.  
- Prohibits an employer from **refusing to hire, discharging, or otherwise discriminating** regarding compensation, terms, conditions, or privileges of employment based on the same political information.  
- Creates an exception where political affiliation or preference is a **bona fide occupational qualification (BFOQ)**.  
- Defines the BFOQ standard: the employer must establish employment is **“in furtherance of a particular political purpose.”**  
- Creates an exception where an employee’s political contributions **must be disclosed to the employer** under **federal or state law** requiring employers to disclose employee contributions.  
- Makes these changes by amending **19 Del. C. § 711** (unlawful employment practices; employer practices).  
- No dollar amounts, percentages, or numeric thresholds are specified in the bill text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces political coercion or screening in hiring/employment:** Employers are barred from asking about political party, voting history, and political donations (Section 1, new §711(u)(1)).  
- **Strengthens protections against politically motivated adverse actions:** Bars refusal to hire, discharge, or discrimination in compensation/terms/conditions/privileges of employment based on political party/voting history or political contributions (new §711(u)(2)).  
- **Preserves limited role-based flexibility:** Allows political affiliation/preference to be considered only when it is a **BFOQ** and the job furthers a **particular political purpose** (new §711(u)(3)).  
- **Avoids conflict with existing disclosure laws:** Provides an exception where disclosure of employee contributions to the employer is required by federal or state law (new §711(u)(4)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Could limit employer information-gathering:** Employers may no longer request specified political information from applicants/employees, even where an employer believes it relevant, unless an exception applies (new §711(u)(1), (3), (4)).  
- **Potential compliance and HR process changes:** Employers will need to ensure interview questions, forms, and management practices do not solicit or rely on the prohibited information (implied by making the conduct an “unlawful employment practice” in new §711(u)).  
- **BFOQ standard may invite disputes:** The requirement that employment be “in furtherance of a particular political purpose” may lead to disagreements over whether a position qualifies (new §711(u)(3)).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new unlawful employment practice prohibiting an employer from **requesting** from an employee or applicant information about:  
  - the individual’s **political party or voting history**;  
  - **contributions** to **candidates for office**;  
  - **contributions** to **political committees**. (new §711(u)(1))  
- Adds a new unlawful employment practice prohibiting an employer from **refusing to hire**, **discharging**, or otherwise **discriminating** in compensation/terms/conditions/privileges of employment because of those same items. (new §711(u)(2))  
- Establishes a **BFOQ exception** for political affiliation/preference, requiring the employer to show the employment is **in furtherance of a particular political purpose**. (new §711(u)(3))  
- Establishes a legal-compliance exception where the employee’s political contributions **must be disclosed to the employer** under a **federal or state law** requiring employers to disclose employee political contributions. (new §711(u)(4))

## Who Benefits
- **Employees and job applicants** whose political party, voting history, or political contributions could otherwise be requested or used as a basis for adverse employment decisions (new §711(u)(1)-(2)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Employers** that currently request political party/voting history/contribution information or consider it in employment decisions will be prohibited from doing so except under the listed exceptions (new §711(u)(1)-(4)).
