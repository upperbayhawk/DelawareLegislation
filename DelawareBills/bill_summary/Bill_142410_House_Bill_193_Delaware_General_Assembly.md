## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware Code Title 25, § 7042 to (1) change how the Delaware Manufactured Home Relocation Trust Fund (Trust Fund) “cap” is set, and (2) increase certain per-lot monthly assessment amounts/credits and redirected amounts, with changes taking effect **January 1, 2026** (as enacted).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes the **DMHRA Board** to **establish and adjust** a cap on the Trust Fund, rather than maintaining a fixed statutory cap of **$15 million**.
- Ties any Trust Fund cap to “**costs reasonably necessary** to carry out the purposes of the Trust Fund” under **§ 7042(b)**.
- Keeps the Board’s authority to **adjust, eliminate, or reinstate** the cap, subject to the voting requirements in **§ 7041(c)(3)**.
- Maintains the Board’s duty to set a **monthly assessment** for each rented lot in a manufactured home community and to notify landlords and tenants of changes under Board regulations.
- Continues the split of the monthly assessment obligation: **one-half paid by the tenant** and **one-half paid by the landlord**.
- Effective **January 1, 2026**, increases the **landlord portion credit** to **$1.50 per rented lot** (from **$0.50**).
- Effective **January 1, 2026**, increases the amount of the **tenant portion redirected** to the **Delaware Manufactured Home Owner Attorney Fund** to **$1.50 per rented lot** (from **$0.50**).
- The enacted act was **approved September 3, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More flexible Trust Fund cap-setting:** The Board may set/adjust the cap based on “costs reasonably necessary” to fulfill Trust Fund purposes (§ 7042(e)), which may better align fund limits with actual program needs stated in law.
- **Increased support for attorney fund:** Redirected amount from the tenant portion increases from **$0.50 to $1.50 per rented lot** starting **January 1, 2026** (§ 7042(g)(1)d), which increases dedicated funding to the **Delaware Manufactured Home Owner Attorney Fund** (§ 7046).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced statutory certainty on the Trust Fund cap:** The bill removes the fixed **$15 million** cap language and replaces it with a Board-established cap tied to reasonably necessary costs (§ 7042(e)), which may reduce predictability compared with a fixed cap (the bill does not set a new numeric cap).
- **More of tenant-paid assessment is redirected away from the Trust Fund:** The redirected amount rises to **$1.50** from the tenant portion (§ 7042(g)(1)d), which (per the text) increases the diversion of tenant portion dollars to the attorney fund rather than remaining in the Trust Fund.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **No numeric cap specified:** The statute no longer sets a specific dollar cap; it leaves the cap amount to the Board based on “costs reasonably necessary” (§ 7042(e)). The bill text does not define a method, metric, or reporting requirement for how those costs are determined.
- **Net effect on total assessments is unclear:** The bill changes the **credit** and **redirected** dollar amounts to **$1.50**, but does not state the total monthly assessment amount per lot or whether it changes.
- **Interaction with Trust Fund dissolution provisions:** The provided enrolled text shows amendments to subsections (e) and (g); it does not amend the dissolution/liquidation language shown in the earlier version. The practical implications if the fund “ceases to exist” are not addressed in the enacted changes.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Trust Fund cap authority revised (§ 7042(e)):**
  - Replaces the fixed “cap on the Trust Fund is **$15 million**” approach with: **“The DMHRA Board may establish and adjust the cap on the Trust Fund”**, and the cap is to be set **based on costs reasonably necessary** to carry out Trust Fund purposes under § 7042(b).
  - Retains that the cap may be “adjusted, eliminated, or reinstated” by the Board, subject to § 7041(c)(3).
- **Assessment-related dollar amounts updated effective January 1, 2026 (§ 7042(g)(1)c-d)):**
  - Landlord portion credit: **$0.50 → $1.50** per rented lot.
  - Tenant portion redirected to Delaware Manufactured Home Owner Attorney Fund (§ 7046): **$0.50 → $1.50** per rented lot.
- **Assessment split reaffirmed (§ 7042(g)(1)b):** One-half tenant obligation / one-half landlord obligation.

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware Manufactured Home Owner Attorney Fund (and the owners it serves):** Receives **$1.50 per rented lot** from the tenant portion starting **January 1, 2026** (§ 7042(g)(1)d; § 7046 reference).
- **Program administration flexibility (DMHRA Board):** Gains explicit authority to **establish and adjust** the Trust Fund cap based on reasonably necessary costs (§ 7042(e)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Manufactured home community tenants (of rented lots):** A larger fixed amount of the tenant portion—**$1.50 per rented lot**—is redirected to the attorney fund starting **January 1, 2026** (§ 7042(g)(1)d), which may reduce how much of the tenant portion remains available for Trust Fund deposits (the bill does not quantify totals).
- **Stakeholders preferring a fixed statutory cap:** The fixed **$15 million** cap is no longer set in statute; cap-setting is delegated to the Board (§ 7042(e)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
