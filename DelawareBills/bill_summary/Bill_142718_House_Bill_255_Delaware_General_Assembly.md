## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To decouple specified Delaware corporate and personal income tax provisions from Public Law 119-21 (the federal “One Big Beautiful Bill Act” (OBBBA)) so Delaware continues using pre-OBBBA rules for the **timing** of certain deductions (research expensing and depreciation/amortization), rather than adopting the new federal timing rules.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Decouples Delaware from select OBBBA provisions** that would otherwise flow through automatically from federal tax law into Delaware tax law.  
- For **C corporations**, Delaware continues **expensing domestic research and experimental (R&E) expenditures** made **after 12/31/2021 and on or before 12/31/2024**, using pre-OBBBA rules (instead of § 70302 of P.L. 119-21).  
- For **C corporations**, for certain business property **acquired and placed in service after 01/19/2025 and before 01/01/2031**, Delaware requires continued **amortization/depreciation under pre-OBBBA rules** (instead of federal full expensing under § 70301 of P.L. 119-21).  
- For **C corporations**, for **qualified production property** (as defined in § 70307 of P.L. 119-21) **placed in service before 01/01/2031**, Delaware continues **pre-OBBBA amortization/depreciation** (instead of the federal special depreciation allowance under § 70307).  
- For **individuals with business income** (e.g., S corporation/partnership income), similar decoupling applies for property and qualified production property **after 12/31/2025 and before 01/01/2031** (effective **01/01/2026**).  
- **Effective timing differs**: corporate changes apply to **tax years beginning on or after 01/01/2022** (effective upon enactment); personal income tax changes are effective **01/01/2026**.  
- Requires a **Department of Finance report** at the **December 2027 Delaware Economic and Financial Advisory Council (DEFAC) meeting** on actual revenue impacts and federal depreciation updates, including options/recommendations.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Greater state-level control and predictability over taxable income timing** by avoiding automatic adoption of OBBBA timing changes (bill synopsis: modifies timing; does not eliminate depreciation/expensing entirely).  
- **Maintains pre-OBBBA expensing treatment for domestic R&E** for eligible corporate expenditures **(post-12/31/2021 through 12/31/2024)**, potentially reducing Delaware taxable income in the near term for qualifying corporations.  
- **Creates a defined decoupling window ending before 01/01/2031** for specified property/production property, providing a stated time horizon in statute.  
- **Built-in review mechanism**: December **2027** DEFAC presentation and legislative distribution requirement to inform future decisions.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Adds complexity for taxpayers** by requiring Delaware-specific adjustments to federal taxable income for the covered items (corporate § 1903 and personal § 1106 modifications).  
- **May reduce conformity with federal rules**, potentially increasing compliance costs for C corporations and pass-through business owners who otherwise would rely on federal treatment.  
- **Time-limited and multi-date rules** (01/19/2025 start; 12/31/2025 start for PIT; 01/01/2031 end) may create additional recordkeeping needs to track which assets/expenditures fall inside or outside the specified windows.

## Key Policy Changes
1. **Corporate income tax (Title 30 § 1903) modifications** to federal taxable income to decouple from P.L. 119-21:
   - **Domestic R&E expensing:** applies to expenditures made **after 12/31/2021 but on or before 12/31/2024**—Delaware continues expensing under pre-OBBBA rules (instead of § 70302).  
   - **Certain business property expensing:** for property **acquired and placed in service after 01/19/2025 and before 01/01/2031**—Delaware continues pre-OBBBA amortization/depreciation (instead of § 70301 full expensing).  
   - **Qualified production property:** for property (per § 70307 definition) **placed in service before 01/01/2031**—Delaware continues pre-OBBBA amortization/depreciation (instead of § 70307 special depreciation allowance).
2. **Personal income tax (Title 30 § 1106) modifications** for individuals with business income:
   - **Certain business property expensing decoupling:** property **acquired and placed in service after 12/31/2025 and before 01/01/2031**—continue pre-OBBBA amortization/depreciation (instead of § 70301).  
   - **Qualified production property decoupling:** property **acquired and placed in service after 12/31/2025 and before 01/01/2031**—continue pre-OBBBA amortization/depreciation (instead of § 70307).
3. **Severability clause**: if any provision/application is held invalid, remaining provisions continue in effect.
4. **DEFAC reporting requirement** (December 2027) by Department of Finance.

## Who Benefits
- **C corporations** with:
  - eligible **domestic R&E expenditures** made **after 12/31/2021 through 12/31/2024**, and/or
  - eligible business property **placed in service after 01/19/2025 and before 01/01/2031**, and/or
  - **qualified production property** placed in service **before 01/01/2031**,  
  because Delaware tax treatment will follow pre-OBBBA timing rules rather than the OBBBA-linked timing rules.
- **Individuals with pass-through business income** (e.g., from S corporations/partnerships) with eligible property/qualified production property **after 12/31/2025 and before 01/01/2031**, due to the personal income tax decoupling effective 01/01/2026.
- **Legislators/financial planners** benefit from mandated **December 2027 revenue-impact reporting**.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Taxpayers seeking to use federal OBBBA full expensing or special depreciation allowances** for Delaware purposes on covered property, because Delaware requires pre-OBBBA amortization/depreciation timing for specified assets/time periods.  
- **Tax preparers and affected businesses** may face added compliance work to compute Delaware-specific addbacks/subtractions relative to federal taxable income.
