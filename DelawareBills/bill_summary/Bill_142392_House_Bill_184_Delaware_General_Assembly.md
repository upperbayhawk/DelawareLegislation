## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 29, Chapter 8 (General Assembly redistricting) to replace the current legislative redistricting approach with a required, structured “bipartisan protocol” conducted by majority and minority caucus committees in each chamber, with defined deadlines, public transparency requirements, and a judicial backstop if the General Assembly does not enact a plan on time or enacts an unlawful plan.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates four caucus redistricting committees (House Majority, House Minority, Senate Majority, Senate Minority), **3 members each**, to run redistricting in every year ending in **“1”** (e.g., 2031, 2041).  
- Sets a new internal timeline: committee formation by **January 30**; initial chamber maps due **April 15**; preliminary plan for public comment by **August 1**; final enactment by **September 1** (extendable, but **not beyond September 23**); failure trigger at **September 30**.  
- Requires iterative “freeze-and-redraw” exchanges between majority and minority committees, where each side “sets the boundaries” of a specified number of districts at each step (House: ultimately covers all **41** districts; Senate: all **21** districts).  
- Mandates a **redistricting web page(s)** on the General Assembly website with committee contacts, prior maps (including **racial demographics and voter registration**), and drafts posted “at each stage.”  
- Requires a **preliminary plan and report** to include: **population and percentage deviation** for every district, **racial demographics**, **voter registration**, and other data needed to evaluate compliance with Delaware law and the **Voting Rights Act**.  
- Requires **4 public hearings** (1 in each county and 1 in the **City of Wilmington**), with **at least 7 days’ notice** and **live video** available.  
- Gives the **Delaware Supreme Court original and exclusive jurisdiction** to review a plan; lawsuits must be filed within **30 days after adoption**.  
- If the General Assembly misses the deadline or the plan is unlawful, a **judicial panel** must produce a binding plan made public by **November 1**; that judicial plan is **not appealable**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Defined, enforceable process and transparency:** Requires map drafts to be posted online “at each stage” and mandates public access to data/software resources during the process.  
- **Structured minority-party participation:** Explicitly gives minority caucus committees recurring opportunities to “set the boundaries” of specified districts during iterative exchanges (House and Senate processes).  
- **Public input built into timeline:** Requires **4 public hearings**, including one in Wilmington, with notice and live video availability.  
- **Clear judicial backstop:** If no plan is adopted by **September 30** of a year ending in “1,” or if a plan is unconstitutional/violates federal law, the judicial panel must complete the maps by **November 1**.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Resource commitments are mandatory but not quantified:** The State must provide each caucus committee staff, consultants, equipment, software, materials, and possibly secure office space, but the bill provides **no dollar amounts**.  
- **Compressed decision window late in the year:** Final enactment is due **September 1** (extendable only to **September 23**), with a failure trigger at **September 30**, which may constrain legislative scheduling.  
- **No amendment allowed to final plan:** The General Assembly must enact the final plan “**in its entirety, without amendment**,” limiting the ability to make targeted fixes through the normal legislative process.  
- **Judicial plan is final with no appeal:** If the judicial backstop is triggered, the judicial plan “**shall not be subject to any appellate process**.”

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates a formal bipartisan protocol** for legislative redistricting via four caucus committees (House and Senate majority/minority), **3 members each**, formed by **January 30** of each year ending in “1.”  
- **Replaces prior statutory timing language** by establishing a detailed schedule: April 15 (initial chamber maps), August 1 (preliminary plan/report), September 1 (final enactment; extendable to Sept. 23), Sept. 30 (failure trigger), Nov. 1 (judicial plan publication deadline if triggered).  
- **Mandates public transparency tools:** redistricting web page(s) with committee contacts, prior maps with racial demographics and voter registration, and posting of draft maps “at each stage.”  
- **Mandates public hearings:** 4 hearings (one per county plus Wilmington), **7 days’ notice minimum**, live video available.  
- **Sets content requirements** for preliminary and final plan reports: population and % deviation for every district; racial demographics; voter registration; other data to evaluate legal/VRA compliance.  
- **Creates judicial review and remedy structure:** Delaware Supreme Court has original/exclusive jurisdiction; petitions within **30 days**; judicial panel draws maps if deadline missed or plan unlawful; judicial plan final and non-appealable.  
- **Sets staggered Senate election schedule beginning in 2032** (for the listed districts in § 806(a) and § 806(b)).

## Who Benefits
- **Voters/general public:** gain access to posted draft maps, demographic/registration data, and a guaranteed public hearing process.  
- **Minority caucuses in House and Senate:** receive defined roles and recurring opportunities to “set the boundaries” of certain districts during map drafting.  
- **Election administrators (clarity of timelines):** receive a structured statutory calendar and a process for resolving mapping conflicts (working with the Commissioner of Elections via later legislation).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **General Assembly operations and support agencies:** may face increased administrative workload and support obligations (staffing, software, public web postings, hearings, coordination), with costs not specified.  
- **Legislators seeking to modify a plan through amendments:** the final plan must be enacted “without amendment,” limiting flexibility at the enactment stage.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** “This Act shall become effective for any redistricting after **December 31, 2030**.”  
- **Committee formation:** by **January 30** of each year ending in “1.”  
- **Publish committee member names/contact info:** within **7 days** after committee establishment (to media outlets covering the General Assembly and on the required web page(s)).  
- **Initial chamber maps due:** no later than **April 15** of each year ending in “1” (House draws **41** representative districts; Senate draws **21** senatorial districts).  
- **Map exchange turnaround time:** each review/redraw step is due within **5 business days** (multiple iterative steps in both chambers).  
- **Preliminary plan/report for public distribution/comment:** no later than **August 1** of each year ending in “1.”  
- **Public hearings:** **4 hearings** after completion of preliminary plan; **≥ 7 days’ notice** for each.  
- **General Assembly enactment deadline:** by **September 1** of each year ending in “1,” extendable by majority of both chambers but **not beyond September 23**.  
- **Failure trigger:** if no plan adopted by **September 30** of each year ending in “1,” judicial panel draws maps.  
- **Judicial plan publication:** made public by **November 1** of the same year (if judicial backstop triggered).  
- **Judicial review filing deadline:** within **30 days after adoption** of the plan (filed by a resident of the state).  
- **Senate staggered-election timing (as amended):**
  - Districts listed in § 806(a): **4-year terms in 2032 and 2036**, **2-year term in 2040**.  
  - Districts listed in § 806(b): **2-year term in 2032**, **4-year terms in 2034 and 2038**.
