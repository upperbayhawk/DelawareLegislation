## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 10 to clarify and refine the Delaware Family Court’s criminal jurisdiction, including (1) updating the definition of “family,” and (2) expanding/clarifying when Family Court shares (concurrent) jurisdiction with the Court of Common Pleas for certain non-felony matters.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Removes the requirement in the definition of “family” that **both parties be Delaware residents** (10 Del. C. § 901(12)).  
- Clarifies Family Court jurisdiction over certain offenses committed **between persons living separate and apart with a child in common**, including a distinction based on whether **parentage is in dispute** (10 Del. C. § 922(a)(2)d and § 922(d)).  
- Provides **concurrent jurisdiction** in **Family Court and the Court of Common Pleas** for **non-felony** offenses committed between persons living separate and apart with a child in common **where parentage is in dispute** (10 Del. C. § 922(d)).  
- Provides **concurrent jurisdiction** in **Family Court and the Court of Common Pleas** for **adult, non-felony cases** that include charges spanning both courts’ jurisdiction (10 Del. C. § 922(f)).  
- Preserves the rule that if Family Court offenses may be properly joined with a **felony** within Superior Court jurisdiction, the case goes to **Superior Court** (10 Del. C. § 922(e)).  
- States the bill requires a **2/3 vote of members elected to each chamber** due to Delaware Constitution Article IV, § 28 (because it confers misdemeanor jurisdiction on Family Court).  
- Includes technical drafting corrections to align with the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Broader applicability of “family” definition**: Removing the “both parties residents of this State” requirement may allow Family Court jurisdiction to apply in qualifying family-relationship cases even when one party is not a Delaware resident (10 Del. C. § 901(12); Synopsis).  
- **Clearer venue/jurisdiction rules** for non-felony disputes between parents living separate and apart with a child in common, by expressly addressing whether **parentage is in dispute** (10 Del. C. § 922(a)(2)d; § 922(d); Synopsis).  
- **More flexible case handling**: Concurrent jurisdiction with the Court of Common Pleas for certain adult, non-felony cases may reduce procedural barriers where charges overlap (10 Del. C. § 922(f); Synopsis).  
- **Maintains felony joinder pathway** to Superior Court for efficiency when a felony is properly joined with Family Court charges (10 Del. C. § 922(e)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential forum selection/transfer complexity**: Expanding concurrent jurisdiction with the Court of Common Pleas for specified non-felony matters may increase motion practice or administrative sorting between courts (10 Del. C. § 922(d), (f)).  
- **Broader “family” definition reach** (by removing the Delaware-residency condition) may increase the number of matters treated as intra-family for jurisdictional purposes (10 Del. C. § 901(12); Synopsis).  
- **Implementation workload**: Courts may need updated intake/screening processes for cases involving parentage disputes and overlapping charges (10 Del. C. § 922(d), (f)). (Operational impact inferred from changes; no quantified impact stated.)

## Key Policy Changes
- **10 Del. C. § 901(12) (Definition of “Family”)**: Removes language requiring that, for certain listed relationships, **“both parties [be] residents of this State.”**  
- **10 Del. C. § 922(a)(2)d**: Specifies Family Court exclusive original criminal jurisdiction (non-felony) for offenses between persons living separate and apart with a child in common **“where parentage of said child is not in dispute.”**  
- **10 Del. C. § 922(d)**: Establishes **concurrent criminal jurisdiction** between **Family Court and the Court of Common Pleas** for non-felony offenses committed between persons living separate and apart with a child in common **where parentage is in dispute.**  
- **10 Del. C. § 922(f)**: Establishes **concurrent jurisdiction** with the Court of Common Pleas for **adult, non-felony** cases within Family Court’s exclusive jurisdiction that may be properly joined with a case/offense in Court of Common Pleas (and clarifies it **does not apply to felonies**).  
- **10 Del. C. § 922(e)**: Continues the rule that where Family Court offenses can be properly joined with a **felony** in Superior Court, the matter is within **Superior Court** jurisdiction.

## Who Benefits
- **Parties and counsel** in non-felony cases involving parents living separate and apart with a child in common, because jurisdiction is explicitly addressed based on whether **parentage is disputed** (10 Del. C. § 922(a)(2)d; § 922(d)).  
- **Courts (Family Court and Court of Common Pleas)** that handle overlapping, adult, non-felony charges, due to clarified concurrent jurisdiction (10 Del. C. § 922(f)).  
- **Complainants/defendants** in qualifying family-relationship matters involving a nonresident family member, because the “both parties residents” limitation is removed from the “family” definition (10 Del. C. § 901(12)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Court administration and litigants** may face added procedural complexity in determining or litigating the proper forum where jurisdiction is concurrent (10 Del. C. § 922(d), (f)).  
- **Defendants/parties** could experience changes in which court hears a case (Family Court vs. Court of Common Pleas), depending on parentage dispute status or joinder (10 Del. C. § 922(d), (f)).
