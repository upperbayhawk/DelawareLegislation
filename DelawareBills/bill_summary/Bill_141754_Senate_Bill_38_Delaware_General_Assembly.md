## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 16, Chapter 30F, Subchapter VII to expand the existing retired law-enforcement canine support program to include retired law-enforcement horses, including creating an annual reimbursement benefit for adopters/owners and requiring participating departments to set up a reimbursement process by regulation.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Renames/expands Subchapter VII from a retired law-enforcement canine focus to cover **retired law-enforcement animals** (canines and horses) and reorganizes definitions into a single definitions section (§ 3095F).  
- Creates a new benefit for **retired law-enforcement horses**: the responsible department must reimburse an owner **up to $5,000 annually** for **veterinary care expenses and farrier services** paid by the owner (§ 3097F).  
- Maintains the existing **retired law-enforcement canine** reimbursement: **up to $1,500 annually** for veterinary care expenses (§ 3096F).  
- Requires owners seeking reimbursement to provide **documentation** of covered expenses for both canines and horses (§ 3096F; § 3097F).  
- Requires covered state departments to **promulgate regulations** establishing a reimbursement process for canines, and for horses **if applicable** (§ 3096F(d); § 3097F(c)).  
- Applies the horse benefit only to horses used by specified state-level departments (DOC, DSHS for State/Capitol Police, DNREC, State Fire Prevention Commission); it does **not** include local police departments for horses (§ 3095F(4)).  
- Effective **immediately** upon enactment, but must be **implemented 6 months after enactment**; horse provisions do **not** apply to horses retiring before the implementation date (Section 2).  
- Fiscal note projects operating/recurring cost of **$0–$10,000** per year in **FY2026–FY2028**, assuming **1–2 horses** eligible in early years and a **$5,000** annual cap per horse.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Provides defined financial assistance to adopters/owners of retired law-enforcement horses (up to **$5,000/year**) for **veterinary care and farrier services**, potentially supporting successful adoption and ongoing care (§ 3097F(a)).  
- Clarifies covered “veterinary care” (includes prescription medication, surgery to remedy substantial risk to life/health, and cremation; excludes elective surgery), helping standardize reimbursement determinations (§ 3095F(5); § 3096F(a)(4)).  
- Requires departments to establish reimbursement processes via regulation, which can improve consistency and administrative clarity (§ 3096F(d); § 3097F(c)).  

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Creates a new recurring state reimbursement obligation for retired law-enforcement horses (up to **$5,000 annually per eligible horse**) (§ 3097F(a)); fiscal note estimates **up to $10,000/year** in FY2026–FY2028.  
- Excludes horses that retire **before the implementation date**, meaning some adopters/owners may receive no reimbursement depending on retirement timing (Section 2).  
- Limits horse coverage to horses used by specified state departments only (DOC/DSHS/DNREC/FPC), excluding horses associated with “any police department of a political subdivision” (local departments) (§ 3095F(4) vs. § 3095F(1)(e)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- “Implemented 6 months after enactment” is specified, but the bill text does not define what constitutes “implementation” operationally (e.g., whether regulations must be adopted by then) (Section 2; § 3097F(c)).  
- The statute requires “documentation” but does not specify documentation standards (format, timing, audit rights) beyond requiring regulations to establish a process (§ 3096F(c)-(d); § 3097F(b)-(c)).  
- The law does not state whether the annual cap is per animal per calendar year, fiscal year, or rolling 12-month period (§ 3096F(b); § 3097F(a)).

## Budget Impact
- **Fiscal note (as amended)** estimates operating/recurring costs of **$0 minimum – $10,000 maximum** for each of:
  - **FY2026:** $0–$10,000  
  - **FY2027:** $0–$10,000  
  - **FY2028:** $0–$10,000  
- Cost basis: reimbursement up to **$5,000 annually per retired law-enforcement horse**, with projection of **1–2 horses** eligible in the first few fiscal years (fiscal note assumptions).

- For canines, covered departments must promulgate regulations establishing a reimbursement process (§ 3096F(d)).  
- DSHS/DSP Mounted Patrol is identified in the fiscal note as having **eight (8) law-enforcement horses in service**; other listed state entities (FPC, DOC, DNREC) are stated in the fiscal note to have **none currently in service** (fiscal note Assumptions #3–#4).

## Health Impact
- Provides reimbursement support for “veterinary care” (including prescription medication and necessary surgery to remedy substantial risk to life/health, and cremation; excluding elective surgery) for retired law-enforcement animals, potentially affecting access to medically necessary animal care (§ 3095F(5); § 3096F; § 3097F).  

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds retired law-enforcement horses** to Title 16, Chapter 30F, Subchapter VII and changes structure so definitions are consolidated in § 3095F (Synopsis; § 3095F).  
- **Creates § 3097F**: reimbursement for retired law-enforcement horses of **up to $5,000 annually** for veterinary care expenses and farrier services, with required documentation and required regulations if applicable (§ 3097F(a)-(c)).  
- **Retains and conforms canine provisions**: reimbursement cap remains **$1,500 annually** for retired law-enforcement canines; clarifies department responsibilities and documentation requirements (§ 3096F).  
- **Excludes pre-implementation retirements for horses**: horse reimbursements do not apply to horses retiring before the implementation date (Section 2).

## Who Benefits
- Individuals who become “Owner[s]” of a retired law-enforcement horse from DOC, DSHS (State/Capitol Police), DNREC, or the Office of the State Fire Marshal (via the State Fire Prevention Commission), through a written agreement, who may receive up to **$5,000 annually** in reimbursements (§ 3095F(1)-(2), (4); § 3097F(a)).  
- Owners of retired law-enforcement canines continue to benefit from up to **$1,500 annually** in reimbursements (§ 3096F(b)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- State departments covered by the horse provisions (DOC, DSHS, DNREC, State Fire Prevention Commission) may incur new administrative duties and reimbursement costs when applicable (§ 3097F; fiscal note).  
- Owners of law-enforcement horses that retire **before the implementation date** are ineligible for horse reimbursements (Section 2).  
- Local police departments/adopters related to local departments do not receive a horse benefit because “retired law-enforcement horse” is limited to specified state departments (§ 3095F(4)).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approved:** **August 20, 2025** (chaptered law text).  
- **Effective immediately** upon enactment (Section 2).  
- Must be **implemented 6 months after enactment** (Section 2).  
- Horse reimbursement provisions **do not apply** to law-enforcement horses that **retire before the implementation date** (Section 2).

  - Covered state departments must **promulgate regulations** to establish a reimbursement process for canines, and for horses **if applicable** (§ 3096F(d); § 3097F(c)).
