## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 25 of the Delaware Code to require **clear key-surrender procedures in residential leases** when key return is tied to **security deposit** return, and to create **default key-return rules** when procedures are not provided.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires a residential lease to include a **clear procedure for surrendering rental unit keys** if key surrender is a condition for the **return of a security deposit**. (§5123(a))
- Prohibits leases from requiring tenants to surrender keys **before the end of the lease term**. (§5123(a))
- If the lease ends on a day the leasing office is closed (Saturday/Sunday/legal holiday/other closure), possession is considered returned when either keys are surrendered per the lease or the default process is used. (§5123(b))
- For **multiple tenants on one lease**, possession is not considered returned until **all tenants** comply with the lease’s key-surrender procedure or the default process. (§5123(c))
- Creates a default process if the lease lacks a clear procedure **and** the landlord did not provide information before lease end: tenant may leave keys in the unit or leasing office mailbox by lease end and give written notice stating location and date. (§5123(d))
- Applies to **residential leases** under Subchapter I, Chapter 51, Title 25. (Amends §5123)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces ambiguity and disputes** by requiring a “clear procedure” in the lease when key surrender affects security deposit return. (§5123(a))
- **Prevents premature key turnover** requirements by barring key surrender prior to lease end. (§5123(a))
- **Addresses office-closure timing issues** by clarifying when possession is treated as returned if the last day falls on closed days (weekends, legal holidays, or other closures). (§5123(b))
- Provides a **default method** for key return (leave keys + written notice) when no clear procedure/information is provided. (§5123(d))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Landlords may face **additional administrative burden** to ensure leases include a “clear procedure” and to manage written notices under the default process. (§5123(a), (d))
- In multi-tenant situations, requiring compliance by **all tenants** before possession is considered returned could complicate move-outs and coordination. (§5123(c))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill requires a “**clear procedure**” but does not define what level of detail qualifies as “clear.” (§5123(a), (d))
- “**Other day on which the leasing management office is closed**” is not further defined (e.g., weather closures, unexpected closures). (§5123(b))
- The default rule applies when the landlord “has not provided information to the tenant prior to the end of the lease”; the bill does not specify **what form** that information must take (written vs. verbal) or how to prove it. (§5123(d))
- The bill does not specify whether the “**written notice**” in subsection (d) must be delivered by mail, email, hand delivery, or another method. (§5123(d))

## Key Policy Changes
- If key surrender is required as a condition to return a security deposit, the lease must:
  - Provide a **clear procedure** for surrender; and
  - **May not require** surrender **prior to the end** of the lease term. (§5123(a))
- If the lease’s last day is on a weekend, legal holiday, or office-closed day, possession is considered returned when:
  - Keys are surrendered under the lease procedure; **or**
  - Tenant follows the default process under §5123(d). (§5123(b))
- With more than one tenant, possession is returned only when **all tenants** comply with key-surrender requirements (lease procedure or default). (§5123(c))
- Default procedure when the lease lacks a clear key-surrender procedure and the landlord did not provide information before lease end:
  - Tenant may leave keys in the **rental unit** or **leasing office mailbox** by the end of the lease date; and
  - Tenant must provide **written notice** to the landlord stating **where** keys were placed and the **date of return**. (§5123(d))

## Who Benefits
- **Tenants** in residential leases, particularly when:
  - Key return is tied to **security deposit** return; and/or
  - The lease ends on a day the leasing office is closed; and/or
  - The lease lacks clear key-return instructions. (§5123(a), (b), (d))
- **Landlords/leasing offices** may benefit from clearer lease-based procedures that standardize end-of-lease key return when properly drafted. (§5123(a))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Landlords/property managers** who must revise lease language and administer written notices under the default procedure. (§5123(a), (d))
- **Co-tenants** on the same lease who may be affected by the requirement that possession is not considered returned until **all tenants** comply. (§5123(c))
