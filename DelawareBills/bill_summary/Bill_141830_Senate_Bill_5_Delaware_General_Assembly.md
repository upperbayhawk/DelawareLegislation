## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Propose a Delaware Constitution amendment (Article I, new § 22) to establish a **fundamental right to reproductive freedom**, including decisions relating to pregnancy and a range of related health care, and to set limits on when the State may regulate abortion care.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new constitutional provision declaring **“Every individual has a fundamental right to reproductive freedom.”**
- Defines reproductive freedom to include decision-making about **prenatal care, childbirth, postpartum care, contraception, sterilization, abortion care, miscarriage management, and infertility care**.
- Provides that this right **“shall not be denied, burdened, nor infringed upon.”**
- Allows the State to **regulate abortion care after fetal viability**.
- Prohibits the State from **ever prohibiting an abortion** when, **in the professional judgment of an attending health care professional**, it is **medically indicated** to protect the **life or physical or mental health** of the pregnant individual.
- Requires a **two-thirds vote of the members elected to each house** to pass (constitutional amendment requirement cited in bill synopsis).
- Notes this is the **first leg** of the constitutional amendment process; the **same changes must pass again after the next general election** by the next General Assembly.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Constitutional protection**: Elevates reproductive decision-making to a **fundamental right** in the Delaware Constitution (Article I, § 22(a)).
- **Broad scope of covered care**: Explicitly includes multiple aspects of reproductive health care (e.g., contraception, infertility care, miscarriage management) (Article I, § 22(a)).
- **Limits on state interference**: Text bars denial, burdens, or infringement of the right (Article I, § 22(b)).
- **Health exception after viability**: Even where regulation after fetal viability is allowed, the State cannot prohibit abortions medically indicated to protect **life or physical/mental health**, as judged by an attending health care professional (Article I, § 22(c)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Key terms not defined in the amendment text**: The amendment relies on terms like **“fetal viability,” “burdened,” “infringed,”** and **“medically indicated”** without specifying definitions or standards in the text, potentially leaving interpretation to future law and litigation.
- **Allows post-viability regulation**: The State is explicitly permitted to **regulate abortion care after fetal viability** (Article I, § 22(c)), though not to the point of prohibiting medically indicated abortions for life/health.

## Health Impact
- Establishes a constitutional right covering decisions about **prenatal care, childbirth, postpartum care, contraception, sterilization, abortion care, miscarriage management, and infertility care** (Article I, § 22(a)).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds **Article I, § 22** to the Delaware Constitution titled **“Right to reproductive freedom.”**
- Declares a **fundamental right** to reproductive freedom and enumerates covered matters (pregnancy-related and reproductive health services) (§ 22(a)).
- Prohibits the right from being **denied, burdened, or infringed** (§ 22(b)).
- Permits regulation of abortion care **after fetal viability**, but bars any prohibition of abortions medically indicated (in professional judgment) to protect **life** or **physical/mental health** (§ 22(c)).
- Establishes that passage requires a **two-thirds vote** in each chamber (per synopsis referencing Article XVI, § 1 constitutional amendment requirements).
- Identifies this bill as the **first leg**; the identical amendment must pass again **after the next general election** by the next General Assembly (synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- **Individuals in Delaware** seeking to make decisions about pregnancy and reproductive health care listed in § 22(a), including contraception, sterilization, abortion care, miscarriage management, and infertility care.
- **Pregnant individuals post-viability** where abortion is medically indicated to protect life or physical/mental health, because the State cannot prohibit it under § 22(c).
- **Health care professionals/patients** by anchoring medical-judgment language in the constitutional protection for the life/health exception post-viability (§ 22(c)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
