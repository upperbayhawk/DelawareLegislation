## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s toll-evasion civil penalty process (Title 21, §4129(c)) to (1) extend the time before an unpaid toll becomes subject to liability and (2) clarify that the administrative fee may be set at an amount “up to” $25.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Extends the deadline before liability attaches for an unpaid toll and administrative fee from the **31st day** to the **61st day** after the violation is transmitted.  
- Allows the Department of Transportation (DelDOT) or its designee—and for the Delaware Memorial Bridge, the Delaware River & Bay Authority (DRBA) or its designee—to send an **advisory and payment request** by **regular mail** before issuing a summons/complaint.  
- Requires the advisory/payment request to include enough information to identify the **nature, date, time, and location** of the alleged toll violation.  
- Clarifies that the administrative fee included in the advisory/payment request may be a “reasonable administrative fee” of **up to $25** (cap stated explicitly).  
- Applies when nonpayment is evidenced by **visual observation**, **video surveillance**, or another vehicle-identification method.  
- Signed into law as **Volume 85, Chapter 37** and **approved June 24, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More time to resolve toll issues before liability:** Extending the trigger from **31 days to 61 days** gives vehicle owners additional time to pay the toll and fee before being subject to liability.  
- **Potentially clearer fee structure:** Stating the administrative fee may be **“up to $25”** clarifies the maximum amount that may be imposed in the advisory/payment request.  
- **Pre-summons resolution process preserved:** The bill continues to allow an advisory/payment request prior to issuing a summons and complaint, supporting resolution without immediate court action.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Delayed enforcement timeline:** Moving liability from the **31st** to the **61st** day delays when the owner becomes subject to liability for nonpayment.  
- **Administrative fee still permitted:** The bill continues to allow an administrative fee (now explicitly **up to $25**) in addition to the toll; the bill does not reduce or eliminate that fee authority.

- **Administrative fee cap clarified:** Authorizes a “reasonable administrative fee of **up to $25**” to the entity processing the violation as part of the advisory/payment request.
