## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s Medical Debt Protection Act (Title 6, Chapter 25J) to prohibit the reporting of medical debt information to consumer reporting agencies and prohibit use of medical debt information in credit, employment, or housing decisions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Prohibits **any person** from communicating with or reporting **medical debt information** to a **consumer reporting agency**.  
- Prohibits a **consumer reporting agency** from issuing a consumer report that it **knows or should know** contains medical debt information.  
- Prohibits users of consumer reports from treating **medical debt information** as a **negative factor** in **credit, employment, or housing** decisions.  
- Updates the definition of **“extraordinary collection action”** to include selling medical debt unless a written agreement includes multiple restrictions (e.g., no extraordinary collection actions, no interest, no reporting to credit bureaus).  
- Adds a definition for **“medical debt information”**: information pertaining to any debt owed for health care services.  
- Makes conforming changes to definition numbering (e.g., “Patient” and “Time of service”).  
- The bill text replaces prior detailed timing rules (e.g., “1 year” and “3 months” reporting delays) with a broader prohibition on reporting.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Credit protection:** Because the bill bars reporting medical debt information to consumer reporting agencies, consumers’ credit reports would not include medical debt information. (New § 2507J(a)-(b))  
- **Employment and housing protection:** The bill bars using medical debt information as a negative factor in **employment** and **housing** decisions when a consumer report is used. (New § 2507J(c))  
- **Limits downstream harms from debt sales:** If medical debt is sold, the definition of “extraordinary collection action” requires a written agreement with protections including: **no interest**, **no extraordinary collection actions**, **debt recall/return if financial assistance eligibility is found**, and **no reporting to consumer reporting agencies**. (§ 2502J(4)a.1–5)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced access to information for credit underwriting:** The bill prohibits medical debt information from being reported to or included in consumer reports and prohibits its use as a negative factor in credit decisions. (New § 2507J(a)-(c))  
- **Compliance burden for reporting and screening systems:** Consumer reporting agencies must ensure they do not issue reports containing medical debt information they “know or should know” is included, and report users must ensure they do not use medical debt information negatively. (New § 2507J(b)-(c))  
- **Less leverage for collectors via credit reporting:** Medical creditors/debt collectors would no longer be able to report medical debt information to consumer reporting agencies. (New § 2507J(a))

## Key Policy Changes
- **New prohibition on reporting:** “No person may communicate with or report any medical debt information to any consumer reporting agency.” (New § 2507J(a))  
- **New prohibition on inclusion in reports:** A consumer reporting agency may not make a consumer report it “knows or should know” contains medical debt information. (New § 2507J(b))  
- **New prohibition on use:** A person who uses a consumer report may not use medical debt information on the report as a negative factor in **credit, employment, or housing** decisions. (New § 2507J(c))  
- **Debt sale restrictions embedded in definition:** Selling an individual’s debt is treated as an “extraordinary collection action” unless a written agreement includes five listed protections, including a ban on communicating/reporting medical debt information to a consumer reporting agency. (§ 2502J(4)a.1–5)  
- **Adds definition:** “Medical debt information” is defined. (§ 2502J(13))  
- **Conforming renumbering:** Adjusts numbering for “Patient” and “Time of service.” (§ 2502J(13)-(15))

## Who Benefits
- **Patients/consumers with medical debt** whose medical debt information would no longer be reported to consumer reporting agencies or used negatively in credit, employment, or housing decisions. (New § 2507J(a)-(c))  
- **Individuals whose medical debt is sold** where the sale is conditioned on the listed consumer protections (no interest, no extraordinary collection actions, no credit reporting, etc.). (§ 2502J(4)a.1–5)

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Medical creditors and medical debt collectors** who previously used consumer reporting to pursue collection or influence repayment. (New § 2507J(a); changes from prior § 2507J timing-based framework)  
- **Consumer reporting agencies** that must implement processes to avoid including medical debt information they “know or should know” is present. (New § 2507J(b))  
- **Entities using consumer reports** (e.g., lenders, employers, housing providers) that must ensure medical debt information is not used as a negative factor. (New § 2507J(c))
