## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 11, Chapter 45 (new/updated § 4505) to create a postconviction remedy allowing a “convicted person” to seek relief when relevant **forensic scientific evidence** has changed or newly becomes available, and that evidence could reasonably have changed the outcome of the case.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a Superior Court petition process for postconviction relief based on **changes in forensic scientific or technical information** (11 Del. C. § 4505).
- Applies when relevant forensic scientific evidence **(a)** was not available before conviction or **(b)** undermines forensic scientific evidence presented at trial.
- Relief standard requires: (1) evidence is currently available and **was not ascertainable through reasonable diligence** before/during trial or before plea entry; and (2) a **“reasonable likelihood”** exists of a different outcome or no conviction if the evidence had been presented.
- Eligibility is broad and includes people **incarcerated**, on **parole/probation**, subject to **sex offender registry**, those who **completed sentences**, and those convicted by **trial or plea** (including **nolo contendere** and **not guilty by reason of insanity** verdicts).
- Petitions may be filed **“at any time”** for a person contending they did not commit the crime, if the evidence meets the statute’s criteria.
- Superior Court must set a hearing **no later than 90 days** after petition filing and notify the petitioner and Department of Justice (DOJ).
- DOJ may respond **no later than 30 days** after petition filing; petitioner may appeal a dismissal **within 30 days** after dismissal is entered.
- If a prima facie showing is made, the court must allow **additional discovery** (interpreted **broadly**) and hold a hearing; if relief standard is met, the court must **vacate the conviction** and grant a **new trial**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Creates a clear pathway to relief** when forensic science evolves or prior forensic testimony is undermined, potentially correcting wrongful convictions (remedy tied to “reasonable likelihood” of different outcome/no conviction).
- **Broad access**: includes people who pleaded guilty/nolo contendere, those who have completed sentences, and those on the sex offender registry (explicit eligibility list).
- **Timely court action required**: hearing must be set within **90 days** of filing, which may reduce delay in addressing qualifying claims.
- **Enhanced fact-finding**: if prima facie showing is met, the court must grant **additional discovery** and interpret discovery rights **broadly**, potentially improving access to investigative materials.
- **Protects experts from liability** for repudiating prior opinions when later science changes (explicit no-liability provision).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased judicial and prosecutorial workload** due to petitions that may be filed **“at any time”** and the requirement to schedule hearings within **90 days**, plus potential follow-on hearings and discovery.
- **Expanded discovery obligations** (including documents from the original investigation and identities of other suspects) may impose administrative burdens on agencies maintaining records.
- **Undefined scope of “reasonable likelihood”** and “reasonable diligence” (though referenced) could lead to more litigation over thresholds before merits determinations.

## Key Policy Changes
- Establishes/updates § 4505 creating a **postconviction remedy** based on **forensic scientific evidence** that was previously unavailable or that undermines trial forensic evidence.
- Defines key terms, including:
  - **“Conviction”** (includes guilty verdict/plea, nolo contendere plea, and **not guilty by reason of insanity** verdict).
  - **“Forensic science,” “forensic scientific evidence,”** and **“scientific knowledge”** (broadly defined).
- Sets relief criteria requiring:
  - Evidence **not ascertainable through reasonable diligence** before/during trial or before plea entry; and
  - A **reasonable likelihood** of a different outcome/no conviction if presented.
- Creates timing rules:
  - Superior Court hearing set **≤ 90 days** after filing.
  - DOJ response **≤ 30 days** after filing.
  - Appeal of dismissal **≤ 30 days** after dismissal entered.
- Requires a **prima facie screening**:
  - If not met: dismissal in a **written opinion**.
  - If met: **broad discovery** + hearing; if standard met, **vacate conviction** and grant **new trial**.
- Adds a protection: no liability for experts who repudiate prior opinions later undermined by science/technology.

## Who Benefits
- Convicted persons (including those convicted by plea, those no longer incarcerated, and those on supervision/registry) who can identify qualifying **new or undermining forensic scientific evidence**.
- The court system and public interest in accuracy of convictions, through a defined process for handling forensic-science-based claims (as structured in § 4505).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Department of Justice and agencies holding investigative/forensic records, due to response deadlines (**30 days**) and potentially expanded discovery obligations after a prima facie showing.
- Crime victims and witnesses may experience renewed litigation and potential retrials if convictions are vacated (the bill mandates new trials upon meeting the standard).
