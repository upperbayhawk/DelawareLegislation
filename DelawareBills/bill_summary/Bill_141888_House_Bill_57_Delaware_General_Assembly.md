## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware consumer protection and criminal statutes to regulate **home improvement contracts** (Title 6) and strengthen **home improvement fraud** penalties and contractor consequences (Title 11), including a new **debarment** consequence for repeat offenders.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **all home improvement contracts to be in writing**.  
- Creates a buyer **right to cancel** home improvement contracts **over $500** within **3 business days**, extended to **5 business days** for buyers **age 62+**, an **“adult who is impaired”** (Title 31 §3902), or a **“person with a disability”** (Title 12 §3901(a)(2)).  
- Mandates specific **12-point bold** cancellation-right language in contracts and an attached, detachable **“Notice of Cancellation”** form; requires **deposit refunds within 10 days** after cancellation notice is received.  
- Caps deposits at **10%** of contract price, with a limited exception allowing deposits tied to needed materials/services, capped at **33% of the cost** of those materials/professional services needed to commence work.  
- Requires a **payment schedule** for any pre-completion payments beyond the deposit, and requires changes to be documented by **contract amendment**.  
- Requires contractors to **apply for permits** by set deadlines (generally **within 30 days** of certain triggers) and to **start work by the contract start date**; if the contractor misses the start date without an allowed reason, the buyer may **terminate and obtain a refund of all payments**.  
- Makes violations of the new contract rules an **unlawful practice** under Delaware consumer protection law and provides a private right to recover **actual damages, court costs, and reasonable attorney’s fees** in related matters.  
- Adds a Title 11 **debarment** concept for individuals with **2+ qualifying convictions** (with at least **1 entered after the Act’s effective date**) and makes violating debarment a **Class D felony**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger consumer cancellation rights**: A clear rescission period (3 business days; **5 business days** for older adults/people with disabilities) is required for contracts where the buyer pays **more than $500**.  
- **Limits upfront financial exposure**: Deposit cap of **10%** (with a defined exception and a **33% of specified costs** limit) restricts large upfront payments.  
- **Improves transparency and documentation**: Written contract requirement, mandated bold disclosures, detachable cancellation form, and required payment schedules reduce ambiguity.  
- **Timelines for action**: Permit application deadlines (generally **30 days**) and start-date requirements give buyers enforceable timing expectations.  
- **Enhanced remedies**: Violations are treated as **unlawful practices**, and injured parties may seek **actual damages + costs + reasonable attorney’s fees**.  
- **Targets repeat fraud**: “Debarment” for individuals with **2+ qualifying convictions** and criminal consequences for violating debarment may deter repeat offenders.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added compliance and paperwork for contractors**: Requires written contracts, prescribed notices (including **12-point bold** text), detachable cancellation forms, payment schedules, and contract amendments for schedule changes.  
- **Potential cash-flow constraints**: Deposit limits (**10%** generally) may restrict contractor financing, except where the specified materials/services exception applies (capped at **33%** of those commencement-related costs).  
- **Operational timing pressure**: Permit applications must be made within **30 days** of specified triggers, and failure to start by the contract date (absent listed exceptions) gives the buyer termination and full refund rights.  
- **Increased litigation exposure**: Designation as an unlawful practice plus attorney’s fees availability may increase dispute and enforcement risk.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 6—New/expanded Home Improvement Protections (Chapter 36, Subchapter IV):**
  - **Written contract required** for all home improvement contracts.  
  - **Cancellation rights for contracts > $500**:
    - Standard: cancel by **midnight of the 3rd business day** after signing.
    - If **62+**, **adult who is impaired**, or **person with a disability**: cancel by **midnight of the 5th business day** after signing.
  - Contract must include a **12-point bold** cancellation statement; must attach an easily detachable **“Notice of Cancellation”** form including contractor name/address, transaction date, and cancellation deadline date.
  - Contractor must **return all deposits and other money** within **10 days** of receiving a cancellation notice.
  - Permits: contractor must apply for all required permits:
    - **30 days after 50% of any deposit** is paid; or
    - If **no deposit**, **within 30 days** after signing **or** by a mutually agreed date in the contract.
  - Deposit cap:
    - Default max **10%** of contract price.
    - If extra funds are required to purchase materials/professional services needed to reasonably begin, deposit limited to **33% of the cost** of those items needed to commence work.
  - Payment schedule required for pre-completion payments beyond deposit; changes require a **contract amendment**.
  - Contractor must start work by the contract date unless delayed by events beyond contractor control with **written notice** giving reason and new start date, or buyer agrees in writing to later date.
  - If the contractor cannot start by the contract date and no exception applies, buyer may **terminate** and obtain a **refund of all payments**.
  - Prohibits contract terms like **confession of judgment** and waivers of buyer rights; prohibits negotiating/transferring/assigning indebtedness to third parties until **after midnight of the 5th business day** following signing.
  - Enforcement: violating these provisions is an **unlawful practice** under §2513 and Chapter 25, Subchapter II; injured persons may recover **actual damages, court costs, and reasonable attorney’s fees**.
- **Title 6—Door-to-door sales alignment (§4404):**
  - Clarifies rules for door-to-door sales **not involving** a home improvement contract versus those **involving** a home improvement contract.
  - Adds that **beginning 1 year after the effective date of this section**, the standard door-to-door cancellation statement must be printed in **a conspicuous ink color** different from the rest of the contract/receipt.
  - For door-to-door sales involving home improvement contracts, the Notice of Cancellation must reflect the **5-business-day** cancellation right for **62+** or **people with disabilities**.
- **Title 11—Home improvement fraud (§916):**
  - Defines **“debarment”** for contractors with **2+ qualifying convictions**, with at least **1 entered after the Act’s effective date**, preventing registration/retaining registration under Title 19 contractor registration.
  - Allows debarred individuals to be employed by a home improvement business **only if they do not solicit work**.
  - Defines **“qualifying conviction”** (theft, home improvement fraud, or new home construction fraud, or comparable offenses from other jurisdictions) when arising from operating/soliciting/employing/ownership interest in a home improvement business.
  - Maintains/sets penalty tiers including a **$1,500** loss threshold and higher tiers at **$50,000–<$100,000** (Class D felony) and **≥ $100,000** (Class B felony).
  - Makes violating debarment a **Class D felony**, regardless of whether fraud occurred on existing projects.

## Who Benefits
- **Homeowners/consumers** entering home improvement contracts, especially those paying **> $500**.  
- **Consumers age 62+**, **adults who are impaired**, and **persons with disabilities**, who receive an extended **5-business-day** cancellation period and enhanced fraud penalty provisions.  
- Consumers seeking refunds or enforcement, due to **unlawful practice** designation and potential recovery of **actual damages, costs, and reasonable attorney’s fees**.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Home improvement contractors/sellers**, due to deposit caps, disclosure/form requirements, permit application deadlines, start-date requirements, refund obligations, and unlawful practice exposure.  
- **Individuals with 2+ qualifying convictions** (with at least 1 after the effective date), who may face **debarment** from contractor registration and a **Class D felony** for violating debarment.
