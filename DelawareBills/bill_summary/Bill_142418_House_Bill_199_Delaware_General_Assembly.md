## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 18 Del. C. § 705 to ensure that when two or more fire companies or departments merge or consolidate within a county, the resulting entity does not lose (and instead retains) the combined shares of distributions derived from insurance premium taxes allocated to fire companies/departments.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates the allocation rules in **18 Del. C. § 705(b)(2)** governing distribution of the sum described in **§ 705(c)** to Wilmington and to fire companies/departments statewide.  
- Keeps the **City of Wilmington** allocation tied to the **premiums attributable to the City of Wilmington** (no change described beyond reaffirming this rule).  
- For **New Castle County outside Wilmington**, distributions are divided **equally among the fire companies/departments** in that area.  
- For **Kent County**, distributions are divided **equally among the fire companies/departments** in the county.  
- For **Sussex County**, distributions are divided **equally among the fire companies/departments** in the county.  
- **New rule after the bill’s effective date:** if fire companies/departments in a county **merge or consolidate**, the resulting entity receives the **same combined allocations** the merging entities would have received before the merger/consolidation (i.e., no reduction due to consolidation).  
- The Insurance Commissioner’s annual reporting deadline to the State Treasurer remains **“on or before May 15 of each year.”**

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Removes a financial disincentive to consolidation:** The synopsis states the Act allows fire companies/departments to **merge or consolidate without reduction** of their share of the insurance-premium-tax distributions.  
- **Funding stability during restructuring:** Explicitly provides that the post-merger entity receives the **allocations the prior entities would have been entitled to** (county-by-county provisions).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **May preserve legacy distribution levels despite fewer entities:** Because a merged entity keeps the combined allocations, equal-per-company distribution formulas will no longer reduce total payouts to a consolidated structure within a county (text explicitly preserves prior entitlements post-merger).  
- **Potential distribution rigidity:** The bill ties post-merger allocations to what entities “would have been entitled to receive” prior to merger/consolidation, potentially limiting redistribution effects that might otherwise occur under an equal-split-per-company approach (as described in the amended allocation language).
