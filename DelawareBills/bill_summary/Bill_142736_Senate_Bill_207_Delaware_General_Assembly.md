## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s aviation jet fuel tax exemption for “economic development and job creation” to **disqualify and require revocation** of the exemption for commercial airlines that transport individuals detained by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) for deportation **without specified warrant and due-process safeguards**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates **30 Del. C. § 5172(b)(4)** (aviation jet fuel tax exemption tied to economic development/job creation).
- Keeps the existing framework that allows a **temporary exemption for up to 5 years**.
- Requires the exemption to be **approved** by the **Council on Development Finance** and the **Director of Small Business** (references **29 Del. C. §§ 8705A, 8707A** and **30 Del. C. § 5178**).
- Adds a prohibition: the exemption **may not be approved** and **must be revoked** for any commercial airline that transports ICE detainees for deportation **without** meeting at least one listed condition.
- Condition (a): **Presentation of a valid judicial warrant or order** issued by a **federal judge or magistrate**; **ICE administrative immigration detainers do not qualify**.
- Condition (b): **Demonstration of due process** afforded to the detained individual, including **right to counsel** and a **hearing before an immigration judge**.
- Effective date: **30 days after enactment into law**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Creates clear eligibility limits** for a state tax benefit by tying the jet fuel tax exemption to specified legal safeguards (judicial warrant/order or demonstrated due process).
- **Strengthens oversight of the exemption** by directing that it “**may not be approved**” and “**must be revoked**” for noncompliant commercial airlines (explicit mandatory language).
- May help ensure state economic development incentives are not provided to airlines participating in deportation transport **without** the listed warrant/due-process standards (as stated in the bill text).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Could **reduce access to the aviation jet fuel tax exemption** for some commercial airlines, which may **reduce the incentive’s usefulness** for the stated purpose of “economic development and job creation in the aviation industry” (30 Del. C. § 5172(b)(4)).
- May increase administrative burden on the approving authorities by requiring determinations about whether an airline has satisfied condition (a) or (b) before approval and for continued eligibility (revocation requirement).

## Key Policy Changes
- Amends **30 Del. C. § 5172(b)(4)** to add a new disqualification/mandatory revocation rule:
  - The jet fuel tax exemption for economic development/job creation **cannot be approved** and **must be revoked** for a commercial airline transporting ICE detainees for deportation **unless** the airline has:
    - **(a)** a **valid judicial warrant/order** issued by a **federal judge or magistrate** (excluding ICE administrative detainers), **or**
    - **(b)** a **demonstration** that the detained individual received **due process**, including **right to counsel** and a **hearing before an immigration judge**.
- Retains the cap that the exemption is **temporary** and may not exceed **5 years**.

## Who Benefits
- Individuals detained by ICE for deportation, to the extent the policy encourages transport only where a **judicial warrant/order** is presented or **due process** (including counsel and an immigration judge hearing) is demonstrated.
- State policymakers seeking to condition an **economic development tax benefit** on specified legal-process safeguards.
