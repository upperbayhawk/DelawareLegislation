## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Exempt certain “military family child care providers” from Delaware’s state child care licensing requirements under Title 14, and assign responsibility to the U.S. Department of Defense (DoD) or U.S. Coast Guard (USCG) for approving/determining which children those exempt providers serve.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Exempts “military family child care providers” from the definition of “child care” for purposes of Chapter 30A, Title 14.  
- Defines “military family child care provider” as a family child care home/large family child care home serving **only** dependent children eligible for **DoD subsidized care** and that is **(a)** located on a **military base or federal property**, **or (b)** certified by a branch of **DoD or USCG**, **or both**.  
- Exempts military family child care providers from § 3004A child care licensing/investigation/requirements/notice/hearings/appeals (i.e., OCCL licensing requirements do not apply).  
- Specifies that DoD/USCG (or their agents, including an installation commander) must assume responsibility for **approving or determining which children are served** by the exempt military providers.  
- Leaves in place the separate exemption for a **maternity ward of a general hospital** (minor conforming edit).  
- Effective **60 days after enactment**; the act was **approved July 23, 2025**, making the effective date **September 21, 2025** (60 days after July 23, 2025).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces state regulatory burden** for qualifying military child care homes by exempting them from Delaware OCCL licensing requirements (§ 3004A does not apply to them).  
- **Clarifies oversight responsibility** by placing the determination of eligible children served with **DoD/USCG or their agents** (including installation commanders).  
- **Provides a clear statutory definition** of “military family child care provider,” including eligibility limits (must serve only dependent children eligible for DoD subsidized care) and qualifying conditions (location on base/federal property and/or DoD/USCG certification).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduces Delaware’s direct oversight** of exempt providers by removing them from state licensing, investigation, and related procedures under § 3004A.  
- **Limits applicability to a narrow population**, because providers must serve **only** dependent children eligible for **DoD subsidized care** to qualify for the exemption.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Amends § 3002A (Definitions):**
  - Adds **“Military family child care providers”** to the list of arrangements that “child care” **does not include** for Chapter 30A purposes.
  - Creates/sets the definition of **“military family child care provider”**:
    - Must be a **family child care home or large family child care home**;
    - Must serve **only dependent children eligible for DoD subsidized care**; and
    - Must be **located on a military base or federal property** and/or **certified by DoD or USCG**.
- **Amends § 3004A (Child care licenses; investigation; requirements; notice; hearings and appeals):**
  - Specifies § 3004A **does not apply** to a **military family child care provider**.
  - Assigns DoD/USCG (or agents, including installation commanders) responsibility for **approving/determining which children are served** by exempt providers.

## Who Benefits
- **Military family child care providers** meeting the statutory definition (exempt from Delaware licensing requirements).  
- **Military families** whose dependent children are eligible for **DoD subsidized care** and use these providers.  
- **DoD/USCG** installations, by having explicit statutory recognition that they control eligibility determinations for children served by exempt providers.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Delaware OCCL/state regulators**, to the extent the state’s licensing authority and related oversight processes do not apply to exempt providers.  
- **Non-DoD-subsidized families/children**, because providers must serve **only** dependent children eligible for DoD subsidized care to qualify for the exemption (potentially limiting who can be served by exempt providers).
