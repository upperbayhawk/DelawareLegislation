## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 21 of the Delaware Code to require removal of accumulated ice and snow from vehicle exterior surfaces before operation, establish civil penalties for violations (including higher penalties when dislodged snow/ice causes damage or injury), and authorize administrative traffic stops to enforce the requirement.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires a person operating a vehicle to remove accumulated ice and snow from the vehicle’s exterior surfaces before driving.  
- Sets a civil fine of **$25 to $75** for violating the basic removal requirement, with limits on how often penalties may be issued (**no more than 1 penalty per 24-hour period**).  
- Treats **each day** of noncompliance as a **separate violation** for the basic fine provision.  
- Creates higher civil penalties when snow/ice dislodges from a moving vehicle and causes **property damage or physical injury**: **$200–$1,000** (non-commercial) and **$500–$1,500** (commercial vehicle-related parties).  
- Exempts removal of accumulating snow/ice (other than the windshield) **during continuous operation** of the vehicle.  
- Prohibits driver license points and prohibits entries on a person’s driving record for failing to comply with this section.  
- Authorizes police to make an **administrative stop** on **reasonable and articulable suspicion** of a violation.  
- Exempts operation on roads with speed limits of **25 mph or less**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Road safety / reduced debris risk:** By requiring removal of accumulated snow/ice before operation and imposing higher penalties when dislodged snow/ice causes damage or injury, the bill targets hazards from snow/ice leaving vehicles and striking other vehicles or people. (Sections (a) and (d))  
- **Stronger deterrence when harm occurs:** Escalated penalties tied to actual **property damage or physical injury** create a clearer deterrent for dangerous noncompliance. (Section (d))  
- **Enforcement tool added:** Authorizing an administrative stop based on reasonable suspicion may improve enforceability. (Section (f))  
- **Limits collateral licensing consequences:** No points and no driving-record entry for violations of this section reduces long-term licensing impacts. (Section (e))  
- **Operational practicality:** Continuous-operation exception (other than windshield) acknowledges that snow/ice may accumulate while driving. (Section (c))

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Expanded police stop authority:** The bill expressly authorizes administrative stops to enforce this section, which could increase traffic stops for snow/ice compliance. (Section (f))  
- **Uneven applicability by roadway type:** The law does not apply on roads with speed limits **25 mph or less**, potentially creating inconsistent safety expectations and enforcement. (Section (g))  
- **Potential compliance burden:** Drivers must clear “exterior surfaces” prior to operation, which may require additional time and effort in winter conditions. (Section (a))  
- **Higher exposure for commercial vehicle stakeholders:** Penalties may be imposed on the **operator, owner, lessee, or bailee** of a commercial vehicle when dislodged snow/ice causes damage or injury. (Section (d)(2))

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/updated duty:** Requires removal of accumulated ice and snow from vehicle exterior surfaces **prior to operation**. (Section (a))  
- **Basic civil penalty:** **$25–$75** per violation; **each day** is a separate violation; **no more than 1 penalty per 24-hour period**. (Section (b))  
- **Continuous-operation exception:** No requirement to remove accumulating ice/snow (except windshield) while the vehicle is being continuously operated. (Section (c))  
- **Enhanced penalties when harm occurs:**  
  - Non-commercial driver: **$200–$1,000** per occurrence causing property damage or physical injury. (Section (d)(1))  
  - Commercial vehicle: **$500–$1,500** per occurrence, applicable to **operator, owner, lessee, or bailee**. (Section (d)(2))  
- **No points / no driving record entry:** No motor vehicle points and no entry on driving record for failing to comply. (Section (e))  
- **Administrative stop authority:** Police may stop a vehicle on reasonable and articulable suspicion to enforce this section. (Section (f))  
- **Speed-limit exemption:** Does not apply on roadways with speed limits of **25 miles per hour or less**. (Section (g))
