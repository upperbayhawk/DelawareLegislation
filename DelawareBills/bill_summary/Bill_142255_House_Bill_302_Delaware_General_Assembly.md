## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 29 of the Delaware Code to require state government public-facing websites to be available, free of charge, in any language spoken by at least **0.5%** of Delaware’s overall population (per the most recent U.S. Census), with standards set by the Government Information Center and specific exceptions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires **all State department, agency, and program websites** used by the general public to be available in languages spoken by **limited English proficient** populations that are at least **0.5%** of Delaware’s population (most recent U.S. Census).  
- Translated access must be **free of charge** to the user.  
- Allows **machine translation** but requires a **conspicuous disclaimer** that the agency (a) does **not guarantee accuracy/reliability** and (b) is **not liable** for losses from use/reliance on translated content.  
- Creates exceptions: translation is not required if the agency determines inaccurate translation could cause a **denial of services/benefits**, or if content cannot be translated due to machine translation limitations (including **PDFs, images, videos**).  
- Directs the **Government Information Center (GIC)** to set **minimum standards**, including **prominent placement of links** on English pages to each required translated version.  
- **Effective immediately** upon enactment; GIC standards due within **6 months**, and full implementation due within **2 years** of enactment.  
- Fiscal note estimates **FY 2026 one-time costs of $6,263,852–$6,752,140** and **FY 2026 recurring costs of $297,194–$637,194**, with recurring costs assumed to grow **5%–10% annually**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- Improves language access to state online services for Delaware residents with **limited English proficiency** by requiring translations for languages meeting the **0.5%** threshold.  
- Ensures translated access is **free of charge**, reducing barriers to information and services.  
- Establishes statewide consistency via GIC **minimum standards**, including required **prominent links** from English pages to translated versions.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Significant projected costs to build and maintain translation capabilities across state websites and applications (e.g., **DTI one-time $6,180,000**; FY 2026 total one-time **$6.26M–$6.75M**).  
- Translation may be incomplete for certain content types due to stated limitations (explicitly including **PDF format, images, and videos**), potentially leaving gaps in access.  
- Use of machine translation expressly disclaims accuracy and agency liability, which may reduce reliability of translated content for users (disclaimer required when machine translation is used).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds/updates **29 Del. C. § 10608 (State government websites)** to require translation availability for public-facing state websites into languages meeting the **0.5%** population threshold (most recent U.S. Census).  
- Requires translation to be **free of charge**.  
- Permits **machine translation** with mandatory disclaimers on accuracy and liability when used.  
- Creates two explicit exceptions to translation requirement:  
  1) agency determination that inaccurate translation could lead to **denial of services/benefits**;  
  2) content cannot be translated due to machine translation limitations, including **PDFs, images, videos**.  
- Requires GIC to establish **minimum standards**, including **prominent placement** of links from the English site to each required translated version.

## Who Benefits
- Delaware residents and members of the public with **limited English proficiency** who speak languages that meet the **0.5%** statewide population threshold (per most recent U.S. Census).
