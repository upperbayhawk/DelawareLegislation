## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Direct the Delaware Department of Safety and Homeland Security (DSHS) to create and distribute a “Firearm Responsibilities Notice,” and require licensed firearm sellers (and dealers facilitating private-party transfers) to have prospective buyers review and sign the notice, provide the buyer a copy, and retain signed copies as part of dealer records.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires licensed importers, manufacturers, and dealers to **obtain a buyer’s signed Firearm Responsibilities Notice** before selling/transferring/delivering a firearm from inventory (Title 11, § 1448A(n)).  
- Requires dealers facilitating transfers **between unlicensed persons** to: run the background check, **have the buyer review and sign the Notice**, and **provide the buyer a copy** (Title 24, § 904A(b)).  
- Requires licensed deadly weapons dealers to **keep a copy of signed Notices** (when required) as part of their transaction records (Title 24, § 904(a)(1)f.).  
- Sets a minimum **record-retention period of 3 years** for signed Firearm Responsibilities Notices; allows destruction after 3 years; limits disclosure except as required by law (Title 24, § 904(c)).  
- Limits inspections of dealer records by state officials to specific circumstances (e.g., **open criminal investigation/prosecution**, or **valid warrant/subpoena**, including an Attorney General investigative subpoena) (Title 24, § 904(d)).  
- Directs DSHS to publish the Notice in **plain, concise language** covering enumerated topics (lost/stolen reporting duties; straw purchasing; lawful transfers; false statements/ID; safety; suicide prevention hotline; safe storage/child access laws; sensitive locations) (Title 24, § 907(a)).  
- Requires DSHS to **annually consult** with DOJ and stakeholders on updates, publish proposed revisions in the **Register of Regulations** with a means for **public comment**, post the Notice online, and provide it **free of charge** to licensed sellers (Title 24, § 907(b)).  
- **Effective 180 days after enactment**; DSHS must conduct **pre-effective-date outreach** to licensed sellers about the new requirements and provide the Notice or an internet address to access it (Sections 3–4).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardized consumer notice on legal duties and safety**: The Notice must include safe storage/child access prevention summaries, firearm safety, and sensitive-location restrictions (Title 24, § 907(a)(5), (7), (8)).  
- **Suicide-prevention information required**: Must include firearm suicide-prevention information and **national suicide prevention hotline contact information** (Title 24, § 907(a)(6)).  
- **Deterrence/education related to illegal transfers**: Must include penalties and examples of **straw purchases** and steps to lawfully transfer a firearm (Title 24, § 907(a)(2)–(3)); synopsis states the Notice is intended to deter straw purchases and illegal transfers.  
- **Improved awareness of lost/stolen reporting obligations**: Must describe responsibilities and penalties related to lost/stolen firearms and violations of **11 Del. C. § 1461** (Title 24, § 907(a)(1)).  
- **Clear retention and confidentiality rules**: Requires secure custody/confidentiality of records and restricts disclosure of signed Notices except as required by law (Title 24, § 904(c), (e)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Added compliance steps at point of sale/transfer**: Licensed sellers must ensure the buyer reviews and signs the Notice and must provide a copy (Title 11, § 1448A(n); Title 24, § 904A(b)).  
- **Recordkeeping and storage burden**: Dealers must retain signed Notices for **no less than 3 years** and securely maintain confidentiality (Title 24, § 904(c), (e)).  
- **Potential criminal exposure for violations**: Violations of §§ 901, 903, 904, or 904A are punishable by **up to $500 fine or up to 6 months imprisonment, or both** (Title 24, § 906) (and the bill expands the scope of these misdemeanors per synopsis).  
- **Limits on routine inspections may affect oversight**: Inspections must be “reasonable” and limited to an open criminal investigation/prosecution or require a **valid warrant/subpoena** (including AG investigative subpoena) (Title 24, § 904(d)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **New buyer-signature requirement** for a DSHS-published Firearm Responsibilities Notice before licensed sellers may sell/transfer/deliver a firearm from inventory (Title 11, § 1448A(n)(1)–(2)).  
- **Private-sale facilitation updates**: When a dealer facilitates a transfer between unlicensed persons, dealer must have buyer sign the Notice and provide a copy, in addition to conducting the background check (Title 24, § 904A(b)(2)).  
- **Dealer records expanded**: Transaction record must include **a copy of a signed Firearm Responsibilities Notice** when required (Title 24, § 904(a)(1)f.).  
- **Record retention and confidentiality**: Signed Notices must be retained for **at least 3 years**, may be destroyed after; cannot be disclosed/copied/published except as required by law; confidentiality and secure custody required (Title 24, § 904(c), (e)).  
- **Inspections constrained**: Record inspections by state officials are limited to defined circumstances (open criminal investigation/prosecution or warrant/subpoena/AG investigative subpoena) (Title 24, § 904(d)).  
- **DSHS duties created**: Publish/maintain the Notice, annual consultation, Register of Regulations process with public comment, web posting, and free distribution to licensees (Title 24, § 907).  
- **Penalties reference**: Violations of §§ 901, 903, 904, or 904A are punishable by **up to $500** or **up to 6 months** imprisonment, or both (Title 24, § 906). (Synopsis notes the Act expands the scope of existing misdemeanors.)

## Who Benefits
- Firearm purchasers, who must be provided a copy of the Firearm Responsibilities Notice (Title 11, § 1448A(n)(2); Title 24, § 904A(b)(2)c.).  
- The general public, to the extent standardized information on safe storage, lawful transfers, and suicide prevention is disseminated (Title 24, § 907(a)).  
- Licensed sellers, who receive the Notice **free of charge** from DSHS and can rely on a standardized state-issued notice (Title 24, § 907(b)(3)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Licensed importers, manufacturers, and dealers, who must add a signature-and-copy step to covered transactions and retain signed notices for **3 years** (Title 11, § 1448A(n); Title 24, § 904(c)).  
- Dealers facilitating private-party transfers, who must implement the Notice process and retain records (Title 24, § 904A(b); § 904).  
- Individuals subject to enforcement for noncompliance, given misdemeanor penalties (Title 24, § 906).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements (licensed inventory sales)**: Seller must (1) require buyer to review and sign the Firearm Responsibilities Notice and (2) provide buyer a copy (Title 11, § 1448A(n)(1)–(2)).  
- **Compliance requirements (dealer-facilitated private transfers)**: Dealer must run background check under 11 Del. C. § 1448A and require buyer to review/sign the Notice and provide a copy; dealer must securely maintain records per § 904 (Title 24, § 904A(b)).  
- **Recordkeeping**: Dealers must include a copy of signed Notice in required records when applicable; must keep records securely; must keep signed Notices **≥ 3 years**; confidentiality/secure custody required; restrictions on disclosure (Title 24, § 904(a), (c), (e)).  
- **Inspections**: Record inspection by certain state officials must be reasonable and limited to an open criminal investigation/prosecution, or conducted pursuant to a **valid warrant or subpoena** (court) or **valid investigative subpoena issued by the Attorney General** (Title 24, § 904(d)).  
- **Penalties**: Violation of §§ 901, 903, 904, or 904A is punishable by a **fine up to $500** or **imprisonment up to 6 months**, or both (Title 24, § 906).
