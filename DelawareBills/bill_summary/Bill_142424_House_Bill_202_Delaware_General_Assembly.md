## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s electronic speed monitoring civil-penalty statute (21 Del. C. § 4170A) to allow a specific additional **$10 assessment** (11 Del. C. § 4101(j)) to be added to certain electronically-enforced speeding violations beyond just work zones, and to make technical drafting corrections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Keeps electronically-detected speeding penalties tied to **21 Del. C. § 4169(c)** for the base civil penalty amount.  
- Changes what “assessments” can be added to electronic speed monitoring violations under **21 Del. C. § 4170A(g)**.  
- Previously, listed assessments “**shall**” only be assessed for violations in a **“work zone”**; the bill changes this to “**may** only be assessed” for work-zone violations (text change in § 4170A(g)(1)).  
- The bill’s synopsis states it newly allows the **11 Del. C. § 4101(j)** assessment to be imposed for an electronically-proven speeding violation even when it is **not** in a work zone.  
- The **§ 4101(j)** assessment amount is **$10**.  
- The $10 assessment is paid to the **Volunteer Ambulance Company Fund**.  
- Makes technical corrections to conform to the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual.  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Increases dedicated funding** for emergency services: the bill expressly directs a **$10 assessment** to the **Volunteer Ambulance Company Fund** for qualifying violations.  
- **Expands applicability** of the $10 assessment: per the synopsis, it is no longer limited to speeding occurring in a **work zone** when proven by electronic speed monitoring.  
- **Clarifies/updates statutory language** via technical corrections (as stated in the synopsis).  

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher total amount owed by drivers** cited via electronic speed monitoring where the **$10 assessment** is added (synopsis).  
- **Potentially uneven fee application**: the text still limits certain other assessments to **work zone** violations, while the synopsis indicates at least one additional assessment is newly allowed more broadly; the combined effect may be confusing to the public (see ambiguities below).
