## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Update and standardize Delaware Code terminology and cross-references for people who can make **financial and health-care decisions** for someone else (third-party decision making), to align with the **Delaware Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act of 2023** and related laws, and to make technical drafting corrections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Standardizes terms across Titles **12, 16, 21, and 31** by replacing older/variant phrasing (e.g., “medical durable power of attorney for health-care decisions”) with references to **“advance health-care directive”** as defined in **Title 16 § 2502**.
- Updates Public Guardian “last resort” definition to explicitly include situations involving **agents under a durable power of attorney**, health-care POA agents, **default surrogates**, guardians, **representative payees**, and **VA fiduciaries** (Title 12 § 3982).
- Clarifies that the Public Guardian may recommend or refer a **guardian or default surrogate decision maker** (Title 12 § 3983).
- In long-term care “resident’s rights,” removes a provision listing who receives devolved rights (repeals **Title 16 § 1121(b)(40)**) and relies on the devolution statute in § 1122 instead.
- Revises the **standard for when resident rights devolve** to a representative in facilities, including adding triggers tied to **federal law** and to rebuttal of capacity under **Title 16 § 2504** (Title 16 § 1122).
- Adds/clarifies that rights may devolve to a **representative payee or sponsoring agency** selected under **Social Security Act § 205(j) (42 U.S.C. § 405(j))**, with a limitation when the **facility itself** is the representative payee (Title 16 § 1122(6)).
- Updates DMV “medical condition” designation to include execution of an **advance health-care directive (Chapter 25, Title 16)** and **DMOST (Chapter 25A, Title 16)**, including **effective out-of-state orders** (Title 21 § 2718).
- Effective date is **September 30, 2025**; the act was **approved July 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved consistency and clarity in law:** Multiple sections replace synonyms and correct citations by referencing “advance health-care directive” under **Title 16 § 2502**, reducing conflicting terminology across agencies and settings.
- **Clearer prioritization/documentation expectations for clinicians:** DMOST statute reiterates that practitioners determine the authorized representative by **referencing documentation** giving authority, and regulations must explain priority (Title 16 § 2503A(12)).
- **More complete framework for facility rights devolution:** Explicitly includes a **representative payee/sponsoring agency** under federal law in the devolution list, with a guardrail where the facility is the payee (Title 16 § 1122(6)).
- **More accurate DMV designation references:** DMV “medical condition” definition now directly references the relevant planning documents (advance directive and DMOST), including out-of-state orders effective under Delaware law (Title 21 § 2718(c)(5)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Removal of a DDDS disqualification provision:** Repeals Title 16 § 5530(e), which had disqualified an alternate decision maker if there was a **protection from abuse petition** or **civil/criminal no-contact order** involving the DDDS service recipient.
- **Facility devolution trigger “unable to communicate” removed from § 1122 text:** The amended § 1122 deletes the explicit standard of being “unable to communicate with others” and instead relies on federal-law representative need and rebuttal of capacity under § 2504 (may change how facilities interpret triggers, depending on how other law applies).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effect of repealing Title 16 § 5530(e):** The bill repeals the explicit DDDS disqualification language but does not specify in this text what substitute safeguard applies within DDDS decision-maker selection beyond “citing directly to DUHCDA 2023” (as described in the Synopsis).
- **Implementation details for authorized representative priority:** Title 16 § 2503A(12) states regulations “shall explain the priority set by law,” but this act does not include the regulatory text or timelines for updating those regulations.
- **Practical meaning of revised § 1122 trigger language:** The new devolution standard references federal law and § 2504 rebuttal of capacity, but the bill does not describe operational criteria for facilities beyond those cross-references.

## Health Impact
- Updates multiple statutes governing **health-care decision-making authority** when a person lacks capacity, including:
  - Facility residents’ rights compliance with **advance health-care directives** and **DMOST** (Title 16 § 1121(b)(39)).
  - Who may act as an **authorized representative** for DMOST execution (Title 16 § 2503A(12)) and in related contexts (e.g., background checks definition in Title 16 § 1145(b)(2)).
  - How resident rights devolve in facilities when capacity is not presumed or is rebutted, including inclusion of **representative payees** under federal law (Title 16 § 1122).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 12 (Public Guardian)**
  - Updates “last resort” definition to include lack of a suitable person willing/able to serve as:
    - agent under a **durable power of attorney**,
    - agent under a **power of attorney for health care**,
    - **default surrogate decision maker**,
    - guardian, representative payee, or VA fiduciary (Title 12 § 3982(4)a).
  - Clarifies “last resort” also includes when such a person exists but a court finds the person **not suitable** and appointing the Public Guardian is in the incapacitated person’s **best interest** (Title 12 § 3982(4)b).
  - Public Guardian may recommend/referral to a **guardian or default surrogate decision maker**, or refer to entities willing to serve as guardian/representative payee/VA fiduciary (Title 12 § 3983(3)a).

- **Title 16 (Long-term care resident rights; DMOST; DDDS)**
  - **Repeals** Title 16 § 1121(b)(40) (the resident-rights section listing representatives).
  - Revises Title 16 § 1122 to define when rights devolve and to include:
    - federal-law representative need,
    - rebuttal of capacity presumption under **Title 16 § 2504**,
    - and a **representative payee/sponsoring agency** under **42 U.S.C. § 405(j)** (with limitation if the facility is the payee).
  - Updates “authorized representative” definitions (Title 16 § 1145(b)(2); Title 16 § 2503A(12)) to use “advance health-care directive” (as defined under **§ 2502**) and “default surrogate.”
  - DDDS: updates definition cross-references in “alternative decision maker” (Title 16 § 5530(a)) and **repeals** Title 16 § 5530(e) (disqualification language).
  - DDDS elective surgery consent statute updated to reference “advance health-care directive” terminology (Title 16 § 5531(a)(3)).

- **Title 21 (DMV)**
  - DMV “medical condition” designation includes execution of an **advance health-care directive (Chapter 25)** or **DMOST (Chapter 25A)**, including effective out-of-state DMOST orders (Title 21 § 2718(c)(5)).

- **Title 31 (Confidentiality/APS-type records)**
  - Allows consent for disclosure when the recipient lacks capacity to be given by:
    - the recipient’s **POA agent** under Title 12 Chapters 49 or 49A,
    - an **agent or default surrogate** under Title 16 Chapter 25,
    - or legal guardian (Title 31 § 3912(a)(1)b).

## Who Benefits
- Individuals who lack capacity and rely on **advance directives, default surrogates, guardians, representative payees, or VA fiduciaries**, through clearer statutory alignment and cross-references.
- Health-care providers and facilities that must identify an **authorized representative** and comply with **advance directives/DMOST**, through standardized terminology and clarified reference points.
- Public Guardian office and courts, through clarified “last resort” circumstances and consistent terminology.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- DDDS service recipients who previously benefited from the explicit statutory **disqualification** of certain alternate decision makers under **Title 16 § 5530(e)** (e.g., where there is a protection from abuse petition or no-contact order), because that subsection is **repealed**.
