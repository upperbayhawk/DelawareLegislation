## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Create a new Chapter 69A in Title 21 regulating **non-consensual towing** (tows without the vehicle owner/operator’s consent) by **private tow companies** from **private and public parking areas**, including **signage, documentation, fee limits, storage access requirements, and consumer remedies**, and make violations enforceable as an **“unlawful practice”** under Delaware consumer protection law.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Applies to **non-consensual towing of non-abandoned motor vehicles weighing 5,000 pounds or less** from **private or public parking areas** (with specified exceptions).  
- Requires **pre-tow photos** documenting unauthorized parking and **2-year record retention**; requires **free copies** to the vehicle owner/operator on request (including by email if requested).  
- For tows from **private parking areas held open to the public**, requires **prior written authorization** from the property owner (or authorized person) for the **specific tow**, including name, contact info, date/time, and signature (electronic allowed).  
- Mandates **detailed signage** at entrances (minimum **1-inch lettering**) and **tow truck door signage** (minimum **3-inch lettering**) identifying tow company and key terms (including fees and storage location/hours).  
- Creates rules for “incomplete tows,” including **mandatory stop** if the owner returns before the vehicle leaves, a **drop/decoupling fee cap at 50%** of the tow fee, and **no fee** if the owner returns **before physical attachment**.  
- Regulates storage facilities: must be open **8 a.m.–6 p.m., at least 5 days/week (excluding holidays)**; must allow **free retrieval of personal items** during business hours; must accept **major credit/debit cards** or provide an ATM with **reasonable fees**; must allow after-hours access with a **$50 maximum** after-hours fee and a **90-minute access rule** tied to posted instructions.  
- Caps consumer charges for certain vehicles: **towing fee max $250** and **daily storage max $50** for an **owner/operator of a non-commercial motor vehicle or a rental vehicle under 18 Del. C. § 2002**, and **prohibits any other towing-related charges** beyond those maximums.  
- Makes any violation an **unlawful practice** (Title 6, Chapter 25) enforceable by the **Department of Justice Consumer Protection Unit**; provides reimbursement/damages, joint-and-several liability, and potential attorneys’ fees (with a **10-business-day** settlement-offer prerequisite).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces surprise or excessive fees** by: (1) requiring public posting of rates, (2) requiring fees be “reasonable” and not “excessive,” and (3) imposing explicit caps of **$250 towing** and **$50/day storage** for covered vehicles, with **no other towing-related charges** allowed.  
- **Improves documentation and dispute resolution** through mandatory **photographs** before towing, **free access** to those records for vehicle owners/operators, and **2-year retention**.  
- **Limits predatory towing practices** by prohibiting paying benefits for “tips” about unauthorized vehicles and restricting patrol/surveillance towing unless specific conditions (including contract and compliance with pre-tow requirements) are met.  
- **Expands consumer access and fairness at storage lots**: requires business hours (**8 a.m.–6 p.m.**, **≥5 days/week**), **after-hours accommodations**, and **free retrieval of personal items** during business hours.  
- **Enhances payment flexibility** by requiring acceptance of **major credit/debit cards** or an ATM with **reasonable** access/service fees.  
- **Strengthens enforcement tools** by defining violations as an **unlawful practice** under Title 6 consumer protection law and providing **reimbursement and damages** for illegal tows.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased compliance burden and operating constraints** on private tow companies and storage facilities (e.g., required signage content/format, photo documentation, **2-year** recordkeeping, payment system requirements, and business-hour/after-hours access logistics).  
- **Revenue limitations** for certain non-consensual tow situations due to explicit caps (**$250** tow, **$50/day** storage) and the ban on “other towing-related charges” for covered vehicle owners/operators.  
- **Potential administrative burden for property owners/managers** who must provide **prior written authorization** for certain private-lot tows (when the lot is held open to the public) and ensure compliant signage is posted.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The statute requires “reasonable accommodations” for after-hours release, but **does not define what accommodations are sufficient** beyond the posting requirements and the **$50 maximum** after-hours fee.  
- Fees must be “reasonable and not excessive,” with “presumptively excessive” thresholds (over **25%** vs the company’s consensual fees; over **50%** vs other county operators), but the bill text **does not specify how evidence is gathered or who determines county comparators** outside of litigation/enforcement.  
- The cap in § 6907A(c) applies to an “owner or operator of a non-commercial motor vehicle or rental vehicle under § 2002 of Title 18.” The bill text **does not define “non-commercial motor vehicle” within Chapter 69A**.  
- The chapter applies to vehicles **weighing 5,000 pounds or less**; the bill text **does not specify how weight is determined** at the time of towing for borderline cases.  
- For public parking area towing, the bill requires a contract with the State/political subdivision and signage placement rules; the text **does not address how quickly signage must be installed/updated** when contractors or rates change.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates new Title 21, Chapter 69A** governing private, non-consensual towing of **≤ 5,000-lb** non-abandoned vehicles from **private/public parking areas**.  
- **Pre-towing requirements**: signage must already comply; tow operator must **photograph** unauthorized parking; for certain private-lot tows, must obtain **prior written authorization** containing: authorizer name, phone/email, date/time, and signature (electronic permitted).  
- **Recordkeeping and consumer access**: photos/docs retained **minimum 2 years** and provided **free** to the owner/operator upon request (email/electronic if requested).  
- **Signage mandates**: entrance signs with **≥1-inch letters** listing authorization rules/times, towing warning, tow company name/address/phone, **charges**, storage facility address and redemption times, and parking-area owner/operator contact; tow trucks must have door signage with **≥3-inch letters** (name + phone).  
- **Incomplete tow rules**: stop tow if owner returns before vehicle leaves; drop/decoupling fee capped at **≤50%** of tow fee; **no fee** if owner returns before **physical attachment**; if retrieved within **24 hours** of tow being undertaken (before storage), no storage or ancillary fees beyond tow fee.  
- **Storage facility standards**: open **8 a.m.–6 p.m.**, **≥5 days/week** (excluding holidays); secured and outdoor lots lighted dusk-to-dawn; free personal-item retrieval during business hours; must accept major credit/debit or provide ATM with **reasonable fees**; must post after-hours instructions in three locations; after-hours fee cap **$50**; **no storage/after-hours fees** for any portion of a day when access during **8 a.m.–6 p.m.** is not provided within **90 minutes** after following posted instructions.  
- **Fee regulation**: fees must be “reasonable”; “presumptively excessive” if **>25%** above the company’s consensual fees or **>50%** above other county operators’ nonconsensual fees; cannot exceed any applicable local fee schedule; for covered vehicles, max **$250 towing** and **$50/day storage**, and **no additional towing-related charges**.  
- **Lien limits**: no lien unless notice sent by **certified U.S. mail within 30 days** of tow to owner/lessor/lienholder (if applicable), with itemized fees; lien may be asserted if not retrieved/paid within **60 days** of delivery of notice.  
- **Prohibited practices**: no paying benefits for information about unauthorized vehicles; no charging to retrieve personal property during normal business hours; restrictions on monitoring/patrolling/surveillance towing unless requested and contractual/authorization requirements met.  
- **Enforcement and remedies**: any violation is an “unlawful practice” under Title 6; illegal tow entitles owner/operator to **reimbursement of all fees paid** plus **actual damages** to retrieve vehicle; failure to allow personal-property access triggers **consequential damages**; **joint and several liability** for all involved tow companies/storage facilities; potential **attorneys’ fees and costs** with specified settlement-offer conditions.

## Who Benefits
- **Vehicle owners/operators** whose vehicles are towed non-consensually (fee caps for covered vehicles; access to documentation; reimbursement/damages for violations; easier access to vehicles and personal property; payment method protections).  
- **Consumers broadly** through DOJ consumer-protection enforcement classification (“unlawful practice”).  
- **Property owners/managers and public entities** seeking standardized, documented towing practices and signage requirements for non-consensual towing programs.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Private tow companies and storage facilities** due to fee caps (**$250** tow; **$50/day** storage for covered vehicles), operational mandates (hours, after-hours procedures), recordkeeping (**2 years**), and expanded liability (including joint-and-several liability and potential attorneys’ fees).  
- **Owners/operators/agents of parking areas** who must ensure compliant signage and (for certain private-lot tows) provide **prior written authorization** for specific tows.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approved September 25, 2025**. (No separate effective date specified in the source text.)  
- **Record retention**: photos/documentation must be kept **at least 2 years** from the date of the non-consensual tow.  
- **No storage/ancillary fees** if vehicle retrieved within **24 hours** from when the tow company first undertakes a tow (when returned before storage).  
- **Storage access performance standard**: during **8 a.m.–6 p.m.**, access/release must be provided within **90 minutes** after following posted instructions (or storage/after-hours fees cannot be charged for that portion of the day).  
- **Lien notice deadline**: certified mail notice must be sent **within 30 days** of the tow.  
- **Lien warning window**: notice must state lien may be asserted if vehicle not retrieved/fees unpaid within **60 days** of delivery of the notice.  
- **Attorneys’ fees condition**: settlement offer must be made **at least 10 business days** before filing suit; non-response within **10 business days** is deemed rejection.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirements** include (non-exhaustive): mandatory signage (entrance and tow truck), pre-tow photos, written authorization for certain private-lot tows, 2-year record retention, free provision of records to owner/operator, limits on incomplete tow fees, storage-facility operational standards, payment acceptance rules, fee reasonableness standards and explicit caps for covered vehicles, lien notice procedures, and prohibitions on certain towing practices.  
- **Enforcement mechanism**: any violation is an **unlawful practice** under **6 Del. C. § 2513** and Subchapter II of Chapter 25 of Title 6 (enforceable by the **DOJ Consumer Protection Unit**, per synopsis).  
- **Civil remedies**: for a tow initiated/completed in violation—**reimbursement of all fees paid** plus **actual damages** to retrieve the vehicle; **consequential damages** for denial of required personal-property access; **joint and several liability** among involved tow companies/storage facilities.  
- **Attorneys’ fees and costs**: court must award reasonable attorneys’ fees/costs to the owner/operator if the statutory settlement-offer conditions are met (offer made **≥10 business days** pre-suit; rejected or no response within **10 business days**; final award ≥ offer).
