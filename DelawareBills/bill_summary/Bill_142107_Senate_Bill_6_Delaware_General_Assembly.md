## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware insurance law (Title 18) to reform health insurance pre-authorization (prior authorization) standards—especially notice of rule changes, reviewer qualifications, timelines for determinations/appeals, electronic submission requirements, and limits on repeated authorizations—applicable to individual, group, and blanket health insurance, and to drive compliance in State employee and (to the extent feasible) Medicaid-related contracts.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Applies new/updated pre-authorization standards to health insurance policies, contracts, or certificates **issued, renewed, modified, altered, amended, or reissued on or after December 31, 2026**.  
- Requires **at least 6 months’ notice** to covered persons before changes to utilization review terms (e.g., clinical criteria) for a health-care service, **with exceptions** (clinical guideline status changes, recalls, market withdrawals, or relevant FDA published safety information).  
- Provides that new utilization review term changes **do not apply to covered persons with an existing authorization**; they apply **only upon re-authorization** of the service.  
- Shortens decision timelines for pre-authorization determinations: generally **2 business days** (pharmaceutical), **5 business days** (non-electronic non-urgent service), **3 business days** (electronic non-urgent service), and **24 hours** (electronic urgent service and electronic patient transfer).  
- Establishes reviewer qualification rules for adverse determinations and appeals, including that certain denials/appeals must be made/reviewed by appropriately qualified, licensed clinicians and that compensation **may not be contingent on outcome**.  
- Sets appeal process requirements, including **15-day** appeal decision notice (with a possible additional **15 days** after receipt of requested additional information) and minimum **30 days** to submit an appeal after an adverse determination.  
- Requires reporting of **de-identified** pre-authorization approval/denial/appeal statistics to the **Delaware Health Information Network** **no less than twice annually** (as requested) and allows the Department to request data at any time.  
- Requires electronic pre-authorization capabilities and a provider portal **no later than January 1, 2027**; after the portal is established, plans may require portal use **within 12 months**, with specified exemptions.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More predictable rule changes for patients already approved for care:** Utilization review term changes generally **won’t apply mid-authorization** and require **6 months’ notice** before changes (with specified exceptions).  
- **Faster coverage decisions:** Statutory timelines require decisions/notifications within **2 business days** (pharmaceutical), **3 business days** (electronic), **5 business days** (non-electronic), and **24 hours** (certain urgent/transfer requests submitted electronically).  
- **Stronger clinical accountability in denials/appeals:** Adverse determinations and appeals must be made by clinicians with specified licensure/specialty alignment, with **no outcome-contingent compensation**.  
- **Improved transparency:** De-identified reporting of approvals/denials/appeals to DHIN at least **twice annually** upon request, plus Department access to data “at any time.”  
- **Reduced repeat administrative burden within a course of treatment:** Plans may not require more than **1 pre-authorization per “episode of care”** (as defined), and bundled-service approvals deem other included in-network services approved.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative/operational burden on insurers and utilization review entities:** Must meet shorter statutory timelines (as short as **24 hours**), perform utilization review on **weekends**, and provide access to a medical director/clinical decision maker **7:00 AM–7:00 PM Monday–Friday** plus reasonable weekend hours.  
- **Technology build/maintenance requirements:** Must implement electronic acceptance/response and establish a provider portal **by January 1, 2027**, with specified portal features and downtime instructions.  
- **Potential compliance complexity across markets:** Parallel amendments apply in both **Chapter 33** and **Chapter 35** frameworks, and State employee and Medicaid-related contracting provisions add additional administrative oversight layers.  
- **Fiscal impact unknown:** Fiscal note states impacts to the State Employee Group Health Insurance Program are **indeterminable**, and DHSS cannot determine Medicaid fiscal impact due to lack of data.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- “**Reasonable and customary**” validity period is constrained by a **90-day minimum**, but the upper bound is not specified.  
- “**Reasonable business hours**” on weekends (for access to a medical director/clinical decision maker) is not defined.  
- “**To the extent feasible**” for DHSS ensuring Medicaid-related contract compliance is not defined and may affect how uniformly standards are applied in Medicaid managed care contracts.  
- The bill references the Department’s ability to request data “**at any time**,” but does not specify enforcement mechanisms or penalties for non-reporting in the provided text.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New/expanded definitions:** Adds “**Episode of Care**” and “**Urgent health-care service**” in both Chapter 33 and Chapter 35 sections.  
- **Notice of utilization review term changes:**  
  - Existing: notice **no less than 60 days** before implementing a new/amended pre-authorization requirement/restriction.  
  - New: for changes to utilization review terms (e.g., clinical criteria), must give covered persons **at least 6 months’ notice**, except for **clinical guideline status changes, recalls, market withdrawals, or relevant FDA safety information**; changes apply **only upon re-authorization** and not to existing authorizations.  
- **Reporting to DHIN:** Insurers/health plans must report **de-identified** approval/denial/appeal statistics in DHIN-requested format/frequency, **no less than twice annually**, with specified minimum categories (e.g., denial reasons; appeal specialty/type/indication/underlying denial reason; number overturned).  
- **Reviewer qualifications and independence:**  
  - Physician-submitted clean requests: adverse determinations must be made by a physician with specialty-aligned training/experience (or in consultation with a qualified third party); compensation **not contingent on outcome**.  
  - Appeals: must be reviewed by a physician with a current unrestricted license, **not involved** in the original denial, and must consider all clinical aspects/records submitted.  
  - Non-physician requests: adverse determinations/appeals must be made by a similarly licensed professional (or in consultation with qualified third party).  
- **Appeal timelines and required content:** Appeal determination notice within **15 days** of receipt; if more information is needed, written request within **15 days**, then determination within **15 days** of receiving that information; notice must include findings summary, reviewer qualifications, and relationship of diagnosis/disease to review criteria and specific basis.  
- **Utilization review entity operational requirements:** Must perform review on **weekends**; provide access to a medical director/clinical decision maker **7:00 AM–7:00 PM Mon–Fri** and reasonable weekend hours; allow appeals submission in writing/electronically/by phone; and provide at least **30 days** from adverse determination to submit an appeal.  
- **Shorter pre-authorization decision timelines (non-emergency):**  
  - Pharmaceutical: notify provider within **2 business days** of clean request.  
  - Non-urgent health-care service: **5 business days** if not submitted using electronic services; **3 business days** if submitted electronically.  
  - Urgent health-care service: **24 hours** if submitted electronically; **48 hours** if not.  
  - Patient transfer: **24 hours** if submitted electronically; **48 hours** if not.  
  - If a lower level of care is deemed clinically appropriate, plan **may not require pre-authorization** for medically necessary interfacility transport.  
- **Longer authorization validity:** Minimum validity increases from **60 days to 90 days** from when the provider receives the pre-authorization (subject to coverage/eligibility confirmation and validly delivered policy changes).  
- **Limit to one authorization per episode of care:** May not require more than **1** pre-authorization per “episode of care” (with ability to require separate authorization for new/additional testing/procedures related or unrelated to the specific problem/condition/illness).  
- **Bundled payments:** If a service in a bundled group is pre-authorized, other included in-network covered services in the group are **deemed approved**.  
- **Electronic standards and portal requirements:**  
  - By **January 1, 2027**, must accept/respond to electronic pre-authorization requests through the **same platform** used for submission.  
  - By **January 1, 2027**, must establish a provider portal with specified features (submission, medical policies, peer-to-peer info, contacts, forms, downtime instructions).  
  - **Within 12 months** after establishing the portal, plan/UR entity **may require** providers to submit via the portal unless: portal not operational at submission; provider lacks access; or the provider qualifies for an allowed alternative method.

## Who Benefits
- **Covered persons/enrollees** receiving services subject to pre-authorization, due to notice protections, appeal content/timing requirements, and shorter determination timelines.  
- **Health-care providers** (physicians and other licensed providers) submitting pre-authorization requests and appeals, due to standardized timelines, portal functionality requirements, and limitations on repeated authorizations per episode of care.  
- **State oversight entities** (and potentially the public) via de-identified utilization review statistics reported to the Delaware Health Information Network.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Insurers, health-benefit plans, health-service corporations, and utilization review entities** required to meet accelerated timelines, expanded staffing availability (including weekends and extended hours), reporting obligations, and technology/portal requirements.  
- **State contracting administrators** (State Employee Benefits Committee; DHSS for Medicaid-related contracts) who must incorporate and monitor compliance requirements.

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective upon signature** (fiscal note assumption).  
- Act applies to health insurance policies/contracts/certificates **issued, renewed, modified, altered, amended, or reissued after December 31, 2026** (bill Section 4; fiscal note also describes this applicability date).  
- **January 1, 2027:** Deadline for insurers/health plans/health-service corporations/utilization review entities to (1) accept/respond to electronic pre-authorization requests via the same platform, and (2) establish the required provider portal.  
- **Within 12 months following establishment of the provider portal:** Entity may require providers to submit pre-authorizations via the portal, subject to stated exemptions.  
- **At least 6 months before** changes to utilization review terms for a health-care service (with specified exceptions): required notice to covered persons.  
- **At least 60 days before** implementing a new or amended pre-authorization requirement/restriction: required written notice to covered persons currently authorized for the affected service and contracted providers of the affected service.  
- **Appeals:** minimum **30 days** from date of adverse determination to submit an appeal; decision notice within **15 days** of appeal receipt (or within **15 days** of receiving additional requested information, after timely written request).
