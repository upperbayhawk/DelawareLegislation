## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware law to replace the term “child pornography” with “child sexual abuse material” in specified criminal and child-protection statutes, reflecting terminology that emphasizes the abuse depicted.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Renames “child pornography” to “child sexual abuse material” across multiple sections of **Title 11** (criminal code) and **Title 16** (health and social services-related provisions).  
- Updates the human trafficking definition of **“sexually explicit performance”** to include “child sexual abuse material.” (Title 11, § 787)  
- Updates the offenses of **dealing in** and **possession of** child pornography to refer to **sexual abuse material**, without changing the described prohibited conduct. (Title 11, §§ 1109, 1111)  
- Keeps the existing felony grading structure for dealing offenses: **Class B felony**, or **Class D felony** if the prohibited sex act is **“partial nudity”** (as defined in § 1100). (Title 11, § 1109)  
- Maintains the existing age limitation: when the underlying prohibited sexual act is **“partial nudity,”** a person can only be guilty under §§ 1108 or 1109 if the person is **18 years of age or older**. (Title 11, § 1110A)  
- Ensures the renamed dealing offense remains listed for **cash-only secured bail presumption** and as a **preventive-detention eligible** charge (subject to existing contingency language in those sections). (Title 11, §§ 2107, 2116)  
- Keeps these offenses included in sex-offender risk tiering (**Tier II**) and violent-felony/violent-crime cross-references where applicable. (Title 11, §§ 4121, 4201, 6703)  
- Adds/updates references in **Child Protection Level IV** criteria and **EMT/ambulance attendant certification denial** grounds to match the new terminology. (Title 16, §§ 923, 6712)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Victim-centered terminology:** The bill expressly aims to use “correct and widely accepted terminology” that “better reflects the abuse” and trauma to the child (Synopsis).  
- **Consistency across statutes:** Aligns language across trafficking definitions, criminal offenses, bail/detention references, sex-offender notification tiers, violent felony/crime definitions, child protection registry consequences, and EMT certification screening.  
- **Administrative clarity in communications:** Using the same term in multiple code sections may reduce confusion in charging documents, registry checks, and eligibility determinations (changes across Titles 11 and 16).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Terminology update across criminal statutes:**  
  - Updates trafficking definition to include “production of pornography or child sexual abuse material.” (Title 11, § 787(a)(25))  
  - Renames offense headings and internal references for:  
    - **Dealing in child pornography; sexual abuse material** (Title 11, § 1109)  
    - **Possession of child pornography; sexual abuse material** (Title 11, § 1111)  
- **Felony classifications retained (expressly stated in amended text):**  
  - Dealing offense remains **Class B felony**, except **Class D felony** when the prohibited sex act is **“partial nudity”** (Title 11, § 1109).  
  - Possession remains **Class F felony** (Title 11, § 1111).  
- **Age threshold retained for “partial nudity” cases:** Only persons **18+** can be guilty under § 1108 or § 1109 where the underlying prohibited sexual act is “partial nudity.” (Title 11, § 1110A)  
- **Pretrial release and detention cross-references updated:**  
  - Adds the renamed § 1109 offense to the **cash-only secured bond presumption** list (Title 11, § 2107(c)(23)).  
  - Adds the renamed § 1109 offense to the **preventive detention eligibility** list (Title 11, § 2116(b)(1)(v)).  
- **Sex offender tier designation updated:** Includes “unlawfully dealing in… sexual abuse material” and “possession of… sexual abuse material” in **Risk Assessment Tier II** list. (Title 11, § 4121(d)(2))  
- **Violent felony / violent crime references updated:**  
  - Designates **§ 1109 Unlawfully Dealing in Child Pornography Sexual Abuse Material** as a violent felony in transition provisions. (Title 11, § 4201(c)(1))  
  - Updates “violent crime” definition list to include “dealing with child pornography, in child sexual abuse material, second offense” (as written in the amended list). (Title 11, § 6703)  
- **Child Protection Registry consequences updated:** Adds “possession of child pornography, sexual abuse material” and “unlawfully dealing in… sexual abuse material” to the conviction list tied to **Child Protection Level IV** substantiations. (Title 16, § 923(b)(4)b.)  
- **EMT/ambulance attendant certification screening updated:** Lists “making or distributing child pornography, sexual abuse material” among examples of disqualifying felonies involving sexual/physical abuse of a child. (Title 16, § 6712(b)(1)b.)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **No new conduct standards described; terminology updated while penalties/classifications remain stated in the amended sections.** Key enforcement/penalty elements included in the text:  
  - **Dealing in child pornography/sexual abuse material (§ 1109):**

  - **Sex offender tiering (§ 4121(d)(2)):** Convictions/adjudications for these offenses are designated **Risk Assessment Tier II**.  
  - **Child Protection Level IV registry consequences (§ 923(b)(4)):** A person substantiated at Level IV must be reported as “substantiated,” is **ineligible for employment** in child-serving entities and specified health-care facilities, and generally **may not be removed from the Registry** (except as provided in § 929(c)).  
  - **EMT/ambulance attendant certification (§ 6712(b)):** Certification **must be denied** to applicants convicted of specified crimes, including felonies involving sexual/physical abuse of a child such as “making or distributing child pornography, sexual abuse material.”
