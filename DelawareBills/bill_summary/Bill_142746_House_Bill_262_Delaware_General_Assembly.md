## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 14, §1333 of the Delaware Code to clarify when an eligible school-employee’s **12 weeks of paid leave for adoption** may **begin**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Clarifies that **paid leave for adoption** under **14 Del. C. §1333** may start at one of **three employee-selected points**: **placement**, **petition filing**, or **completion** of the adoption.
- Does **not change** the underlying entitlement: eligible employees remain entitled to **12 weeks of paid leave** for birth or adoption (child **age 6 or younger**).
- Maintains the rule that the **12-week leave entitlement expires at the end of the 12-month period** beginning on the date of the **birth or adoption**.
- Keeps existing eligibility language: applies to **full-time employees** or employees employed **at least 1 year** by a reorganized school district, charter school, or vocational school district.
- Keeps existing sick-leave provision: **full-time or part-time employees**, **regardless of length of employment**, may use **accumulated sick leave** for birth/adoption (child **age 6 or younger**).
- Continues “concurrency” rule: benefits run concurrently with **FMLA (29 U.S.C. §2601 et seq.)** and **short-term disability under 29 Del. C. §5253** (for birth).
- Retains anti-retaliation protection: covered employers may not **alter/terminate benefits** or **terminate employment** as a result of taking leave under this section.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Greater clarity and predictability for adoption leave timing:** The bill explicitly allows the employee to begin adoption leave at **placement**, **petition initiation**, or **completion** (new §1333(f)(a)-(c)).
- **Employee choice is expressly recognized:** Timing is “**to be determined by the employee**” (§1333(f)), which can help align leave with key adoption milestones identified in the statute.
- **May reduce administrative disputes:** By listing three specific start points tied to legal/administrative events (e.g., **§904 of Title 13 placement**; **Family Court petition**), employers and employees have clearer reference points.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential scheduling complexity for employers:** Because the start date is chosen by the employee among three options (§1333(f)), school districts/charters/vocational districts may face more variable leave timing (operational impact implied by the flexibility granted in text).
- **No change to the statutory “12-month expiration” anchor:** The entitlement still expires **12 months beginning on the date of birth or adoption** (§1333(b)); the bill does not clarify how this interacts with a leave start at **placement** or **petition** when those events occur before adoption completion.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Interaction between “start of leave” and the “12-month expiration” window:** §1333(b) ties expiration to “the date of such birth or adoption,” while §1333(f) allows leave to begin at placement or petition. The bill does not explicitly state how the **12-month clock** applies if leave begins **before adoption is completed**.
- **Definition of “completion of the adoption process”:** The bill uses this phrase (§1333(f)(c)) but does not define the precise event/date that constitutes completion.
- **Documentation/notice requirements:** The bill does not specify what proof or notice an employee must provide to select one of the three start points.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New clarification on adoption leave start timing:** For purposes of §1333, “the entitlement to leave for the adoption of a child may begin” at one of the following, **as determined by the employee** (§1333(f)):
  1. **When the child is placed for adoption** with the employee under **13 Del. C. §904** (§1333(f)(a));
  2. **When the employee initiates a petition for adoption in Family Court** (§1333(f)(b));
  3. **Upon completion of the adoption process** (§1333(f)(c)).
- No other substantive changes are stated in the bill text to eligibility, duration (**12 weeks**), child age threshold (**6 years or younger**), or concurrency/anti-retaliation provisions.
