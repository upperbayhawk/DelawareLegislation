## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Protect the personal information of Delaware Department of Correction (DOC) correctional officers and probation and parole officers—and their families—by allowing them to opt in via written request to restrict public posting/display of specified personal information by government agencies and other persons.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a privacy protection process in Title 11, Chapter 65, Part IV, Subchapter XII for DOC correctional officers (defined to include probation and parole officers) who serve or served **on or after the Act’s effective date**.  
- Upon receiving a valid written request, **government agencies must remove covered personal information from publicly available content within 72 hours** and generally may not release it without the officer’s consent.  
- Upon receiving a valid written request, **persons** (non-government entities/individuals) must remove covered personal information from public display **within 72 hours** (or **by the next update** for a printed directory) and may not transfer the information thereafter.  
- Prohibits any person from **sharing/soliciting/selling/trading** an officer’s or family’s personal information **with intent to pose an imminent and serious threat** to health and safety.  
- DOC must develop a policy/procedure to accept requests and **enroll** participating officers in an **online privacy vendor** service; DOC must distribute request lists **quarterly** to supervisory officials for dissemination to agencies.  
- Provides civil remedies: injunctive/declaratory relief (with **costs and reasonable attorneys’ fees** if granted) and a private right of action with damages **up to 3× actual damages** and **not less than $10,000**.  
- Criminal exposure is indirect: a post/display after a request “**may result in criminal penalties under Title 11**” if existing crime elements are met (no new standalone crime specified).  
- **Effective date:** **180 days after enactment**; Act was **approved August 12, 2025** (so effective **February 8, 2026**, counting 180 days after approval).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves safety/privacy** for officers and families by requiring removal of sensitive data (e.g., home address, phone numbers, SSN, financial account numbers, identity of children under 18) from public display after a request.  
- **Fast removal standard:** 72-hour removal requirement for both government agencies and persons (with a tailored rule for printed directories).  
- **Clear opt-in mechanism and centralized support:** DOC procedure plus quarterly distribution lists can streamline notifications to multiple agencies.  
- **Meaningful civil enforcement tools:** minimum **$10,000** damages (and up to **3× actual damages**) and fee-shifting for injunctive/declaratory relief may incentivize compliance.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative burden on agencies and private parties:** mandatory removal within **72 hours** after receipt of requests; ongoing obligation not to repost/release without consent.  
- **Potential litigation exposure for persons:** private right of action with **minimum $10,000** damages and potential **treble damages** increases financial risk for violations.  
- **Program costs to DOC/state:** fiscal note anticipates staffing and vendor expenses (see Budget Impact).  
- **Scope limited by opt-in:** protections apply only after a valid written request is made; absent a request, no violation can be found.

## Key Policy Changes
- Establishes Subchapter XII privacy protections for “correctional officers” (defined to include **probation and parole officers**) and their “family” (as defined in **10 Del. C. § 901**).  
- Defines “personal information” to include: **home address** (including identified secondary residence), home/mobile numbers, direct line to private office, personal email, **SSN**, federal tax ID, bank account numbers, credit/debit numbers, birth/marital records, property tax records, and **identity of children under 18**.  
- **Government agencies:** prohibited from publicly posting/displaying covered personal information after a request; must **remove within 72 hours**; cannot re-release without consent.  
- **Persons:** prohibited from publicly posting/displaying after a request; must **remove within 72 hours** (or next directory update); must prevent display on controlled websites; cannot transfer the information after receiving the request.  
- Creates civil remedies: injunctive/declaratory relief with **costs and reasonable attorneys’ fees** if relief is granted; private right of action with damages **≥ $10,000** and **≤ 3× actual damages**.  
- Requires DOC to develop procedures, notify agencies/persons, and enroll officers in an **online privacy vendor** service; establishes **2-year** request duration with renewals and post-employment extension rules.

## Who Benefits
- Active, formerly active, and retired DOC correctional officers (including probation and parole officers) who **serve or served on or after the Act’s effective date** and who submit written requests.  
- Eligible family members whose personal information is shielded to the extent it could reasonably be expected to reveal the officer’s personal information.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Government agencies required to remove information within **72 hours** and maintain non-disclosure absent consent (administrative workload and compliance risk).  
- Private individuals/entities (“persons,” including businesses and associations) that publish information and must remove it within **72 hours** (or next directory update) and face civil liability for violations (including **minimum $10,000** damages).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Approved:** **August 12, 2025**.  
- **Effective:** **180 days following enactment into law** (Section 2) → **February 8, 2026**.  
- **Removal deadline after receipt of written request:** **72 hours** (government agencies and persons).  
- **Printed directory exception:** removal **no later than the next update** to the directory.  
- **DOC distribution cadence:** **each quarter of a calendar year** (quarterly lists).  
- **Written request duration:** **2 years**.  
- **Extension after retirement/leave in good standing:** eligible for a **2-year extension** from date of retirement/leave.  
- **Immediate removal from DOC list:** if officer is “removed for cause or fired,” their name must be **immediately removed** by DOC.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Condition precedent:** No government agency or person can be found in violation unless the officer has submitted a valid **written request**.  
- **Compliance duties (government agencies):** remove covered personal information from publicly available content within **72 hours** of receiving the request; do not repost/release absent officer consent.  
- **Compliance duties (persons):** remove within **72 hours** (or next printed directory update); ensure non-display on controlled websites; do not transfer the information after receiving the request.  
- **Civil enforcement:**  
  - Injunctive or declaratory relief available; if granted, violator must pay **costs and reasonable attorneys’ fees**.  
  - Private right of action: damages **not less than $10,000** and **not more than 3× actual damages**.  
- **Criminal penalties:** posting/display after a written request **may** trigger existing **Title 11** criminal penalties if the elements of an existing crime are met (no new specific criminal offense detailed).  
- **Affirmative defense / limitation:** statute does not limit or enlarge **47 U.S.C. § 230** protections for interactive computer services regarding third-party content.
