## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend 29 Del. C. § 2707 to eliminate the use of checks for most State of Delaware vendor payments and require that vendor payments be made only through specified electronic/payment-card methods, with limited exceptions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires the Office of the State Treasurer to pay State vendors for goods or services **only** by **ACH direct deposit**, **Single Use Account (SUA)**, or the State **Procurement Card (PCARD)**.  
- Effectively **eliminates checks** as a routine payment method for vendor payments covered by the bill.  
- Allows an exception to the “ACH/SUA/PCARD only” rule **only if approved by both** the **Office of the State Treasurer** and the **Division of Accounting**.  
- Carves out exclusions: the mandate **does not apply** to **single payment vouchers**, **one-time suppliers**, **forced year-end payouts** by the Division of Accounting, or **legal settlements**.  
- Retains existing general language in § 2707(a) that payment methods “may include checks, drafts and electronic funds transfers,” but § 2707(b) overrides that for covered vendor payments.  
- Enacted as **Chapter 77, Volume 85** of the Laws of Delaware; **approved July 14, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved payment security and efficiency**: The synopsis states the Act aims to “enhance payment efficiency and security while streamlining state financial operations” by moving away from checks to ACH/SUA/PCARD.
- **Standardization of payment methods**: Limits vendor payment methods to three specified options (ACH, SUA, PCARD), which can simplify processes for the Treasury and agencies.
- **Centralized exception control**: Requires joint approval by the **Office of the State Treasurer** and the **Division of Accounting** for exceptions, creating a defined control point.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced flexibility for vendors**: Vendors who prefer or require checks may need to adopt ACH/SUA/PCARD, unless they qualify under an exclusion or obtain an approved exception.
- **Potential administrative burden to request exceptions**: Any deviation from ACH/SUA/PCARD requires approval by **two entities** (State Treasurer’s Office and Division of Accounting).
- **Possible inconsistency within the statute text**: § 2707(a) still lists checks as a permissible method generally, while § 2707(b) prohibits checks for covered vendor payments—this may require training/clarification to avoid confusion.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Criteria for exceptions**: The bill allows exceptions but does not specify standards, documentation, or timelines for approval by the Office of the State Treasurer and the Division of Accounting.
- **Scope of “vendors for goods or services”**: The text does not further define “vendor,” “goods,” or “services” for purposes of applying the mandate.
- **Operational definitions**: The statute references **ACH Payment**, **SUA Payment**, and **PCARD Payment** but does not define them within the bill text itself.
- **Interaction with § 2707(a)**: The relationship between the permissive language in subsection (a) and the mandate in subsection (b) could be unclear to implementers without additional guidance.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New mandate (29 Del. C. § 2707(b))**: For vendor payments for goods or services provided to any State department, commission, or board, the Office of the State Treasurer must pay **via ACH**, **SUA**, or **PCARD only**.
- **Exception process**: Payment by other methods is allowed **only if** an exception is approved by **both**:
  - the **Office of the State Treasurer**, and  
  - the **Division of Accounting**.
- **Exclusions from the mandate** (the requirement “does not apply” to):
  - **single payment vouchers**,
  - **one-time suppliers**,
  - **forced payouts conducted at year end by the Division of Accounting**, and
  - **legal settlements**.

## Who Benefits
- **State financial operations (Treasury/Accounting)**: The Act’s stated aim is to streamline operations and improve security/efficiency by reducing check usage and standardizing payment methods.
- **Vendors able to accept ACH/SUA/PCARD**: May benefit from faster or more secure electronic payments (as described in the synopsis’ stated objectives).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Vendors that rely on checks**: Vendors who do not use ACH/SUA/PCARD and do not fall under an exclusion may face process changes or need to seek an exception.
- **Agencies/vendors needing non-standard payment methods**: Could be impacted by the requirement to secure approvals from both the State Treasurer’s Office and the Division of Accounting.
