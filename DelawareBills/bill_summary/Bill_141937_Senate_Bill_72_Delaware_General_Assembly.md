## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To require the Delaware Division of Public Health (DPH) to (1) publish online PFAS (“forever chemicals”) levels for each public drinking water system in Delaware and (2) ensure customers are notified by their water utility when PFAS levels exceed specified maximum contaminant levels (MCLs).  

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates “Chapter 30R. The Forever Chemicals Mandatory Reporting Act” in Title 16, directing DPH to post PFAS levels for each public drinking water system on a **user-friendly public website**.  
- Requires the website to be reachable via a **prominently displayed link** on the **Office of Drinking Water** homepage and to include an **MCL threshold chart** for comparison.  
- Defines enforceable PFAS MCL thresholds, including **PFOA 4.0 ppt**, **PFOS 4.0 ppt**, **PFHxS 10 ppt**, **PFNA 10 ppt**, **HFPO-DA (GenX) 10 ppt**, and a **Hazard Index MCL of 1** for certain mixtures.  
- Adds an “EPA floor” provision: if the **U.S. EPA sets a lower MCL** than Delaware’s listed MCL for a substance, the **EPA’s lower MCL becomes the MCL** for this chapter.  
- If DPH finds PFAS exceeding an MCL while monitoring for compliance, DPH must **promptly notify** the responsible water utility.  
- Upon DPH notification of exceedance, the water utility must **promptly notify all Delaware residents served** by that system via **U.S. Postal Service mail**, including a DPH-developed **PFAS brochure** and the **exact PFAS levels** that exceeded MCLs.  
- Fiscal note: **$250,000 one-time (FY2026)** and **$50,000 annually (FY2027 and FY2028)** in **non-appropriated special funds (NSF)** from **PFAS Settlement Funds** to build and maintain the website (including linking to **SDWIS**).  
- Effective date in enacted version: **January 15, 2026** (approved **September 19, 2025**).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improved public access to PFAS data:** DPH must provide an online tool showing PFAS levels by system, with MCL comparison information.  
- **Direct, mailed customer notification when exceedances occur:** Utilities must mail notice (and brochure) to customers served by a system exceeding MCLs, including the **exact exceedance levels**.  
- **Alignment with stricter federal standards:** If EPA adopts a **lower** MCL for a PFAS substance, that lower standard automatically applies under the chapter.  

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative and cost burden on DPH:** Requires development and maintenance of a new website; fiscal note estimates **$250,000 one-time** plus **$50,000 per year** ongoing.  
- **Administrative and mailing burden on water utilities:** When notified of an exceedance, utilities must mail notices via **U.S. Postal Service** to all residents served by the affected system and include required materials.  
- **Timelines and content standards use “promptly” without a fixed number of days:** Could create implementation uncertainty for agencies/utilities (the statute does not set a specific deadline in days for notices).  

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **“Promptly” is not defined** for DPH notification to utilities or utility notification to residents (no specific day-count deadline stated).  
- Website scope depends on data availability: the site must include PFAS levels “**to the extent**” information is available; when not available, DPH must post an **approximate date** and then post results “as soon as” available (no fixed schedule).  
- **Penalty structure is not specified** in the chapter text for noncompliance by utilities or DPH.  
- The bill references monitoring “for purposes of compliance with this chapter” but does not specify **testing frequency**, sampling protocols, or which entity funds/does additional monitoring (beyond general references).

## Health Impact
- Establishes enforceable public reporting and notification around PFAS in drinking water with specific MCL thresholds (e.g., **PFOA 4.0 ppt**, **PFOS 4.0 ppt**), and requires resident notification when exceedances occur.  
- (Beyond statutory requirements) The bill synopsis cites health concerns linked to PFAS, but the operative provisions focus on reporting and notification.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Title 16, Chapter 30R** (“The Forever Chemicals Mandatory Reporting Act”).  
- **Defines PFAS MCLs** for regulated PFAS and a **Hazard Index MCL of 1** for certain mixtures.  
- **Adopts lower EPA MCLs automatically** when EPA’s MCL is lower than the chapter’s listed MCL for a substance.  
- Requires DPH to maintain a **public PFAS website** meeting specific design and content requirements.  
- Requires a **DPH-to-utility notification** process when MCL exceedances are found.  
- Requires **utility-to-customer mailed notification** with specific mandated contents and a DPH-developed brochure.  
- Amends **16 Del. C. § 122** to direct DHSS to “implement and enforce” provisions regarding PFAS in public drinking water systems.  
- Authorizes DPH to **promulgate regulations** to implement and enforce Chapter 30R.

## Who Benefits
- **Delaware residents served by public drinking water systems** who gain online access to PFAS levels and mailed notice of exceedances.  
- **Customers of systems with PFAS exceedances**, who must receive mailed notice including the **exact levels** exceeding MCLs.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Water utility companies/public water suppliers** that must provide **prompt mailed notices** and associated administrative work/costs when exceedances occur.  
- **DPH/DHSS** due to implementation and ongoing operational responsibilities (website creation/maintenance and brochure development), though costs are stated to be covered by PFAS Settlement Funds (NSF).
