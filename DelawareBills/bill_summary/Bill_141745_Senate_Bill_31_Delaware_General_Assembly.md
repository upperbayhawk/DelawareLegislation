## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Increase the statutory burial benefit paid upon the death of a pensioner under the State Employees’ Pension Plan from **$7,000 to $10,000**, and (by cross-reference) raise the same benefit amount used in certain other pension plans and in the cap on “reasonable funeral expenses” recoverable in Delaware wrongful death actions.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Raises the State Employees’ Pension Plan burial benefit from **$7,000 → $10,000** (Title 29, § 5546).
- Applies when an individual receiving a pension dies; payment goes to a **designated beneficiary**, or if none, to the **pensioner’s estate**.
- Because other laws tie their benefit amounts to § 5546, the change also increases funeral/burial-related benefits under:
  - **State Police Pension Plan** (Title 11, Ch. 83),
  - **Delaware County and Municipal Police and Firefighter Pension Plan** (Title 11, Ch. 88),
  - **Early retirement option for state employees** (Title 29, Ch. 53),
  - and increases the cap on wrongful death “reasonable funeral expenses” under **Title 10, § 3724(d)(4)**.
- Effective date is **October 1, 2025** (bill text).
- Fiscal note estimates additional State costs of **$7,084,310 (FY2026)**, **$7,250,000 (FY2027)**, **$7,419,890 (FY2028)**.
- Fiscal note identifies new unfunded actuarial liabilities of **$41,524,500** (State Employee plan) and **$443,200** (New State Police plan), amortized over **5 years**, with assumed **2.5% annual increases** in costs during subsequent amortization years.
- Fiscal note assumes **70%** of increased required contributions (UAL + normal cost) come from the **General Fund**, and **100% General Fund** for the **Closed State Police** plan’s added pay-go costs.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Higher burial benefit**: Increases the payment upon a pensioner’s death from **$7,000 to $10,000**, providing more funds to beneficiaries/estates for burial/funeral costs (Title 29, § 5546 as amended).
- **Broader impact via cross-references**: Automatically increases equivalent benefits in multiple related pension systems and increases the wrongful death funeral-expense cap tied to § 5546 (Synopsis).
- **Updates an amount unchanged since 2001** (as stated in the Synopsis).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Significant fiscal cost / increased pension liabilities**: Fiscal note projects new unfunded actuarial liability of **$41,524,500** (State Employee plan) and **$443,200** (New State Police plan), plus ongoing costs including an **annual normal cost of $544,400** (fiscal note).
- **General Fund exposure**: Fiscal note assumes **70%** of increased required contributions will be paid from the **General Fund**, and **100%** of the Closed State Police plan’s added costs from the General Fund (fiscal note).
- **Ongoing cost escalation**: Fiscal note projects amortization-related costs increase by **2.5%** in each of the subsequent four fiscal years of amortization (fiscal note).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 29, § 5546 (Burial benefits)**:
  - Changes the burial benefit amount payable upon the death of a pension recipient from **$7,000** to **$10,000**.
  - Clarifies payment goes to a **designated beneficiary**, or if none, to the **deceased pensioner’s estate** (text edits shown).
- **Cross-referenced increases (by operation of existing law)** per Synopsis:
  - Raises funeral/burial benefit amounts in other pension plans that match § 5546.
  - Raises the maximum “reasonable funeral expenses” recoverable in wrongful death actions under **Title 10, § 3724(d)(4)** because that cap is tied to § 5546.

## Who Benefits
- **Beneficiaries or estates** of deceased individuals receiving a pension under the State Employees’ Pension Plan (payment increases to **$10,000**).
- Participants/beneficiaries under pension systems whose funeral/burial benefit amounts are pegged to § 5546 (as listed in the Synopsis), including certain state police and municipal police/firefighter pension plans.
- Plaintiffs in wrongful death actions seeking funeral expenses up to the statutory cap tied to § 5546 (Synopsis).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State finances / General Fund** due to increased required contributions and pay-go costs (fiscal note assumptions and cost estimates).
- Pension funding status: the bill increases **unfunded actuarial liabilities** as stated in the fiscal note.
