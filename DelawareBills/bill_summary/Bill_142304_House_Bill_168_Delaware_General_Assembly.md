## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 11 of the Delaware Code to make **knowing breaches of “no contact” conditions in a sentencing order** a **new criminal offense**, with the grade of offense depending on whether the underlying sentence involved **felony** or **misdemeanor** charges.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new crime for **knowingly breaching** any **no contact provision** in a defendant’s **sentencing order**.  
- Specifies that **each breach is a separate charge**.  
- If the no-contact sentencing order was for **at least 1 felony charge**, a breach is a **Class G felony**.  
- If the no-contact sentencing order was for **at least 1 misdemeanor charge** (and **no felonies**, per the synopsis), a breach is a **Class A misdemeanor**.  
- Applies only to breaches of **sentencing order** no-contact conditions (not other orders, unless they are part of sentencing).  
- States the new crime does **not interfere with a court’s power to punish probation violations**.  
- Bill release date shown on the PDF: **05/15/2025 (11:55 AM)**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger enforcement tool**: Explicitly makes breach of sentencing no-contact conditions a **new crime**, which may enhance enforceability beyond probation sanctions. (Text: “that breach is a new crime”.)
- **Clear penalty structure tied to underlying offense level**: Distinguishes consequences based on whether the sentencing order was for **≥1 felony** (Class G felony) or **≥1 misdemeanor** (Class A misdemeanor). (Text §1312B(a)(1)-(2).)
- **Allows multiple charges for repeated conduct**: Treats **each breach as a separate charge**, enabling proportional charging for repeated violations. (Text: “each breach is a separate charge”.)
- **Preserves existing probation enforcement**: Explicitly states it does not limit courts’ ability to punish **probation violations**. (Text §1312B(b).)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased criminal exposure for violations**: Converts breaches into **new criminal offenses** (Class G felony or Class A misdemeanor), potentially increasing arrests, prosecutions, and incarceration. (Text §1312B(a).)
- **Multiple charges from repeated breaches**: Because **each breach is a separate charge**, a pattern of violations could result in numerous counts from related conduct. (Text: “each breach is a separate charge”.)
- **Potential overlap with probation enforcement**: Although the bill preserves probation powers, it also creates a separate crime for the same underlying conduct, which may increase legal proceedings. (Text §1312B(a) and (b).)
