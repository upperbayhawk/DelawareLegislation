## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Create a new criminal offense in Title 11 for “endangering the welfare of a vulnerable adult,” including conduct and omissions (such as failing to report a missing vulnerable adult) by a guardian or other person responsible for the adult’s care or supervision.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new offense in **11 Del. C. § 1105A**: **endangering the welfare of a vulnerable adult**.  
- Applies to a **guardian** or **any other person who has assumed responsibility** for the vulnerable adult’s **care or supervision**.  
- Criminalizes acting **intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly** in a manner likely to be injurious to the adult’s **physical, mental, or moral welfare**.  
- Criminalizes **intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly** doing or failing to do an act (explicitly including **failing to report a missing vulnerable adult**) that results in the adult becoming a **neglected or abused adult**.  
- Defines “vulnerable adult” as a person **18 years of age or older** who is easily susceptible to abuse/neglect/exploitation due to specified conditions (e.g., isolation, sickness, disability).  
- States that “vulnerable adult” includes (without limitation) any adult for whom a **guardian of the person or property** has been appointed.  
- Sets the offense level as a **Class A misdemeanor**.  
- Notes the bill requires a **two-thirds vote in each house** for passage (Delaware Constitution, Art. IV, § 28).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Creates an explicit criminal tool** to address harm to vulnerable adults by caregivers/guardians, including harmful omissions, by establishing a specific offense in **Title 11**.  
- **Covers failures to act**, expressly including **failing to report a missing vulnerable adult**, when that omission results in the adult becoming neglected or abused.  
- **Broad definition of “vulnerable adult” (18+)** may increase protection for adults with disabilities, illness, debilitation, or isolation who are susceptible to exploitation or mistreatment.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- The offense applies to **“any other person who has assumed responsibility”** for care or supervision, which may be **broad** and could sweep in informal caregivers (the bill text does not narrow or define “assumed responsibility”).  
- The standard includes **“moral welfare”** injury, which is not defined in the bill text and may be **less concrete** than physical or mental harm.  
- The bill creates a new crime but does not specify **guidance** on what constitutes “failing to report” (e.g., to whom, within what timeframe), leaving potential uncertainty for responsible persons.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What constitutes **“assumed responsibility”** for care or supervision (formal vs. informal, temporary vs. ongoing)?  
- What does “**moral welfare**” mean for purposes of criminal liability?  
- What does it mean to “**report a missing vulnerable adult**” (report to police, APS, guardian, facility administration, etc.), and is there a required **timeframe**?  
- The bill references the adult becoming a **“neglected or abused adult”**—the bill text does not specify which statutory definitions apply or where those definitions are located.

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds **11 Del. C. § 1105A** establishing the crime of **endangering the welfare of a vulnerable adult**.  
- Defines “vulnerable adult” as a person **18 years of age or older** who is easily susceptible to abuse/neglect/mistreatment/exploitation due to **isolation, sickness, debilitation, mental illness, or physical/mental/cognitive disability**, and includes those with an appointed **guardian of the person or property**.  
- Establishes two main prohibited categories for responsible persons acting **intentionally, knowingly, or recklessly**:  
  1) Acting in a manner likely to be injurious to **physical, mental, or moral welfare**; or  
  2) Doing or failing to do an act (including **failing to report a missing vulnerable adult**) resulting in the adult becoming a **neglected or abused adult**.  
- Classifies the offense as a **Class A misdemeanor**.

## Who Benefits
- **Vulnerable adults (age 18+)** who are susceptible to abuse, neglect, exploitation, coercion, or mistreatment.  
- Potentially, **law enforcement and prosecutors**, by having a specific statutory offense tailored to endangerment of vulnerable adults.  
- **Guardians and responsible caregivers** seeking clearer criminal boundaries for harmful conduct toward vulnerable adults (as created by this new offense).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Guardians** and **any person who has assumed responsibility** for a vulnerable adult’s care or supervision, who may face **Class A misdemeanor** exposure for intentional/knowing/reckless acts or omissions meeting the statute.  
- Individuals in informal caregiving roles, to the extent they are deemed to have **assumed responsibility** (the scope is not further defined in the bill text).
