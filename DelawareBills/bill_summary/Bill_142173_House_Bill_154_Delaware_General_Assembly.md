## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To provide civil-liability immunity to nonprofit organizations that distribute **new secure gun storage or safety devices** free of charge, if specific distribution conditions are met. (Amends **Title 16, Chapter 68, Subchapter VIII**.)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Grants **civil immunity** to a **nonprofit organization** that distributes **new** secure gun storage or safety devices **free of charge** to an individual, for damages from alleged defects in the device.  
- Immunity applies only when the device is distributed **in original packaging and unopened**.  
- Immunity also requires the nonprofit to provide an **information pamphlet about gun safety**.  
- The pamphlet must be developed by a **local or national gun safety organization or hospital**.  
- The definition of “secure gun storage or safety device” is tied to **18 U.S.C. § 921(a)(34)** and explicitly includes **cable locks, trigger locks, or gun safes**.  
- The Act explicitly states it does **not** limit liability of the **manufacturer, distributor, or retailer** of the device.  
- The bill is framed as an “Immunity for Distribution” provision within Title 16.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces legal risk for nonprofits** distributing gun safety devices, because it provides immunity from civil damages for injuries or property damage proximately caused by alleged defects in the device’s **design, manufacturing, maintenance, or operation** (if conditions are met).  
- **Encourages distribution with safety education**, because immunity requires a gun safety pamphlet from a qualifying organization or hospital.  
- **Preserves consumer/product accountability** for commercial actors by stating the Act does not limit liability for manufacturers, distributors, or retailers.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits injured parties’ ability to recover damages from nonprofits** that distributed the device (when statutory conditions are met), even if harm is alleged to stem from defects in the device’s design/manufacture/maintenance/operation.  
- **Conditional immunity may constrain nonprofit operations**, because nonprofits must ensure devices are **new, unopened, and in original packaging**, and must include a qualifying pamphlet with each distribution.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill requires the device to be distributed “free of charge to an individual,” but does not define **“individual”** (e.g., whether it includes minors or requires residency).  
- The bill does not define what qualifies as an **“information pamphlet about gun safety”** beyond being developed by a specified type of organization/hospital (e.g., content requirements, language access, format).  
- The bill references alleged defects in “maintenance” or “operation” of the device but does not specify how those concepts apply to a nonprofit distributor versus an end user.

## Key Policy Changes
- Creates/updates statutory provisions in **Title 16, Chapter 68, Subchapter VIII**:
  - **§ 6860 (Definition):** “Secure gun storage or safety device” uses the meaning in **18 U.S.C. § 921(a)(34)** and includes **cable locks, trigger locks, or gun safes**.  
  - **§ 6861(a) (Immunity):** A nonprofit distributing **new** devices **free of charge** is **not liable for civil damages** for injury to person or property proximately caused by an alleged defect in the device’s **design, manufacturing, maintenance, or operation**.  
  - **§ 6861(b) (Conditions):** Immunity applies only if:
    1) Distributed **in original packaging and unopened**; and  
    2) Distributed with a **gun safety pamphlet** developed by a **local or national gun safety organization or hospital**.  
  - **§ 6861(c) (No limitation on others):** Does not limit liability of the **manufacturer, distributor, or retailer**.

## Who Benefits
- **Nonprofit organizations** that distribute new gun locks/safes to individuals for free, because they receive **civil-liability immunity** when they meet the packaging and pamphlet requirements.  
- **Individuals receiving the devices**, to the extent immunity may facilitate expanded nonprofit distribution (the bill’s operative mechanism is reduced nonprofit liability).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Individuals who suffer injury or property damage** allegedly caused by a defect in a device distributed by a nonprofit, because the nonprofit may be **immune from civil damages** if statutory conditions are satisfied.
