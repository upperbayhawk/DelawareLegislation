## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Titles 11, 16, and 29 of the Delaware Code to explicitly integrate **(suspected) human trafficking of a child** into Delaware’s child welfare **mandatory reporting**, **investigation**, **multidisciplinary response**, and **Child Protection Accountability Commission (CPAC)** oversight and reporting framework, including structured information sharing with the **Delaware Anti-Trafficking Action Council**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Adds **“human trafficking of a child”** and **“suspected human trafficking of a child”** throughout child protection statutes, including the child welfare purpose statement, definitions, reporting, investigations, and multidisciplinary case handling (Title 16, §§ 901–906; definitions in § 902).
- Expands mandatory reporting so a person who **knows or in good faith suspects** **abuse, neglect, or human trafficking** must report to the Department (Title 16 § 903), and in certain higher-risk circumstances the report must be made **orally by immediately contacting** the Department’s report line (Title 16 § 904(a)(1)).
- Requires Department notification to the **Investigation Coordinator** (OCA attorney) for specified reports including suspected child trafficking, and expands case tracking through **final criminal and civil disposition** (Title 16 §§ 902, 903(g), 906(c)(1)).
- Requires the Investigation Coordinator to **forward allegations within 5 business days** to the appropriate Delaware licensing/certifying body when the alleged perpetrator/reporter is known to be **licensed/certified** (Title 16 § 906(c)(1)c).
- Establishes that, **absent good cause**, **children ages 3–12** and **all suspected child victims of human trafficking** must be interviewed in a **children’s advocacy center** (Title 16 § 906(d)(6) and § 906(e)(22)).
- Requires reporting/notification and oversight expansion for serious cases (including **death or near-death** due to abuse/neglect/suspected trafficking) to **DOJ and CPAC**, and death cases to the **Maternal and Child Death Review Commission** (Title 16; specific section citations not provided in notes).
- Expands CPAC authority and timelines for suspected child trafficking reviews: review must **commence within 3 months** of a report to CPAC and **conclude within 12 months** absent good cause; approved findings/recommendations must be released within **20 days of approval** (Title 16 § 932A(c) and release provisions described in notes).
- Requires CPAC to provide **quarterly updates and annual reports** to the **Delaware Anti-Trafficking Action Council** regarding suspected child trafficking efforts (Title 11 § 787(k)(3)i; Title 16 § 931(b)(11) as referenced in notes).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Clearer coverage of trafficking in child protection workflows:** explicitly treats suspected child trafficking as a child protection matter for reporting, investigation, tracking, and multidisciplinary response (Title 16 §§ 901–906).
- **Stronger oversight and transparency (within statutory limits):** CPAC must provide **quarterly updates** and **annual reports** to the Anti-Trafficking Action Council, and must release approved findings/recommendations within **20 days** (Title 11 § 787(k)(3)i; Title 16 § 931(b)(11); release timeline described in notes).
- **More standardized, child-centered interviewing:** requires use of a **children’s advocacy center** for ages **3–12** and all suspected child trafficking victims (absent good cause) (Title 16 § 906(d)(6), § 906(e)(22)).
- **Structured review timelines for trafficking cases:** CPAC panel review must start within **3 months** and finish within **12 months** absent good cause (Title 16 § 932A(c)).
- **Improved routing to regulators where applicable:** allegations involving **licensed/certified** individuals must be forwarded to the appropriate licensing/certifying entity within **5 business days** (Title 16 § 906(c)(1)c).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative workload and costs:** fiscal note anticipates **1 additional FTE** for the Office of the Child Advocate (OCA) plus related costs (Fiscal Note Assumption #3; cost table).
- **Reduced reporter anonymity for some professions:** persons licensed through the **Division of Professional Regulation** and the **Department of Education** must provide **name and address** when reporting (Title 16 § 903(e) as described in notes).
- **Expanded authority to obtain medical exams without consent:** Department may authorize and arrange medical exams (including transport) **without caregiver consent** under specified conditions (Title 16 § 906(e)(7)), affecting family autonomy.
- **Limited access to CPAC materials:** CPAC records for death/near-death/suspected child trafficking are confidential and generally **not public**, **not subject to subpoena/discovery**, and **generally inadmissible** in proceedings (Title 16 § 934), which may limit external oversight and litigants’ access.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Mandatory reporting expanded/clarified:** report required for known or good-faith suspected **abuse, neglect, or human trafficking** (Title 16 § 903).
- **Immediate oral reporting in certain cases:** including suspected human trafficking where the alleged perpetrator has access to the alleged victim (Title 16 § 904(a)(1)).
- **Investigation Coordinator tracking expanded:** includes suspected child trafficking through final civil/criminal disposition (Title 16 §§ 902, 906(c)(1) as referenced).
- **5-business-day forwarding:** Investigation Coordinator must forward allegations to licensing/certifying entities within **5 business days** when a subject is known to be licensed/certified (Title 16 § 906(c)(1)c).
- **Children’s advocacy center interview mandate:** absent good cause, applies to **ages 3–12** and **all suspected child trafficking victims** (Title 16 § 906(d)(6), § 906(e)(22)).
- **Death/near-death reporting expanded:** includes suspected child trafficking (DOJ/CPAC notification; deaths to Maternal and Child Death Review Commission) (described in notes; specific citations not fully provided).
- **CPAC trafficking review process and deadlines:** commence within **3 months**, conclude within **12 months** absent good cause; release findings/recommendations within **20 days of approval**; trafficking-related public summary release only after **completion of the prosecution** (Title 16 § 932A(c) and release provisions described in notes).
- **Confidentiality and immunity expanded:** CPAC records on suspected child trafficking treated as confidential/non-public with subpoena/discovery/admissibility limits (Title 16 § 934); immunity applies for actions under §§ 932, 932A, and 933 if in good faith and without malice, with burden on complainant (Title 16 § 935).
- **Anti-Trafficking Action Council reporting:** CPAC must provide **quarterly updates and annual reports** on suspected child trafficking efforts (Title 11 § 787(k)(3)i; Title 16 § 931(b)(11) as referenced).

## Who Benefits
- **Children** who are victims or suspected victims of human trafficking (and abuse/neglect), through explicit inclusion in reporting, investigation, multidisciplinary response, and CPAC oversight (Title 16 §§ 901–906, 931–932A as referenced).
- **Delaware Anti-Trafficking Action Council** through required **quarterly updates** and **annual reports** from CPAC (Title 11 § 787(k)(3)i).
- **CPAC/OCA participants** through clarified authority, confidentiality protections, and good-faith immunity (Title 16 §§ 934–935; Title 29 § 9005A as referenced).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **State agencies and staff** (Department, OCA, CPAC, DOJ, law enforcement, licensing boards) due to increased reporting, tracking, coordination, and review workload (multiple Title 16 provisions referenced; fiscal note staffing impact).
- **Licensed/certified individuals** who may face additional referral/notification to licensing or regulatory bodies when allegations involve them (Title 16 § 906(c)(1)c).
- **Families/caregivers** in cases where the Department authorizes medical examinations/transport **without consent** under specified conditions (Title 16 § 906(e)(7)).
- **Litigants/public** seeking CPAC records, due to confidentiality and limits on subpoena/discovery/admissibility (Title 16 § 934).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Mandatory reporting compliance:** persons who know or in good faith suspect abuse/neglect/human trafficking must report (Title 16 § 903); in specified cases must report **immediately** by oral contact (Title 16 § 904(a)(1)).
- **Reporter identification:** licensed reporters through the Division of Professional Regulation and Department of Education must provide **name and address** when reporting (Title 16 § 903(e) as described).

- **Confidentiality compliance:** CPAC records are confidential and restricted from release/use except as expressly allowed (Title 16 § 934); public releases must not identify individuals or nongovernmental entities and, for trafficking summaries, may occur only after **completion of the prosecution** as defined (release provisions described in notes).
