## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 12 (estates) to allow a person convicted of a crime that would otherwise disqualify them from receiving letters testamentary or letters of administration to receive those letters if the Register of Wills grants an exception for good cause, and to authorize the Register to require attorney representation as a condition of the exception.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates **12 Del. C. § 1508** (persons not qualified to receive letters testamentary or of administration).
- Continues to bar issuance of letters to a **minor** or a person who is **incapacitated/mentally incapacitated**.
- Keeps the general rule that letters **may not** be granted to a person **convicted of a crime disqualifying the person from taking an oath**.
- Creates a new **exception process**: the **Register of Wills** may grant an exception **if good cause is shown**.
- Makes the exception **discretionary** (“unless an exception is granted by the Register of Wills”).
- Authorizes the Register, when granting an exception, to require the petitioner to **retain an attorney admitted to practice before the Delaware Supreme Court**.
- If imposed, the attorney must be retained for the **entirety of the administration of the estate**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Adds flexibility** by allowing otherwise-disqualified individuals (due to conviction) to serve as executor/administrator **when the Register of Wills finds “good cause.”**
- **Court oversight tool**: the Register may condition an exception on **ongoing Delaware-admitted attorney representation for the entire estate administration**, potentially supporting compliance with probate requirements.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Discretion-based standard**: eligibility for a convicted person depends on the Register of Wills’ discretion and a **“good cause”** showing, which may lead to inconsistent outcomes (the bill states “good cause” but does not define it).
- **Potential added cost burden**: if the Register requires counsel, the petitioner must **retain an attorney for the entirety of the administration**, which could increase estate administration costs.
