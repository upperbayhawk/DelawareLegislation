## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware trust and fiduciary law (Titles 12 and 13) to (1) clarify the legal status and powers of “enforcers” for noncharitable purpose trusts, (2) clarify that discretionary trust interests are mere expectancies that generally cannot be compelled by beneficiaries or creditors, (3) broaden nonjudicial pathways for resignation of trustees and other trust officeholders, and (4) clarify when a spouse’s beneficial interest in a “gift in trust” from the other spouse is excluded from “marital property” in divorce.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Adds “enforcers” to key definitions** in Title 12, treating an enforcer as a **fiduciary by default** when exercising authority, unless the trust instrument provides otherwise.  
- **Clarifies discretionary trust interests**: a beneficiary’s discretionary interest is **“a mere expectancy, not a property right”** and the beneficiary **cannot compel distributions**.  
- **Limits creditor reach**: a creditor generally **may not compel distribution** of a discretionary interest, except if the governing instrument **expressly grants** that right consistent with **12 Del. C. § 3536(a)**.  
- **Expands resignation mechanisms for trust officeholders** (trustees, advisers, designated representatives, and enforcers): resignation may be effectuated via **§ 3342 trust modification** or **§ 3338 nonjudicial settlement agreement**, reducing need for Court of Chancery petitions.  
- **Modernizes purpose trust enforcement** under **12 Del. C. § 3556** by (a) stating recipients of purpose-trust disbursements are **not beneficiaries solely for that reason**, (b) defining “enforcer,” and (c) allowing **exclusive standing** to enforce in the governing instrument.  
- **Subjects enforcers to Delaware personal jurisdiction** upon acceptance of appointment for any matter related to the trust.  
- **Divorce/marital property clarification (Title 13)**: unless a trust instrument says otherwise, **property held in a trust created by a donor spouse for the donee spouse** is treated as a **non-marital gift in trust** for purposes of **13 Del. C. § 1513**.  
- **Applicability**: effective upon enactment and **applies to trusts whenever created**; the Act was **approved August 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduced litigation / administrative friction**: expressly authorizing resignation via **§ 3342** (modification) and **§ 3338** (nonjudicial settlement agreement) may reduce reliance on **Court of Chancery approval** (purpose stated in synopsis).  
- **Clearer creditor-beneficiary boundaries**: codifies that beneficiaries with discretionary interests **cannot compel distributions**, and creditors generally **cannot compel distributions** absent express governing-instrument authorization (12 Del. C. § 3315(b)).  
- **Stronger, clearer purpose-trust governance**: defines “enforcer,” permits **exclusive standing** provisions, and clarifies that disbursement recipients are **not beneficiaries solely due to receipt** (12 Del. C. § 3556).  
- **Delaware forum clarity**: enforcers submit to **Delaware personal jurisdiction** upon accepting appointment (12 Del. C. § 3556(f)).  
- **Divorce clarity for “gift in trust” between spouses**: resolves ambiguity by excluding (by default) the donee spouse’s interest in a donor-spouse-created trust from marital property **unless the trust instrument provides otherwise** (13 Del. C. § 1513(b)(1)a.5).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced beneficiary leverage to obtain distributions**: beneficiaries with discretionary interests are expressly denied any right to compel distributions (12 Del. C. § 3315(b)).  
- **Potentially reduced creditor remedies**: creditors are expressly constrained from compelling distributions of discretionary interests except where the governing instrument expressly grants such rights (12 Del. C. § 3315(b); cross-reference § 3536(a)).  
- **Exclusive standing could narrow oversight**: a purpose trust instrument may give the enforcer or another person **exclusive standing** to enforce the trust terms (12 Del. C. § 3556(c)), potentially limiting who may bring enforcement actions.

## Key Policy Changes
- **12 Del. C. § 3301(d) (definitions)**:  
  - Adds **enforcers acting in a fiduciary capacity** under **§ 3556(c)** to the definition of **“fiduciary.”**  
  - Adds **enforcers not acting in a fiduciary capacity** to **“nonfiduciary,”** and clarifies others not acting in a fiduciary capacity are nonfiduciaries.
- **12 Del. C. § 3315(b) (discretionary interests)**:  
  - States explicitly: discretionary interest is **“a mere expectancy, not a property right.”**  
  - Clarifies that even if subject to an **ascertainable standard** (Treas. Reg. **§ 20.2041-1(c)(2)**), it remains discretionary.  
  - Treats “shall” language as discretionary if qualified by discretionary language.  
  - Provides beneficiary **cannot compel** distribution; preserves the **§ 3315(a)** abuse-of-discretion review standard.  
  - Provides creditors may not compel distributions except as expressly allowed by the governing instrument consistent with **§ 3536(a)**.
- **12 Del. C. § 3326 (resignation of an officeholder)**:  
  - Expands “officeholder” to include an **enforcer** under **§ 3556(c)**.  
  - Adds/clarifies resignation paths: by **§ 3342** modification (including when other paths apply), by **§ 3338** nonjudicial settlement agreement when (b)(1)-(b)(3) do not apply, or otherwise by **Court of Chancery** approval.  
  - Retains **30 days written notice** requirement in the scenario where the governing instrument is silent on resignation but provides a successor appointment process (notice to beneficiaries, appointing power holders, and other officeholders).
- **12 Del. C. § 3556 (noncharitable purpose trusts)**:  
  - Validates a declared-purpose trust that is **“not impossible of attainment.”**  
  - States a purpose trust is **not invalid** for lack of an identifiable beneficiary; disbursement recipients are **not beneficiaries solely** due to receiving disbursements.  
  - Defines “enforcer” (person appointed by the trust or by Court of Chancery).  
  - Allows a person with an interest in the declared purpose (other than general public interest) to petition to appoint/remove an enforcer, and allows petitions regarding nominated/current enforcers.  
  - Allows governing instrument to grant **exclusive standing** to enforce.  
  - Requires trust property be used only for intended use; on termination, remaining property **must** be distributed per trust terms or, absent terms, under **§ 3592**.  
  - Enforcer is a **fiduciary by default** unless the governing instrument provides otherwise.  
  - Enforcer accepting appointment submits to **Delaware personal jurisdiction** for trust-related matters.  
  - If no identifiable beneficiaries: **§§ 3338 and 3342** may be used without any beneficiary as an “interested person” / without beneficiary consent or nonobjection; if enforcers are serving, each enforcer is an “interested person” for § 3338 and each enforcer’s written consent/nonobjection is required for § 3342.
- **12 Del. C. § 3580 (definition of “trustee” for Subchapter VII, Ch. 35)**:  
  - Expands “trustee” to expressly include **enforcers under § 3556**.
- **13 Del. C. § 1513(b)(1)a (marital property definition for divorce)**:  
  - Adds clause: **Unless the trust instrument expressly provides otherwise**, gifted property held in a trust created by a **donor spouse** for the **donee spouse** (regardless of other beneficiaries) is treated as excluded from “marital property” as a gift in trust.

## Who Benefits
- **Settlors and fiduciaries** administering Delaware trusts (clearer rules for discretionary interests, resignation mechanisms, and purpose trust enforcement structure).  
- **Purpose trust enforcers** (clear statutory status, default fiduciary classification, and procedural roles in nonjudicial settlement/modification when no beneficiaries exist).  
- **Spouses receiving gifts in trust** in divorce proceedings (clarification that such interests are generally not marital property unless the trust instrument provides otherwise).  
- **Trusts lacking identifiable beneficiaries** (ability to use §§ 3338 and 3342 without beneficiary participation; enforcer fills “interested person”/consent roles).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Trust beneficiaries with discretionary interests** who seek distributions (statute expressly states they cannot compel distributions).  
- **Creditors** attempting to reach discretionary trust interests (express restriction on compelling distributions absent express governing-instrument authorization consistent with § 3536(a)).  
- **Individuals who might otherwise seek to enforce a purpose trust** (governing instrument may confer **exclusive standing** on the enforcer or another person).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Enforcer jurisdiction/consent**: accepting appointment as enforcer submits the person to **Delaware personal jurisdiction** for trust-related matters (12 Del. C. § 3556(f)).  
- **Use-of-property rule**: property of a purpose trust may be applied **only to its intended use** (12 Del. C. § 3556(d)).  
- **Resignation notice**: **30 days written notice** required in the § 3326(b)(2) resignation scenario (notice to beneficiaries, successor-appointing power holders, and other officeholders).
