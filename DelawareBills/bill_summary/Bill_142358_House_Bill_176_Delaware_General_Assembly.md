## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Promote equipment owner choice and competition for agricultural equipment repair and maintenance by requiring original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) to provide owners and independent repair providers access—on “fair and reasonable terms”—to the same documentation, parts, and tools the OEM uses to diagnose, maintain, and repair the equipment. (New Title 3, Chapter 18, § 1801)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires OEMs of agricultural equipment sold or used in Delaware to make available, on “fair and reasonable terms,” any documentation, parts, and tools required for diagnosis, maintenance, or repair, including updates. (§ 1803(a))
- Prohibits OEMs from using “parts pairing” or other mechanisms to block installation/functioning of otherwise-functional parts (including non-OEM parts), degrade functionality, generate false/misleading alerts, charge extra fees for future repairs, or restrict who can buy repair resources or perform repairs. (§ 1803(b))
- Requires OEMs to provide the documentation/tools/parts needed to access and reset electronic locks for repair purposes, available to the owner or to an independent repair provider with the owner’s express permission; may be provided via a secure release system. (§ 1803(c))
- Defines “fair and reasonable terms” to include: no minimum/maximum purchase quantities for parts; no extra contracts beyond a purchase order for parts; documentation available without contract/account creation and at no charge (except reasonable actual printing/shipping); tools available at no charge (except reasonable actual physical preparation/shipping) and not requiring authorization or internet access. (§ 1804(5))
- Enforceable by the Delaware Attorney General through existing consumer protection/unfair trade practice authority under Chapter 25 of Title 6 (as referenced in § 1805). (§ 1805)
- Protects trade secrets (not required to be divulged except as necessary to diagnose/maintain/repair on fair and reasonable terms). (§ 1806(a))
- Applies only to equipment first manufactured and sold on or after the Act’s effective date. (§ 1807)
- Takes effect **6 months after enactment** (exact calendar date depends on enactment date). (Section 2)

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More repair options and competition:** Independent repair providers and owners gain access to OEM documentation/parts/tools needed for repairs, which may broaden repair choices. (§§ 1801, 1803(a))
- **Reduces repair lockouts tied to “parts pairing”:** Explicitly bars software-based pairing or similar tactics that prevent functioning of otherwise-functional parts or reduce performance after independent repair. (§ 1803(b); § 1804(10))
- **Improves access to repair information and tools at low/no cost:** Documentation must be available at **no charge** (except reasonable actual costs for printed copies), and tools at **no charge** (except reasonable actual costs for physical copies). (§ 1804(5)b, (5)b.1)
- **Addresses electronic locks:** Requires OEM support to access/reset electronic locks for repair with owner permission, potentially reducing downtime for locked systems. (§ 1803(c))
- **Leverages existing enforcement framework:** Attorney General can use existing Title 6 consumer protection/unfair trade practice remedies. (§ 1805)

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **OEM compliance burden:** OEMs must provide broad categories of documentation, parts, and tools (including updates) and may need systems (including secure release systems) to support access and electronic lock resets. (§ 1803(a), (c))
- **Potential disputes over “fair and reasonable terms”:** Although defined, the statute still relies on reasonableness standards (e.g., “substantial obligation,” “reasonably necessary,” “reasonable actual costs”), which may create compliance uncertainty. (§ 1804(5))
- **Trade secret boundary could limit usefulness:** OEMs are not required to divulge trade secrets except as “necessary,” which could constrain access to certain information. (§ 1806(a))

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective-date specificity:** The Act takes effect “6 months after it is enacted,” but the enactment date is not provided in the source text, so the exact effective date cannot be calculated here. (Section 2)
- **Scope of “property maintenance” equipment:** “Agricultural equipment” includes “products used in property maintenance,” which could be interpreted broadly; boundaries are not further defined. (§ 1804(1))
- **Pricing standard for printed materials/physical tools:** Allowed charges are “reasonable actual costs of preparing and sending,” but the bill does not define what documentation is required to substantiate costs. (§ 1804(5)b; § 1804(5)b.1)
- **Enforcement trigger standard:** Attorney General enforcement depends on determining a failure “constitutes an unfair method of competition, false advertising, or unfair or deceptive trade practices,” but the statute does not specify a complaint process or standards for that determination. (§ 1805)

## Key Policy Changes
- **Mandatory repair resource access:** OEMs must provide owners and independent repair providers documentation, parts, and tools required for diagnosis/maintenance/repair, including updates, for agricultural equipment and parts sold or used in Delaware, on “fair and reasonable terms.” (§ 1803(a))
- **Anti–parts pairing rules:** OEMs cannot use parts pairing or other mechanisms to:
  - prevent installation/functioning of otherwise-functional parts (including non-OEM parts),
  - reduce functioning/performance after independent repair,
  - create false/misleading/deceptive/non-dismissible alerts,
  - charge additional fees or increased prices for future repairs, or
  - limit who can buy documentation/parts/tools or perform repairs. (§ 1803(b))
- **Electronic lock access for repair:** OEMs must provide access/reset materials for electronic locks for repair, to owners or to independent repair providers with owner permission, possibly via a secure release system. (§ 1803(c))
- **Defined “fair and reasonable terms”:**
  - Parts: no unreasonable restrictions; no min/max quantities; no contracts beyond purchase order. (§ 1804(5)a)
  - Documentation: no contract/account creation; no charge except reasonable actual printing/shipping for physical copies. (§ 1804(5)b)
  - Tools: no charge except reasonable actual costs for physical form; no authorization/internet required; no impediments to full-function repairs. (§ 1804(5)b.1–b.3)
- **Attorney General enforcement via Title 6 Chapter 25 remedies:** Noncompliance can be treated as an unfair/deceptive practice with associated remedies/penalties under that law. (§ 1805)
- **Limitations and protections:** Trade secret protection; does not alter authorized repair provider agreements (but waivers of OEM obligations are void); limits OEM/authorized provider liability for damage caused by independent repair; does not require disabling anti-theft measures without owner authorization; no duty to sell unavailable parts; no duty to sell illegal-to-use service materials; allows dealer markup. (§ 1806)

## Who Benefits
- **Owners/lessees of agricultural equipment purchased or used in Delaware** (including farmers and businesses) through broader access to repair documentation, tools, and parts. (§§ 1803, 1804(8))
- **Independent repair providers operating in Delaware** who lack OEM authorization arrangements, by granting access to OEM-equivalent repair resources. (§§ 1803, 1804(6))
- **Potentially authorized repair providers/distributors** insofar as OEMs may provide access “via an authorized repair provider or distributor.” (§ 1803(a))

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Original equipment manufacturers (OEMs):** Required to provide repair documentation/parts/tools (including lock reset support) under specified terms and subject to Attorney General enforcement. (§§ 1803, 1805)
- **OEM-controlled service models:** Prohibitions on parts pairing and restrictions on who may purchase repair resources or perform repairs may reduce OEM control over repair channels. (§ 1803(b))

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- **Effective date:** The Act takes effect **6 months after it is enacted**. (Section 2)
- **Applicability threshold:** Applies to equipment **first manufactured and sold on or after the effective date** of the Act. (§ 1807)

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Enforcement authority:** If the Attorney General determines noncompliance with § 1803 is an unfair method of competition, false advertising, or unfair/deceptive trade practice under **Title 6, Chapter 25**, then **all remedies, penalties, and authority** available under that law are available to enforce this chapter. (§ 1805)
- **Core compliance duties (OEMs):**
  - Provide documentation/parts/tools (including updates) on “fair and reasonable terms.” (§ 1803(a))
  - Do not use parts pairing (or similar mechanisms) for the prohibited purposes listed in § 1803(b).
  - Provide tools/documentation/parts needed to access/reset electronic locks for repair, with owner permission safeguards. (§ 1803(c))
- **Void contract terms:** Any authorized repair provider agreement term that purports to waive/avoid/restrict/limit OEM compliance obligations under the Act is “void and unenforceable.” (§ 1806(b))
