## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend the Town of Millsboro charter to (1) require periodic redistricting of the Town’s **3 municipal election districts** using federal decennial census data, (2) revise how **Town Council vacancies** are filled based on time remaining in the term, and (3) clarify that the **Town Secretary** is a Town Council member and specify additional Secretary duties.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires Millsboro Town Council to redraw boundaries of the **3 election districts** using the **2020 federal decennial census** **no later than January 1, 2026**.
- Requires subsequent redistricting **no later than January 1 of the second year following each federal decennial census**.
- Sets districting standards: districts must be **contiguous** and, to the extent possible, **nearly equal in population** based on the most recent federal decennial census.
- Provides when new district boundaries take effect: immediately for the **next annual municipal election (including nominations)** upon enactment; for “all other purposes” on the date of the annual meeting when newly elected council members take office.
- Changes vacancy process: if **less than 1 year** remains in the term, vacancy is filled by **Town Council appointment** until the next regular municipal election.
- If **more than 1 year** remains, Town Council must call a **special election** held **not less than 30 days nor more than 90 days** after the vacancy occurs.
- If **no qualified person files** to run in the special election, Town Council fills the vacancy by **appointment**.
- Clarifies Secretary governance: Secretary must be **selected from members of Town Council** and must (among other duties) **record and provide executive-session minutes**, **sign adopted resolutions/ordinances**, and **coordinate with the Town Clerk** on minutes.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More equal representation:** Requires districts be “nearly equal in population” (to the extent possible/practicable) using the most recent decennial census.
- **Clear, time-bound redistricting:** Establishes a firm first deadline (**January 1, 2026**) and a recurring schedule (by **January 1** of the **second year** after each decennial census).
- **More democratic vacancy filling for longer terms:** Requires a **special election** when a vacancy occurs with **more than 1 year** remaining.
- **Administrative clarity:** Specifies voting thresholds and recordkeeping for appointments (majority vote of remaining members; results recorded in minutes).
- **Defined Secretary responsibilities:** Adds explicit duties regarding minutes (including executive sessions), document execution (signing ordinances/resolutions), and coordination with the Town Clerk.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Special election administrative burden:** When vacancies occur with **more than 1 year** remaining, the Town must hold a special election within **30–90 days**, which can increase administrative workload. (No cost amounts are stated in the bill text.)
- **Redistricting workload:** Requires council action to adjust district boundaries by **January 1, 2026** and after each census cycle; the bill does not provide staffing or resources.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The bill requires districts be “nearly equal in population” **“to the extent possible and practicable,”** but does not define an allowable deviation or standard.
- The bill states new districts supersede prior boundaries “upon enactment” for the next annual municipal election, but does not define how quickly maps must be finalized relative to nomination deadlines.
- The bill requires executive-session minutes be circulated for approval “at the following meeting or as soon thereafter as possible,” but does not define a specific timeframe.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Redistricting mandate and schedule**
  - Must adjust boundaries of **3** election districts using **2020 decennial census data** by **January 1, 2026**.
  - Thereafter, must redistrict by **January 1 of the second year following the federal decennial census**.
  - District requirements: **contiguous territory**; **nearly equal population** (to the extent possible and practicable) based on most recent decennial census.
  - **Effectiveness of new boundaries:** Upon enactment for the next annual municipal election (including nominations); for all other purposes when newly elected council takes office at the annual meeting.
- **Vacancy-filling process revised**
  - **< 1 year remaining:** Town Council **appoints** a qualified person (and, if applicable, resident of the relevant election district) until the next regular municipal election.
  - **> 1 year remaining:** Town Council must call a **special election** held **30–90 days** after the vacancy occurs; winner serves remainder of term.
  - **No candidates file:** Town Council fills vacancy by **appointment**.
  - Appointment requires **majority vote of remaining members** and must be **recorded in Town Council minutes**.
- **Town Secretary clarified and duties expanded**
  - Secretary must be **selected from members of the Town Council**.
  - Secretary responsible for **recording/providing executive-session minutes** and circulating them for approval at the next meeting or as soon thereafter as possible.
  - Secretary must **sign all resolutions and ordinances** once adopted.
  - Secretary must **coordinate with the Town Clerk** on minutes for all Town Council meetings.

## Who Benefits
- **Millsboro voters**, through updated district boundaries intended to be more population-balanced after the 2020 census and future censuses.
- **Candidates and election administrators**, through clearer rules on when new district boundaries apply (including nominations).
- **Residents of districts with vacancies**, when **more than 1 year** remains, because the vacancy is filled by a **special election** rather than appointment.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Town of Millsboro administration/Town Council**, due to added procedural obligations (redistricting by set deadlines; administering special elections within **30–90 days**).
- **Potential appointees/insiders**, because vacancies with **more than 1 year** remaining must generally be filled by election rather than appointment.
