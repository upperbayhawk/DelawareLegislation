## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amends Title 14 to require the Department of Health and Social Services (DHSS), in consultation with the Department of Education (DOE), to post existing Type 1 diabetes informational materials online and require school districts to make those materials available to parents/guardians.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires DHSS, **in consultation with DOE**, to **post existing appropriate Type 1 diabetes informational materials** on the **DHSS website**.  
- Requires **each school district** to **make the informational materials available** to **parents/guardians** of enrolled students.  
- Specifies required content of the materials, including **description**, **risk factors and warning signs**, and **screening and implications of results**.  
- Includes a recommendation that parents/guardians of a student showing warning signs **consult the student’s primary care provider** to determine if **immediate screening** is appropriate.  
- Includes a recommendation that after a diagnosis, parents/guardians consult a primary care provider to develop a treatment plan, potentially including a **qualified specialist (e.g., endocrinologist)**.  
- Establishes an effective-date rule tied to **August 1, 2025**, with the law taking effect on an **August 1** depending on enactment timing.  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardizes access to Type 1 diabetes information statewide** by requiring DHSS to post materials and school districts to make them available to parents/guardians.  
- May support **earlier recognition of warning signs** by requiring materials to describe **risk factors and warning signs** and recommend consultation for **immediate screening** when signs appear.  
- Provides clearer guidance to families after diagnosis by requiring materials to recommend consultation for an **appropriate treatment plan**, including specialist care (e.g., **endocrinologist**) when appropriate.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- Creates an **administrative requirement** for DHSS (posting materials) and **each school district** (making materials available), but the bill text does **not specify implementation methods**, which could create inconsistent practices across districts.  
- The bill requires posting **“existing appropriate informational materials”** rather than developing new or Delaware-specific materials, which may limit tailoring; the text does not require updating or expanding materials.
