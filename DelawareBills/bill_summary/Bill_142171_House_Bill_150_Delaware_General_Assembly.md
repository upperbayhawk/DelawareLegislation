## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To protect access to Delaware courthouses and judicial proceedings by prohibiting “civil arrests” in and around courthouses and for people traveling to/from court for matters related to a judicial proceeding, unless the arrest is made pursuant to a judicial order authorizing the arrest.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a statutory prohibition on “civil arrest” while a person is going to, remaining at, or returning from a courthouse for matters related to a judicial proceeding.  
- Extends the prohibition to people “participating in or attending a judicial proceeding” as a party, witness, attorney, or observer.  
- Applies the protection not only inside the courthouse but also to “grounds, parking areas, and immediate surroundings” of courthouses.  
- Defines “civil arrest” as an arrest “for matters not related to criminal law enforcement or other judicial order authorizing such arrest.”  
- Defines “courthouse” as any building or facility where State judicial proceedings occur or where judicial officers/employees are housed.  
- Creates remedies: habeas corpus relief for unlawful arrest/detention; civil damages exposure for knowing violations (false imprisonment); potential attorney’s fees and costs awards to prevailing parties.  
- Preserves an exception for arrests made “pursuant to a judicial order authorizing such arrest.”  
- Places enforcement authority with judicial officers to prohibit activities that threaten access to courthouses/court proceedings or interrupt judicial administration.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves courthouse access and participation:** The bill expressly protects parties, witnesses, attorneys, and observers from civil arrest tied to courthouse attendance, which may reduce deterrence from appearing in court (Sections 8202(a)(1)-(2)).  
- **Clarifies protected areas:** Explicitly covers courthouse “grounds, parking areas, and immediate surroundings,” addressing arrests occurring near—not just inside—courthouses (Section 8202(b)).  
- **Creates enforceable remedies:** Provides habeas corpus relief and civil liability for knowing violators, plus possible attorney’s fees/costs awards, which may strengthen compliance (Section 8203(b)-(d)).  
- **Preserves court-authorized arrests:** Allows arrests made pursuant to a “judicial order,” maintaining a pathway for court-approved civil enforcement (Section 8202(c)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits certain civil enforcement actions near courts:** By prohibiting “civil arrest” in/around courthouses and for travel to/from court (unless judicially authorized), the bill restricts non-criminal arrest activities in these settings (Section 8202).  
- **Creates potential civil liability for violators:** A “knowing” violation can lead to damages for false imprisonment and potential attorney’s fees/costs, increasing legal exposure for those who conduct civil arrests (Section 8203(c)-(d)).  
- **May increase litigation over violations:** The availability of habeas corpus and damages actions may increase motions/petitions and civil suits related to alleged prohibited arrests (Section 8203(b)-(d)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “civil arrest”:** Defined as arrests “not related to criminal law enforcement or other judicial order authorizing such arrest” (Section 8201(1)), but the bill does not specify which categories of civil processes or detentions count (e.g., civil contempt, immigration-related detainers, or other administrative arrests), beyond the definition’s general language.  
- **Meaning of “immediate surroundings”:** The bill does not define how far beyond courthouse property lines “immediate surroundings” extends (Section 8202(b)).  
- **Coverage for non-State proceedings/buildings:** “Courthouse” includes buildings where “State judicial proceedings” occur or where judicial officers/employees are housed (Section 8201(2)); the treatment of locations used for proceedings not fitting that description is not specified.  
- **Standard for “knowingly violates”:** The bill uses “knowingly” as the threshold for civil liability but does not define the standard or evidentiary requirements (Section 8203(c)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Adds new definitions (Title 10, Chapter 82):**
  - “Civil arrest” = arrest for matters not related to criminal law enforcement or other judicial order authorizing the arrest (Section 8201(1)).  
  - “Courthouse” = any building/facility where State judicial proceedings are conducted or where judicial officers/employees are housed (Section 8201(2)).  
- **Creates a prohibition on civil arrests in connection with court participation:**
  - Prohibits civil arrest while going to, remaining at, or returning from a courthouse for matters related to a judicial proceeding (Section 8202(a)(1)).  
  - Prohibits civil arrest while participating in/attending judicial proceedings as a party, witness, attorney, or observer (Section 8202(a)(2)).  
  - Applies to courthouse grounds, parking areas, and immediate surroundings (Section 8202(b)).  
  - Exception: does not apply to arrests made pursuant to a judicial order authorizing the arrest (Section 8202(c)).  
- **Establishes enforcement/remedies:**
  - Judicial officers may prohibit activities that threaten access to courthouses/court proceedings or interrupt judicial administration, including protecting the privilege from civil arrest (Section 8203(a)).  
  - Unlawfully arrested/detained persons may seek habeas corpus (Section 8203(b)).  
  - Knowing violators may be liable for damages for false imprisonment (Section 8203(c)).  
  - Courts may award attorney’s fees and costs to prevailing parties (Section 8203(d)).

## Who Benefits
- Individuals going to, attending, or leaving court for matters related to a judicial proceeding (Section 8202(a)(1)).  
- Parties, witnesses, attorneys, and observers participating in or attending judicial proceedings (Section 8202(a)(2)).  
- Courts and court administration, through explicit authority to protect access and prevent interruptions to judicial administration (Section 8203(a)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Persons or entities that conduct or seek to conduct “civil arrests” in or around courthouses, or against individuals traveling to/from court for judicial-proceeding-related matters, unless the arrest is authorized by a judicial order (Section 8202).  
- Individuals who “knowingly” violate the prohibition, due to exposure to damages, attorney’s fees, and costs (Section 8203(c)-(d)).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Enforcement authority:** Judicial officers may prohibit activities threatening courthouse access or interrupting judicial administration, including protecting the privilege from civil arrest (Section 8203(a)).  
- **Remedy for violation:** A person arrested or detained in violation may seek a writ of habeas corpus (Section 8203(b)).  
- **Civil liability/penalty mechanism:** A person who **knowingly** violates the chapter may be liable for damages in a civil action for **false imprisonment** (Section 8203(c)).  
- **Fee shifting:** Courts may award **attorney’s fees and costs** to prevailing parties in actions under Section 8203 (Section 8203(d)).
