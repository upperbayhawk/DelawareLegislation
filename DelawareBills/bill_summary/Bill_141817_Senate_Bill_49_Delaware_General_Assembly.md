## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To amend Delaware emergency management and motor vehicle laws to (1) add “first informers” (certain broadcast personnel) as an exempt category under emergency driving restrictions, (2) clarify who qualifies as “essential personnel,” and (3) align enforcement references and technical drafting in Titles 20 and 21 regarding Level 2 and Level 3 driving restrictions during a state of emergency.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new legal category, **“first informer,”** defined as certain **FCC-licensed radio/TV station** broadcast engineers or on-air employees engaged in broadcasting or maintaining/operating/repairing broadcast equipment (Title 20 §3102).  
- Expands the **“essential personnel”** definition to expressly include **emergency broadcasting services** (in addition to utilities, health-care services, and food/fuel deliveries) during a state of emergency (Title 20 §3102).  
- Updates **Level 3 Driving Ban** exemptions to include **first informers** and reiterates exemptions for first responders, essential personnel, utilities, and snow removal operators (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a).  
- Maintains/clarifies employee protections: employers (public or private) **may not take adverse employment action** against employees who fail/refuse to report to work due to an activated **Level 3 Driving Ban** (and for Level 2 restrictions, where no waiver has been granted) (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b).  
- For **Level 2 Driving Restriction**, confirms it limits driving except for **essential personnel** (including snow removal operators), and allows **waivers** for “significant safety, health or business necessity” (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)b).  
- Directs the **Secretary of the Department of Safety and Homeland Security** to promulgate rules and regulations necessary to carry out the Level 2/Level 3 driving restriction provisions (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)b and §3116(d)).  
- Aligns Title 21 to make violation of activated Level 2/Level 3 restrictions unlawful **unless exempt or waived**, including adding **first informer** as an exempt person (Title 21 §4176D).  
- Provides that violations of an activated **Level 3 Driving Ban** are penalized under **Title 20 §3125**, while violations of an activated **Level 2 Driving Restriction** are violations of **Title 21 §4176D** (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Improves emergency communications continuity:** By exempting “first informers” from Level 2/Level 3 driving restrictions, the bill supports continued operation/repair of broadcast systems needed to inform the public during emergencies (Synopsis; Title 20 §3102(7); Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a; Title 21 §4176D).  
- **Clarifies eligible essential services:** The definition of “essential personnel” explicitly includes **emergency broadcasting services** alongside utilities, health care, and food/fuel deliveries (Title 20 §3102(6)).  
- **Workforce protection during bans/restrictions:** Prohibits employers from disciplining employees who do not report to work due to an activated Level 3 ban; and for Level 2 restrictions, protects employees when the employer/entity **has not been granted a waiver** (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b).  
- **Regulatory framework:** Requires DS&H Security to issue implementing rules/regulations for driving restrictions (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)b; §3116(d)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Broader exempt driving population during emergencies:** Adding “first informers” as exempt from driving restrictions may increase the number of vehicles on roads during Level 2/Level 3 conditions (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a; Title 21 §4176D; Synopsis).  
- **Employer operational constraints:** Employers are limited in taking adverse employment action when employees cannot or refuse to report during an activated Level 3 ban, and during Level 2 restrictions when no waiver is granted (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b).  
- **Administrative burden for waivers and regulations:** The waiver process (“significant safety, health or business necessity”) and required regulations may add administrative workload for the Department of Safety and Homeland Security (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)b; §3116(d)).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities

- **Scope of “emergency broadcasting services”:** Added to “essential personnel” definition, but not further defined (Title 20 §3102(6)).  
- **Waiver standards/process details:** Waiver available upon “significant safety, health or business necessity,” but the bill does not specify timelines, application procedures, or appeal rights (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)b).  
- **Enforcement mechanics:** The bill references penalties/violations (Title 20 §3125; Title 21 §4176D) but does not restate penalty amounts or enforcement procedures in this text.

## Key Policy Changes
- **New definition:** Adds **“first informer”** to Title 20 definitions (broadcast engineer/on-air employee of FCC-licensed radio/TV station involved in broadcasting or maintaining/repairing broadcast equipment) (Title 20 §3102(7)).  
- **Updated definition:** Revises **“essential personnel”** to include providing **emergency broadcasting services** during a state of emergency (Title 20 §3102(6)).  
- **Level 3 Driving Ban exemptions:** Explicitly includes **first informers** among those who may drive during a Level 3 ban (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a).  
- **Employee protections (Level 3):** Employers (public or private) may not take adverse employment action against an employee who fails/refuses to report due to an activated Level 3 ban (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a).  
- **Level 2 Driving Restriction structure:** Confirms restriction with exceptions for essential personnel (including snow removal operators), authorizes waivers for “significant safety, health or business necessity,” and requires DS&H Security regulations (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)b).  
- **Employee protections (Level 2):** Employers/entities **without a waiver** may not take adverse employment action against employees who fail/refuse to report during an activated Level 2 restriction (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)b).  
- **Enforcement cross-references:** Level 3 ban violations penalized under **Title 20 §3125**; Level 2 restriction violations are violations of **Title 21 §4176D**; Title 21 is amended to include first informers and waiver reference (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b; Title 21 §4176D).

## Who Benefits
- **Broadcast engineers and on-air employees** of FCC-licensed radio/TV stations who meet the “first informer” definition (Title 20 §3102(7)).  
- **Delaware residents** who rely on emergency broadcast information during states of emergency (Synopsis).  
- **Essential service providers** (utilities, health-care, food/fuel delivery, emergency broadcasting services) by clarified inclusion in “essential personnel” definition (Title 20 §3102(6)).  
- **Employees generally** who gain explicit job-protection language tied to Level 3 bans and certain Level 2 restrictions (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Employers (public and private):** restricted from disciplining employees who do not report due to Level 3 bans, and for Level 2 restrictions where the employer/entity lacks a waiver (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b).  
- **Non-exempt drivers/businesses:** may face continued limits on roadway use during Level 2/Level 3 declarations, with enforcement under Title 20 §3125 (Level 3) or Title 21 §4176D (Level 2) (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a–b; Title 21 §4176D).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements

  - **Level 3 Driving Ban:** No person may operate a motor vehicle on Delaware roadways when activated, **except** first responders, **first informers**, essential personnel (including public utilities), and snow removal operators employed/contracted by public or private entities, as authorized by the Governor’s executive order and emergency plans (Title 20 §3116(b)(12)a).
