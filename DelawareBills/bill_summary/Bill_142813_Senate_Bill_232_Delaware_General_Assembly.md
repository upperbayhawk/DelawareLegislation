## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Delaware’s criminal mischief statute (11 Del. C. § 811) to create a higher felony level for intentionally damaging an **authorized emergency vehicle** in certain circumstances, and to make technical drafting corrections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Creates a new **Class F felony** for **intentionally** damaging an **authorized emergency vehicle** when the damage either (a) causes **$5,000 or more** in pecuniary loss, or (b) makes the vehicle **temporarily unable to respond to an emergency**.  
- Defines “authorized emergency vehicle” broadly to include certain **vehicles, aircraft, watercraft, and unmanned aircraft systems** owned/leased/used by **fire departments/companies, law-enforcement agencies, and ambulance companies**, plus **specialized vehicles used by paramedics**.  
- Keeps existing criminal mischief grading for other property damage, including **Class G felony** for **$5,000+** loss or substantial interruption/impairment of specified public services.  
- Maintains misdemeanor tiers, including **Class A misdemeanor** when pecuniary loss is **in excess of $1,000** and higher felony provisions do not apply.  
- Retains a **minimum mandatory fine of at least $500** for criminal mischief “of any degree” committed **on or along a Delaware byway** (as defined in 17 Del. C. § 101).  
- Retains defenses, including a defense based on a **reasonable ground to believe** the person had a right to engage in the conduct.  
- Adds/clarifies statutory definitions (e.g., “aircraft,” “watercraft,” “unmanned aircraft system” (by cross-reference)) to support the emergency-vehicle provision.  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Enhanced protection/deterrence for emergency response assets:** Elevates certain conduct to a **Class F felony** when an emergency vehicle is damaged with **$5,000+ loss** or **temporary inability to respond**, potentially discouraging interference with emergency response capability (new § 811(b)(1)).  
- **Clearer coverage of modern emergency response equipment:** Explicitly includes **aircraft, watercraft, and unmanned aircraft systems** within “authorized emergency vehicle,” clarifying applicability to these assets (new definitions in § 811(a)(4)).  
- **Targets operational impact even without a dollar threshold:** Felony applies if the vehicle becomes **temporarily unable to be used to respond to an emergency**, regardless of whether $5,000 damage is proven (new § 811(b)(1)).  

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Increased criminal severity for a specific category of property:** Conduct that would otherwise be graded under other pecuniary-loss thresholds may become a higher-level felony when it involves an authorized emergency vehicle and meets the stated conditions (new § 811(b)(1)).  
- **Potential for litigation over new terms:** The provision hinges on whether a vehicle became “**temporarily unable to be used to respond to an emergency**,” which may be contested in prosecutions (new § 811(b)(1)).  

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- What qualifies as “**temporarily unable to be used to respond to an emergency**” (e.g., duration, partial capability, backup availability) is **not defined** in the bill text.  
- How “**resulting in pecuniary loss of $5,000 or more**” will be calculated (repair estimates vs. replacement value vs. out-of-service costs) is **not specified** in the bill text.  
- The relationship between the new emergency-vehicle felony and existing provisions (e.g., overlap with Class G felony criteria) is not further explained beyond the re-numbering and insertion.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates a new grading tier:** Criminal mischief becomes a **Class F felony** if a person **intentionally** damages an **authorized emergency vehicle** and either:  
  - causes **pecuniary loss of $5,000 or more**, **or**  
  - causes the emergency vehicle to become **temporarily unable to respond to an emergency**.  
  (11 Del. C. § 811(b)(1), as amended)
- **Adds definitions tied to the new offense**, including:  
  - “Authorized emergency vehicle” including certain **vehicles/aircraft/watercraft/unmanned aircraft systems** used by **fire departments/companies, law-enforcement agencies, ambulance companies**, and **specialized vehicles used by paramedics**.  
  - “Aircraft” and “Watercraft” definitions; “Unmanned aircraft system” defined by cross-reference to **11 Del. C. § 1334**; “Vehicle” by cross-reference to **11 Del. C. § 222**; “Law-enforcement agency” defined by reference to **11 Del. C. § 222**.  
  (11 Del. C. § 811(a)(4), as amended)
- **Re-numbers existing penalty subsections** to accommodate the new Class F felony, while maintaining:  
  - **Class G felony** for **$5,000+** loss or substantial interruption/impairment of specified public services (now § 811(b)(2));  
  - **Class A misdemeanor** for pecuniary loss **in excess of $1,000** when higher felony paragraphs do not apply (now § 811(b)(3));  
  - otherwise an **unclassified misdemeanor** (now § 811(b)(4));  
  - **minimum mandatory fine of at least $500** for acts “on or along a Delaware byway” (now § 811(b)(5)).  
- **Technical corrections** to align with the Delaware Legislative Drafting Manual (per Synopsis).

## Who Benefits
- **Fire departments/companies, law-enforcement agencies, and ambulance companies**, and **paramedics** whose operational vehicles (including aircraft/watercraft/UAS used in duties) receive additional statutory protection through heightened penalties.  
- The public relying on emergency response availability, to the extent the statute deters conduct that disables emergency response vehicles.

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Individuals charged with criminal mischief involving an “authorized emergency vehicle,” who may face **Class F felony** exposure when the conduct meets the new thresholds (intentional damage plus **$5,000+** loss or temporary inability to respond).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- Establishes/clarifies the following penalties for criminal mischief (11 Del. C. § 811(b), as amended):  
  - **Class F felony:** intentional damage to an authorized emergency vehicle with **$5,000+** pecuniary loss **or** temporary inability to respond to an emergency.  
  - **Class G felony:** intentional pecuniary loss of **$5,000 or more**, or intentional substantial interruption/impairment of public communication, transportation, supply of water, gas, power, or other public service.  
  - **Class A misdemeanor:** intentional or reckless pecuniary loss **in excess of $1,000** when the Class F/Class G felony provisions do not apply.  
  - **Unclassified misdemeanor:** when the above grading paragraphs do not apply.  
  - **Minimum mandatory fine of at least $500** if criminal mischief of any degree is committed **on or along a Delaware byway**.  
- Retains defenses in § 811(c), including that the person had a **reasonable ground to believe** they had a right to engage in the conduct, and retains the affirmative defense for lawful purpose in prosecutions involving tampering with utility property (as reflected in § 811(a)(3)).
