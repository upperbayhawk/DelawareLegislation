## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 7, § 6046 (Auction revenue) to keep Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative (RGGI) CO₂ allowance auction proceeds allocated to existing statutory purposes at **the total dollar amount generated in 2025**, and direct **any proceeds above 2025 levels** to a **Delmarva Power & Light bill-credit rebate program** to reduce retail electric customers’ energy costs, beginning **January 1, 2026**.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Keeps existing RGGI auction revenue allocations (e.g., **65% to SEU**, **15% low-income programs**, **10% greenhouse gas reduction projects**, **up to 10%** for RGGI program/administration) **but caps the total dollars sent to these purposes at 2025 total proceeds**.  
- Creates a new requirement that **starting January 1, 2026**, any **CO₂ allowance proceeds exceeding the total dollar amount generated in 2025** must go to an **electricity cost rebate program**.  
- Requires **DNREC** to **promulgate regulations** and **administer** the rebate program.  
- Requires rebate funds to be provided to **Delmarva Power & Light** for **pro rata distribution** to **retail electric customers** (as defined in **Title 26, § 1001(21)**).  
- Requires the rebate to appear as a reduction on the customer’s **Delmarva Power & Light bill** issued under **Title 26, § 1011**.  
- Fiscal note anticipates DNREC implementation staffing costs of **$30,883 in FY2026**, **$31,500 in FY2027**, **$32,130 in FY2028**, with future personnel costs estimated to grow **2%** annually.  
- Fiscal note states it is **unknown** whether future RGGI proceeds will exceed 2025 levels, so it is **unknown** whether the rebate program will have funds in future years.  
- Assumption in fiscal note: Act is effective upon Governor’s signature and **takes effect January 1, 2026**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Direct bill relief if RGGI proceeds rise:** Any CO₂ allowance proceeds above **2025** totals must be used to **defray electricity costs** through **bill reductions** for **Delmarva Power & Light retail electric customers** (effective **January 1, 2026**).  
- **Preserves 2025-level support for existing programs:** Statutory uses (SEU, WAP/LIHEAP, GHG reduction projects, and RGGI administration) are maintained at **2025 total-dollar levels**, per synopsis/fiscal note.  
- **Standardized distribution method:** Rebate is **pro rata** and must be **reflected on customer bills**, which may simplify delivery compared to application-based programs (as described in the bill text).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Limits growth of funding for existing programs:** By fixing existing purposes to **2025 total-dollar levels**, any increase in auction proceeds after 2025 would no longer increase funding for SEU, WAP/LIHEAP, or greenhouse gas reduction projects beyond the 2025 total (synopsis and new § 6046(c)(6) “excess over 2025” rule).  
- **Administrative workload/cost for DNREC:** DNREC must promulgate regulations and administer the program; fiscal note estimates recurring staffing-related costs of **$30,883 (FY2026)** increasing thereafter.  
- **Program may not operate if proceeds do not exceed 2025:** Fiscal note states it is **unknown** whether proceeds will exceed 2025 levels, creating uncertainty about whether rebates will occur in “future years.”

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **2025 baseline amount not stated:** The bill references “the total dollar amount generated in **2025**” but does not specify that dollar figure in the text provided.  
- **Rebate program design details unspecified:** The bill requires DNREC regulations but does not specify (in the text provided) details such as timing/frequency of rebates, how “pro rata” is calculated (e.g., by kWh, customer class, billing period), treatment of new/moved accounts, or handling of arrears.

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds new § 6046(c)(6):  
  - **Beginning January 1, 2026**, and “each year after,” any CO₂ allowance proceeds **in excess of the total dollar amount generated in 2025** must be directed to a **rebate program** to reduce energy costs for **retail electric customers**.  
  - **DNREC** must **promulgate regulations** and **administer** the rebate program.  
  - Rebate proceeds must be provided to **Delmarva Power & Light** for **pro rata distribution** to retail electric customers, reflected as a reduction on **Delmarva Power & Light bills** issued under **Title 26, § 1011**.  
- Per synopsis: “fixes the overall amount of proceeds directed to purposes currently in the Delaware Code to **2025 levels**” and directs any amounts above that to the rebate program.

## Who Benefits
- **Delmarva Power & Light retail electric customers** (Title 26, § 1001(21)), who may receive **bill reductions** if RGGI proceeds exceed 2025 levels.  
- Potentially, **ratepayers broadly within Delmarva Power & Light’s retail customer base**, because distribution is required to be **pro rata** (bill text).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Programs currently funded by RGGI proceeds (e.g., **SEU**, **WAP**, **LIHEAP**, and **Greenhouse Gas Reduction Projects**) may no longer receive increases above **2025 total-dollar levels** if auction proceeds rise (synopsis and “excess over 2025” redirection).  
- **DNREC** may face additional administrative burden and recurring staffing costs (fiscal note).

## Effective Dates and Deadlines
- Fiscal note assumption: Effective upon Governor’s signature and **takes effect January 1, 2026**.  
- Statutory change in bill text: **“Beginning on January 1, 2026 and each year after”** excess proceeds above 2025 must be directed to the rebate program.
