## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend the City of Harrington Charter to revise governance and financial administration rules, including (as described in the bill’s synopsis) removing term limits, changing where City funds may be deposited, adjusting budget and audit deadlines, revising emergency succession, and changing who may sign/approve City checks.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- **Term limits:** The bill’s synopsis states it **removes limitations** on consecutive terms for Mayor and Council; however, the Charter language shown in the bill text **still includes** “Mayor…no more than two (2) consecutive terms” and Council “no more than three (3) consecutive terms,” with an exception if no eligible candidate files.  
- **Banking/deposits:** Allows City funds to be deposited in **federally-insured banking institutions approved by City Council**, rather than requiring banks **located in the City**.  
- **Budget deadline change:** Moves the annual budget submission deadline from **May 1 to June 1** (“On or before the first day of…June each year”).  
- **Revenue-need determination deadline:** Moves deadline to determine annual revenue needs from **May 1 to June 1**.  
- **Emergency succession:** Revises succession when Mayor is unavailable in a “sudden emergency,” removing the **Clerk of Council** and listing order: **Vice Mayor; remaining Council members by years served; City Manager; Chief of Police**.  
- **Check/warrant signing:** Allows, in the Mayor’s absence, a **Vice Mayor or a Council member designated/approved by the Mayor** to sign/countersign checks; also removes the **Treasurer** from the approval/countersign role for paying claims (shifts to City Manager + Mayor or Mayor-approved Council member).  
- **Audit report timing and notice:** Extends deadline for audit report from **within 120 days** to **within 150 days** after fiscal year end; requires public notice in a newspaper **within 30 days** after City Council receives the report.  
- **Enactment:** The enacted version is shown as **Approved June 30, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **More flexible banking options:** Permits deposits in any **federally-insured** bank **approved by City Council**, potentially expanding options beyond banks physically located in Harrington.  
- **More time for budget preparation:** Shifts the budget submission deadline to **June 1** (from **May 1**), giving the Budget Committee and City Manager additional time.  
- **More time for completion of audit report:** Extends the audit report deadline to **150 days** after fiscal year end (from **120 days**).  
- **Clearer continuity of operations during emergencies:** Specifies a defined order of succession and removes the Clerk of Council from the chain, listing specific officials in sequence.  
- **Operational continuity for payments:** Allows a Mayor-approved Council member to sign when the Mayor is absent, and clarifies roles in payment approval (City Manager approval required).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential confusion about term limits:** The synopsis says term limits are removed, but the Charter language displayed still states **2 consecutive terms for Mayor** and **3 consecutive terms for Council**, with an exception when no eligible candidate files.  
- **Reduced local-banking requirement:** Removing the requirement that City funds be placed in a bank **located in the City** could reduce preference for local institutions (the text instead requires a **federally-insured** institution approved by Council).  
- **Longer time to public audit information:** Extending the audit report deadline from **120 to 150 days** could delay availability of audited financial information to the public.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Term limits (as characterized by synopsis):** Synopsis states the bill removes term limits for Mayor and Council; however, the shown Charter text still states:  
  - Mayor: **no more than two (2) consecutive terms**  
  - Council: **no more than three (3) consecutive terms**  
  - Exception: if **no eligible candidate files a petition** for the term-limited incumbent’s office within the Charter’s prescribed time, the incumbent **may remain** for another term.  
- **Vice Mayor selection clarification:** Vice Mayor is elected **from among City Council members** and serves **1 year** or until the organizational meeting the **June** following the next succeeding election.  
- **Bank deposits:** From “federally-insured banking institutions located in the City” to **federally-insured banking institutions approved by City Council**.  
- **Emergency succession:** Order becomes: **Vice Mayor → Council members by years served → City Manager → Chief of Police** (removes **Clerk of the Council**).  
- **Budget submission deadline:** **May 1 → June 1**.  
- **Revenue needs determination deadline:** **May 1 → June 1**.  
- **Check/warrant authorization and signatures:**  
  - Treasurer’s role adjusted so payments are not made except on warrant/check countersigned by **Mayor**, or in Mayor’s absence by **Vice Mayor or Council member designated by the Mayor**.  
  - For claims against the City: payment only by warrant/check **approved by City Manager** and **approved and countersigned by Mayor** or **Council member approved by Mayor** (removes “Mayor and/or Treasurer” structure).  
- **Audit deadlines and notice:**  
  - Audit report due **within 150 days** from fiscal year end (was **120 days**).  
  - Newspaper notice must be published at least once within **30 days** after City Council receives the report.  
  - Audit report must be available for **public inspection**.

## Who Benefits
- **City government administration (Budget Committee/City Manager/City Council):** More time to prepare budget and complete audit deliverables (June 1 deadlines; **150-day** audit report window).  
- **City operations and payables processing:** Clearer authority for signing/countersigning checks when Mayor is absent; City Manager’s approval role is explicit for claims.  
- **Residents/public:** Continued requirement that audit report be publicly inspectable and notice published within **30 days** after Council receipt.

- **Public stakeholders seeking quicker audited financials:** Audit report timeline is extended from **120 to 150 days** after fiscal year end.
