## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 26, §1014 to require electric suppliers’ net-metering interconnection rules to be aligned with the most recent Interstate Renewable Energy Council (IREC) Model Interconnection Procedures within a defined timeframe.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Requires electric suppliers’ interconnection rules for net-metering to be **aligned with the most recent IREC Model Interconnection Procedures**.
- Sets a compliance timeline: alignment must occur **within 12 months of the Procedures’ latest publishing date**.
- Keeps existing statutory direction that net-metering rules must consider **reliability, safety, and capacity** of the electric distribution system.
- Continues to require net-metering customer equipment to meet applicable safety and performance standards (e.g., **National Electrical Code; IEEE; UL; Society of Automotive Engineers**).
- Reaffirms limits on supplier requirements: suppliers may not require compliant customers to install **excessive controls**, perform/pay for **unnecessary tests**, or buy **excessive liability insurance**.
- The bill text references a past municipal deadline (“**no later than July 24, 2008**”), while adding the new 12-month alignment requirement tied to IREC publication timing.
- Does not state an appropriation, rate impact, or explicit enforcement penalty.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Standardization and modernization:** Requires alignment with the **most recent** IREC Model Interconnection Procedures, which may promote more up-to-date and consistent interconnection practices across suppliers (explicitly required by the bill).
- **Predictability for customers:** Incorporating a recognized model procedure on a regular update cycle (**within 12 months** of latest publication) may improve clarity for eligible net-metering customers seeking interconnection (supported by the alignment requirement).
- **Customer protection:** Reinforces that suppliers cannot impose **excessive controls, unnecessary tests, or excessive liability insurance** on eligible customers who meet applicable standards (explicitly stated in §1014(e)(5)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Administrative burden on suppliers:** Electric suppliers must track IREC publication updates and revise interconnection rules **within 12 months** of the latest publishing date (explicit requirement).
- **Ongoing update cadence:** Because the trigger is the “most recent version” and “latest publishing date,” suppliers may face recurring compliance updates whenever IREC publishes revisions (implied by the update mechanism in the text).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Scope of “electric supplier”:** The amended language applies to “an electric supplier’s interconnection rules,” but the text does not clarify whether and how this applies to **municipal electric companies and electric cooperatives** beyond existing references.
- **Meaning of “aligned with”:** The bill requires rules be “aligned” with IREC Procedures, but does not define whether partial deviations are allowed or what constitutes sufficient alignment.
- **Which IREC document controls:** The synopsis refers to “Model Interconnection **Procedures**,” while the amended line references “Model Interconnection **Rules**” as a guide and then inserts “Procedures (Procedures).” The controlling document and terminology could be interpreted inconsistently.
- **Effect of the July 24, 2008 municipal deadline:** The text retains “Municipal electric companies shall establish interconnection rules no later than **July 24, 2008**,” creating potential confusion about how that historic deadline interacts with the new 12-month updating requirement.
- **Enforcement mechanism:** The bill does not specify how compliance with the 12-month alignment requirement will be enforced or adjudicated.
