## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Allow municipalities with populations of **50,000 or more** to adopt **more restrictive local ordinances** governing the **location of new off-premises liquor establishments**, and require the Alcoholic Beverage Control Commissioner to **deny licenses** that do not comply with those ordinances.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Amends **Title 4, § 543** of the Delaware Code to add municipal authority over liquor-store location rules.
- Applies only to **municipalities with a population of 50,000 or more**.
- Covers **new establishments** seeking licenses to sell alcoholic liquor **for consumption off the premises** (i.e., off-premises sales).
- Authorizes eligible municipalities to set location rules **more restrictive than state law** by **ordinance**.
- Requires the **Alcoholic Beverage Control Commissioner** to **refuse** an off-premises license application that **does not comply** with a qualifying municipal ordinance.
- Does not specify any changes to on-premises licensing (e.g., bars/restaurants) in the provided text.
- Does not specify a statewide effective date, funding, or implementation timeline in the provided text.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Greater local control (municipal option):** Municipalities with **50,000+** population may adopt ordinances with **more restrictive** location rules than state law for new off-premises liquor establishments (Section 1, §543(k)(1)).
- **Clear licensing consequence:** The Commissioner **must refuse** an off-premises license that does not comply with the municipal ordinance adopted under §543(k)(1), strengthening enforceability through the licensing process (Section 1, §543(k)(2)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Reduced uniformity across the state:** Location requirements for new off-premises liquor licenses may vary by municipality (limited to those with **50,000+** population) because local ordinances may be **more restrictive than state law** (§543(k)(1)).
- **Potential barrier to new businesses in covered municipalities:** New off-premises license applicants can be denied solely for failing to meet municipal location rules that exceed state requirements (§543(k)(2)).

## Key Policy Changes
- Adds a new subsection **§543(k)** to Title 4 to:
  - Permit any municipality with a **population ≥ 50,000** to adopt, by **ordinance**, **more restrictive** rules on the **location** of **new off-premises alcoholic liquor establishments** than those in state law (§543(k)(1)).
  - Require the **Alcoholic Beverage Control Commissioner** to **refuse** an off-premises license that fails to comply with such a municipal ordinance (§543(k)(2)).

## Who Benefits
- **Municipal governments** in municipalities with **50,000+** population that want additional authority to restrict where new off-premises liquor establishments may locate.
- **Residents** of those municipalities to the extent the municipality uses this authority to shape local placement of new off-premises alcohol outlets (authority granted by §543(k)(1)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Applicants for new off-premises liquor licenses** in municipalities with **50,000+** population, if local ordinances impose stricter location limits and lead to license refusal (§543(k)(2)).
- **Businesses seeking to open new off-premises alcohol outlets** in covered municipalities, if potential sites are disqualified by local ordinance.
