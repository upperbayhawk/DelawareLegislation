## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Modernize and increase penalties for commercial environmental violations in Delaware, and direct a higher share of collected penalties to community environmental projects in areas impacted by violations (the “Pollution Accountability Act”).

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Increases multiple civil, administrative, and criminal penalty caps and minimums across Titles 3, 7, and 16, commonly raising maximums to **$40,000** per violation/day in several programs.  
- Reduces DNREC’s retained share of certain civil/administrative penalties under **7 Del. C. § 6005(d)** from **75% to 60%**, increasing the share available for community projects.  
- Increases the **Community Environmental Project Fund** share of covered civil/administrative penalties from **25% to 40%** (**7 Del. C. § 6042(b)**).  
- Requires **no less than 40%** of certain **criminal penalties** under **7 Del. C. § 6013(h)** to be deposited into the Community Environmental Project Fund, with **60%** appropriated to DNREC.  
- Adds/clarifies community-project prioritization near violations: projects are evaluated for location within the affected watershed/airshed and within a **2-mile radius** of the violation/infraction (**7 Del. C. § 6042(d)**).  
- Expands appeal rights in chronic violator proceedings so **either party** may appeal the Environmental Appeals Board decision to Superior Court (**7 Del. C. § 7904(a)(4)b**).  
- Keeps existing administrative process elements in place, including the **30-day** period to request a public hearing after receipt of notice for an administrative penalty under **7 Del. C. § 6005(b)(3)**.  

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Stronger deterrence through higher penalties:** Multiple programs increase minimums/maximums (e.g., **$5,000–$40,000** ranges in several civil penalty provisions), potentially increasing compliance incentives.  
- **More penalty money directed to impacted communities:** Community Environmental Project Fund allocation increases from **25% to 40%** for covered civil/administrative penalties (**§ 6042(b)**), and **≥40%** of specified criminal penalties must go to the Fund (**§ 6013(h)(4)**).  
- **Geographic targeting of benefits:** The Fund prioritizes projects within the affected watershed/airshed and within a **2-mile radius** of the violation/infraction (**§ 6042(d)**).  
- **Clearer due process/appeal symmetry for chronic violator cases:** Allows **either party** to appeal the Environmental Appeals Board decision (**§ 7904(a)(4)b**).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Higher financial exposure for regulated entities:** Increased per-day/per-violation penalties (often up to **$40,000**) increase potential liability for violations across multiple chapters.  
- **Reduced share of certain penalties retained by DNREC under § 6005(d):** DNREC’s portion is reduced from **75% to 60%**, which may reduce funds available to DNREC for chapter purposes relative to prior law (while increasing community-directed funds).  
- **Community project spending may be waived:** Even with prioritization, **§ 6042(d)** allows the Secretary to waive certain expenditure requirements with concurrence of the **Director of OMB** and **Controller General**, potentially limiting local project funding in some cases.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Effective date mechanics for operational changes:** The bill is “Approved July 16, 2025,” but does not otherwise state whether changes apply to violations occurring before that date.  
- **How “two-mile radius” is measured and applied:** § 6042(d) references a **two-mile radius** as part of the evaluation process, but does not define measurement method or what qualifies as “adversely affected.”  
- **Criteria for “insufficient or too large” penalty amounts:** § 6042(d) allows the Secretary to determine amounts are “insufficient or too large” for appropriate expenditures, without numeric thresholds.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Title 3 (Nutrient management certification penalties):**  
  - **3 Del. C. § 2244(b)** fines increase from **$25–$1,000** to **$250–$5,000** per violation; daily continued violation cap increases from **$10,000** to **$40,000**.
- **Title 7 (DNREC general enforcement):**  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6005(b)(1)** civil penalties increase from **$1,000–$10,000** to **$5,000–$40,000** per completed violation; each day of continued violation is a separate violation.  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6005(b)(3)** administrative penalties cap increases from **$10,000** to **$40,000** per day; violator has **30 days** from receipt of notice to request a public hearing.  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6005(d)** DNREC share of civil/administrative penalties changes from **75% to 60%** (with expenditures approved by OMB Director and Controller General; quarterly and annual reporting requirements).  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6013(h)(2)** subsequent criminal conviction max increases from **$1,500** to **$5,000** per day (within a **10-year** period).  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6013(h)(4)** sets distribution: **≥40%** of penalties under (h)(1)-(3) to Community Environmental Project Fund; **60%** to DNREC.  
- **Title 7 (Oil pollution liability):**  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6205(a)** civil penalties increase from **$1,000–$10,000** to **$5,000–$40,000** per day.  
- **Title 7 (Hazardous waste management):**  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6309(b)** civil penalties increase from **$1,000–$25,000** to **$5,000–$40,000** per day.  
- **Title 7 (Noise control):**  
  - **7 Del. C. § 7108(h)** fines increase from **$25–$500** to **$250–$2,500** per violation/day.  
  - **7 Del. C. § 7108(i)** willful/negligent penalties increase from **$500–$3,000** to **$1,000–$10,000** per day.  
- **Title 7 (Chronic violators):**  
  - **7 Del. C. § 7904(a)(4)b** allows **either party** to appeal Environmental Appeals Board chronic-violator decisions to Superior Court.  
  - **7 Del. C. § 7906** increases chronic-violator administrative penalty cap from **$10,000** to **$40,000** per day; allows more granular counting of simultaneous/multiple intermittent violations as separately punishable within each day.  
- **Title 7 (Hazardous Substances Cleanup Act enforcement):**  
  - **7 Del. C. § 9109(h)(2)** civil penalty for refusing to comply increases from **$10,000** to **$40,000** per day; noncompliance period deemed to commence on the day of the Secretary’s decision and continue until full compliance.  
  - Maintains remedial cost multiplier: at least equal to but not more than **3 times** remedial costs (**§ 9109(h)(1)**).  
- **Community Environmental Project Fund changes (Title 7):**  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6042(b)** Fund share increases **25% → 40%** of covered civil/administrative penalties.  
  - **7 Del. C. § 6042(d)** prioritizes projects benefiting most-impacted communities; evaluates whether located within watershed/airshed adversely affected and within a **2-mile radius**; requires records identifying location of each civil/administrative penalty; includes waiver provisions with OMB Director and Controller General concurrence.  
- **Title 16 (Stream pollution supplying drinking water):**  
  - **16 Del. C. § 1301(b)** fines increase from **$1,000–$5,000** to **$5,000–$15,000**.

## Who Benefits
- **Communities near violating facilities/locations**, through increased Community Environmental Project Fund allocation (**40%**) and prioritization for projects within a **2-mile radius** and affected watershed/airshed (**§ 6042(d)**).  
- **State environmental enforcement efforts (DNREC and related programs)** through higher penalty ceilings and maintained authority to seek injunctions and collect penalties (e.g., **§ 6005(b)(1)**, **§ 9109(h)**).  

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Regulated commercial entities and facilities** subject to Titles 3, 7, and 16 environmental requirements, due to increased fines/penalties (often up to **$40,000 per day/violation** in specified sections).  
- **Entities accused of chronic violator status**, due to increased daily penalty cap (**$40,000**) and potential for multiple punishable violations within a day under **§ 7906**.

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Higher penalty schedules** across multiple chapters (see “Key Policy Changes”), including:  
  - Up to **$40,000** per completed violation (**§ 6005(b)(1)**) and up to **$40,000 per day** administrative penalties (**§ 6005(b)(3)**).  
  - Chronic violators: up to **$40,000 per day per violation** (**§ 7906**), with potential separate punishments for simultaneous/multiple intermittent violations within each day.  
  - Refusal to comply with cleanup orders: up to **$40,000 per day** plus remedial costs up to **3×** (**§ 9109(h)**).  
- **Fund allocation requirements:**  
  - **40%** of covered civil/administrative penalties must go to the Community Environmental Project Fund (**§ 6042(b)**).  
  - **≥40%** of certain criminal penalties under **§ 6013(h)** must go to the Fund (**§ 6013(h)(4)**).  
- **Appeals:** In chronic violator cases, **either party** may appeal Environmental Appeals Board decisions to Superior Court (**§ 7904(a)(4)b**).  
- **Spending oversight/reporting:** For § 6005 penalties, expenditures/transfers require approval by **OMB Director** and **Controller General**; DNREC must submit **quarterly reports** and provide **annual reporting** to the **Joint Finance Committee** (**§ 6005(d)**).
