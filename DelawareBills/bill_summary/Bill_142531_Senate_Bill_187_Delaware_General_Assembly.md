## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
To consolidate and modernize Delaware’s educator and school-based support professional student loan assistance programs by shifting from lender-directed loan repayments to employee-paid stipends through the State’s central payroll, and by combining eligible roles into one “Public School Employee Support Program for High Need Areas.”

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Combines three student loan repayment programs into one program renamed the **“Public School Employee Support Program for High Need Areas”** (amending **14 Del. C. § 3437**).  
- Expands the consolidated program’s covered workforce to include **educators, speech-language pathologists, school counselors, school psychologists, and school social workers** (definition of “public school employee”).  
- Changes disbursement from **payments sent to lenders** to **stipends paid to employees via the State’s central payroll operation** (program disbursement mechanism revised in § 3437).  
- Sets award size under the consolidated program at **minimum $1,000** and **maximum $2,000** per award, with limits of **1 award per year** and **no more than 5 lifetime awards**.  
- Requires eligibility baseline of being a **full-time public school employee at a school in a high-needs area**, plus **at least 1 school year** of service (with role-specific requirements).  
- If funding is insufficient, prioritizes applicants (1) in both a **high-needs certification area** and **high-needs school**, and/or (2) with **greatest financial need**, as determined by the Office.  
- Maintains and updates annual reporting to House and Senate Education Committee chairs with de-identified summary data each fiscal year, included in the annual report under **14 Del. C. § 3402**.  
- Leaves scholarship programs for **Speech-Language Pathologists** (§ 3434) and **Mental Health Services** (§ 3439) in place; these scholarships remain **contingent on General Assembly funding** and renewable for **up to 2 years** of graduate study.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Reduces administrative payment failures/delays** by paying employees through the **State central payroll operation** rather than issuing “600 or more individual checks each year” to lenders (Synopsis; § 3437(f)(6)).  
- **Addresses tax-withholding confusion/hardship described in the Synopsis** by changing the payment method away from lender-directed payments that created taxable-income withholding issues.  
- **Streamlines program structure** by consolidating previously separate programs into a single framework and a single application process referenced in the Synopsis.  
- **Ensures a predictable award range** under the consolidated program (**$1,000–$2,000**) with clear annual and lifetime limits (**1/year; 5 lifetime**) (§ 3437(g); § 3437(f)(7)).  
- **Transparency and legislative oversight** via required annual reporting metrics (applications/awards, demographics, certification areas, school names and student subgroup percentages) (§ 3437(j)).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Award cap may be lower than prior loan repayment caps**: the prior lender-payment programs in §§ 3434 and 3439 specify loan payments up to **$5,000 per year** with **up to 3 lifetime payments**; the consolidated program award is capped at **$2,000** (§ 3437(g)), potentially reducing maximum annual benefit compared with those program caps as written in the amended sections.  
- **Eligibility is tied to “high-needs area” schools** for the consolidated program (must be a full-time public school employee at a school in a high-needs area), which may exclude otherwise similar employees not working in a designated high-needs area (§ 3437(d)(1)).  
- **Financial-need prioritization is largely discretionary**: the Office has “sole discretion” to determine financial need and to prioritize and determine awards (§ 3437(f)(4)-(5)), which may reduce predictability for applicants.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- The Synopsis states the Act combines programs “into a single program under **§ 4163** of Title 14,” but the bill text shown amends **§§ 3434, 3437, and 3439** and renames § 3437; the relationship to § 4163 is **unclear in the provided text**.  
- The bill does not specify **how “high-needs area” is defined** within the excerpted amendments, beyond references to Office identification and regulations (§ 3437(f)(3)(a); § 3437(h)).  
- The bill states the Office will request information for financial need and has discretion on weighting (income, spousal income, dependents, loan totals), but does not specify **what thresholds** qualify as “greatest financial need” (§ 3437(f)(4)).  
- Not specified in source text: whether the stipend payment method changes the **state tax treatment**; the Synopsis discusses tax impacts but does not codify tax rules.

## Educational Impact
- Program purpose is to improve educational outcomes by retaining:  
  - educators in shortage certification areas and hardest-to-staff schools; and  
  - support professionals (speech-language pathologists, school counselors, school psychologists, school social workers) (§ 3437(a)(2)).  
- Scholarships remain for training pipelines:  
  - **Speech-Language Pathologist Scholarship** for full-time master’s students in qualifying Delaware programs, renewable up to **2 years** (§ 3434(a)).  
  - **Mental Health Services Scholarship** for full-time master’s students leading to certification as counselor/psychologist/social worker, renewable up to **2 years** (§ 3439(a)).

## Health Impact
- Indirect support for student mental health services by supporting retention of **school counselors, school psychologists, and school social workers** as covered public school employees under the consolidated program (§ 3437(a)(2)(b); § 3437(b)(9)) and by maintaining the mental health services scholarship (§ 3439(a)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Renames and reframes** § 3437 from “High Needs Educator Student Loan Payment Program” to **“Public School Employee Support Program for High Need Areas.”**  
- **Expands covered roles** under § 3437 to include **speech-language pathologists** and **school-based mental health practitioners** (counselors, psychologists, social workers) via “public school employee” definition (§ 3437(b)(9)).  
- **Shifts disbursement** from lender payments to **stipend payment through the State central payroll operation** (§ 3437(f)(6)).  
- **Sets award parameters**: **$1,000 minimum; $2,000 maximum**; **1 per year**; **5 lifetime maximum** (§ 3437(g); § 3437(f)(7)).  
- **Eligibility structure**: must be **full-time** in a **high-needs area school**, have a **qualified educational loan**, and meet role-specific criteria including **1 school year** employment/service and (for certain roles) DOE licensure/certification (§ 3437(d)).  
- **Prioritization when underfunded**: preference for applicants in high-needs certification areas and high-needs schools and/or those with greatest financial need (§ 3437(f)(3)-(4)).  
- **Reporting**: requires annual de-identified summary reporting with specified data elements, included in the § 3402 annual report (§ 3437(j)).

## Who Benefits
- **Full-time public school employees in high-needs area schools** who have qualified educational loans, including:  
  - educators,  
  - speech-language pathologists,  
  - school counselors,  
  - school psychologists, and  
  - school social workers (§ 3437(b)(9); § 3437(d)).  
- Delaware public schools and students in high-need contexts, through intended retention effects stated in the purpose (§ 3437(a)(2)).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- Applicants who previously could receive up to **$5,000/year** in loan payments under the separate SLP or mental health loan repayment program caps as written in §§ 3434 and 3439, if the consolidated award cap of **$2,000** is the operative benefit available to them under the combined structure (§ 3437(g) vs. §§ 3434(b)(8), 3439(b)(8)).  
- Public school employees who are not in a **high-needs area school**, because § 3437 eligibility requires employment at a school in a high-needs area (§ 3437(d)(1)).
