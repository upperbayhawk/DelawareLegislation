## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Amend Title 25 of the Delaware Code to expand and clarify tenants’ **right of redemption** and related payment rules in **evictions for nonpayment of rent**, allowing tenants to **avoid eviction by paying all court-determined past-due amounts and court-awarded costs/fees before the eviction is executed**, with specified limits.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Allows a tenant facing **summary possession for failure to pay rent** to **redeem** the rental unit/lot by paying **all past-due amounts as determined by the court + all court-awarded costs and fees** **any time before actual execution of the eviction order**.  
- Requires landlord/agent, after receiving redemption payment, to **immediately notify the court and the constable/sheriff** and make **all reasonable efforts** to prevent execution of the judgment.  
- Requires the court, upon notification/evidence of redemption, to issue an **immediate stay** and provide a **minimum of 5 days** for appropriate evidence/filings to be submitted.  
- Requires the court to **vacate any judgment of possession** and **terminate any writs** once notified or provided adequate evidence of redemption.  
- Limits redemption: it **does not apply** to a tenant with **3 judgments of possession for failure to pay rent in the 12 months** before the action is initiated.  
- Prohibits landlords from **refusing to accept rental payments** made **by the tenant or by another person/entity on the tenant’s behalf**.  
- Updates notice language in nonpayment provisions to reflect that the landlord may **file a court action to terminate** the rental agreement after required notice periods (including a **7-day** period in Chapter 70).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Prevents eviction when arrears are cured:** Tenants can remain housed if they pay **all past-due amounts + court-awarded costs/fees** before execution of the eviction order (§5716A).  
- **Clear stay and vacatur process:** The bill requires an **immediate stay** and eventual **vacatur** of the possession judgment/writs upon redemption, providing a defined mechanism to halt eviction once paid (§5716A(3), (5)).  
- **Facilitates third-party payment:** Payments may be made by **another person or entity**, and an **electronic or written check issued by any entity (including a governmental entity)** is legally effective (§5716A(4); §§5502(c)-(d); §7016(b)(3)a-b).  
- **Reduces payment refusal risk:** Landlords are expressly barred from refusing rent payments made by or on behalf of tenants (§5502(c)-(d); §7016(b)(3)a-b).

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential delay/administrative burden in eviction execution:** Required **immediate stay** and **minimum 5-day** period for filings/evidence may extend timelines in some cases (§5716A(3)).  
- **Landlord collection/leverage reduced once payment tendered/accepted:** If rent is paid (before or after action) the landlord may be limited in initiating or maintaining actions for past-due rent, as specified (§5502(c)-(d); §7016(b)(3)a-b).  
- **Redemption limited only by a “3 judgments in 12 months” threshold:** Landlords may view the threshold as insufficient to address repeat nonpayment behaviors, while the bill still provides redemption broadly up to execution (§5716A(6)).

## Key Policy Changes
- **Chapter 57 (Right of redemption / stay of proceedings):**
  - Creates/clarifies a tenant right to redeem **any time before actual execution of the eviction order** by paying **all past-due amounts (as determined by the court) + court-awarded costs and fees** (§5716A(b)(1)).  
  - Requires landlord/agent to **immediately notify** court and constable/sheriff and make **reasonable efforts** to stop execution after payment (§5716A(b)(2)).  
  - Requires the court to issue an **immediate stay** and provide a **minimum of 5 days** for submissions upon notification/evidence (§5716A(b)(3)).  
  - Treats an **electronic or written check issued by any entity (including a governmental entity)** as having the same legal effect as tenant payment (§5716A(b)(4)).  
  - Requires the court to **vacate** possession judgments and **terminate** writs upon adequate notice/evidence (§5716A(b)(5)).  
  - **Excludes** tenants with **3 judgments of possession for failure to pay rent** in the **12 months** before action initiation (§5716A(b)(6)).  

- **Chapter 55 (Landlord remedies for failure to pay rent):**
  - Adjusts notice/termination language (including changing a referenced minimum notice period to **not less than 7 days** in the amended text shown) and clarifies landlord may file court action after notice (§5502(a)).  
  - Prohibits landlords from refusing rental payments by or on behalf of tenant; if rent is paid and accepted (without written reservation of rights), landlord may be limited from initiating or maintaining certain actions (§5502(c)-(d)).  

- **Chapter 70 (Manufactured home communities / due cause noncompliance):**
  - For nonpayment, keeps a **7-day** cure window from date of mailing/personal service and revises language to state landlord will **file a court action** to terminate agreement (§7016(b)(3)).  
  - Adds/clarifies that payment by tenant or another person/entity before action prevents initiation of summary possession; payment after action prevents maintaining the action for past-due rent; landlord may not refuse such payments (§7016(b)(3)a-b).

## Who Benefits
- **Tenants** facing eviction for nonpayment who can cure arrears before the eviction is executed (§5716A).  
- **Third-party payors** (including governmental entities) assisting tenants with arrears, whose checks are recognized as legally effective (§5716A(4)).  

## Who May Be Negatively Affected
- **Landlords/landlord agents** who may experience additional procedural steps and potential delays once redemption payment is made (immediate stay; minimum 5-day submission period) (§5716A(3)).  
- **Constables/sheriffs** may face additional coordination requirements due to immediate notification and stay/vacatur processes (§5716A(2), (5)).
