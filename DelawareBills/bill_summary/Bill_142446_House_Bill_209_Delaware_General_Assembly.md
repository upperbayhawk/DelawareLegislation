## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Update Delaware Title 18 health insurance provisions that incorporate federal Affordable Care Act (ACA) requirements by updating the referenced “as in effect” date for federal law/regulations to **January 1, 2025** (from **January 1, 2018**) across several sections.

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Updates multiple Title 18 provisions to reference ACA law and implementing regulations **as in effect on January 1, 2025** (instead of January 1, 2018).
- Applies to key ACA-related insurance rules in Delaware law, including **essential health benefits**, **cost-sharing limits**, **special enrollment periods**, **guaranteed availability**, and **nondiscrimination**.
- Maintains Delaware’s incorporation of **ACA § 1302** (essential health benefits and cost-sharing) and **ACA § 1557** (nondiscrimination), but refreshes the regulatory reference date to **1/1/2025**.
- Keeps the requirement that enrollees have **30 calendar days** after a qualifying event to elect coverage during a special enrollment period (group and individual markets).
- Keeps special enrollment effective-date alignment with **45 C.F.R. § 155.420(b)**, now referenced **as in effect on January 1, 2025**.
- Continues “child-only plan” requirements for insurers offering any ACA metal level plan: must also offer that level for individuals **under age 21** (as of beginning of plan year).
- Act was **approved July 21, 2025**.

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Modernizes statutory cross-references:** Updates incorporated federal ACA regulatory date to **January 1, 2025**, reducing reliance on outdated **January 1, 2018** regulatory language in state insurance requirements.
- **Clarity/consistency for compliance:** Insurers and regulators can align Delaware requirements with ACA rules **as of 1/1/2025** in areas expressly tied to federal provisions (e.g., **essential health benefits**, **cost-sharing limits**, **special enrollment** timing rules).
- **Continues consumer protections referenced in statute:** The bill retains Delaware’s application of ACA-related provisions such as **no lifetime/annual limits** (in referenced sections), **nondiscrimination (ACA § 1557)**, and **EHB/cost-sharing parameters (ACA § 1302)** while updating the reference date.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Does not describe substantive policy outcomes:** The bill text states it “updates a cross-reference,” and does not detail how the **January 1, 2025** regulatory baseline differs from **January 1, 2018**, making impacts hard to assess from the text alone.
- **Potential compliance adjustment burden:** If federal rules changed between **1/1/2018** and **1/1/2025**, insurers and plans subject to these Delaware provisions may need to adjust policies/administration accordingly; the bill text does not quantify the extent of change.
- **Regulatory dependence:** By incorporating federal law/regulations “as in effect” on a specific date (**1/1/2025**), future federal changes after that date would not automatically be incorporated unless Delaware updates the reference again.

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Which specific federal regulatory changes are being incorporated?** The bill updates the reference date to **January 1, 2025**, but does not identify what changed from **January 1, 2018** to **January 1, 2025** for each cited federal provision (e.g., §1302(b), §1557, 45 C.F.R. §155.420(b)).
- **Commissioner regulation timing:** Sections requiring the Commissioner to issue regulations defining “essential health benefits” remain, but the bill does not specify deadlines for issuing/updating those regulations.
- **Scope of “Delaware law; and applicable state regulations”:** The amended EHB definition includes “Delaware law; and applicable state regulations,” but the bill does not specify how conflicts between incorporated federal definitions and Delaware regulations are resolved.
