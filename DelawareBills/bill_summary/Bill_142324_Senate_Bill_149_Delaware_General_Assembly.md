## Distilled Summary

## Purpose
Create a uniform, optional local framework allowing counties and municipalities to exempt Low-Income Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) properties from property taxes and instead require an annual payment in lieu of taxes (PILOT), and to make those exempt LIHTC properties also exempt from school district property taxes. (Titles 9 and 14)

## Executive Summary (5-8 bullets)
- Authorizes a county or municipality, **by ordinance**, to exempt **LIHTC properties** from **property taxes for public purposes**.  
- Requires any LIHTC property receiving the exemption to make an **annual PILOT equal to 5% of the property’s “annual revenue”** for the most recent fiscal year.  
- Defines **“annual revenue”** as the **total net revenue** reported on the **DSHA Statement of Profit and Loss**, including **rent**, **financial**, and **other** revenue.  
- Limits eligibility to LIHTC properties **in compliance with all applicable LIHTC requirements** and that **apply for the exemption** like other exemptions.  
- Directs that PILOT amounts collected be **divided among political subdivisions** that can levy taxes on the property **in proportion to their respective tax rates**.  
- Provides that PILOTs are collected **in the same manner and at the same time** as property taxes under **Chapter 80, Title 9**, with **penalties on unpaid balances** allowed at a rate set by local ordinance.  
- Amends school tax law so that LIHTC properties exempted under a local ordinance are **also exempt from school district taxation**.  
- Fiscal note estimates **local school revenue loss** statewide of **$2,576,277–$3,011,617 per year** for **FY2027, FY2028, and FY2029** (assumes all jurisdictions adopt ordinances).

## Pros (Potential Benefits)
- **Uniform statewide framework:** Establishes consistent statutory rules (definitions, 5% formula, collection method) for local governments choosing to implement a LIHTC PILOT program.  
- **Predictable PILOT formula:** Sets a clear payment amount—**5% of annual revenue (as defined and reported to DSHA)**—instead of standard property taxation.  
- **Recognizes affordable housing role (as stated in synopsis):** The bill explicitly frames PILOTs as acknowledging “the public benefit of the affordable housing provided by LIHTC properties.”  
- **Clear collection authority:** Payments are collected like other taxes under **Title 9, Chapter 80**, and local ordinances may set **penalties** and **abatement procedures** for nonpayment.

## Cons (Potential Drawbacks)
- **Potential reduction in local school revenues:** Fiscal note projects statewide **local school revenue loss** of **$2,576,277–$3,011,617 annually** (FY2027–FY2029) in areas adopting the exemption.  
- **School district tax exemption is automatic once local exemption is granted:** Under amended **Title 14 § 1902(a)(9)**, real estate exempted as a LIHTC property under the local ordinance is **excluded from school district taxation**, which may reduce districts’ local tax base.  
- **Local policy variability:** Because adoption is **optional (“may, by ordinance”)**, outcomes and revenue effects may differ across counties/municipalities based on whether and how they enact ordinances (including penalty rates).

## Open Questions / Ambiguities
- **Ordinance specifics not set in statute:** The bill allows penalties “at a rate and in a manner set by ordinance” and abatement procedures set locally; the statute does not specify uniform penalty caps or standards.  
- **Scope of “annual revenue” terminology:** Defined as “the sum reported as total net revenue” on DSHA Statement of Profit and Loss, but also says it includes “total rent revenue, total financial revenue, and total other revenue,” which may create interpretive questions about whether “total net revenue” is meant to be a grossing concept or a specific DSHA line item.  
- **Eligibility coverage for LIHTC properties not reporting through DSHA (fiscal note limitation):** Fiscal note states roughly **one-third** of LIHTC properties are “not required to make property tax payments through DSHA,” may not be eligible, and were not calculated—how those properties are treated is not resolved in the bill text.  
- **Whether municipalities must participate for municipal tax relief:** The bill authorizes counties or municipalities to act; it does not specify how PILOT treatment works if only one level of government adopts an ordinance.

## Key Policy Changes
- **Creates Title 9, Chapter 81, Subchapter IV (LIHTC properties):**  
  - Adds definitions for **annual revenue**, **DSHA**, **LIHTC**, and **LIHTC property**.  
  - Authorizes **county/municipal ordinances** to exempt LIHTC properties from property taxation for public purposes.  
  - Requires exempt LIHTC properties to pay **annual PILOT = 5% of annual revenue** for the most recent fiscal year.  
  - Requires LIHTC compliance and an exemption application process consistent with other exemptions.  
  - Allocates PILOT proceeds among taxing subdivisions **proportionate to tax rates**.  
  - Sets collection timing/method parallel to property taxes; authorizes **ordinance-set penalties** and **abatement procedures**.
- **Amends New Castle County exemption application statute (Title 9 § 8106A):** Adds LIHTC property exemption under an ordinance as a covered exemption category.  
- **Amends school district tax authority (Title 14 § 1902):** Excludes LIHTC properties exempted under the ordinance framework from school district property taxation.

## Who Benefits
- **Owners/operators of LIHTC properties** in jurisdictions adopting an exemption ordinance (potentially reduced or restructured tax liability through a **5% of annual revenue PILOT** in place of property taxes).  
- **Counties/municipalities seeking a standardized approach** to LIHTC property tax treatment through local ordinance authority.  
- **Affordable housing stakeholders** to the extent the bill’s stated purpose recognizes LIHTC public benefits (as described in the synopsis).

## Who May Be Negatively Affected

- **Other local taxing entities** that would otherwise collect property taxes on LIHTC properties, depending on how the 5% PILOT compares to prior tax collections (no comparative amounts specified in bill text).

## Enforcement, Penalties, or Compliance Requirements
- **Compliance requirement:** LIHTC property must be **in compliance with all applicable LIHTC requirements** to receive/retain the exemption.  
- **Application requirement:** LIHTC property must **apply for the exemption** “in the same manner as required for other exemptions.”  
- **Collection:** Receiver of taxes and county treasurer collect PILOTs **like property taxes** under Chapter 80.
